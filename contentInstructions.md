# OurMocks — Content Instructions (v2.2)

**How every notes PDF for CUET UG · IISER IAT · NISER NEST · COMEDK UGET must be written, designed and checked.**
Read this file BEFORE writing or editing any topic. If a rule here conflicts with an old PDF, this file wins.

Ultimate goal (the one-line test): **a Class 11–12 student reads our notes once and gains real concept clarity — without a teacher beside them.**

---

## 1. WHO WE ARE WRITING FOR (the POV rule)

Our reader is a **16–18 year old in Class 11 or 12**, studying under coaching/school load, reading on a phone screen or a printout, often at night, usually tired.

- They **cannot read huge paragraphs**. Walls of text get skipped. If a paragraph must exist, it carries ONE idea in 3–4 short sentences, then a bullet list takes over.
- They think in **pictures, examples and steps** — not in definitions or jargon.
- They lose interest the moment text sounds like a textbook written for a graduation student. If a sentence would look at home in a B.Sc. book, rewrite it simpler.
- **The POV test for every page you write:** "Will a 17-year-old understand this on the first read, without re-reading?" If not — split it, bullet it, add an example, or delete it.

### 1.1 Writing format rules (non-negotiable)

| Rule | How it looks on the page |
|---|---|
| Points over paragraphs | Default to bullets/numbered points. A full paragraph is the exception, used only to open a section (3–4 sentences max) or to tell a one-line story. |
| One idea per bullet | Each bullet = one fact, one step, or one example. Never pack three facts into one bullet. |
| Bold the anchor | Every bullet starts with a **bold keyword** (e.g. `- *Frequency never changes.* The source sets...`). The student should revise from bold words alone. |
| Short sentences | Average sentence ≤ 15–18 words. If a sentence has a comma and an "and" and a "because", split it. |
| No jargon without a translation | Every technical term is explained in the same sentence it first appears, in plain words. |
| Steps for anything procedural | Any method (solving, deriving, identifying) becomes a numbered step list: Step 1 → Step 2 → ... |
| Numbers and units always | "60 cm", "+4 for correct", "109.5°". Never "about sixty centimetres". |

### 1.1b THE SIMPLE-LANGUAGE RULE (v2.2 — strict)

The reader is **not a PhD**. They should feel **someone is teaching the topic to them**, personally, while they read. This is a hard rule, not a style preference:

- **Choose the shortest everyday word.** "Show" not "elucidate", "use" not "utilise", "start" not "commence", "leftover" not "residual", "spread out" not "distributed". If a word needs a dictionary for a 16-year-old, replace it.
- **One teacher, talking.** Write like a friendly senior sitting beside the student: "Look at what happens when...", "Here is the trick you will actually use", "Do not worry — this looks scarier than it is".
- **Never graduation-level phrasing.** If a sentence would survive in a B.Sc./B.Com. textbook unchanged, rewrite it simpler. Terms like "notwithstanding", "in terms of the paradigm", "it behoves" are banned.
- **Explain the term the moment it appears**, in the same sentence, in everyday words — then reuse it freely.
- **Test every section aloud:** if you cannot imagine saying it to a Class 11 student without them glazing over, rewrite it as bullets + examples.

### 1.2 Subject-specific design rules

**Mathematics & Physics — "break it down, then hand me the shortcuts":**
- **Break every concept into micro-steps.** Before any formula, show what is happening physically/geometrically in 2–3 bullet points. Formula first = confusion; picture first = clarity.
- **Simpler techniques:** after each concept, give the shortest exam-practical way to get the answer (the "do this in the exam" version), clearly separated from the understanding version.
- **Formula Cheatsheet (mandatory):** every Maths/Physics PDF ends its content portion with a one-stop *Formula Cheatsheet* section — every formula of the topic in a compact table (formula + what each letter means + when to use). A student should be able to revise the whole topic from this one page.
- Show one **worked example per major technique**, with every step written out (no "it can be shown that", no skipped lines).

**Chemistry & Physics — "tell me the exceptions":**
- **Exceptions & Traps section (mandatory):** every Chemistry PDF, and every Physics PDF where genuine exceptions exist, carries an *Exceptions & Traps* section near the end — a table or bullet list of: the rule → the exception → why (one line) → the trap an MCQ sets around it.
  - Chemistry examples of the type: octet rule exceptions (BeCl₂, BF₃, PCl₅, SF₆, NO, NO₂), O₂ paramagnetism, Fajan's rule reversals, H-bond anomalies.
  - Physics examples of the type: sign-convention flips between mirrors and lenses, lens behaviour inside a denser liquid, TIR direction conditions.
- Exceptions are **not** buried inside prose — they get their own visible box/table so a repeater student can find them in seconds.

**Biology (and other memory-heavy subjects):**
- Structure–function pairings in tables; contrasts (prokaryote vs eukaryote, mitosis vs meiosis) as side-by-side tables.
- Everyday examples for every abstract structure (e.g. "lysosome = the cell's recycling plant").

**Commerce & Accounts subjects (Accountancy, Business Studies, Economics) — "show me the entry":**
- **Journal entries are king.** Present every transaction treatment as a *Journal Entry table* (Particulars | Dr amount | Cr amount) with a one-line "why" under each entry.
- **Show the ledger shape.** Where a concept lives in the books (account format, T-shape, Balance Sheet extract), draw the actual format in a table — students must recognise the statement on sight.
- **Flow diagrams for processes.** The accounting cycle and any procedural flow (transaction → journal → ledger → trial balance → statements) becomes a numbered flow visual, not a paragraph.
- **Formats & proformas:** every standard format the exam needs (ledger, trial balance, Trading A/c, P&L, Balance Sheet…) appears in its official shape at least once.
- **Common Errors & Special Cases** replaces the science Exceptions box — the classic mistakes students make (wrong side of the account, forgetting the goodwill adjustment…) in a visible table.
- Numbers stay realistic and round (₹1,00,000, not ₹1,03,847) so the *method* stays visible; every calculation is written out line by line.

## 2. DOCUMENT STRUCTURE (fixed page plan)

Every notes PDF follows this exact skeleton, in this order:

1. **Page 1 — Full-page branded cover.** Navy canvas, covers the ENTIRE page. Contains: OurMocks logo (white), exam name badge, subject badge, big topic title, one-line subtitle, "inside this PDF" strip, www.ourmocks.com. No content starts on the cover.
2. **Page 2 onwards — Syllabus Alignment table ONLY.** No "This PDF covers...", no welcome text, no philosophy. The table maps each sub-topic → official syllabus pointer (verbatim, with source named) → the skill questions test. Below it, one `exam-pointer` box with **officially published pattern facts only** (question count, duration, marking scheme — with the source named, e.g. "2026 bulletin, the current 2027 baseline"). Then content begins on the same page — no forced page breaks.
3. **Content sections** — one `=` section per sub-topic, each **at least 2 full pages**, flowing continuously (no `pagebreak()` between sections). Sections fill pages completely; a page that is more than ~25% empty means the content is thin — add a worked example, a table, a diagram, or an analogy box.
4. **Formula Cheatsheet** (Maths/Physics mandatory; Chemistry: "Key Reactions & Rules" equivalent).
5. **Exceptions & Traps** (Chemistry mandatory; Physics when exceptions exist).
6. **Practice Questions — 10 MCQs** in the exact exam pattern of the target exam.
7. **Detailed Solutions** — each solution ends with an inline `Answer: (X)` line. **No separate answer-key table/box anywhere.**
8. Last line of the PDF: small grey centred copyright strip: `© OurMocks · www.ourmocks.com · <exam> <subject> · <topic> · 2027 cycle`.

### 2.1 Page furniture

- **Header** (content pages): `OURMOCKS · <EXAM> · 2027 NOTES` left, `www.ourmocks.com` right, thin navy rule below.
- **Footer**: `OurMocks · <exam> <subject>` left, page number right.
- **Watermark**: 15%-opacity OurMocks logo, rotated, centred, on every content page (via `assets/logo_watermark.svg`). Never on the cover.
- **No Telegram links, no social handles, no "join our channel" — only `www.ourmocks.com`.**

## 3. VOICE & TONE

- Friendly, direct, second person where natural: "you see", "watch what happens".
- Confident and plain — never academic-posturing, never exam-hype.
- **Curiosity openers:** open each major section with a real phenomenon, question or everyday object (a spoon, a swimming pool, a speedometer) — 2–3 sentences, then bullets take over.
- Analogy boxes (`analogy-box`) replace memory hooks. Every analogy must be physically honest — the mapping between analogy and concept must be stated, not implied.
- `exam-pointer` boxes state **only officially published facts** (see §5).

## 4. VISUAL STANDARDS

- **Typography (v2.1):** body text 11.5pt (the +15% Class 11–12 readability size over the old 10pt), headings scaled accordingly (H1 banner 16pt, H2 13.5pt, H3 12pt). Figures/captions ≥ 9.5pt.
- **THE VERTICAL-BOX RULE (v2.2 — strict, fixes real overflow bugs):** inside every box (`formula-box`, `cheatsheet-box`, `exception-box`, `analogy-box`…) content stacks **vertically, one relation per line** — never side-by-side. Display math **never wraps** in Typst: one more `quad quad` item past the text width and the line pokes outside the box. Rules:
  - Max **two short items per math line**; three or more items → one item per line.
  - Letter-meaning lists ("V = …, M = …, C = …") get one line per pair, not one crowded line.
  - Long shortcut mappings ("H = 2 → sp …") split across two lines.
  - The same discipline applies to worked examples: never chain more than one "=" conclusion and one result on a single math line.
  - **Verify visually:** render every box page at ~95 dpi and confirm nothing pokes outside its box before publishing.
- **Tables are our default structure** — for maps (object position → image), contrasts, fact clusters. Header row navy-tint, generous insets.
- **Mindmaps & visualizations are expected, not optional, in upcoming subjects:**
  - Every topic gets at least one full-width diagram (HTML/SVG → Playwright PNG at 2x device scale, ≥1480px logical width).
  - Chapter-closing **mindmap**: one image that shows the whole topic as branches (e.g. Chemical Bonding → ionic / covalent / VSEPR / hybridisation / MOT / H-bond, each with 2–3 leaf facts). Build as HTML with brand colours (#071A4D, #1F5EFF, #B8FF34), render at 2x, embed full-width.
  - **Rotate the visualization menu (v2.2):** vary and add visual types so each topic gets the picture that fits it — mindmap (chapter overview), numbered flow/process diagram (accounting cycle, reaction sequence), comparison chart (two methods side by side), timeline (history/law topics), icon-legend strip, T-account/ledger visual, statement format mock-up. Students remember pictures, not paragraphs — a new visualization kind is always welcome.
  - Label every figure: `Figure N — <what it shows>.` Caption 9.5pt grey.
- Brand palette: navy `#071A4D`, blue `#1F5EFF`, lime `#B8FF34`; box accents per component (blue formula, lime analogy, navy exam-pointer, red-amber exceptions).
- Lists are left-aligned, never justified. Page fills matter: dense pages, no big whitespace, no orphan headings at page bottom (if a heading would be the last thing on a page, add content or let Typst push it).

## 5. LEGAL & ETHICAL RULES — THE CLAIMS POLICY (strict)

**We never claim to know what a specific exam "asks", "loves", "prefers", "usually wants" or "has engineered".** We are not the exam board; such claims are legally and ethically wrong, and they date badly.

- ❌ BANNED: "IAT asks the Riemann definition in reverse", "CUET loves this", "COMEDK asks almost every alternate year", "NEST's questions are engineered around...", "the paper is built so that...", "a favourite NEST distractor".
- ❌ BANNED: invented question-frequency stats ("typically contributes 3–5 questions") presented as fact.
- ✅ ALLOWED with source: officially published, verifiable facts — number of questions, duration, marking scheme, syllabus wording — always attributed ("2026 bulletin, the current 2027 baseline", "official syllabus, Unit 6").
- ✅ ALLOWED: neutral pedagogy language — "a classic question type", "often tested in one-mark checks", "watch for this trap" — which describes how the *concept* behaves, not what a *specific exam* does.
- When in doubt, write about the **concept and the common trap**, never about the **exam's intentions**.

## 7. DO'S AND DON'TS (quick reference)

**DO**
- Write from the Class 11–12 student's POV in every sentence.
- Use bullet points as the default format; bold the first keyword of each bullet.
- Break Maths/Physics concepts into steps before formulas; give the quick exam technique after.
- End Maths/Physics topics with a Formula Cheatsheet; end Chemistry/Physics topics with Exceptions & Traps.
- Use tables, diagrams and mindmaps to carry facts instead of prose.
- Fill pages completely — every sub-topic ≥ 2 full pages.
- Use real-world analogies and everyday examples (soup spoon, odometer, marching band).
- Verify every physics number/chemistry fact against NCERT-level truth.
- Name the source for every official exam fact.

**DON'T**
- ❌ Huge paragraphs (3+ sentences of unbroken prose is already suspicious — justify every one).
- ❌ Jargon dumps or graduation-level phrasing.
- ❌ Memory hooks ("CUET loves this" mnemonic-style shortcuts tied to exam personality).
- ❌ Claims about what any exam asks/prefers/engineers, or invented question-frequency stats.
- ❌ A separate answer-key table or box.
- ❌ Telegram links or any URL except www.ourmocks.com.
- ❌ An "About/This PDF covers" page or artificial intro copy.
- ❌ Forced page breaks inside content; pages with big empty gaps.
- ❌ Skipping worked examples, or showing only the final line of a calculation.
