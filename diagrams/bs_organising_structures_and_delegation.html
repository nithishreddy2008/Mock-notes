Organisation Structures: Functional vs. Divisional Departmental Architecture
1. Functional Structure (Grouping by Functions) Single Product Line
• Mechanism: Activities are grouped strictly on the basis of specific functions (e.g., Managing Director $arrow$ Production, Marketing, Finance, HR).
• Advantages: Occupational specialization, minimal duplication of efforts, economies of scale, simplified departmental training.
• Disadvantages: Functional empires (departmental interest supersedes corporate goals), inter-departmental conflicts, inflexibility.
2. Divisional Structure (Grouping by Products) Multi-Product Diversification
• Mechanism: Units are grouped on the basis of distinct product lines (e.g., MD $arrow$ Cosmetics, Garments, Footwear, Electronics). Each product division is an autonomous profit center with its own functional heads.
• Advantages: Product specialization, clear profit center accountability, rapid decision making, facilitates diversification.
• Disadvantages: Costly duplication of functional facilities across divisions, inter-divisional allocation conflicts.
Basis Functional Structure Divisional Structure
Formation Based on functions of work Based on product lines & geography
Specialization Functional / Occupational specialization Product / Divisional specialization
Accountability Difficult to fix on a single department Easy to fix (autonomous profit centers)
Managerial Growth Difficult (narrow specialized perspective) Easy (holistic general manager experience)
Cost Structure Economical (zero facility duplication) Expensive (duplicated functional teams)
The Delegation Triad & Decentralisation Authority Allocation
1. Authority (The Right to Command) Downward Flow
Official right of a manager to make decisions, give orders, and allocate resources. Arises from formal position in the scalar chain. Flows downward from superior to subordinate. Can be delegated.
2. Responsibility (Obligation to Perform) Upward Flow
Correlative obligation of a subordinate to properly carry out assigned duties. Arises from superior-subordinate reporting relationship. Flows upward. Can be shared, but superior remains responsible.
3. Accountability (Answerability for Outcome) Upward Flow · Inviolable
Answerability for the final outcome of the assigned task. Arises from responsibility. Flows upward. ABSOLUTELY CANNOT BE DELEGATED! Even after delegating authority, the superior remains 100% accountable to higher management.
Delegation vs. Decentralisation Horizon Scope & Autonomy
• Delegation: Mandatory two-person devolution of routine authority from superior to immediate subordinate; essential for day-to-day operations.
• Decentralisation: Optional, systematic enterprise-wide policy philosophy dispersing decision-making power down to the lowest operational tiers.
CUET 2027 Key Distinction
Core Conceptual Anchors: Span of Management determines the number of hierarchical levels; Accountability is absolute and cannot be shifted; Functional structure suits single-product enterprises seeking cost efficiency; Divisional structure suits diversified conglomerates requiring autonomous profit center accountability; Decentralisation is an enterprise-wide extension of delegation.
