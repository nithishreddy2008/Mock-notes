SECURITY IN CONTEMPORARY WORLD
CONTEMPORARY WORLD POLITICS · CH. 5 (TOPIC 14)

1. Traditional Security: External Threats

Core Threat: Military invasion by another state threatening sovereignty, territory, and civilian lives.
State Responses: Deterrence (preventing war), Defense (defeating an attack), or surrender.
Balance of Power: Building economic/military strength to avoid falling vulnerable to aggressive neighbors.
Alliance Building: Coalitions of states to deter aggression (e.g., NATO); collective defense under treaty pacts.

2. Traditional Security: Arms Control

Disarmament Treaties: BWC (1972) banned biological weapons; CWC (1992) banned chemical weapons.
Nuclear Arms Control: NPT (1968) created 1 Jan 1967 cutoff; ABM Treaty (1972); SALT I & II; START I & II treaties.
Confidence Building Measures: Military transparency, advance notification of drills, and hotlines between generals.
Internal Security: Containing civil war, separatist rebellions, and maintaining internal constitutional order.

3. Non-Traditional: Human Security

Shift in Referent: Protection of individual people and communities rather than state borders alone.
Broad vs Narrow: Freedom from Fear (violence/torture) vs Freedom from Want (hunger, disease, natural disasters).
Global Health Epidemics: HIV-AIDS, SARS, Ebola, COVID-19 cross international borders, crippling health systems.
Human Rights Violations: International community invoked Responsibility to Protect (R2P) against genocides (Rwanda 1994).

4. Non-Traditional: Global Threats

International Terrorism: Deliberate, indiscriminate targeting of civilians to generate terror (e.g. 9/11, 26/11 Mumbai).
Global Poverty & Inequality: Demographic divide between aging wealthy Global North and rapidly growing Global South.
Climate & Environmental Insecurity: Global warming, rising sea levels, desertification, and conflict over scarce clean water.
Cooperative Security: Requires global multilateral collaboration; unilateral military force is useless against pandemics.

5. Migration: Refugees vs IDPs

Refugees: Flee across international borders due to war, persecution, or natural disasters; protected by 1951 Refugee Convention.
Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs): Flee homes but remain within their own national borders (e.g. Kashmiri Pandits 1990).
Economic Migrants: Voluntarily move for better livelihoods; do not possess automatic legal right of international asylum.
Host State Backlash: Influx of migrants triggers political friction over jobs, scarce resources, and cultural identity.

6. India's Fourfold Security Strategy

1. Military Strength: Deterred nuclear/conventional threats; fought 4 wars with Pakistan; nuclear tests (1974 & 1998).
2. International Norms: Supported universal non-discriminatory disarmament, Asian solidarity, NAM, and UN peacekeeping.
3. Internal Cohesion: Accommodated regional, linguistic, and tribal autonomy demands to prevent national balkanization.
4. Economic Growth: Eradicating mass poverty and structural deprivation to maintain democratic stability.
