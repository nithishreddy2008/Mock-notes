Six primary farming regimes: input intensities, labor organization, land-yield ratios, and regional geography

Slash-and-Burn Tribal Subsistence
Primitive Subsistence (Shifting)
Forest plots cleared by fire; ash enriches soil. Primitive tools (machete, digging sticks); plot abandoned when fertility drops.
Land / Labor YieldLow per hectare; low per worker.
Primary CropsYams, cassava, millets, maize.
Regional Names: Jhuming (NE India), Milpa (Central America/Mexico), Ladang (Malaysia/Indonesia), Taungya (Myanmar).
High Density Family Labor
Intensive Subsistence Farming
Dense monsoon population; small fragmented holdings; manual human labor and animal draft power; intensive multi-cropping.
Wet Paddy DominatedGanga plain, SE Asia, southern China.
Non-Paddy DominatedWheat, barley, pulses (drier regions).
Economic Paradox: High yield per unit area (intensive land use) but low productivity per labor unit.
Commercial Estate Colonial Origin
Plantation Agriculture
Large estates managed scientifically; introduced by Europeans in tropics; single cash crop focus; factory processing on estate.
Capital & LaborHigh capital, cheap local labor, rail links.
Key Cash CropsTea, coffee, rubber, cocoa, sugarcane.
NCERT Benchmarks: Tea in India/Sri Lanka; Rubber in Malaysia; Coffee (Fazendas) in Brazil; Sugarcane in Cuba.
Mechanized Market Monoculture
Extensive Commercial Grain Cultivation
Practiced in interior semi-arid continental grasslands; giant farms (hundreds of hectares); complete mechanization from plough to combine.
Core MonocultureWheat is dominant; corn and barley.
Yield DynamicsLow per hectare; very high per worker.
Global Grasslands: Prairies (N. America), Steppes (Eurasia), Pampas (Argentina), Velds (S. Africa), Downs (Australia).
Capital Intensive Livestock & Crops
Dairy & Commercial Mixed Farming
Highly specialized dairy cattle rearing; stall feeding, veterinary care, mechanical milking; mixed farming balances crops and livestock.
Labor & CapitalHeavy capital (sheds, silos, refrigeration).
Market ProximityLocated close to urban-industrial centers.
Leading Regions: NW Europe (Denmark, Netherlands), SE Australia, New Zealand; mixed farming in temperate Europe/North America.
Specialized Commercial Orchard
Mediterranean Agriculture
Unique climate with mild, wet winters and hot, dry summers; commercial fruit and vegetable cultivation commanding premium global prices.
Viticulture (Grapes)Best quality wines; dried raisins & currants.
Citrus & OlivesOranges, lemons, figs, premium olive oil.
Zones: Mediterranean basin (S. Europe & N. Africa), Central California, Central Chile, SW South Africa, SW Australia.
Core Exam Rule: Differentiate yield per hectare (highest in Intensive Subsistence) vs yield per person/worker (highest in Extensive Commercial Grain).
