SOUTH ASIAN DEMOCRATIC TRAJECTORIES & CONFLICTS — THE REGIONAL MATRIX

FOUR THEATRES OF SOUTH ASIAN TRANSFORMATION

1. Pakistan's Coup Cycles
Ayub Khan (1958) → Yahya (1969) → Zia (1977) → Musharraf (1999). Military-clergy-landowner nexus halts democracy.
2. Nepal: Monarchy to Republic
1990 People's Movement → 1996–2006 Maoist insurgency → April 2006 Mass Uprising → Monarchy abolished in 2008.
3. Sri Lanka's Ethnic Conflict
Sinhala majoritarianism vs LTTE armed revolt (1976–2009); IPKF intervention (1987–90); high HDI despite war.
4. SAARC & Regional Trade
Founded in Dhaka (1985); Afghanistan added in 2007; SAFTA signed 2004 (effective 2006); Indo-Pak deadlock.

Pakistan: Fragile Democracy & Military Rule

Ayub Khan & Yahya Khan (1958–1971): Military regimes; loss of East Pakistan led to Yahya's resignation.
Zulfikar Ali Bhutto (1971–1977): Democratic civilian rule ended when General Zia-ul-Haq staged a coup and hanged Bhutto.
Benazir & Nawaz Era (1988–1999): Competitive democracy marred by mutual dismissals and economic instability.
Musharraf Coup (1999): Overthrew Nawaz Sharif; declared himself President in 2001; ruled until forced out in 2008.
Structural Flaws: Domination of the Army, religious orthodoxy, feudal landlords, and strategic funding from US & China.

Sri Lanka: Ethnic Strife & Tamil Eelam

Root Causes: Post-1948 Sinhala-dominated governments denied equal language (Sinhala Only Act 1956) and university access to Tamils.
LTTE Formed (1976): Velupillai Prabhakaran launched guerrilla war for an independent Tamil state ('Tamil Eelam').
Indian Peace Keeping Force (1987): Rajiv Gandhi signed Indo-Sri Lanka Accord; IPKF fought LTTE, but had to withdraw in 1990.
Norwegian Mediation: Ceasefire signed in 2002; peace talks collapsed over devolution of power.
Military Endgame (May 2009): Sri Lankan military crushed the LTTE in Mullaitivu, killing Prabhakaran and ending the civil war.

CHRONOLOGY OF DIPLOMACY, CONFLICT & DEMOCRATISATION IN SOUTH ASIA

Sept 1960

Indus Treaty: Nehru & Ayub Khan sign water-sharing accord brokered by World Bank; still functioning peacefully.

Dec 1971

Birth of Bangladesh: Pakistani army surrenders in Dhaka; Sheikh Mujibur Rahman leads new republic.

Dec 1985

SAARC Founded: 7 nations sign charter in Dhaka; secretariat established in Kathmandu, Nepal.

1987–1990

IPKF in Sri Lanka: Indian armed forces deploy to enforce Tamil disarmament; withdrawn by PM V.P. Singh.

May 1998

Nuclearization: India conducts Pokhran-II; Pakistan responds with tests at Chagai Hills, Balochistan.

2006–2008

Nepal Republic: April 2006 uprising strips King Gyanendra of power; 2008 constituent assembly abolishes monarchy.

REGIONAL ACCORDS & PEACE EFFORTS IN SOUTH ASIA

SAFTA Free Trade Agreement (2004–2006) Signed at the 12th SAARC Summit in Islamabad in January 2004; became operational on 1 January 2006. Aims to reduce customs duties on traded goods by 20% across all South Asian member states.
Indo-Bangladesh Water Accord (1996) Historic 30-year treaty signed by Prime Minister H.D. Deve Gowda and Sheikh Hasina for sharing the dry-season waters of the River Ganga at the Farakka Barrage in West Bengal.
Operation Cactus (Maldives, 1988) Indian paratroopers and navy launched swift military intervention to neutralize Sri Lankan Tamil mercenaries (PLOTE) who staged a coup against President Maumoon Abdul Gayoom.

PITFALL 1: SAARC Membership Timeline

SAARC had 7 original members in 1985 (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bhutan, Maldives). Afghanistan joined as the 8th member in 2007 at the 14th Summit in New Delhi.

PITFALL 2: Maldives Constitutional Transition

Maldives was a Sultanate until 1968, when it became a presidential republic. Multi-party democracy was officially legalized in June 2005, leading to the election of Mohamed Nasheed (MDP) in 2008.

PITFALL 3: Nepal Monarchy Abolition Date

The popular democratic revolution happened in April 2006, but the 240-year-old Hindu monarchy was formally abolished by the Constituent Assembly on 28 May 2008.
