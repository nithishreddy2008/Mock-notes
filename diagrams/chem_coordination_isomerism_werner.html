1. Primary Valence (Oxidation State)

• Ionisable & satisfied exclusively by negative ions.
• Non-directional in space.
• Lies outside the square coordination bracket [ ] in modern IUPAC notation.

2. Secondary Valence (Coord. Number)

• Non-ionisable; satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions.
• Directional in space → defines the geometry (Octahedral, Tetrahedral, Square Planar).

Molar Conductivity Ladder at 298 K

Conductivity tracks the number of electrolyte ions per formula unit:
[Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ (1:3 electrolyte, ~430 Ω⁻¹cm²mol⁻¹) > [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ (1:2, ~260) > [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl (1:1, ~100) > [Co(NH₃)₃Cl₃] (Non-electrolyte, ~0).

Dual Behaviour of Chloride in [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂

The single inner-sphere Cl⁻ satisfies both the primary valence (neutralising +1 charge) AND the secondary valence (occupying 1 coordination site). It does NOT precipitate with AgNO₃.

Complete Classification of Coordination Isomerism

1. Structural Isomerism (Different Bond Connectivity)

4 Sub-Types
Ionisation Isomerism

Exchange of counter ion with a ligand inside coordination sphere.

[Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Br (AgBr ↓) vs [Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄ (BaSO₄ ↓)
Hydrate / Solvate Isomerism

Water functions as ligand inside sphere vs crystal water of hydration outside.

[Cr(H₂O)₆]Cl₃ (violet) vs [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O (grey-green)
Linkage Isomerism

Ambidentate ligand coordinates via different donor atoms (NO₂⁻/ONO⁻, SCN⁻/NCS⁻).

[Co(NH₃)₅(NO₂)]Cl₂ (yellow, N-bound) vs [Co(NH₃)₅(ONO)]Cl₂ (red, O-bound)
Coordination Isomerism

Interchange of ligands between cationic and anionic complex entities.

[Co(NH₃)₆][Cr(CN)₆] vs [Cr(NH₃)₆][Co(CN)₆]

2. Stereoisomerism (Same Bonds, Different Spatial Arrangement)

Geometrical & Optical
Geometrical: Cis / Trans

Adjacent (90°, cis) vs opposite (180°, trans) positions. E.g. Square planar [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] — cis-platin is anti-cancer; trans-platin is inactive.

Octahedral: cis-[Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ (violet) vs trans (green)
Geometrical: Fac / Mer in [Ma₃b₃]

Facial (fac): 3 identical ligands occupy corners of one octahedral face (all 90°).
Meridional (mer): 3 ligands form a semi-meridian plane (two are 180° trans).

[Co(NH₃)₃(NO₂)₃] — fac and mer isomers
Optical Isomerism (Chirality & Enantiomers)

Non-superimposable mirror images that rotate plane-polarised light (dextro d and laevo l). Must lack an alternating axis of symmetry (no plane or center of inversion).
• Tris-chelate complexes: [Co(en)₃]³⁺ and [Fe(ox)₃]³⁻ are strictly chiral (exist as d and l enantiomers).
• Bis-chelate cis vs trans: cis-[PtCl₂(en)₂]²⁺ lacks a plane of symmetry → optically active; whereas trans-[PtCl₂(en)₂]²⁺ possesses an internal plane of symmetry → optically inactive (meso/achiral).
