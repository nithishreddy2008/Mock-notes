1. Rural Settlement Typologies

4 Core Types
  • Clustered / Nucleated: Compact, contiguous dwellings; defense (Bundelkhand, Nagaland) or water security; fertile plains.
  • Semi-Clustered: Social segregation; dominant land-owning caste in central village, laborers on outer periphery (Gujarat/Rajasthan).
  • Hamleted: Physically segmented units (Panna, Para, Palli, Nagla, Dhani) bearing common name; Middle/Lower Ganga plain.
  • Dispersed: Isolated solitary huts on slopes; Meghalaya, Uttarakhand, Himachal, and coastal Kerala.
Determinants: Physical relief, water sources, social caste cleavage & defense.

2. Historical Epochs of Indian Towns

3 Epochs
  • Ancient Towns (>2,000 yrs): Cultural & religious origins; continuous survival; Varanasi, Prayag, Pataliputra, Madurai.
  • Medieval Towns (8th–18th C): Fortified headquarters of kingdoms, sultans & Mughals; Delhi, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Agra, Lucknow.
  • Modern Colonial Towns: British trading footholds (Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai); hill stations (Shimla, Darjeeling); cantonments.
  • Post-1947 Planned Towns: Administrative capitals (Chandigarh, Gandhinagar) & industrial steel cities (Bhilai, Rourkela, Bokaro).
Planned Stature: Chandigarh designed by Swiss-French architect Le Corbusier.

3. Census Urban Hierarchy

Class I – VI Towns
  • Class I Cities: Population ≥ 1,00,000; account for 60%+ of total urban populace, exhibiting top-heavy urbanization.
  • Million-Plus (Metropolitan) Cities: Population 10 to 50 lakh; count grew from 35 (2001) to 53 cities (2011).
  • Mega-Cities: Population > 50 lakh; Greater Mumbai (18.4 M), Delhi (16.3 M), Kolkata (14.1 M), Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad.
  • Urban Agglomeration (UA): Continuous urban spread consisting of a town and its adjoining outgrowths (OGs).
Top-Heavy Urbanization: Class I cities dominate aggregate urban growth.

4. 9-Fold Functional Classification

Census Matrix
  • Administrative & Industrial: State capitals (New Delhi, Chandigarh) & manufacturing hubs (Jamshedpur, Bhilai).
  • Transport & Commercial: Seaports (Kandla, Kochi, Vizag) & commercial hubs (Mumbai).
  • Mining & Energy: Mineral basins (Raniganj, Jharia, Bokaro, Digboi, Ankleshwar).
  • Garrison, Educational & Religious: Cantonments (Mhow), academia (Roorkee), pilgrimage (Puri, Tirupati).
Multi-Functional Shift: Static single functions evolve into complex metropolis.

5. Urban Morphology & Smart Cities

Mission 100 Cities
  • Morphological Duality: Historic congested walled core (narrow alleys, chowks) vs planned colonial/modern civil lines and suburban sectors.
  • Peri-Urban Fringe: Rapid ribbon sprawl along transport highways; conversion of rural agricultural land into real estate layouts.
  • Smart Cities Mission: Core infrastructure, clean environment, digital governance, retrofitting, redevelopment & greenfield expansion.
  • Sustainable Transit: High-speed metro rail networks, bus rapid transit (BRT), and smart traffic management.
Mission Objective: Quality of life, clean sustainable environment & smart solutions.

6. Urban Problems & Slum Geography

Challenges
  • Acute Housing Shortage: Escalating land costs force low-income migrants into unhygienic squatter settlements and slums.
  • Dharavi (Mumbai): One of Asia's largest slums; intense informal recycling, leather, and pottery micro-enterprises alongside civic deficits.
  • Severe Infrastructure Stress: Drinking water deficits, uncollected municipal solid waste, clogged drains, open sewers, and flash floods.
  • Environmental Pollution: Hazardous air quality (PM2.5), vehicular traffic gridlocks, and toxic industrial effluent contamination.
Policy Mandate: AMRUT & PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana) housing missions.
