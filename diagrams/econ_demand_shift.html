MOVEMENT vs SHIFT — THE #1 DEMAND TRAP

MOVEMENT along the SAME curve

Cause: the good's OWN PRICE changes

Price falls ↓ → you slide down the curve: quantity demanded rises (expansion of demand).

Price rises ↑ → slide up: quantity demanded falls (contraction of demand).

The curve itself never moves — only your position on it.

Same curve · new point on it • = "change in QUANTITY demanded"

SHIFT of the WHOLE curve

Cause: any NON-PRICE factor changes

Income rises (normal good), price of a substitute rises, taste turns in your favour, buyers expect higher prices tomorrow, population grows → curve jumps right = increase in demand.

Income falls, a complement gets costlier, taste fades → curve jumps left = decrease in demand.

Own price is held constant the whole time — buyers simply want more (or less) at EVERY price.

Whole curve jumps • = "change in DEMAND"

TRAP 1

Own price NEVER shifts the curve. It only slides you along it. Any option that says "demand shifted because the price fell" is bait.

TRAP 2

Vocabulary decides marks: movement = "change in quantity demanded"; shift = "change in demand". Read which one the question uses.

TRAP 3

Axes discipline: price on the vertical axis, quantity on the horizontal axis — in every demand diagram you ever draw.
