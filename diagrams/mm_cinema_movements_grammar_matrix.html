Comparative Matrix: Landmark Film Movements & Cinematic Aesthetics

Systematic classification of international film movements, aesthetic manifestos, grammar innovations, and seminal auteurs

CUET Mass Media · Film Movements Matrix
GE
German Expressionism (1919–26)
Aesthetic Core & Philosophy
Rejection of realism; externalizing internal psychological madness, paranoia, and moral corruption through distorted geometric sets.
Visual Grammar Innovations
Chiaroscuro Extreme sharp light/shadow.
Painted Shadows Sharp angular walls.
Canted Tilts Skewed Dutch angles.
Seminal Works & Auteurs
Robert Wiene (Dr. Caligari, 1920); Fritz Lang (Metropolis, 1927); F.W. Murnau (Nosferatu, 1922).
CUET High-Yield Invariant
Psychological Space Precursor to Film Noir and psychological horror mise-en-scène.
SM
Soviet Montage (1920s)
Aesthetic Core & Philosophy
Editing as the supreme essence of cinema. Meaning arises not from a single shot, but from the collision/juxtaposition of two shots.
Visual Grammar Innovations
Kuleshov Effect Shot A + Shot B = New Concept.
Dialectical Cuts Thesis + Antithesis = Synthesis.
5 Montage Types Metric, Rhythmic, Tonal, etc.
Seminal Works & Auteurs
Sergei Eisenstein (Battleship Potemkin, 1925 - Odessa Steps); Dziga Vertov (Man with a Movie Camera, 1929).
CUET High-Yield Invariant
Collision Editing Political agitation; intellectual concept generation via editing.
IN
Italian Neorealism (1943–52)
Aesthetic Core & Philosophy
Capturing raw socio-economic reality, post-war poverty, and moral despair of the working class without bourgeois melodrama.
Visual Grammar Innovations
On-Location Actual streets, bombed buildings.
Non-Actors Real laborers and children.
Natural Light Handheld mobile cameras.
Seminal Works & Auteurs
Roberto Rossellini (Rome, Open City, 1945); Vittorio De Sica (Bicycle Thieves, 1948); Cesare Zavattini (manifesto).
CUET High-Yield Invariant
Anti-Studio Inspired world parallel cinema, including Satyajit Ray's Pather Panchali.
FN
French New Wave (1958–68)
Aesthetic Core & Philosophy
Cahiers du Cinéma critics turned filmmakers. Championed the Auteur Theory (director as true author); smashed conventional Hollywood rules.
Visual Grammar Innovations
Jump Cuts Deliberate temporal disruption.
Breaking 4th Wall Direct actor address to camera.
Long Tracking Roaming handheld mobility.
Seminal Works & Auteurs
François Truffaut (The 400 Blows, 1959); Jean-Luc Godard (Breathless, 1960); Alain Resnais (Hiroshima Mon Amour).
CUET High-Yield Invariant
Auteur Principle Director's personal artistic vision overrides commercial studio formula.
Key Exam Anchor: Soviet Montage champions editing collision; Neorealism champions non-actors on location; French New Wave pioneered the Jump Cut and Auteur Theory.
