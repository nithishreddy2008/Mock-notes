THE DIAZONIUM FACTORY — EVERY ARYL GROUP PASSES THROUGH N₂⁺
Diazotisation at 273–278 K makes benzenediazonium chloride; from there, seven exit doors build the aromatic toolbox

MAKING THE SALT (diazotisation)

1
Cool aniline in dilute HCl to 273–278 K (ice bath) — cold, or the salt decomposes.
C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O
2
NaNO₂ + HCl generates nitrous acid in the flask; the amine grabs its NO⁺.
3
Use it immediately — dry diazonium salts are shock-explosive; solutions stay stable in the cold.

SEVEN EXITS (synthesis doors)

A
Sandmeyer: Cu₂Cl₂/Cu₂Br₂/CuCN → Ar–Cl, Ar–Br, Ar–CN
B
Gattermann: Cu powder + HX — the gentler version
C
Balz-Schiemann: HBF₄ then heat → Ar–F (the fluorine door)
D
KI, warm: → Ar–I (no catalyst needed)
E
H₂O, warm: → phenol (the mild OH door)
F
H₃PO₂ or ethanol: → benzene (H replaces N₂⁺ — the eraser)
G
Coupling: phenol/aniline + base → azo dyes (p-hydroxyazobenzene, orange-red)

Why this matters: direct ring substitution cannot put F, I, CN or OH exactly where you want them with clean control — but every one of them is one diazonium step away. The NH₂ group is a temporary handle: install it by nitration + reduction, then swap it for whatever the target needs. That two-step indirection — nitration → reduction → diazotisation → exit — is the standard route map for dozens of conversion questions.
