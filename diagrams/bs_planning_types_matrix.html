Taxonomy of Organizational Plans: Standing vs Single-Use Plans & Comparative Matrix

Unit 4: Planning — Hierarchical Classification, Structural Parameters & Applied Corporate Archetypes

NCERT & NTA CUET 2027 SPECIFICATION
Standing Plans (Recurring & Multi-Use) Long-Term Continuity
1. Objectives
The end points towards which all management activities are directed. Measurable, qualitative or quantitative targets.
2. Strategy
Comprehensive blueprint defining long-term goals, adopting course of action, and allocating scarce resources against rivals.
3. Policy
General decision-making boundaries guiding managerial discretion (e.g., "Recruitment strictly through open merit").
4. Procedure
Chronological, step-by-step sequence of specific actions required to execute routine departmental activities.
5. Method
The standardized, prescribed technique of executing a single specific task within a procedure (e.g., FIFO inventory).
6. Rule
Specific, rigid directives stating what must or must not be done, permitting zero managerial discretion (e.g., "No Smoking").
Single-Use Plans (Unique & Non-Recurring) Project-Specific
7. Programme
• Composite Architecture: Detailed, multi-faceted operational blueprint encompassing objectives, policies, procedures, rules, task allocations, and physical/financial resources.
• Execution Horizon: Designed for an entire major undertaking (e.g., establishing a new EV manufacturing plant or expanding into international retail). Discarded once the project concludes.
8. Budget
• Quantified Blueprint: Statement of expected future results expressed strictly in numerical financial or quantitative physical terms.
• Dual Function: Operates as a foundational planning tool and an essential controlling standard (e.g., Cash Budget forecasting monthly liquidity surpluses and deficits).

CUET 2027 Key Anchors
Essential Distinctions: Objectives & Strategies are neither standing nor single-use; Rules allow zero discretion and enforce penalties; Policies guide thinking; Procedures guide action in chronological steps; Budgets are always quantified and act as controlling benchmarks; Programmes integrate objectives, policies, procedures, and budgets into a single composite project architecture.
