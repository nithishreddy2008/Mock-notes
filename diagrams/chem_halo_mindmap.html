1. Classification & PreparationCore Routes

  • C-X Dipole Moment: CH₃Cl (1.86 D) > CH₃F (1.84 D) > CH₃Br (1.83 D) > CH₃I (1.64 D). C-F is shortest/strongest; C-I is longest/weakest.
  • From Alcohols: SOCl₂ + Pyridine (Darzens Process, best route as byproducts SO₂ & HCl are escapable gases). PCl₅ → POCl₃; PCl₃ → H₃PO₃.
  • Halide Exchange: Finkelstein: R-Cl/Br + NaI →[dry acetone] R-I (NaCl/NaBr precipitates). Swarts: R-Br/Cl + AgF (or Hg₂F₂, CoF₃, SbF₃) → R-F.
  • From Hydrocarbons: Free-radical allylic halogenation via NBS; Markovnikov & Anti-Markovnikov (Peroxide / Kharasch effect with HBr only).

2. Nucleophilic Substitution (S_N1 & S_N2)Mechanisms

  • S_N2 (Concerted): Rate = k[R-X][Nu⁻], 1 step, pentacoordinate TS, CH₃X > 1° > 2° >> 3°. Complete 100% Walden inversion. Polar aprotic solvent.
  • S_N1 (Stepwise): Rate = k[R-X], 2 steps via planar sp² carbocation, 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃X. Racemisation (inversion > retention). Polar protic solvent.
  • Ambidentate Nucleophiles: KCN (ionic) → R-CN (nitrile); AgCN (covalent) → R-NC (isocyanide). KNO₂ → R-ONO (nitrite); AgNO₂ → R-NO₂ (nitroalkane).
  • Leaving Group Ability: I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ >> F⁻ (matches conjugate acid strength HI > HBr > HCl > HF).

3. Elimination Reactions (β-Elimination)Regiochemistry

  • Dehydrohalogenation (E2): Alkyl halide heated with alcoholic KOH eliminates HX across α-C and β-C, forming an alkene.
  • Saytzeff (Zaitsev) Rule: Eliminates β-H from carbon with fewer hydrogens → yields the more substituted, thermodynamically stable alkene (major).
  • Hofmann Elimination: Bulky bases (t-BuO⁻K⁺) or poor leaving groups (fluoride R-F) eliminate from less hindered β-C → less substituted alkene (major).
  • Competition: 1° halides favor S_N2; 3° halides with hot alcoholic base undergo exclusive E2 elimination over substitution.

4. Organometallics & CouplingsC-C Bonds

  • Grignard Reagent: R-X + Mg →[dry ether] R-MgX. Strong carbanion source; hydrolyzes violently with active hydrogen (H₂O, ROH, RNH₂) to form alkanes (R-H).
  • Wurtz Reaction: 2 R-X + 2 Na →[dry ether] R-R + 2 NaX (symmetrical alkanes with even number of carbons; methane cannot be prepared).
  • Fittig & Wurtz-Fittig: Ar-X + R-X + 2 Na → Ar-R (Wurtz-Fittig → alkylarene); 2 Ar-X + 2 Na → Ar-Ar (Fittig → biphenyl). Ullmann uses Cu for Ar-I.
  • Frankland Reaction: 2 R-X + Zn → R-R + ZnX₂ (symmetrical coupling using dialkylzinc).

5. Chemistry of Haloarenes (Ar-X)Aromatic C-X

  • Low S_N Reactivity: C-Cl has partial double bond character (+R resonance), sp² hybridised C (more s-character, shorter C-Cl), unstable phenyl cation, π-repulsion.
  • Dow's Process: Chlorobenzene + NaOH at 623 K, 300 atm → Sodium phenoxide →[H⁺] Phenol.
  • Activating Effect of -NO₂: Nitro groups at ortho and para positions stabilize Meisenheimer carbanion intermediate. 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene hydrolyzes in warm water → Picric acid!
  • Electrophilic Substitution: Halogens are deactivating (-I effect) but ortho/para directing (+R resonance). Para-isomer is major due to steric relief.

6. Polyhalogen Compounds & EnvironmentApplied & Ecology

  • Dichloromethane (CH₂Cl₂): Paint remover, aerosol propellant; CNS depressant, damages cornea.
  • Chloroform (CHCl₃): Anaesthetic; slowly oxidized by air and light to lethal Phosgene (COCl₂). Stored in dark amber bottles completely filled with 1% ethanol (stabilizer).
  • Iodoform (CHI₃): Yellow crystalline solid with antiseptic properties due to slow liberation of free iodine.
  • Carbon Tetrachloride (CCl₄): Fire extinguisher (Pyrene); causes liver cancer, major cause of stratospheric ozone depletion.
  • Freons (CFCs): Freon-12 (CF₂Cl₂) refrigerant; radical chain catalyst for ozone layer destruction. DDT: Non-biodegradable persistent insecticide.
