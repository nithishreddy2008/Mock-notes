RECENT ISSUES AND CHALLENGES
POLITICS IN INDIA SINCE INDEPENDENCE · CH. 8 (TOPIC 9)

1. Globalisation & Economic Reforms (1991)

1991 Crisis: Forex dropped to 2 weeks of imports; rupee devalued; gold pledged to Bank of England.
LPG Policies: Industrial delicensing, tariff reductions, FDI promotion, privatisation of loss-making PSUs.
Opposition to Reforms: Left trade unions protested job insecurity; farmers opposed patent regimes & WTO subsidies.
Cultural Resistance: Protests against Valentine's Day and Western consumerism by both Left and Right fronts.

2. Rise of OBCs in North Indian Politics

Intermediate Castes: Land-owning peasant castes (Yadavs, Kurmis, Jats) mobilized following the Green Revolution.
Mandal Catalyst: 27% job quota sparked political consciousness; established OBCs as decisive electoral voting blocs.
Regional Powerhouses: Samajwadi Party (Mulayam Singh), RJD (Lalu Yadav), and JD(U) displaced upper-caste hegemony.
Sub-categorization: Most Backward Classes (MBCs) demanded separate quotas to prevent dominant OBC monopoly.

3. Dalit Politics: Electoral Assertion (BSP)

Kanshi Ram's Groundwork: BAMCEF (1978) employee union → DS-4 (1981) activist front → BSP founded in 1984.
Bahujan Paradigm: United SC, ST, OBC, and religious minorities (the 85% majority) against elite hegemony.
Mayawati Leadership: First Dalit woman Chief Minister of UP (1995); engineered historic Dalit-Brahmin social coalition (2007).
Pragmatic Power: Formed tactical alliances with both SP and BJP to demonstrate Dalit state power.

4. Dalit Politics: Non-Electoral Assertion

Dalit Panthers (1972): Founded in Maharashtra by Namdeo Dhasal and J.V. Pawar inspired by the US Black Panthers.
Direct Action: Fought violent atrocities, untouchability, and caste oppression; demanded strict enforcement of legal rights.
Dalit Literature: Pioneered revolutionary Marathi literature, poetry, and autobiographies exposing caste apartheid.
Legislative Fruit: Persistent struggles led to the passage of the SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act, 1989.

5. The Ayodhya Dispute & Communalism

1986 Unlocking: Faizabad district judge ordered locks of Babri structure opened for Hindu worship.
Rath Yatra (1990): L.K. Advani's mobilization from Somnath mobilized mass support for Ram Janmabhoomi temple.
Demolition (6 Dec 1992): Structure pulled down by Karsevaks; Kalyan Singh government resigned; riots in Mumbai.
Constitutional Fallouts: Central govt dismissed 4 BJP state governments under Art. 356; Liberhan Commission appointed.

6. Gujarat Riots (2002) & Secularism Debates

Godhra Trigger: Burning of Sabarmati Express coach at Godhra (27 Feb 2002), killing 57 passengers.
Subsequent Riots: Widespread communal violence across Gujarat; over 1,000 casualties; NHRC intervened for victims.
Supreme Court Oversight: Transferred key riot trials outside Gujarat (Best Bakery case to Maharashtra) for fair justice.
Two Models of Secularism: Indian principled distance (equal respect for all faiths) vs Western strict separation of state & religion.

EXAM PITFALLS — ORGANISATIONS, LEADERS & DATES

BAMCEF (1978) vs BSP (1984): BAMCEF was an employee union; BSP was the political party! Both founded by Kanshi Ram.
Dalit Panthers (1972): Founded in Maharashtra (Namdeo Dhasal/J.V. Pawar), NOT in Uttar Pradesh or Bihar!
SC/ST Atrocities Act (1989): Passed during Rajiv Gandhi's tenure, just before the Mandal implementation.
