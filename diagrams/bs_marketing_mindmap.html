MARKETING
UNIT XI · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Meaning, Functions & Philosophies

Marketing = a social process of identifying needs and satisfying them profitably — starts before production, continues after the sale
Functions: gather market info · plan · design · standardise · package · brand · support customers · price · promote · distribute
Philosophies: production $arrow$ product $arrow$ selling $arrow$ marketing $arrow$ societal — each era widens the focus from factory to society

Marketing vs Selling

Focus: marketing = customer needs; selling = the product
Aim: satisfaction + profit through it vs only profit through volume
Starts: marketing before production; selling after it
Scope: marketing is wider — selling is one part of it

Product & Price

Consumer goods: convenience (bought often, little effort) · shopping (compared) · specialty (special effort)
Industrial goods: materials & parts, capital items, supplies & services
Branding = name/mark identifying the product; label = written description; packaging = protection + promotion
Price factors: cost, demand & utility, competition, government regulation, pricing objectives, marketing method

The Marketing Mix — 4 Ps

Product — what is offered (goods, services, brand, package)
Price — what the customer pays; the only revenue-earning P
Place — how it reaches the buyer (channels, transport, warehouses)
Promotion — how people hear and are persuaded
All four must fit together — a premium product on a weak channel fails

Physical Distribution & Channels

Components: transportation · warehousing · inventory control · order processing
Channel levels: zero (direct) $arrow$ one (retailer) $arrow$ two (wholesaler + retailer) $arrow$ three (agent added)
Choice factors: product nature (perishable = short), company funds, market size & spread, competition, legal rules

Promotion — The Four Tools

Advertising: paid, non-personal, mass medium — wide reach, low per-head cost
Personal selling: face-to-face persuasion — flexible, relationship-building
Sales promotion: short-run incentives — discounts, coupons, contests
Public relations: free image-building — news, events, sponsorships
Objections to advertising: adds cost, undermines social values, confuses buyers; replies: scale economies, information, consumer education

Traps that catch most students

Marketing ≠ selling — selling is one part; marketing begins before production
Convenience vs shopping goods — effort decides the class, not the price alone
Zero-level channel = direct — no middleman at all; count the middlemen, not the customers
Price is the only revenue-earning P — product, place and promotion all cost money
