Selected Issues & Environmental Problems

Anthropogenic Pressures & Ecological Balance

1. Water Pollution & River Depletion

Aquatic Ecology
  • Source Dualism: Point sources (industrial pipes, municipal sewer outfalls) vs Non-point sources (agricultural runoff, open urban runoff).
  • Oxygen Depletion: High organic waste elevates Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD), exhausting Dissolved Oxygen and causing severe fish kills.
  • Critical Hotspots: Ganga from Kannauj to Varanasi (Kanpur tanneries, chromium toxicity); Yamuna in Delhi (Wazirabad to Okhla, zero DO).
  • Remediation Missions: Ganga Action Plan (GAP, 1985) succeeded by the comprehensive Namami Gange Mission (2014).
Water Quality Index: Clean river water maintains BOD under 2 mg/L; polluted stretches often exceed 15-30 mg/L.

2. Air Pollution & Atmospheric Toxins

Atmospheric Stress
  • Major Emission Vectors: Coal-based thermal power plants (SO₂, NOₓ, fly ash) and explosive urban vehicular growth (CO, unburnt HC, lead).
  • Particulate Matter: PM2.5 & PM10 penetrate deep pulmonary alveoli, causing chronic bronchitis, asthma, and cardiovascular mortality.
  • Winter Inversion & Smog: Atmospheric temperature inversion in Indo-Gangetic plains trapping smoke, exacerbated by post-monsoon stubble burning.
  • Acid Precipitation: Atmospheric SO₂ and NO₂ convert into dilute sulfuric and nitric acids, damaging monuments and aquatic ecosystems.
Policy Response: National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) and leapfrogging directly to BS-VI vehicular fuel standards.

3. Noise Pollution & Sound Stress

Acoustic Stress
  • Logarithmic Metrics: Measured in Decibels (dB); residential daytime limit: 55 dB, night: 45 dB; commercial daytime limit: 65 dB; industrial: 75 dB.
  • Acoustic Vectors: Heavy motor traffic, aircraft take-offs, express rail corridors, construction machinery, and industrial sirens.
  • Health Impacts: Chronic auditory fatigue, permanent hearing impairment (above 85 dB), hypertension, constriction of blood vessels, and insomnia.
  • Statutory Zoning: Demarcation of Silence Zones within a 100-metre radius surrounding hospitals, educational campuses, and courts.
Regulatory Norms: Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000 under the Environment Protection Act.

4. Urban Solid Waste Crisis

Civic Waste
  • Generation Burden: Urban India generates ~62 million tonnes of municipal solid waste annually; ~50% compostable organic, 20% recyclable.
  • Open Dumping Dilemma: Massive unlined landfills (Ghazipur, Bhalswa in Delhi, Deonar in Mumbai) leaching toxic effluents into aquifers.
  • Spontaneous Fires: Anaerobic decomposition deep within landfill layers generates methane (CH₄), fueling uncontrollable subterranean fires.
  • Plastic & E-Waste: Non-biodegradable single-use plastics choke urban storm drainage; informal e-waste scraping releases cadmium and lead.
Swachh Bharat 2.0: Focus on complete source segregation, 100% scientific processing, and remediation of legacy dumpsites.

5. Rural-Urban Migration & Slums

Informal Habitats
  • Agrarian Push vs Urban Pull: Rural poverty, land fragmentation, and drought push migrants toward cities seeking casual informal employment.
  • Shelter Poverty: Prohibitive formal housing costs force migrants to squat illegally on railway margins, canal embankments, and marshlands.
  • Dharavi (Mumbai) Ecosystem: World's densest slum (~300,000/sq km) spanning 2.16 sq km; generates $1B+ informal industrial output (leather, pottery).
  • Sanitary Deprivation: Up to 80% households lack private toilets; open drains, contaminated water, and high incidence of tuberculosis.
Redevelopment Dilemma: Vertical high-rise rehabilitation risks dismantling community artisan manufacturing networks.

6. Land Degradation & Watersheds

Landscape Restoration
  • Wasteland Atlas: NRSC categorizes 13 wasteland classes (~17% of total area); gullied lands, ravenous tracts, desert dunes, degraded forests.
  • Chemical Degradation: Over-irrigation in canal commands (Indira Gandhi Canal, Punjab) induces waterlogging, salinization, and alkalization (Reh/Kallar).
  • Jhabua District (MP) Model: Rejuvenation of severely degraded tribal land through contour bunding, check-dams, and social fencing (Charnoi).
  • Ralegan Siddhi (MH): Anna Hazare's community watershed paradigm: bans on tree-felling (Kurhad-bandi), open grazing (Chara-bandi), and Shramdaan.
Ecological Balance: Community-led watershed management replenishes water tables and restores soil organic fertility.
