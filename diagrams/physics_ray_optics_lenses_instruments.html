Lensmaker & Combinations Refracting Surfaces
Lensmaker: 1/f = (μ_g/μ_m - 1) · (1/R₁ - 1/R₂)
Thin Lens: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f & m = v/u
Power in Contact: P = P₁ + P₂ ⟹ 1/F = 1/f₁ + 1/f₂
In Medium: f_med = f_air · (μ_g - 1) / (μ_g/μ_m - 1)
  • Sign Convention: Biconvex lens has $R_1 > 0$, $R_2 < 0 \implies 1/f = (\mu-1)(1/R_1 + 1/R_2) > 0$ ($+P$, converging).
  • Liquid Immersion Flips: If glass lens ($\mu_g = 1.5$) is placed in carbon disulphide ($\mu_m = 1.63 > 1.5$), $( \mu_g/\mu_m - 1 ) < 0$: convex lens diverges!
  • Lens Cutting Rules: Cut longitudinally (along axis): $f' = f$, intensity halved. Cut transversely (perpendicular): $f' = 2f$, power halved.
Power: P = 1 / f(m) in Dioptres (D) m = f / (f + u)
Compound Microscope High Magnification
Total Magnification: M = m_o · m_e = -(v_o/u_o) · (D/u_e)
Near Point (v_e = -D): M = -(v_o/u_o) · (1 + D/f_e)
Normal Adjustment (v_e = ∞): M = -(L/f_o) · (D/f_e)
Tube Length: L ≈ v_o + f_e (Normal adjustment)
  • Objective Constraints: Both focal length $f_o$ and aperture are tiny so that $m_o \approx L/f_o$ is as large as possible.
  • Two-Stage Relay: Objective forms a real, inverted, magnified image $A'B'$ that sits just inside the focal point $F_e$ of the eyepiece.
  • Eyepiece Function: Eyepiece acts as a simple magnifier producing a final virtual, highly enlarged, inverted image.
Least Distance: D = 25 cm Eyepiece: f_e > f_o
Astronomical & Reflecting Telescopes Distant Imaging
Normal Adjustment (v = ∞): M = -f_o / f_e & L = f_o + f_e
Near Point (v = -D): M = -(f_o / f_e) · (1 + f_e / D)
Resolving Power: RP = D_a / (1.22 λ) (D_a = aperture dia)
Brightness Ratio: Intensity ∝ D_a²
  • Objective Demands: To resolve distant stars, $f_o \gg f_e$ and objective aperture $D_a$ must be very large to maximise light collection ($I \propto D_a^2$).
  • Reflecting Advantages: Zero chromatic aberration (reflection is achromatic), parabolic mirrors eliminate spherical aberration, mirror supported fully underneath.
  • Rayleigh Limit: Angular resolution limit $\theta = 1.22 \lambda / D_a$; larger aperture yields sharper diffraction resolution.
Telescope: f_o >> f_e Microscope: f_e > f_o
