GLOBALISATION DYNAMICS & SOCIAL RESISTANCE — THE CONTESTATION MATRIX

FOUR CORE DIMENSIONS OF GLOBAL INTERCONNECTEDNESS

1. Political Dimension
Retreat of welfare state to minimal core (law & order) vs digital surveillance enhancing state capacity.
2. Economic Dimension
Trade liberalization & FDI boom vs unequal distributional impacts; free capital vs restricted labor mobility.
3. Cultural Dimension
Western cultural homogenisation ('McDonaldization') vs creative cultural heterogenisation (hybridity).
4. Anti-Globalisation Fronts
1999 Seattle WTO protests; World Social Forum (WSF); Indian trade union strikes and farmer resistance.

The World Social Forum (WSF) Architecture

Counter-Hegemonic Platform: Conceived as a direct alternative to the corporate World Economic Forum (Davos).
Guiding Slogan: "Another World is Possible" — advocating human-centered democratic globalisation.
Inaugural Summit (2001): Convened in Porto Alegre, Brazil, uniting trade unionists, environmentalists, and indigenous groups.
Fourth WSF in Mumbai (2004): Landmark gathering in India, mobilizing mass participation of Dalits, peasants, and workers.
Decentralized Movement: Operates as an open space without a rigid central command, encouraging grassroots autonomy.

Cultural Homogenisation vs Heterogenisation

Homogenisation (Uniformity): Rise of a standardized, Western consumer culture; dominance of American corporate symbols.
Cultural Imperialism: Threatens to submerge and destroy indigenous languages, culinary traditions, and local crafts.
Heterogenisation (Hybridity): External influences are not blindly copied; they are modified to suit local tastes and values.
Examples of Hybridity: Eating a vegetable burger alongside masala dosa; wearing a handspun khadi kurta over denim jeans.
Cultural Dynamism: Shows that cultures are living systems that can expand choices and enrich traditional identity.

CHRONOLOGY: FROM ECONOMIC REFORMS TO ANTI-GLOBALISATION BATTLES

July 1991

India's NEP: P.V. Narasimha Rao & Manmohan Singh launch LPG reforms; delicensing & tariff reductions.

1 Jan 1995

WTO Enters: World Trade Organization begins enforcing global trade rules, TRIPS, and market access agreements.

Nov 1999

Battle of Seattle: 50,000 protesters shut down the WTO Ministerial Conference; anti-globalisation goes global.

Jan 2001

First WSF: World Social Forum convenes in Porto Alegre, Brazil; champions alternative democratic economics.

Jan 2004

Mumbai WSF: 4th World Social Forum meets in Mumbai, India; historic mobilization of South Asian civil society.

Contemporary

Neo-Protectionism: Backlash against hyper-globalisation; supply chain re-shoring, tariffs, and self-reliance drives.

RESISTANCE TO GLOBALISATION IN INDIA: THE THREE ARENAS

Organized Labor & Trade Unions Central trade unions (AITUC, CITU, BMS, INTUC) staged nationwide strikes against disinvestment of profit-making PSUs, labor casualization, privatization of public utilities, and dilution of protective factory labor laws.
Agrarian & Peasant Struggles Farmer groups (BKU, KRRS) agitated against WTO patent rules (TRIPS), entry of multinational agro-giants (Monsanto, Cargill), patenting of indigenous plants (Neem, Basmati, Turmeric), and withdrawal of power subsidies.
Socio-Cultural Opposition Both Left cultural forums and right-wing nationalist groups (Swadeshi Jagran Manch) protested against Western consumerism, commercialization of education, and vulgar public celebrations (e.g., Valentine's Day protests).

PITFALL 1: WSF vs WEF Conflation

The World Economic Forum (WEF) meets annually in Davos, Switzerland representing corporate business elites. The World Social Forum (WSF) meets in the Global South representing grassroots civil society.

PITFALL 2: State Sovereignty Myth

Globalisation has NOT made the nation-state obsolete! While welfare roles have receded, the state remains the supreme legal entity with a monopoly on coercion, borders, and national defense.

PITFALL 3: Asymmetry of Global Flows

Globalisation is NOT completely borderless. While capital, finance, and manufactured commodities flow with minimal friction, the cross-border movement of human labor remains heavily restricted by passport and visa walls.
