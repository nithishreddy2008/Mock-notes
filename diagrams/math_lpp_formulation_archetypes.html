Mathematical Structure
The 4 Pillars of Every LPP
1. Decision Variables x, y
Quantities to be determined (e.g., units of product A and B). In CUET UG, strictly limited to two variables for 2D graphical solvability.
2. Objective Function Z = a·x + b·y
Linear criterion function to be Maximized (profit, yield, efficiency) or Minimized (cost, time, distance, defect count). Linearity is mandatory.
3. Structural Constraints aᵢx + bᵢy ≤/≥ cᵢ
Linear inequalities representing physical resource limitations (machine hours, raw material supplies, labor capacities, nutritional demands).
4. Non-Negativity Limits x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0
Mandates that real physical goods cannot be negative. Restricts the entire feasible problem universe strictly to the First Quadrant.
A. Manufacturing / Production Profit Max
Maximize Z = p₁·x + p₂·y (Total Profit)
  • Resource Ceiling: Time on Machine 1 ≤ Available Hours ($m_{11}x + m_{12}y \le T_1$).
  • Raw Material Cap: Units of wood/steel ≤ Storage/inventory supply.
  • Key Phrasing: "At most", "not more than", "available limit" $\implies \le$.
B. Diet & Nutrition Problem Cost Min
Minimize Z = c₁·x + c₂·y (Total Cost)
  • Nutrient Floor: Intake of Vitamin A $\ge$ Daily Recommended Intake.
  • Protein Requirement: Total protein content $\ge$ Threshold ($p_1 x + p_2 y \ge P$).
  • Key Phrasing: "At least", "minimum requirement", "no less than" $\implies \ge$.
C. Storage & Allocation Problem Dual Cap
Maximize Z = Profit (Subject to Space & Capital)
  • Capital Constraint: Total purchase price $c_1 x + c_2 y \le \text{Total Capital}$.
  • Storage Capacity: Physical floor units $x + y \le \text{Max Inventory Slots}$.
  • High-Yield Trap: Both constraints are active; corner point intersection gives optimal batch size.
D. Transportation & Routing Cost Min
Minimize Z = ∑ Cost per km · Units Shipped
  • Plant Supply Conservation: Units dispatched $\le$ Total plant production.
  • Depot Demand Fulfillment: Units received $\ge$ Target customer requirement.
  • Surplus Expression: Residual supply to depot 3 expressed as $(S - x - y \ge 0)$.
CUET Inequality Decoders
• "At least c" / "Minimum c" $\implies \mathbf{a x + b y \ge c}$.
• "At most c" / "Not exceeding c" $\implies \mathbf{a x + b y \le c}$.
• Always normalize time units: hours to minutes before setting up equations!
Linear Objective Invariance
• The objective function $Z = a x + b y$ must be strictly linear.
• Terms like $x^2$, $x y$, or $\sqrt{x}$ invalidate the linear programming framework.
• $a$ and $b$ represent constant marginal unit profits or costs.
Redundant Constraint Trap
• A constraint is redundant if removing it leaves the feasible region unchanged.
• Example: If $x + y \le 4$ and $x + y \le 8$ are both given with $x, y \ge 0$, the constraint $x + y \le 8$ is completely redundant!
