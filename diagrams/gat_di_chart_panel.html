Axis: each unit = Rs 100 of sales · the units line is the FIRST thing to read

1Read the frame: title, axes, units

Title says what is shown, axes say in what units. Here: sales in Rs hundreds — a bar marked 40 means Rs 4,000. Skipping units is the #1 DI error.

2Read the question before the bars

"Highest month?" needs only comparisons. "Percentage increase Jan to Apr?" needs the two exact values — 40 and 70. The question tells you which bars to touch.

3Pull the numbers, compute, sanity-check

Work with the bar values; check the answer against the picture — a "75% rise" between the shortest and tallest bar passes the eye test.

Solved on this chart

Highest month: April (70). % increase Jan to Apr: rise = 70 − 40 = 30; on base 40 → 30/40 = 75%. Average of the four months: (40+55+45+70) ÷ 4 = 210 ÷ 4 = 52.5 (Rs 5,250).

Traps that catch most students

Ignoring the units line — hundreds vs thousands flips every answer
Comparing bar heights by eye for exact questions — pull the numbers instead
Percent-change on the wrong base — change is always divided by the ORIGINAL value
