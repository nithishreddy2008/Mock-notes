The Syndicate & Successions
Organizational Bosses vs Executive Leadership
The Syndicate Power Troika Key Figures
  • K. Kamaraj: Former TN CM & Congress President (creator of Kamaraj Plan 1963). Kingmaker in both 1964 and 1966.
  • S. Nijalingappa: Powerful Karnataka leader; Congress President during the 1969 split.
  • S.K. Patil (Bombay), Atulya Ghosh (WB), N. Sanjiva Reddy (AP): Regional organisational stalwarts.
  • Ideological Tilt: Conservative, pro-business, cautious, party-machine supremacy over the Prime Minister.
Organisational Bosses Party Supremacy
Succession Trials (1964 & 1966) Smooth Transitions
  • 1964 (Nehru Death): Kamaraj consulted MPs/party workers; consensus selected Lal Bahadur Shastri. Gulzarilal Nanda served as caretaker.
  • Shastri Premiership: Guided India through 1965 War (coined Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan); died Tashkent (10 Jan 1966).
  • 1966 Contest: Morarji Desai forced secret ballot. Indira won 355 to 169 with Syndicate backing.
  • Syndicate Calculation: Expected Indira to be inexperienced (Gungi Gudiya) and dependent on party bosses.
Consensus (1964) Secret Ballot (1966)
CUET Distinction: Gulzarilal Nanda was twice caretaker PM (1964, 1966); Shastri and Indira were the duly designated Prime Ministers.
2
1967 Shock & The 1969 Split
Non-Congressism, Populism & Factional Rupture
1967 Fourth General Election "Political Earthquake"
  • Economic Crises: Consecutive monsoon failures, food shortages, price rise, and 1966 Rupee devaluation.
  • Non-Congressism: Dr. Rammanohar Lohia united disparate opposition forces against the Congress umbrella.
  • Electoral Upset: Congress Lok Sabha majority slashed to 283; lost power in 8 states (7 at polls + Haryana via defection).
  • SVD Coalitions & Defections: Samyukta Vidhayak Dal ministries formed; Gaya Lal coined Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram.
283 LS Seats SVD Coalitions
The Great Split of 1969 Conscience Vote
  • Socialist Pivot: Indira floated 10-Point Programme (1967); nationalised 14 banks (July 1969); stripped Morarji of Finance.
  • Presidential Crisis: Zakir Husain died (May 1969). Syndicate nominated N. Sanjiva Reddy; Indira backed VP V.V. Giri.
  • Conscience Vote: Indira urged MPs/MLAs to vote conscience; V.V. Giri won as Independent.
  • Formal Split: Nijalingappa expelled Indira (Nov 1969). Split into Congress (O) [Syndicate] & Congress (R) [Indira].
14 Banks Nationalised Congress (R) vs (O)
Core Fact: Indira headed a minority government (1969–1971) surviving on external floor support from CPI and DMK.
3
1971 Landslide & Restoration
Electoral Triumph & Altered Institutional Fabric
1971 Fifth Lok Sabha Elections Garibi Hatao
  • The Grand Alliance: Cong(O), Swatantra, Jana Sangh, SSP, PSP formed anti-Indira bloc with slogan Indira Hatao.
  • Indira's Positive Narrative: Countered with pro-poor, radical agenda: Garibi Hatao (Abolish Poverty).
  • Abolition of Privy Purses: Passed 26th Amendment (1971) ending royal pensions after Supreme Court initially blocked it.
  • Tectonic Verdict: Congress (R) won 352 seats (375 with CPI ally; 48.4% vote). Cong(O) won only 16 seats.
352 Cong(R) Seats Cong(O) Collapsed
Restoration or Reinvention? Structural Shift
  • Nehruvian Congress: Inclusive umbrella accommodating diverse factions, state satraps, and internal debates.
  • Indira's Congress: Centralised, highly personalised command structure centred entirely around supreme leader.
  • Erosion of Satraps: State CMs became central nominees rather than independent mass leaders.
  • Bangladesh Triumph: Dec 1971 war victory solidified Indira's unchallenged authority across national politics.
Centralised Command Personalised Mandate
Analytical Core: Indira restored the Congress to supreme power, but NOT the original internal consensus-based 'Congress System'.
