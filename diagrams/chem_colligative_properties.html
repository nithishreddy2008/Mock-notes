The Four Colligative Properties & Van 't Hoff Factor (i) Matrix

Particle-Number Dependence • Molar Mass Calculations • Electrolytic Dissociation & Molecular Association

CUET UG Chemistry · Unit 1 Solutions

1. Relative Lowering of VP

Raoult's Law
(p₁° - p₁) / p₁° = i · x₂
M₂ = [i · w₂ · M₁] / [w₁ · (Δp/p₁°)]
  • Surface Blockage: Involatile solute molecules occupy surface sites, reducing solvent vaporisation rate.
  • Direct Mole Fraction: Relative lowering equals solute mole fraction (x₂), independent of temperature.
  • Dilute Solutions: When n₂ << n₁, x₂ ≈ n₂/n₁ = (w₂ M₁) / (M₂ w₁).
Key Distinction: Lowering of VP (Δp) depends on temperature; *Relative* lowering (Δp/p₁°) is temperature-invariant.

2. Elevation of Boiling Point

Ebullioscopy
ΔT_b = T_b - T_b° = i · K_b · m
M₂ = [1000 · i · K_b · w₂] / [ΔT_b · w₁]
  • Vapour Pressure Deficit: Solution boils when VP = 1 atm. Because VP is lowered, a higher temperature is required.
  • Molal Constant (K_b): Ebullioscopic constant depends solely on solvent nature: K_b = (R · M₁ · T_b°²) / (1000 · Δ_vapH).
  • Water Constant: K_b for H₂O = 0.52 K·kg·mol⁻¹ (units: K·kg·mol⁻¹ or °C/m).
Exam Trap: 1 molal NaCl elevates boiling point by 2 × 0.52 = 1.04 K, whereas 1 molal glucose elevates it by only 0.52 K.

3. Depression of Freezing Point

Cryoscopy
ΔT_f = T_f° - T_f = i · K_f · m
M₂ = [1000 · i · K_f · w₂] / [ΔT_f · w₁]
  • Solid-Liquid Equilibrium: Solution freezes when liquid solvent VP equals pure solid solvent VP.
  • Cryoscopic Constant (K_f): Depends solely on solvent properties: K_f = (R · M₁ · T_f°²) / (1000 · Δ_fusH).
  • Water Constant: K_f for H₂O = 1.86 K·kg·mol⁻¹. Camphor has exceptionally high K_f (39.7) for Rast method.
Everyday Anti-Freeze: Ethylene glycol added to car radiators lowers freezing point in winter and elevates boiling point in summer.

4. Osmotic Pressure & RO

Macromolecules
Π = i · C · R · T = i · (n₂ / V) · R · T
M₂ = [i · w₂ · R · T] / [Π · V]
  • Semipermeable Membrane: Pure solvent flows spontaneously from dilute to concentrated solution through SPM.
  • Pressure Definition: Hydrostatic excess pressure applied on solution to halt solvent ingress.
  • Reverse Osmosis (RO): Applying external pressure P > Π forces pure solvent from solution into pure water side (cellulose acetate SPM).
Best for Polymers: Preferred for proteins and polymers because Π is measurable at room temp and avoids thermal denaturation.

Van 't Hoff Factor (i) & Abnormal Molar Mass Determination

i = Observed Colligative Property / Calculated Value = Normal Molar Mass / Experimental Molar Mass

Non-Electrolytes (Ideal) i = 1

i = 1 | M_exp = M_normal
No association or dissociation takes place in solution. Particle count equals dissolved formula unit count.
Examples: Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), Urea (NH₂CONH₂), Sucrose (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁).

Electrolytic Dissociation i > 1

i = 1 + (n - 1)·α | M_exp < M_normal
Solute splits into n ions. Colligative property increases proportionally, causing experimental molar mass to read artificially low.
Examples: NaCl (n=2, i≈2), K₂SO₄ (n=3, i≈3), Al₂(SO₄)₃ (n=5, i≈5), BaCl₂ (n=3).

Molecular Association i < 1

i = 1 + (1/n - 1)·β | M_exp > M_normal
Solute molecules dimerise or polymerise via intermolecular H-bonding. Particle count drops, causing molar mass to read artificially high.
Examples: Benzoic acid in benzene (n=2, i≈0.5, M_exp≈244 vs 122), Acetic acid in benzene.
