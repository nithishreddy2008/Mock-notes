IMMUNOLOGY & RETROVIRAL PATHOGENESIS BLUEPRINT
Part 1: Human Immune System Architecture
Innate vs Acquired
Innate Immunity (Non-Specific, Present from Birth) 4 Barriers
• Physical Barriers: Skin (keratinized stratum corneum blocks entry); Mucous coating of respiratory, gastrointestinal & urogenital tracts traps microbes.
• Physiological Barriers: Acid (HCl) in stomach, saliva in mouth, lysozyme in tears prevent microbial growth.
• Cellular Barriers: Leukocytes such as PMNL-neutrophils, monocytes, Natural Killer (NK) lymphocytes, and tissue macrophages phagocytose pathogens.
• Cytokine Barriers: Interferons (proteins secreted by virus-infected cells) protect non-infected neighboring cells from viral infection.
Acquired: Humoral Immunity (Antibody-Mediated / AMI) B-Cells · H₂L₂
• B-Lymphocytes: Produce an army of proteins called antibodies in response to antigens.
• Antibody Molecule Structure: H2L2 configuration — 2 identical Heavy chains + 2 identical Light chains connected by disulfide bonds. Tips contain antigen-binding sites (*paratopes*).
• Antibody Classes: IgG (most abundant, crosses placenta); IgA (abundant in yellowish fluid colostrum secreted during early lactation, protects infant); IgM (pentamer, primary response); IgE (mediates allergic reactions by binding mast cells); IgD.
Acquired: Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI) T-Cells · Graft Rejection
• T-Lymphocytes: Do not secrete antibodies directly, but help B-cells produce them (TH helper cells) and directly lyse infected/tumor cells (TC cytotoxic cells).
• Organ Transplantation & Graft Rejection: CMI is exclusively responsible for graft rejection. Body differentiates "self" from "non-self". Tissue typing (HLA) and blood matching mandatory; patients require life-long immunosuppressants (Cyclosporin A).
Primary vs. Secondary Lymphoid Organs MALT = 50%
• Primary (Origin & Maturation): Bone marrow (all blood cells including B & T-cells produce; B-cells mature) & Thymus (lobular organ near heart, T-cells mature; shrinks with age).
• Secondary (Interaction with Antigens): Spleen (large bean-shaped reservoir of erythrocytes & phagocytes), lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches of small intestine.
• MALT (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue): Lines respiratory, digestive & urogenital tracts; constitutes about 50% of lymphoid tissue in human body!
CUET Top Immunology Traps:
• Graft Rejection: Governed by Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI), NOT Humoral / B-cells!
• Colostrum: Provides Passive Immunity via ready-made IgA antibodies.
• Auto-Immune Disorder: Body's immune system attacks self-cells (e.g., Rheumatoid arthritis).
Part 2: Retrovirus (HIV) Replication & AIDS Progression
Reverse Transcription
1
Viral Attachment: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) binds via glycoprotein spikes (gp120) to CD4 receptor molecules on host Macrophages and Helper T-lymphocytes (TH).
2
Viral Entry & Uncoating: Viral protein coat fuses with host cell membrane; viral single-stranded RNA (ssRNA) core and Reverse Transcriptase enzyme enter host cytoplasm.
3
Reverse Transcription: Viral enzyme Reverse Transcriptase copies viral ssRNA into complementary viral double-stranded DNA (cDNA).
4
Integration into Host Genome: Viral DNA migrates into host cell nucleus and integrates into host chromosomal DNA, catalyzed by viral Integrase.
5
"HIV Factory" in Macrophages: The infected macrophage transcribes viral DNA, producing viral RNA genomes and viral proteins. Macrophage continues to produce virus particles acting as an HIV factory.
6
Attack on Helper T-Cells (TH): Progeny HIV particles enter bloodstream and specifically attack TH lymphocytes, replicate within them, burst the cells, and release progeny to infect new TH cells.
7
Immune Collapse & Opportunistic Infections: Progressive depletion of TH count impairs cell-mediated and humoral immunity. Patient develops bouts of fever, diarrhea, weight loss, and succumbs to infections caused by Mycobacterium, viruses, fungi, and Toxoplasma.
CUET AIDS Diagnostics & Prevention Rules:
• Diagnostic Test: Widely screened using ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay); confirmatory test is Western Blot.
• Transmission Modes: Sexual contact, contaminated blood transfusion, infected needles, infected mother to child via placenta.
• Treatment: Anti-retroviral drugs (only partially effective; prolongs life but cannot prevent death). NACO (National AIDS Control Organisation) and NGOs lead public education.
