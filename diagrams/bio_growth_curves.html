Population Growth Models — Exponential (J-Curve) vs. Logistic (S-Curve)

NCERT Class 12 Biology · Unit X · Organisms and Populations · Mathematical Derivations & Ecological Realism

OurMocks CUET UG

Model 1: Exponential / Geometric Growth

J-Shaped Curve
Differential & Integral Forms dN/dt = rN | N(t) = N(0) · e^(rt)
Underlying Condition: Occurs when resources (food, space, mates) are completely unlimited and non-exhaustible in the habitat. Zero intraspecific competition.
Intrinsic Rate of Natural Increase (r): Measure of inherent biotic potential ($r = b - d$).
• Norway rat: $r = 0.015$ | • Flour beetle: $r = 0.12$ | • Human in India (1981): $r = 0.0205$.
Ecological Consequence: Results in population explosion. In nature, unchecked exponential growth cannot continue indefinitely—abrupt population crash occurs when resources deplete.

Model 2: Logistic / Verhulst-Pearl Growth

Sigmoid S-Curve
Verhulst-Pearl Equation dN/dt = rN · [(K - N) / K]
Carrying Capacity (K): The maximum sustainable population density that a given habitat can support indefinitely with available finite resources without degradation.
Environmental Resistance [(K - N) / K]: Quantifies the fractional carrying capacity still available. When $N = K$, term becomes $0$, and growth ceases completely ($d N / d t = 0$).
Four Successive Phases: Lag phase $arrow$ Acceleration phase $arrow$ Deceleration phase $arrow$ Asymptote. Considered biologically realistic for all natural populations.
High-Yield NCERT Exam Distinction
Exponential: Unrealistic; assumes infinite food/space; J-curve without asymptote ($N$ climbs without limit).
Ecological Realism Rule
Logistic: Realistic; accounts for intraspecific competition and environmental carrying capacity ($K$); Sigmoid S-curve.
Population Density Formula
$N_{t+1} = N_t + [(B + I) - (D + E)]$: Births ($B$) + Immigrations ($I$) increase $N$; Deaths ($D$) + Emigrations ($E$) decrease $N$.
