ORGANISING
UNIT V · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Meaning, Process & Importance

Organising = defining and grouping activities and establishing authority relationships among them
Process (4 steps): divide work → departmentalise → assign duties → establish reporting relationships
Importance: specialisation · clarity in relationships · optimum use of resources · adaptation to change · effective administration · growth · development of personnel

Structures: Functional vs Divisional

Functional: grouped by functions — production, marketing, finance, HR. Best for large, single-product firms
+ specialisation, efficiency, less duplication → − one function's interest dominates, coordination harder
Divisional: grouped by product lines or regions, each with its own functions. Best for multi-product firms
+ product accountability, fast decisions, easy expansion → − duplication of resources, rivalry, high cost

Formal vs Informal Organisation

Formal: deliberately designed structure of authority-responsibility relationships; shown in the org chart
+ fixed accountability, no overlapping → − rigid, slow, impersonal
Informal: arises from social interaction — friends at work, no written rules, fast grapevine
+ speed, belongingness, fills gaps → − rumours, resistance to change, no accountability

Delegation — Elements & Importance

Delegation = manager grants authority to a subordinate to perform assigned duties
Elements (3): Authority (right to decide) → Responsibility (duty to perform) → Accountability (answerability — cannot be delegated!)
Authority = responsibility; accountability flows always upward to the boss
Importance: reduces workload → employee development → quick decisions → expansion · specialisation → basis of hierarchy

Decentralisation — Meaning & Importance

Decentralisation = systematic dispersal of decision-making authority to all levels of management
It extends delegation to the whole organisation — the top deliberately keeps less power
Importance: develops initiative at all levels → develops future managers → quick decisions at the spot → relieves top of routine burden → better control (profit centres) → growth

Delegation vs Decentralisation

Origin: delegation arises from law/position (compulsory); decentralisation is the top's voluntary choice
Scope: delegation is between two individuals; decentralisation spreads across all levels
Control: delegation keeps control with the boss; decentralisation needs result-based control (ROIs)
Freedom: delegation is more freedom for subordinates; decentralisation is the maximum

Traps that catch most students

Accountability cannot be delegated — the boss stays answerable even after passing the duty
Authority and responsibility must match — authority without responsibility corrupts; responsibility without authority frustrates
Delegation = compulsory (all managers delegate); decentralisation = optional (top management's policy choice)
Informal organisation has no org chart — it grows from friendships, not design; and it can spread rumours
