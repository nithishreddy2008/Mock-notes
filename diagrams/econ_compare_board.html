INDIA vs CHINA vs PAKISTAN — THREE ROADS FROM ONE STARTING LINE
Dimension INDIA 🇮🇳 CHINA 🇨🇳 PAKISTAN 🇵🇰
Development strategy Mixed economy + planning — public sector at the heights, private coexistence, import substitution Central planning — communes, state industry, collective farming (1950s-70s) Mixed economy — agriculture-centred, supported by foreign aid and remittances
Reforms — when and how 1991, crisis-forced, democratic debate, gradual LPG 1978 — EARLIEST; state-led, fast: agriculture decollectivised, Special Economic Zones, FDI invited Late 1980s (≈1988) — IMF-backed structural adjustment, later and slower
Sectoral development Services-led — IT and services grew fastest; manufacturing skipped a stage; agriculture still employs many Manufacturing powerhouse — the factory of the world; exports boomed; then services rising Agriculture first — then services; industry remains the weak leg; remittances carry the economy
Population policy Family planning — voluntary, uneven success One-child policy (strict, later relaxed) — growth controlled fast, but ageing begins Moderate family planning — population still growing fast
Human development Middle of the pack — literacy and life expectancy above Pakistan's, below China's; gender gaps persist The leader — highest life expectancy, literacy and incomes of the three; mass health and education push The weakest — lowest literacy and life expectancy among the three; heavy gender gaps
Political system's role Democracy — reforms need consensus, so slower but stable One-party state — reforms could be fast, top-down, unopposed Alternating civilian-military rule — policy continuity suffers
The lesson each teaches Democratic + services route: growth with freedom, but jobs must catch up Speed, SEZs, infrastructure and investment in people pay off — at the cost of freedoms Neglecting industry and education holds development back; remittance dependence is fragile

TRAP 1

Reform dates: China 1978 → Pakistan late 1980s → India 1991. Reversing this order is the most common MCQ slip.

TRAP 2

Sector leaders: China = manufacturing, India = services, Pakistan = agriculture-first. Do not swap the engines.

TRAP 3

Shared start, different finish: all three began as low-income planned economies — the divergence is POLICY, not destiny.
