INTERNATIONAL ORGANISATIONS
CONTEMPORARY WORLD POLITICS · CH. 4 (TOPIC 13)

1. Evolution & Organs of the UN

Founded 24 Oct 1945: Succeeded failed League of Nations; 51 founding members (Poland signed later); India joined 30 Oct 1945.
General Assembly: 193 member states; one-nation one-vote; debates all global issues and passes non-binding resolutions.
Security Council (UNSC): 15 members; 5 permanent (P5: US, UK, France, Russia, China) with veto power + 10 non-permanent (2-yr terms).
Secretariat & ICJ: Headed by Secretary-General (António Guterres); International Court of Justice based in The Hague.

2. UN Reforms & Structural Overhaul

Post-Cold War Realities: Disintegration of USSR and rise of developing powers necessitate UNSC democratization.
Kofi Annan's 1997 Criteria: Major economic power, major military power, substantial budget contributor, population, democracy.
New Institutional Bodies (2005): Peacebuilding Commission, Human Rights Council (UNHRC 2006), Responsibility to Protect (R2P).
Veto Controversy: Abolition vs expansion; P5 fiercely protect veto; veto prevents UN action in conflicts (Syria, Ukraine).

3. India's Claim for UNSC Permanent Seat

Pillars of Candidature: World's most populous nation, largest democracy, 5th largest global economy, nuclear restraint.
Peacekeeping Legacy: Among the top cumulative troop contributors to UN Peacekeeping Operations worldwide.
The G4 Coalition: Joint diplomatic bid with Brazil, Germany, and Japan for mutual permanent seats.
Obstacles: Chinese opposition; Pakistan-led 'Uniting for Consensus' (Coffee Club); P5 reluctance to share veto power.

4. Bretton Woods Bodies: IMF & World Bank

IMF (1944): Oversees global monetary system; lends foreign exchange in crisis under strict austerity conditionalities.
Weighted Voting: Voting power tied to financial quota; US possesses virtual veto power with over 16.5% voting share.
World Bank (1944): Provides concessional development loans, infrastructure financing, and poverty reduction funding to Global South.
Democratic Deficit: US traditionally nominates World Bank President; European powers nominate IMF Managing Director.

5. WTO & International Atomic Energy

World Trade Organization (1995): Succeeded GATT (1948); 164 member nations; sets rules for global trade and settles disputes.
Power Disparities: Wealthy nations dominate agricultural subsidy negotiations and Intellectual Property (TRIPS) enforcement.
IAEA (1957): International Atomic Energy Agency founded under Eisenhower's 'Atoms for Peace'; headquartered in Vienna.
Nuclear Safeguards: Inspects civilian nuclear reactors worldwide to prevent military diversion of nuclear fuel.

6. Human Rights NGOs & Unipolar Power

Amnesty International (1961): Global NGO campaigning for political prisoners, abolition of death penalty, and human dignity.
Human Rights Watch: Researches and spotlights wartime atrocities, civilian abuses, and authoritarian repressions.
Limits of the UN: UN cannot act against the US superpower due to veto power, host status in New York, and US military supremacy.
Indispensability: Despite flaws, the UN remains the only universal forum for international negotiation and global public goods.
