Deep Ecology Arne Næss (1973), George Sessions, Gary Snyder The Shallow and the Deep, Long-Range Ecology Movement Biocentric Egalitarianism; intrinsic value of all living entities independent of human utility; self-realization beyond ego. Rejection of anthropocentrism; 8-point platform; Earth First! activism.
Shallow / Reform Ecology Mainstream technocrats, Resource managers Brundtland Report (Our Common Future, 1987) Anthropocentric environmentalism; fighting pollution & resource depletion primarily to safeguard human affluence and health. End-of-pipe tech fixes; market instruments; contrasts sharply with Deep Ecology.
Social Ecology Murray Bookchin (1960s-1980s) The Ecology of Freedom (1982), Post-Scarcity Anarchism Ecological crises stem directly from human-on-human hierarchical domination, social inequality, and capitalist exploitation. Rejects biocentrism as anti-human; advocates libertarian municipalism.
Ecofeminism Françoise d'Eaubonne (1974), Vandana Shiva, Maria Mies Staying Alive (Shiva, 1988), Ecofeminism (Mies & Shiva) Interconnected domination of nature and women under patriarchal-capitalist systems; nurturance vs violent reductionism. Chipko movement connection; seed sovereignty (Navdanya); critique of Green Revolution.
Land Ethic / Stewardship Aldo Leopold (1949), Wendell Berry A Sand County Almanac, The Unsettling of America "A thing is right when it tends to preserve the integrity, stability, and beauty of the biotic community. It is wrong otherwise." Enlarges boundary of community to include soils, waters, plants; ethical stewardship.
Green Politics / Ecosocialism Barry Commoner, Petra Kelly (German Greens) The Closing Circle (1971), Thinking Green! Four Pillars: Ecological wisdom, social justice, grassroots democracy, non-violence. First Law: "Everything is connected to everything else." Commoner's 4 Laws of Ecology; institutionalizing green legislative mandates.
