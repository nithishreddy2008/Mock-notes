ERA OF ONE-PARTY DOMINANCE 1947-67
POLITICS IN INDIA SINCE INDEPENDENCE · CH. 2

The First Three Elections

Universal adult franchise: every adult could vote from day one — 17 crore+ voters, 85% illiterate
1951-52: Congress 364/489; symbols made voting possible for non-readers
1957: Congress 371/494; Kerala elects the world's first communist government
1962: Congress 361/494; opposition bench grows (CPI 29, Swatantra 18, BJS 14)

Nature of Congress Dominance

National level: office at the Centre and in almost every state for two decades
Consensus-based: dominance won by agreement and inclusion, not by force or fraud
"Congress system" (Rajni Kothari): one dominant party + opposition acting as pressure groups
Elections stayed free: rivals campaigned openly; Nehru accepted defeats and defied his own party when needed

Coalitional Nature of Congress

Ideological rainbow: socialists, conservatives, traditionalists — all housed in one party
Social rainbow: upper castes, peasants, workers, Dalits, minorities — factions for each
Faction management: state bosses bargained with the high command; conflict stayed inside the party
Effect: groups fought for power WITHIN Congress instead of leaving it — dominance fed on inclusion

Uneven Dominance in the States

Kerala: CPI's 1957 win — Congress out of power in a full state
J&K: National Conference dominated, not Congress
Punjab / PEPSU: Akali Dal strong; Madras: DMK rising
West Bengal, Orissa: left and regional pockets resisted Congress sweeps

Major Opposition Parties

Socialists (SP → PSP 1952): JP Narayan, Lohia — democratic socialism, "third force"
CPI: 1951 programme chose constitutional path; Kerala 1957 its landmark
Jana Sangh (1951): S.P. Mookerjee — one nation one culture, Hindi, uniform civil code
Swatantra (1959): C. Rajagopalachari — free enterprise against the licence raj

What the Opposition Did

Parties of pressure: they shaped policy without forming the government — socialists pushed land reform, Swatantra pushed free-market debate
Kerala model: showed a non-Congress government was possible — and survivable
Idea benches: Jana Sangh's cultural nationalism and CPI's class politics broadened the national debate
Seed of 1967: the scattered opposition of this era regrouped to break Congress in half the states

Traps that catch most students

Votes vs seats: Congress polled ~45% of votes but won ~75% of seats — first-past-the-post magnifies leads
PSP ≠ SP: the Socialist Party merged with Kripalani's KMPP in 1952 to form the Praja Socialist Party
Swatantra is 1959 — it fought only the 1962 election in this chapter's span; do not list it in 1952
Dominance ≠ absence of democracy — the era had free elections and real defeats
