FINANCIAL MARKET
WHERE SAVINGS MEET THOSE WHO NEED FUNDS
MONEY MARKET
SHORT-TERM FUNDS — UP TO ONE YEAR
CAPITAL MARKET
LONG-TERM FUNDS — MORE THAN ONE YEAR

Money market instruments

Call money — overnight borrowing between banks to balance cash reserves
Treasury bills (T-bills) — RBI's short-term papers issued at a discount for the government
Commercial paper — big, creditworthy firms' unsecured promissory notes (15 days – 1 year)
Certificate of deposit — banks' time-deposit receipts, tradable
Commercial bill — trade bill for goods bought on credit, discountable before maturity

Primary market — new issues

Securities are sold for the first time — money flows to the company
IPO / public issue through an offer document · Rights issue to existing shareholders
Private placement to select investors · e-IPO through the internet
Capital formation happens here — the firm's bank account actually receives funds

Secondary market — existing securities

Buying and selling of already-issued securities on stock exchanges
Money flows investor to investor — the company gets nothing from the trade
Gives liquidity to primary-market buyers — exit is always possible

Two one-line distinctions

Money vs capital: one year is the wall — instruments up to 1 year sit in the money market
Primary vs secondary: company receives funds only in the primary market
Both markets need each other — liquidity in the secondary keeps primary buyers willing
Exam anchor: classification questions dominate this unit — read the instrument's tenure (≤ 1 year = money market) and the destination of the money (to the company = primary; to another investor = secondary), and the answer falls out.
