India: Urban Waste, Slum Dynamics & Land Degradation Taxonomy

Municipal Solid Waste Dilemmas, Informal Squatter Ecosystems, Dharavi Case & Watershed Interventions

NCERT Class XII Geography · Unit 9

1. Solid Waste Crisis

Urban Accumulation
Volume & Generation Pattern 62M Tonnes/Yr

Composition: ~50% biodegradable organic waste, ~20% recyclable materials (paper, plastics, glass), ~30% inert construction debris.

Generation Driver: Consumerist urban lifestyle, rapid packaging expansion, and disposable plastic culture.

Open Dumping Hazards Leachate & Fires

Unlined Landfills: Massive open garbage mountains (Ghazipur/Bhallswa in Delhi, Deonar in Mumbai).

Aquifer Poisoning: Rainwater percolation through unlined dumps generates toxic black leachate, contaminating groundwater with heavy metals.
Atmospheric Emissions & Vectors Methane Plumes

Spontaneous Fires: Anaerobic digestion generates methane gas (CH₄), triggering toxic persistent landfill infernos.

Biological Disease Vectors: Uncontrolled breeding ground for flies, rodents, mosquitoes, transmitting typhoid, cholera, and dengue.

Scientific Management Cycle Swachh Bharat

Hierarchical Approach: Source segregation (wet vs dry), decentralized composting, material recovery facilities (MRFs), sanitary landfilling, and waste-to-energy conversion.

2. Migration & Slums

Informal Growth
Rural Push Factors Agrarian Distress

Underlying Drivers: Severe land fragmentation, rural underemployment, recurrent crop failures, indebtedness, and caste discrimination in feudal agrarian setups.

Ecological Push: Chronic droughts and desertification in rainfed drylands.

Urban Pull Dynamics Wage & Services

Attractions: Higher wage opportunities in construction, transport, domestic help, and casual informal manufacturing, alongside access to civic services.

Shelter Poverty & Encroachment Squatter Shanties

Genesis: Inability of low-income rural migrants to afford exorbitant urban real estate rents forces occupation of marginal public lands.

Locations: Canal banks, railway tracks, marshy floodplains, and precarious steep hillslopes prone to flash floods.
Deprivation & Living Conditions Sanitary Deficit

Characteristics: Dilapidated tin/polythene huts, extreme overcrowding (6-8 persons per tiny room), lack of piped drinking water, open drainage, and lack of private toilets.

3. Dharavi Case Study

Microcosm of Slum
Geographic & Demographic Profile Central Mumbai

Location: Spans 2.16 sq km sandwiched between Western and Central suburban rail arteries; population estimated between 700,000 to 1,000,000.

Density: World's highest population density (~300,000+ people/sq km), with multi-story tin tenements lining 1-metre alleys.
Informal Industrial Powerhouse $1B+ Turnover

Thriving Micro-Economy: Operates thousands of micro-enterprises: leather tanning, garment fabrication, Kumbharwada pottery, brass metal work.

Recycling Capital: Recycles over 80% of Mumbai's recyclable plastic waste through highly specialized labour networks.

Acuteness of Infrastructure Deficits Sanitary Stress

Deficit Indicators: Up to 80% households rely on shared public community toilets; intermittent chlorinated water supply; zero natural ventilation.

Disease Burden: High prevalence of pulmonary tuberculosis, skin infections, and waterborne gastroenteritis.

Redevelopment Paradox In-Situ vs High-Rise

Challenge: Transforming slums into modern towers risks destroying the ground-floor informal manufacturing workspaces and community artisan clusters.

4. Land Degradation

Wasteland Dynamics
NRSC Wasteland Classification 13 Categories

Scope: National Remote Sensing Centre identifies ~17% of India's land as wastelands (gullied/ravinous land, shifting sands, waterlogged areas, saline soils, degraded forest).

Chambal Ravines & Water Erosion Gully Erosion

Genesis: Accelerated hydraulic dissection of deep alluvial soils in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and UP, creating inhospitable "badlands" (Bhihad).

Jhabua Watershed Model (MP) Tribal Rejuvenation

Context: Western MP tribal district (Bhil community) suffered acute environmental degradation and out-migration.

Interventions: Under Rajiv Gandhi Watershed Mission, built contour bunds, check dams, and social fencing of pasturelands (Charnoi), restoring aquifers and cropping.
Ralegan Siddhi Experience Ahmednagar · MH

Community Watershed: Spearheaded by Anna Hazare; implemented water percolation tanks, ban on open grazing (Chara-bandi), tree felling (Kurhad-bandi), and voluntary collective labour (Shramdaan).
