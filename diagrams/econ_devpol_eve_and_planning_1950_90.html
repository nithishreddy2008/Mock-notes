Colonial Inheritance & Planning Framework: 1947 to 1990

The Eve of Independence, Four Goals of Five Year Plans, Land Reforms, Green Revolution & Industrial Policy Resolution 1956

NCERT CLASS 12 · INDIAN ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
1. Colonial Inheritance (1947)
Stagnant & Exploited
Agriculture: Feudal Stagnation
Zamindari System: Landlords extracted maximum rent without capital investment.
Commercialisation: Forced shift to cash crops (indigo, cotton) feeding UK mills.
Partition Shock: Loss of rich irrigated areas & East Pakistan jute tracts.
Industry: Systematic De-industrialisation
Handicrafts Ruin: British tariffs destroyed world-famous Indian textiles.
Raw Material Exporter: India reduced to supplier of raw goods & importer of finished goods.
Bleak Capital Goods: Modern industry limited to TISCO (1907) & tiny public utilities.
Demographic & Drain Metrics
1921: Year of Great Divide: Transition to continuous population growth.
Indicators: Literacy < 16%, IMR 218/1000, Life Expectancy 32-44 yrs.
Drain of Wealth: Export surplus used for British "Home Charges" & war debts.
V.K.R.V. Rao's national income estimates were considered most significant by economists.
2. Five Year Plans Architecture
Mahalanobis Strategy
Institutional Genesis
• Planning Commission: Established in 1950 (PM Nehru as Chairman). Replaced by NITI Aayog on Jan 1, 2015.
• Architect: P.C. Mahalanobis formulated the heavy-industry-focused 2nd Five Year Plan (1956-61).
The Four Core Goals of Planning
1. Growth: Expanding GDP capacity and volume of output produced.
2. Modernisation: Adopting modern technology & social transformation (gender equality).
3. Self-Reliance: Avoiding foreign dependence, especially in foodgrain imports (PL-480).
4. Equity: Ensuring benefits of development reach the poorest, reducing wealth gaps.
Core Philosophy: "Growth with Social Justice" under a Mixed Economy model where Public Sector occupies the "commanding heights".
3. Sectoral Policies (1950-1990)
State-Led Inward Model
Agriculture: Land Reforms & Green Revolution
Land Reforms: Abolished intermediaries (200 lakh tenants freed), Land Ceilings enacted.
Green Revolution: HYV miracle seeds (M.S. Swaminathan), chemical fertilizers, irrigation. Transformed food deficit to Marketed Surplus & buffer stocks.
Industry: IPR 1956 & SSI Protection
IPR 1956: 3-tier schedules (Sched A: 17 state-only; Sched B: 12 mixed; Sched C: private under license).
Karve Committee (1955): SSI promoted for labour-intensity; products reserved; investment cap ₹5 lakh.
Foreign Trade: Inward Import Substitution
• Strategy: Produce domestically instead of importing.
• Tools: Heavy tariffs (import taxes) & strict quotas (volume ceilings).
• Cost: Inefficient domestic monopolies ("License-Permit Raj") with zero foreign competition.
CUET Milestone: 1950 (Planning Commission) → 1955 (Karve SSI) → 1956 (IPR & Mahalanobis 2nd Plan) → Mid-60s (Green Revolution).
