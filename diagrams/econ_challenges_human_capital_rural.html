Human Capital Formation & Rural Development Architecture

Sources of Human Capital, Institutional Rural Credit Systems, Marketing Reforms & Agricultural Diversification

CLASS 12 · INDIAN ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
1. Human Capital Formation (HCF)
People as Resource
Five Sources of Human Capital
1. Education: Expands mental horizons, enhances productivity & earning capacity.
2. Health: Preventive, curative & social medicine; directly raises labour efficiency.
3. On-the-job Training: Apprenticeships enhance specialized firm-level skills.
4. Migration: Seeking higher earnings; viable when wage gains exceed migration cost.
5. Information: Expenditure on labour market data & educational options.
Human Capital vs Human Development
• Human Capital: Views education & health instrumentally as tools to raise labour productivity.
• Human Development: Views education & health as fundamental human rights and ends in themselves.
Policy Mandate: Kothari Commission (1966) & Tapas Majumdar Committee (1999) recommended spending 6% of GDP on education. Current spending hovers around 3-4%.
2. Rural Credit & Banking Institutions
Financial Inclusion
Institutional vs Non-Institutional Credit
Non-Institutional: Moneylenders, rich landlords, traders. Usurious interest rates, manipulated accounts, debt traps.
Institutional (Multi-Agency): Commercial banks (post-1969 nationalisation), Cooperatives, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs 1975).
Apex Agency: NABARD (1982)
Established on 12 July 1982. Serves as the apex refinancing institution for all rural credit institutions (Cooperatives, RRBs, Commercial Banks).
Microfinance & Self-Help Groups (SHGs)
Mechanism: Small community groups pooling modest savings to provide collateral-free micro-credit to members.
Impact: Drives rural women empowerment, financial autonomy, and poverty reduction (e.g. Kudumbashree).
3. Marketing Reforms & Diversification
Beyond Cereal Monoculture
Agricultural Marketing Channels
• Regulated Markets (APMCs): Standardized weights, transparent auctions.
• Cooperative Marketing: Amul dairy cooperative model.
• Direct Farmer Markets: Apni Mandi (Punjab/Haryana), Rythu Bazars (AP/Telangana), Uzhavar Sandhais (TN).
Agricultural Diversification Pillars
Animal Husbandry: Operation Flood / White Revolution (Dr. Kurien) made India world #1 in milk.
Fisheries (Blue Revolution): Inland and marine aquaculture expansion.
Horticulture (Golden Revolution 1991-2003): Fruit, vegetable & honey boom.
Organic Farming (Eco-Agriculture)
Eliminates synthetic fertilizers & pesticides. Restores soil health and commands export premiums. Sikkim became India's first 100% organic state.
CUET Core Anchor: Human Capital creates wealth · NABARD (1982) anchors rural credit · Diversification (Operation Flood & Golden Revolution) insulates farmers.
