Four Historic Phases of Population Growth in India (1901–2011+)

Phase I 1901 – 1921

Period of Stagnant / Dormant Growth

  • Birth Rate & Death Rate both exceptionally high (~48 & 47 per 1,000).
  • Widespread famines, epidemics (influenza, plague, cholera), and food shortages.
  • Negative growth recorded in 1911–1921 decade (-0.31%).
Census Benchmark: 1921 is the "Year of the Great Divide".
Phase II 1921 – 1951

Period of Steady Population Growth

  • Improvements in sanitation, medical hygiene, and public health lowered mortality.
  • Better transport distribution mitigated catastrophic famine mortality.
  • Crude birth rate remained stubbornly high, widening the natural increase gap.
Growth Trend: Modest, steady annual growth rate of 1.0% to 1.3%.
Phase III 1951 – 1981

Period of Population Explosion

  • Developmental planning via Five-Year Plans raised living standards.
  • Massive mortality drop due to antibiotics, vaccines, and disease eradication.
  • High fertility sustained; supplemented by international immigration (Tibet, BD, Pak).
Peak Rate: Decadal growth soared above 24.8% (1961–71).
Phase IV 1981 – 2011+

High Growth with Definite Slowing Down

  • Crude birth rates began a downward trajectory; female literacy surged.
  • Postponement of marriage age and adoption of small family norms.
  • Adolescent population (10–19 yrs) peaked at 20.9% (2011), driving demographic dividend.
Current Decadal: Growth decelerated to 17.64% (2001–2011).

Spatial Density Typologies & Pressure Indices

High Density Zones

> 500 persons/km²

Fertile alluvial river plains, perennial canal irrigation, flat terrain, and historic urban-industrial corridors.

Leading States: Bihar (1,106), West Bengal (1,028), Uttar Pradesh (829), Kerala (860), Delhi UT (11,320).

Moderate Density Zones

251 – 500 persons/km²

Peninsular plateau tracts, rainfed agricultural areas, moderate mineral-industrial development.

States: Assam (398), Gujarat (308), Andhra Pradesh (308), Karnataka (319), Odisha (270), Jharkhand (414).

Low Density Zones

< 250 persons/km²

Rugged montane relief, extreme arid deserts, dense forest canopies, and harsh climate.

States: Arunachal Pradesh (17 — lowest in India), Mizoram (52), Sikkim (86), Nagaland (119), Ladakh.

Core Land-Man Relationship Indices
Arithmetic Density: Total Pop / Total Geographical Area
Physiological Density: Total Population / Net Cultivated Area
Agricultural Density: Total Agricultural Pop / Net Sown Area
