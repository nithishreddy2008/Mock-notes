Section B2 Applied Math
1. Method of Least Squares & The Mid-Period Origin Shortcut
Linear Trend Equation & Origin-Shift Parameters
Y_c = a + b·X quad ⟺ quad a = ΣY / n, quad b = ΣXY / ΣX²
When the time origin is shifted to the chronological midpoint such that ΣX = 0, the normal equations decouple instantly, yielding parameters a (mean level) and b (annual growth slope).
Time Horizon (n) Chronological Origin Placement Transformed X-Values Annual Step (h)
Odd Number of Years (e.g. n = 5) Directly at central year (Year 3) -2, -1, 0, +1, +2 1 year per unit step (ΣX = 0)
Even Number of Years (e.g. n = 6) Midway between Years 3 & 4 -5, -3, -1, +1, +3, +5 0.5 year per unit; 2-year scale (ΣX = 0)
Interpreting Slope (b): The parameter $b$ represents the constant annual rate of change of the series. If $b > 0$, the trend shows secular growth; if $b < 0$, it shows secular decline. For future forecasting, substitute the projected $X$-value into $Y_c = a + b X$.
CUET Trap Alert (Even-Year Scale Trap): For even $n$ with transformed coordinates $X = \pm 1, \pm 3, \dots$, one unit step of $X$ represents ONLY 0.5 years. Therefore, the annual growth rate is $2b$, NOT $b$! When forecasting future years, calculate the $X$-coordinate carefully!
2. Moving Averages Centering & Exponential Smoothing Mechanics
Exponential Smoothing Recursive Formula
F_{t+1} = α · Y_t + (1 - α) · F_t = F_t + α · (Y_t - F_t)
Where F_{t+1} is next period forecast, Y_t is current actual observation, F_t is current forecast, and α ∈ (0, 1] is the smoothing constant.
Technique Operational Algorithm Centering & Horizon Property
Odd Moving Average (e.g. 3-yr, 5-yr) Compute mean of m periods: (Y_{t-1} + Y_t + Y_{t+1}) / 3 Naturally centered against central period $t$; loses $(m-1)/2$ endpoints
Even Moving Average (e.g. 4-quarter) Compute 4-period totals, then take 2-period moving average of totals Mandatory Centering Step: Divides by $4 \times 2 = 8$ to center against exact time point
Exponential Smoothing Weighted geometric decaying average of all historical past data No loss of endpoint data; higher α reacts faster to recent demand shifts
Additive vs Multiplicative Models: - Additive: $Y_t = T_t + S_t + C_t + I_t$ (Components are absolute physical values; $\sum S_t = 0$ over annual cycle). - Multiplicative: $Y_t = T_t \times S_t \times C_t \times I_t$ (Components are relative ratios/percentages; $\sum S_t = 400\%$ for quarterly data).
CUET Trap Alert (Moving Average Centering Fallacy): A 4-yearly moving average cannot be plotted directly against any single year because the midpoint falls between Years 2 and 3. To center it, you MUST compute the 2-point moving average of the 4-year averages!
