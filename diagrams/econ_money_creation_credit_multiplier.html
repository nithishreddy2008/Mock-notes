COMMERCIAL BANKING & CREDIT CREATION ENGINE

FIG 1: DEPOSIT MULTIPLICATION & LRR MECHANICS
CORE PRINCIPLE The Fractional Reserve Engine
Money Multiplier Formula
k = 1 / LRR = 1 / (CRR + SLR)
LRR = Legal Reserve Ratio. High LRR = smaller credit creation; Low LRR = greater credit creation.
Total Deposit Creation Formula
Total Deposits = Primary Deposit × (1 / LRR)
Total Credit Generated = Total Deposits − Primary Initial Deposit.

1. Primary vs Secondary Deposits

Primary Deposits: Cash deposited by the public in banks.
Derivative (Secondary) Deposits: Deposits created by banks while sanctioning loans (loans are never paid in cash; accounts are credited).

2. Two Fundamental Assumptions

• The entire commercial banking sector is treated as a single unified unit ("Banks").
• All transactions are executed via banking channels (no cash withdrawals from the loop).

LIMITATIONS Real-World Leakages & Constraints

1. Currency Drain (Cash Leakage)

When borrowers withdraw cash instead of executing payments via cheque, funds leak out of the banking system, reducing the actual multiplier below 1/LRR.

2. Excess Reserves Leakage

If commercial banks choose to hold idle reserves above statutory LRR due to economic uncertainty or credit risk, loan creation halts prematurely.

3. Availability of Eligible Borrowers

Credit expansion requires creditworthy borrowers willing to take loans at current interest rates. Lack of business demand restricts credit.

Single Bank vs Banking System

An individual bank can lend only its excess reserves (Round 1: ₹9,000). Only the entire banking system together creates ₹90,000 credit.

Inverse Relationship Rule
Money multiplier k is inversely proportional to LRR. If LRR is doubled from 10% to 20%, k falls from 10 to 5, cutting credit creation in half.
Fatal Exam Trap: Vault Cash
Cash reserves kept by commercial banks in their vaults are excluded from money supply. Only currency held by the public counts.
LRR Split: CRR vs SLR
LRR consists of two components: CRR (cash held with the Central Bank) + SLR (liquid assets: cash, gold, unencumbered G-Secs held by the bank itself).
