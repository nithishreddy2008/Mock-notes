Meaning & Staffing within HRM

Staffing = putting the right person at the right job — filling and keeping filled every position
HRM is wider: it also covers job design, welfare, labour relations, grievance handling — staffing is the core part of HRM
Importance: right people = performance · optimum use of human resources · motivation · survival & growth

Recruitment — Positive Process

Recruitment = searching for candidates and stimulating them to apply — it gathers applicants (positive)
Internal sources: transfer (same level) + promotion (higher level)
+ motivates, known person, cheap, quick → − no fresh talent, limited choice, inbreeding, politics
External sources: ads, employment exchanges, agencies, campus, web portals, labour contractors, factory gate, recommendations
+ fresh talent, wide choice, new ideas → − costly, slow, demotivates existing staff

Selection — Negative Process

Selection = choosing the most suitable and rejecting the unsuitable — each step is a hurdle
Process: screening $arrow$ tests (intelligence, aptitude, personality, trade) $arrow$ interview $arrow$ reference & background checks $arrow$ selection decision $arrow$ medical exam $arrow$ job offer $arrow$ employment contract

Staffing Process (8 Steps)

1 Estimate manpower requirements · 2 Recruitment · 3 Selection · 4 Placement & orientation
5 Training & development · 6 Performance appraisal · 7 Promotion & career planning · 8 Compensation
Starts with counting needs (workload + workforce analysis); ends with paying and growing people

On-the-Job Training Methods

Apprenticeship: trainee works under a master for a trade — long period, learns the craft
Internship: theory-trained student works in a real firm — joins knowledge with practice
Job rotation: moved job to job for all-round exposure — builds versatile managers
Induction training: the new employee's introduction to job, colleagues and rules

Off-the-Job Methods & Training vs Development

Classroom lectures / conferences: theory away from the workplace — knowledge transmission
Vestibule training: train on dummy machines in a training room — real equipment feel, zero production risk
Training = skills for the current job (technical) $arrow$ Development = overall growth for future roles (conceptual)

Traps that catch most students

Recruitment is positive (invite applicants); selection is negative (reject the unfit) — do not swap them
Transfer = same level move; promotion = higher post + pay — both internal sources
Vestibule = dummy machines off the floor; internship = student in a real firm
Selection tests come before the interview; the job offer comes after the medical exam
