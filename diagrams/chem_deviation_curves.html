Raoult's Law: Ideal Solutions & Binary Azeotropic Deviations

Vapour Pressure Curves • Intermolecular Force Balances • Enthalpy & Volume of Mixing • Azeotropes

CUET UG Chemistry · Unit 1 Solutions

1. Ideal Solutions

Zero Deviation
Raoult's Law: p_i = p_i° · x_i
ΔH_mix = 0 | ΔV_mix = 0 | ΔS_mix > 0
  • Force Parity: A–B adhesive attractions equal pure A–A and B–B cohesive attractions (f_AB = f_AA = f_BB).
  • Linear Vapour Pressure: Measured vapour pressure strictly coincides with theoretical straight lines across all concentrations.
  • No Azeotrope Formation: Ideal binary mixtures can be completely separated into pure components by fractional distillation.
  • Classic Benchmark Pairs: Benzene + Toluene, n-Hexane + n-Heptane, Bromoethane + Chloroethane.
Key NCERT Fact: Ideal behavior is obeyed only by structurally similar liquid pairs having near-identical molecular sizes and polarities.

2. Positive Deviation

P_exp > P_ideal
Thermodynamics: ΔH_mix > 0 (Endothermic)
ΔV_mix > 0 (Expansion) | p_i > p_i° · x_i
  • Weaker A–B Attractions: Solvent-solute attractive forces are weaker than pure component cohesive forces (f_AB < f_AA, f_BB).
  • Higher Escaping Tendency: Molecules break free more readily, producing vapour pressures strictly *above* Raoult's prediction.
  • Minimum Boiling Azeotrope: Forms a maximum VP peak, producing a constant-boiling mixture boiling *lower* than either pure liquid.
  • Benchmark Systems: Ethanol + Acetone, Carbon Disulphide (CS₂) + Acetone, Ethanol (95%) + Water.
Azeotrope Exam Trap: 95% Ethanol + 5% Water forms a minimum-boiling azeotrope (b.p. 351.15 K), preventing 100% absolute alcohol via distillation.

3. Negative Deviation

P_exp < P_ideal
Thermodynamics: ΔH_mix < 0 (Exothermic)
ΔV_mix < 0 (Contraction) | p_i < p_i° · x_i
  • Stronger A–B Attractions: New intermolecular attractions form between unlike molecules (f_AB > f_AA, f_BB), frequently new H-bonding.
  • Lower Escaping Tendency: Stronger mutual binding traps molecules in the liquid, depressing vapour pressure strictly *below* Raoult's line.
  • Maximum Boiling Azeotrope: Deep VP minimum produces a mixture boiling *higher* than either component.
  • Benchmark Systems: Chloroform + Acetone (H-bonding), Phenol + Aniline, Nitric Acid (68%) + Water (32%).
Azeotrope Exam Trap: 68% HNO₃ + 32% H₂O boils at 393.5 K (higher than water 373.15 K and pure HNO₃ 359 K) — classic maximum-boiling azeotrope.
