The Demographic Transition Model & Population Pyramids

Three progressive demographic epochs, vital rates divergence, and corresponding age-sex structures

CUET UG Geography · Model Architecture
Stage 1: High Fluctuating Primitive Agrarian
High Fertility & Mortality
Stable or extremely slow population growth with low life expectancy
Demographic Mechanism
High birth rates compensate for catastrophic mortality caused by epidemics, famine, and variable food supply. Agriculture is the primary occupation with large families regarded as economic assets.
Socio-Economic Environment
Illiteracy is pervasive; technology is primitive. Life expectancy is exceptionally low (below 35 years). Most of the world was in this stage two centuries ago.
Associated Age-Sex Structure Wide Base & Steep Taper
High proportion of children, very small elderly cohort due to heavy infant and adult mortality.
Benchmark Examples: Rainforest tribal communities in the Amazon/Congo basin; Bangladesh before independence; all human societies prior to the Industrial Revolution.
Stage 2: Expanding Population Early Developing
High Fertility, Falling Mortality
Population explosion driven by a widening gap between birth and death rates
Demographic Mechanism
Fertility remains stubbornly high initially due to cultural norms, while death rates plunge dramatically due to clean sanitation, antibiotics, piped water, and medical advancements.
Socio-Economic Environment
Rapid urbanization, early industrialization, and expansion of basic healthcare. Net natural increase accelerates rapidly, creating massive young cohorts.
Associated Age-Sex Structure Triangular Pyramid
Broad base reflecting large birth cohorts (high child dependency ratio) tapering smoothly toward the apex.
Benchmark Examples: Nigeria, Kenya, Peru, Mexico, Sri Lanka; developing nations experiencing accelerated demographic expansion.
Stage 3: Low Fluctuating Urban Industrial
Low Fertility & Low Mortality
Population stabilizes or grows very slowly with aging demographic composition
Demographic Mechanism
Both birth and death rates converge at low levels. Deliberate family planning, widespread contraception, and female labor force participation depress fertility below replacement level.
Socio-Economic Environment
Highly urbanized, literate, service-oriented society. High technological sophistication, high living standards, and advanced geriatric healthcare.
Associated Age-Sex Structure Bell-Shaped / Contracting
Equal birth/death cohorts (Australia, bell-shaped) or narrow base with bulging top indicating aging population (Japan, contracting).
Benchmark Examples: Japan (contracting population), Australia (constant/bell-shaped), Canada, Germany, United States, Western Europe.
Core Exam Law: The transition progresses from rural, agrarian, illiterate with high rates to urban, industrial, literate with low rates, demonstrating human control over reproduction.
