UN Principal Organs & Powers
Charter Architecture (San Francisco, 1945)
General Assembly (UNGA) 193 Members
  • Universal plenary body: 1 nation = 1 vote.
  • Approves annual UN budget and peacekeeping assessments.
  • Elects non-permanent UNSC members, ECOSOC, ICJ judges.
  • Resolutions are non-binding recommendations.
Security Council (UNSC) 15 Members
  • P5 Permanent: US, UK, France, Russia, China with absolute Veto power.
  • 10 Non-Permanent: 2-year terms, elected regionally, no immediate re-election.
  • Primary responsibility for international peace & security; decisions binding under Chapter VII.
Secretariat & Secretary-General Executive
  • Chief administrative officer appointed by UNGA upon UNSC recommendation (5-year renewable term).
  • First SG: Trygve Lie (Norway); Current SG: António Guterres (Portugal). Dag Hammarskjöld won Nobel Peace Prize (1961).
ICJ & Specialized Bodies Judicial / Social
  • ICJ (The Hague): 15 judges (9-year terms); settles legal disputes between sovereign states.
  • ECOSOC: 54 members (3-year terms); coordinates economic, social, human rights initiatives.
  • Trusteeship Council: Suspended ops in 1994 after Palau independence.
2
Economic Organs & Specialized Agencies
Bretton Woods, WTO, IAEA & Humanitarian Bodies
World Bank & IMF Bretton Woods (1944)
  • IMF: 190 members; oversees global monetary system, balance of payments; weighted voting based on quotas (US holds ~16.5% veto).
  • World Bank (IBRD/IDA): Human development loans, infrastructure financing for developing countries.
  • Headquartered in Washington D.C.; structural adjustment conditionalities.
WTO & Trade Governance Geneva (1995)
  • Successor to 1948 GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade).
  • 164 members; rule-based multilateral trade, dispute settlement mechanism.
  • Decisions nominally by consensus, but heavily shaped by US, EU, and Japan.
IAEA: Atoms for Peace Vienna (1957)
  • Founded following US President Eisenhower's "Atoms for Peace" address.
  • Monitors peaceful use of nuclear energy; inspects nuclear facilities worldwide.
  • Awarded Nobel Peace Prize in 2005 (with Director-General Mohamed ElBaradei).
Humanitarian & Civil Society Actors Advocacy
  • UNHCR & UNICEF: Refugees protection, children's emergency welfare.
  • Amnesty International & Human Rights Watch (HRW): Independent global NGOs exposing human rights violations and lobbying governments.

UN Reforms & India's Candidature
Jurisdiction, Council Expansion & Structural Debates
Two Broad Dimensions of Reform Structural vs Agenda
  • Structural Reform: Restructuring UNSC membership (expanding permanent & non-permanent seats) and reviewing the P5 veto power.
  • Jurisdictional Reform: Shifting focus toward development/humanitarian work vs peace enforcement/counter-terrorism.
Kofi Annan's 1997 Criteria Benchmarking
  • A major economic power.
  • A major military power.
  • A substantial contributor to the UN budget.
  • A large nation in terms of population.
  • A nation that respects democracy and human rights.
  • Capable of making the Council more representative of world diversity.
60th Anniversary Reforms (2005) Institutional
  • Creation of the Peacebuilding Commission.
  • Establishment of the Human Rights Council (operational 2006, replacing Commission on Human Rights).
  • Acceptance of Responsibility to Protect (R2P) for citizens from genocide/war crimes.
  • Agreement to wind up Trusteeship Council and create Democracy Fund.
India's Claim to Permanent Seat G4 Coalition
  • Largest democracy, 2nd most populous, rising economic powerhouse.
  • Foremost historical contributor to UN Peacekeeping operations (UNPKF).
  • Obstacles: Chinese opposition, nuclear status outside NPT, Pakistani resistance ("Uniting for Consensus" / Coffee Club), and reluctance of P5 to dilute veto.
