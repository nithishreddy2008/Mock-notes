Mahatma Gandhi & The Nationalist Era

1915 – 1948 · SATYAGRAHA TO SWARAJ

1. South Africa & Early Satyagrahas

1915–1918
  • Return to India (Jan 1915): G.K. Gokhale advised one year travel to understand the real India.
  • BHU Opening (Feb 1916): Admonished elite; declared salvation lies with peasants and workers.
  • Champaran (1917): Defied tinkathia indigo sharecropping system; first Civil Disobedience test.
  • Ahmedabad & Kheda (1918): Cotton mill wage dispute (first hunger strike) & revenue remission satyagraha.

2. Rowlatt, Khilafat & Non-Cooperation

1919–1922
  • Rowlatt Act (1919): Detention without trial; Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919) by Dyer.
  • Khilafat Alliance: Joined Ali Brothers to unite Hindus and Muslims on an unprecedented scale.
  • Non-Cooperation Movement (1920): Boycott of courts, schools, foreign cloth, and titles.
  • Chauri Chaura (Feb 1922): Violence ended movement; Gandhi sentenced to 6 years by Broomfield.

3. Constructive Programme & Simon Boycott

1923–1929
  • Swarajists vs No-Changers: CR Das and Motilal Nehru entered councils; Gandhi focused on villages.
  • Constructive Program: Hand-spinning (Charkha), removal of untouchability, national schools.
  • Simon Commission (1928): All-white commission boycotted; Lala Lajpat Rai assaulted and died.
  • Lahore Session (Dec 1929): Nehru proclaimed Purna Swaraj; 26 Jan 1930 celebrated as Independence Day.

4. Salt March, CDM & Round Table

1930–1934
  • Dandi Salt March: 12 March – 6 April 1930; 240 miles from Sabarmati; universal defiance of salt tax.
  • Mass Resistance: Peshawar Red Shirts (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan); Dharasana raid by Sarojini Naidu.
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931): Truce signed; Gandhi attended Second RTC in London as sole Congress voice.
  • Poona Pact (1932): Fast against Communal Award; agreed with Ambedkar on joint electorates with reserved seats.

5. WWII, Cripps & Quit India

1939–1945
  • Resignation of Ministries (1939): Protest against dragging India into war without legislative consent.
  • Individual Satyagraha (1940): Vinoba Bhave first satyagrahi, followed by Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • Cripps Mission (1942): Rejected as a "post-dated cheque on a crashing bank."
  • Quit India Movement (Aug 1942): "Do or Die" call; spontaneous uprisings; parallel governments in Ballia, Tamluk, Satara.

6. Partition, Martyrdom & Historiography

1946–1948
  • Cabinet Mission (1946): Grouping plan rejected; Direct Action Day (16 Aug 1946) sparked massacres.
  • One-Man Boundary Force: Gandhi walked barefoot through Noakhali, Bihar, and Calcutta to halt riots.
  • Martyrdom (30 Jan 1948): Assassinated by Nathuram Godse during evening prayer at Birla House.
  • Historical Sources: Autobiographies, police confidential reports, private letters, and newspaper archives.
