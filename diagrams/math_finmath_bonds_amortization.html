Section B2 Applied Math
1. Bond Pricing, Yield to Maturity (YTM) & Pricing Relationships
Intrinsic Bond Valuation Formula (PV Model)
V₀ = C · [ (1 - (1 + r)⁻ⁿ) / r ] + F / (1 + r)ⁿ
Where C = Periodic coupon payment (Coupon Rate × F), F = Face/Par value, r = Market yield (YTM per period), and n = Total coupon periods to maturity.
Relationship Bond Market Price (V₀) Valuation Status Capital Gain / Loss
Coupon Rate = YTM V₀ = Face Value (F) At Par Zero capital gain; return equals coupon
Coupon Rate > YTM V₀ > Face Value (F) At Premium Capital loss over life amortized by high coupons
Coupon Rate < YTM V₀ < Face Value (F) At Discount Capital appreciation offsets lower coupon payouts
Inverse Price-Yield Relationship: Bond prices vary inversely with market interest rates. When market yield (YTM) rises, bond price falls ($dV/dr < 0$). For zero-coupon bonds, $C = 0 \implies V_0 = F / (1 + r)^n$.
CUET Trap Alert (Semi-Annual Coupon Frequency): If coupons are paid semi-annually: (1) Periodic coupon $C = (c \cdot F) / 2$, (2) Periodic yield $r = \text{YTM} / 2$, and (3) Number of periods doubles to $2n$. Never forget to halve the rate and double the tenure!
2. Sinking Fund Accumulation vs Loan Amortization Schedule
Sinking Fund vs EMI Mathematical Equations
Sinking Fund Deposit: R = A · [ i / ((1 + i)ⁿ - 1) ]
EMI (Reducing Balance): E = P · [ i(1 + i)ⁿ / ((1 + i)ⁿ - 1) ]
Sinking Fund: Generates future corpus A by end-of-period deposits. EMI: Extinguishes loan principal P today through uniform installments containing both interest and principal repayment.
Dimension Sinking Fund Schedule Loan Amortization (EMI)
Fundamental Objective Accumulating future corpus to retire debt/replace asset Systematic liquidation of an existing debt liability
Cash Flow Timing Annuity ordinary (deposits grow via compound interest) Equal periodic payments covering interest + principal
Interest Component Interest earned increases progressively each period Interest paid decreases progressively as principal drops
Principal Component Uniform periodic deposit ($R$) added each period Principal repayment increases each period ($P_t > P_{t-1}$)
Amortization Breakdown Property: In any installment $t$, $\text{EMI} = \text{Interest}_t + \text{Principal Repaid}_t$. Since outstanding balance decreases each month, the interest slice shrinks while the equity/principal slice expands exponentially.
CUET Trap Alert (Flat Rate vs Reducing Balance): Flat interest computes interest on the original loan $P$ for the entire term: $\text{EMI}_{\text{flat}} = (P + P \cdot r \cdot t) / n$. Reducing balance computes interest strictly on the remaining principal balance, resulting in a substantially lower effective cost.
