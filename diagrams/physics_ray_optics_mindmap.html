Physics Master Mindmap • Unit 6 (Ray Optics)
Spherical Mirrors & Reflection Mirror Formula
1/v + 1/u = 1/f = 2/R  |  m = -v/u = f/(f - u)
  • Cartesian Convention: Pole is origin; distances opposite to incident ray are negative ($u < 0$).
  • Concave Mirror: $f < 0$; forms real inverted images; virtual & enlarged only if object $u < |f|$.
  • Convex Mirror: $f > 0$; always forms virtual, erect, diminished image between $P$ and $F$ ($|v| < f$).
Refraction & Apparent Depth Snell's Law
n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r  |  d' = d/μ  |  Δt = d(1 - 1/μ)
  • Frequency Invariant: Frequency $\nu$ unchanged across interfaces; $v = c/\mu$ and $\lambda' = \lambda/\mu$.
  • Apparent Depth: Object in denser medium appears raised; normal shift $\Delta t$ depends strictly on slab thickness $d$.
  • Lateral Displacement: Parallel glass slab displaces ray sideways without changing propagation angle.
Total Internal Reflection (TIR) Critical Angle
sin θ_c = 1/μ  |  NA = √(μ₁² - μ₂²)  |  i > θ_c
  • Dual Conditions: Ray must travel denser $\to$ rarer, and incidence angle $i > \theta_c$.
  • Optical Fibres: High-index core ($\mu_1$) with low-index cladding ($\mu_2$); 100% reflectance without loss.
  • Prism Reflection: 45°-90°-45° crown glass prisms bend rays by 90° or invert by 180° through TIR ($i = 45^\circ > 42^\circ$).

Thin Lenses & Lensmaker Curvature
1/f = (μ - 1)(1/R₁ - 1/R₂)  |  P = P₁ + P₂  |  m = v/u
  • Medium Immersion: $f_{\text{med}} = f_{\text{air}} \frac{\mu_g - 1}{\mu_g/\mu_m - 1}$; in medium with $\mu_m > \mu_g$, convex lens diverges!
  • Lens Cutting: Along principal axis $\to f' = f$ (intensity halved); perpendicular to axis $\to f' = 2f$ (power halved).
  • Power in Contact: Thin lenses in contact add algebraically: $P = P_1 + P_2 \implies 1/F = 1/f_1 + 1/f_2$.
Prism Deviation & Dispersion Minimum Deviation
μ = sin((A + δ_m)/2) / sin(A/2)  |  δ ≈ (μ - 1)A
  • Symmetry at $\delta_m$: Angle of incidence equals emergence ($i = e$); ray travels parallel to base.
  • Dispersion (Cauchy): Shorter wavelengths refract more ($\mu_{\text{violet}} > \mu_{\text{red}}$); violet deviates most.
  • Dispersive Power: $\omega = (\mu_v - \mu_r)/(\mu_y - 1)$ depends only on material, not prism geometry.
Optical Instruments Magnification
Micro: M ≈ -(L/f_o)(D/f_e)  |  Tele: M = -f_o/f_e, L = f_o + f_e
  • Microscope Design: Small $f_o$ and tiny aperture maximize $m_o$; eyepiece magnifies intermediate image.
  • Telescope Design: Large $f_o$ and wide aperture maximize light grasp and angular resolution ($f_o \gg f_e$).
  • Reflecting Advantage: Eliminates chromatic aberration; parabolic primary mirror eliminates spherical aberration.
