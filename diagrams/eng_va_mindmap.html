VERBAL ABILITY
CUET UG ENGLISH · LANGUAGE TEST

Rearranging the Parts

Opener rule: the first sentence introduces — it opens with a noun, not a dangling pronoun
Link pairs: pronouns (he, it, this) point backwards; connectors (but, then, finally) give the order
Finish with flow: read your full sequence once before marking

Match the Following

Anchor pairs first: lock the matches you are sure of, strike them out
Narrow the rest: match leftovers from what remains — fewer choices, fewer mistakes
Verify all four: one wrong pair sinks the entire answer

Choosing the Correct Word

Three checks: sense — grammar — usual word partners (collocation)
Confusables: affect (verb, to change) vs effect (noun, the result); advice (noun) vs advise (verb)
Both blanks in sync: for two-blank sentences, the pair must work TOGETHER

Synonyms & Antonyms

Context first: the sentence decides which meaning of the word is live
Predict, then match: say your own word before looking at the options
Antonym alert: read the question twice — the synonym is sitting in the options as bait

Word Power That Sticks

Roots multiply words: bene- (good), mal- (bad), chron- (time), spect- (look), port- (carry)
Word diary: one new word a day — meaning, one sentence of your own, one partner word
Charge marking: tag words positive or negative — it settles synonym AND antonym questions

Scoring Habits

Evidence over ear: run the sense-grammar-partner ritual every time
Skip-stamp and return: a stuck word costs 30 seconds, not 3 minutes
Marking maths: +5 right, −1 wrong — attempt with checks, not with hope

Traps that catch most students

Picking a synonym for an antonym question — the #1 verbal-ability slip
Opening a jumble with a pronoun sentence ("He opened the door...")
Trusting sound-alikes — principal/principle, stationary/stationery, lose/loose
Matching only the first two pairs and marking in a hurry
