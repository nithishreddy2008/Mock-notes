1. Common Pathogens & Diseases
Infectious
• Typhoid: Salmonella typhi, food/water, Widal test, intestinal perforation.
• Pneumonia: Streptococcus pneumoniae / H. influenzae, alveoli fluid, blue lips.
• Common Cold: Rhino viruses, infects nose/airway, NOT lungs.
• Amoebiasis: Entamoeba histolytica, housefly vector, stools with blood/mucus.
• Malaria: Plasmodium, female Anopheles, RBC rupture releases haemozoin.
• Filariasis: Wuchereria bancrofti, female Culex, elephantiasis of lower limbs.
• Ringworm: Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton; dry scaly lesions.
2. Immune System Architecture
Defense Lines
• Innate Barriers: Physical (skin, mucus), Physiological (stomach HCl, saliva, tears), Cellular (PMNL, macrophages, NK cells), Cytokine (Interferons protect uninfected cells).
• Humoral (AMI): B-cells produce antibodies (H2L2). IgG (crosses placenta), IgA (colostrum), IgM (first response), IgE (allergies via mast cell histamine).
• Cell-Mediated (CMI): T-cells; exclusively mediates graft rejection.
• Lymphoid Organs: Primary (Bone marrow, Thymus) vs Secondary (Spleen, Lymph nodes, Tonsils, Peyer's patches). MALT = 50% of lymphoid tissue.
3. AIDS & Retrovirus Life Cycle
Immunodeficiency
• Causative Agent: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (Retrovirus, ssRNA with Reverse Transcriptase).
• Replication: Viral RNA reverse transcribed to cDNA → integrates into host DNA → Macrophages act as HIV factory.
• Target: Attacks & destroys Helper T-cells (TH) → count plunges below 200/μL.
• Opportunistic Infections: Mycobacterium, Toxoplasma, fungi, viruses.
• Diagnosis: Screening by ELISA; confirmatory by Western Blot. NACO programs.
4. Cancer (Oncology & Malignancy)
Cell Proliferation
• Hallmark: Loss of contact inhibition; uncontrolled clonal proliferation forming tumors.
• Benign vs Malignant: Benign remain confined. Malignant invade surrounding tissues; enter bloodstream and seed distant organs — Metastasis (most feared property).
• Carcinogens: Ionizing rays (X-rays, gamma), non-ionizing (UV); chemical (tobacco smoke); oncogenic viruses & cellular proto-oncogenes.
• Detection & Therapy: Biopsy, CT, MRI, antibodies. Treated via surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and biological response modifiers (alpha-interferon).
5. Drugs & Substance Abuse
Neuropharmacology
• Opioids: Bind opioid receptors (CNS & GI tract). Morphine (sedative/analgesic from poppy Papaver somniferum); Heroin (smack, diacetylmorphine, depressant).
• Cannabinoids: Receptors in brain; from Cannabis sativa (bhang, ganja, charas); affect cardiovascular system.
• Coca Alkaloid: Cocaine (coke/crack) from Erythroxylum coca; interferes with dopamine transport; CNS stimulant, euphoria; hallucinations in overdose.
• Hallucinogens: Atropa belladonna, Datura, LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide).
6. Autoimmunity, Allergy & Vaccines
Clinical Immunology
• Active vs Passive: Active (host produces antibodies, slow, long-lasting); Passive (ready-made antibodies injected, e.g. Tetanus antitoxin, snake antivenom, colostrum IgA).
• Recombinant Vaccine: Hepatitis B vaccine produced from transgenic yeast.
• Allergies: Exaggerated response to allergens (pollen, mites); mediated by IgE; mast cells release histamine and serotonin; treated with antihistamines.
• Auto-Immunity: Immune system attacks self-antigens (e.g., Rheumatoid arthritis).
CUET UG Top Traps
Graft Rejection: Exclusively mediated by Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI / T-cells). Tissue matching (HLA) and lifelong Cyclosporin A immunosuppression required.
HIV Dynamics: Macrophages act as the continuous "HIV factory"; Helper T-cells (TH) are progressively destroyed until immune collapse occurs.
Drug Chemistry: Heroin is chemically diacetylmorphine (extracted by acetylation of morphine). Morphine is prescribed as a potent post-operative analgesic.
