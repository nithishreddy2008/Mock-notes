The Six Statutory Consumer Rights

1. Right to Safety

Protection against marketing of goods hazardous to life/health (e.g. electrical appliances must carry ISI mark).

2. Right to be Informed

Complete factual disclosure on packaging regarding ingredients, date of mfg, expiry, MRP, batch number, and directions.

3. Right to Choose

Assurance of access to a variety of goods and services at competitive prices without monopolistic or coercive tying.

4. Right to be Heard

Consumer interests receive due consideration in appropriate dispute forums; companies must maintain consumer care cells.

5. Right to Seek Redressal

Right to fair legal remedies against unfair trade practices, including repair, product replacement, or cash compensation.

6. Right to Consumer Education

Acquiring knowledge and awareness to remain a vigilant consumer throughout life (e.g. Jago Grahak Jago campaign).
Responsibilities & Quality Marks
Key Consumer Responsibilities (NCERT)
  • Be aware of product quality, prices, and terms of purchase.
  • Buy only standardized goods with quality certification marks.
  • Learn about the risks associated with manufactured products.
  • Insist on a Cash Memo: Indispensable legal proof of purchase.
  • File legitimate complaints even for small monetary losses.
  • Form consumer organizations to educate the community.

CPA 2019 Landmark Architecture
Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)
Regulatory body with powers to investigate violations, order product recalls, mandate price refunds, and impose fines up to ₹10 Lakhs (₹50 Lakhs for repeat) on misleading advertisements and celebrity endorsers.
Product Liability Provisions
Manufacturers, service providers, and product sellers are held strictly liable to compensate consumers for physical injury, death, or property damage caused by defective products or deficient services.
E-Commerce Inclusion & Mediation
Direct sellers and digital marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart) are held liable for unfair trade practices. Mediation Cells attached to all commissions enable speedy out-of-court settlements.
PECUNIARY JURISDICTIONS (CPA 2019): District Commission: Up to ₹1 Crore; State Commission: ₹1 Crore to ₹10 Crores; National Commission: Exceeds ₹10 Crores. Appeal from National Commission lies to Supreme Court within 30 days.
