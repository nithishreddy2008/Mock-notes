CUET UG History · Unit 8
Stage 01
Surveying & Land Classification
  • Jarib Measurement: Standardized bamboo rods linked with iron rings measured cultivated bighas.
  • Four Soil Categories:
    • Polaj: Annually cultivated.
    • Parauti: Fallow for 1–2 years.
    • Chachar: Fallow for 3–4 years.
    • Banjar: Uncultivated 5+ years.
Key Official
Qanungo & Village Patwari
Stage 02
Dahsala Yield Averaging
  • Todar Mal's Bandobast: Introduced in 1580 (24th regnal year of Akbar).
  • 10-Year Rolling Average: Average produce of crops calculated over 1570–1580.
  • Crop Yield Schedules: Differentiated good, middling, and bad yields to calculate average state share (one-third).
Metric
Ain-i-Dahsala Schedules
Stage 03
Revenue Assessment: Jama
  • Cash Conversion: Commuted grain shares into fixed cash rates (dastur) per bigha.
  • Jama Concept: The theoretical assessed revenue expected from a given territory.
  • Jins-i-Kamil: State actively encouraged high-value cash crops (cotton, sugarcane, oilseeds).
Revenue Valuation
Jama in Dams & Rupees
Stage 04
Collection & Realization: Hasil
  • Hasil Concept: The actual revenue realized and paid into imperial custody.
  • Amil-guzar Duty: Encouraged cash payment, though crop-sharing (kankut / batai) remained permitted.
  • Fotadar's Receipt: Treasurers issued official receipts to prevent double taxation.
Core Official
Amil-guzar & Fotadar
Stage 05
Silver Monetization & Treasury
  • Silver Influx: Favorable foreign trade brought massive streams of American silver bullion.
  • Imperial Mints: Melted imported bullion into high-purity silver rupiya coins.
  • Monetary Stability: Facilitated widespread commercial transactions and tax monetization across rural India.

Jama vs Hasil
Jama = estimated assessed tax; Hasil = actual collected tax revenue.
