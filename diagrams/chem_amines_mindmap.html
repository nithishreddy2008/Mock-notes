AMINES & DIAZONIUM SALTS ROADMAP

Holistic Conceptual Blueprint: Amine Preparations, Basicity Rules, Diagnostic Tests, and Diazonium Conversions

UNIT IX · NCERT / CUET CORE
ORGANIC NITROGEN COMPOUNDS
(AMINES & DIAZONIUM SALTS)
Pyramidal Nitrogen · Lewis Basicity · Diazonium Master Pivot
1. Reductive Preparations of Amines
Nitro / Nitriles / Amides
Reduction of Nitro Compounds: Ar-NO₂ + Fe/HCl or Sn/HCl → Ar-NH₂. Fe/HCl is industrially preferred because FeCl₂ hydrolyses to regenerate HCl, needing only catalytic acid!
Reduction of Nitriles (Mendius): R-CN + H₂/Ni or LiAlH₄ → R-CH₂-NH₂ (Chain length preserved; adds 1 carbon relative to starting halide).
Reduction of Amides: R-CONH₂ + LiAlH₄ / ether → R-CH₂-NH₂ (Same number of carbons).
2. Gabriel Phthalimide & Hoffmann Bromamide
Pure 1° Amines & Degradation
Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis: Phthalimide + ethanolic KOH → Potassium phthalimide + R-X → N-alkylphthalimide → (aq. NaOH) pure 1° aliphatic amine + sodium phthalate.
The Gabriel Limitation: Aromatic 1° amines (Aniline) CANNOT be made this way because aryl halides cannot undergo nucleophilic substitution (SN2) with phthalimide anion!
Hoffmann Bromamide Degradation: R-CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4 NaOH → R-NH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2 NaBr + 2 H₂O. Yields 1° amine with ONE LESS CARBON via isocyanate (R-N=C=O) intermediate!
3. Basicity Laws: Gas vs Aqueous Phase
Inductive · Steric · Hydration
Gas Phase Order: 3° Amine > 2° Amine > 1° Amine > NH₃ (governed strictly by +I inductive effect of alkyl groups).
Aqueous Phase (Methyl series): (CH₃)₂NH (2°) > CH₃NH₂ (1°) > (CH₃)₃N (3°) > NH₃ (213 Rule).
Aqueous Phase (Ethyl series): (C₂H₅)₂NH (2°) > (C₂H₅)₃N (3°) > C₂H₅NH₂ (1°) > NH₃ (231 Rule).
Aniline Suppression: Lone pair delocalises into ring (+R effect, 5 resonance structures). Aniline (pKb = 9.38) is far weaker than aliphatic amines!
4. Diagnostic Chemical Tests
Hinsberg & Carbylamine
Carbylamine Reaction: 1° amine (aliphatic/aromatic) + CHCl₃ + 3 KOH, Δ → R-NC (intolerably foul-smelling isocyanide). 2° and 3° amines fail!
Hinsberg Test (C₆H₅SO₂Cl): 1° amine gives N-alkyl sulphonamide (soluble in KOH); 2° amine gives N,N-dialkyl sulphonamide (insoluble in KOH); 3° amine does not react!
Nitrous Acid (HNO₂): 1° aliphatic gives brisk effervescence of N₂↑ + alcohol; Aniline at 273–278 K gives stable diazonium salt; 2° amine gives yellow oily N-nitrosamine.
5. Electrophilic Reactions of Aniline
Super-Activation & Protection
Bromination: Aniline + Br₂/H₂O → 2,4,6-tribromoaniline (White ppt) instantaneously! Nitrogen lone pair over-activates ring to trisubstitution.
Nitration: Conc. HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ yields 51% para, 47% meta, 2% ortho! In strongly acidic medium, aniline is protonated to anilinium ion (-NH₃⁺), which is meta-directing!
Amino Group Protection: Acetylation with (CH₃CO)₂O / pyridine gives acetanilide (-NHCOCH₃), reducing lone pair donation; hydrolysed back to -NH₂ after reaction.
6. Diazonium Synthetic Conversions
Sandmeyer / Schiemann / Dyes
Diazotisation: C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2 HCl (273–278 K) → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻. Temperature > 278 K gives phenol!
Replacements: Cu₂Cl₂/Cu₂Br₂/CuCN (Sandmeyer); HBF₄ + Δ (Balz-Schiemann Ar-F); KI warm (Ar-I); H₃PO₂ or ethanol (Deamination to Benzene); H₂O warm (Phenol).
Azo Coupling: + Phenol (pH 9–10) → p-hydroxyazobenzene (Orange dye); + Aniline (pH 4–5) → p-aminoazobenzene (Yellow dye).
MASTER VERDICT Gabriel makes pure 1° aliphatic amines; Hoffmann bromamide sheds 1 carbon; 2° amine is most basic in water; Hinsberg separates 1°/2°/3°; Diazonium bridges ring transformations.
