Dissolution of Partnership Firm: Modes, Framework & Settlement Priority

NTA CUET(UG) Accountancy 301 · Unit III Architecture · Sections 39, 40–44, 48 & 49 Defaults

Legal Framework
Dissolution: Partnership vs Firm
Basis Dissolution of Partnership Dissolution of Firm
Business Firm continues; only internal relations change. Business comes to a complete end; operations cease.
Books Books stay open; revaluation entries passed. Books are completely closed via Realisation A/c.
Court Role Voluntary only; court never orders reconstitution. Court can order dissolution under Section 44.
Assets & Liab Assets revalued & liabilities reassessed. Assets sold off & outside liabilities paid off.
Scope Does NOT require dissolution of the firm. Necessarily involves dissolution of partnership!
Key CUET Principle: Dissolution of the firm terminates all legal and economic ties among all partners. Reconstitution merely rewrites the agreement while the firm survives.
Modes of Dissolution (Sec 40–44)

1. By Agreement (Section 40)

With unanimous consent of all partners or in accordance with contract between them.

2. Compulsory Dissolution (Section 41)

When all partners (or all except one) become insolvent, or firm's business becomes unlawful.

3. On Contingencies (Section 42)

Subject to contract: expiry of term, completion of venture, death or insolvency of a partner.

4. By Notice in Will (Section 43)

In partnership at will: any partner giving written notice of dissolution to all other partners.

5. Dissolution by Court (Section 44)

Insanity, permanent incapacity, misconduct, persistent breach of contract, continuous loss, or just & equitable.

Settlement Priority (Sec 48 & 49)
STEP 1

Outside Debts (Third Parties)

Creditors, Bills Payable, Bank Overdraft, and Partner's Wife Loan paid first.

STEP 2

Partner's Loan to Firm

Advances by partners paid pro-rata if assets are insufficient; prior to capital.

STEP 3

Partner Capital Balances

Return of capital to partners rateably according to their adjusted credit balances.

STEP 4

Surplus Distribution (PSR)

Remaining cash distributed strictly in profit-sharing ratio (never capital ratio!).

Section 49 Rule: Firm property pays Firm debts first (surplus to private debts). Private property pays Private debts first (surplus to firm debts).
