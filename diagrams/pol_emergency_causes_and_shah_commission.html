Economic Headwinds & Agitations

Economic Crisis (1972–1975) Fiscal Shock
  • Post-War Burden: Absorbing 10 million Bangladesh refugees and defense outlays depleted forex and capital budgets.
  • US Aid Cut & Oil Shock: US terminated PL-480 shipments; 1973 OPEC oil price quadrupled import bills.
  • Runaway Inflation: Prices rose 23% in 1973 and 30% in 1974; widespread black-marketing and food rationing.
  • Railway Strike (May 1974): George Fernandes led 20-day nationwide rail strike; crushed via mass arrests.
30% Inflation 20-Day Rail Strike
Gujarat & Bihar Movements (1974) Student Spark
  • Gujarat Navnirman (Jan 1974): Student unrest over hostel mess fees expanded against state corruption. Morarji Desai's fast forced assembly dissolution.
  • Bihar Movement (March 1974): Students invited Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) from retirement to lead anti-corruption agitation.
  • JP's Two Conditions: Agitation must remain strictly non-violent and expand nationally beyond Bihar.
  • Total Revolution (Sampoorna Kranti): JP demanded moral, social, economic, educational, and political transformation.
Navnirman (Gujarat) Total Revolution (JP)
CUET Distinction: Lohia propounded Non-Congressism (1967); Jayaprakash Narayan called for Total Revolution (1974).
2
The Judicial Clash & Midnight Emergency
Institutional Rupture & Executive Clampdown
Judicial Supersession & Allahabad Verdict The Sparks
  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Supreme Court established the Basic Structure doctrine; executive demanded a "committed" judiciary.
  • Supersession: Government bypassed 3 senior judges (Shelat, Hegde, Grover) to appoint Justice A.N. Ray as CJI.
  • Allahabad Verdict (12 June 1975): Justice Jagmohan Lal Sinha invalidated Indira's 1971 election on Raj Narain's petition (use of government machinery).
  • Conditional Stay: Vacation Judge Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer allowed Indira to remain PM but barred her from voting in Parliament.
Basic Structure Justice J.L. Sinha
The Proclamation: 25 June 1975 Article 352
  • Ramlila Maidan Rally: JP announced nationwide Satyagraha, appealing to army/police to refuse illegal unconstitutional orders.
  • Midnight Proclamation: Indira advised President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed under Article 352 citing internal disturbance.
  • Cabinet Bypassed: The Union Cabinet was not consulted beforehand; ministers were informed at 6:00 AM on 26 June.
  • Immediate Crackdown: Power to newspaper presses disconnected; opposition leaders (JP, Morarji, Advani) jailed overnight.
Internal Disturbance Cabinet Bypassed
Core Fact: Proclaimed under Article 352 on grounds of "internal disturbance"; "armed rebellion" was only substituted in 1978.
3
Excesses, 1977 Rout & Restoration
Authoritarian Subversion & Democratic Rebirth
Emergency Regimes & Excesses (1975–77) MISA & Censorship
  • MISA & Preventive Arrests: 1.1 lakh citizens detained without trial under MISA; fundamental rights suspended (ADM Jabalpur case).
  • Press Censorship: Pre-censorship enforced; Indian Express and Statesman published blank editorial columns.
  • Sanjay Gandhi & Extra-Constitutional Power: Forced mass sterilisations (vasectomies) and Turkman Gate slum demolitions.
  • 42nd Amendment (1976): "Mini-Constitution"; 6-year Lok Sabha term; inserted "Socialist, Secular, Integrity" into Preamble.
1.1 Lakh Arrests 42nd Amendment
1977 Landslide Rout & Safeguards Janata Party
  • Janata Party Wave: Cong(O), Jana Sangh, BLD, Socialists merged; swept 295 seats; Morarji Desai became first non-Congress PM.
  • North India Wipeout: Congress won zero seats in UP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana; both Indira and Sanjay Gandhi lost their seats.
  • Shah Commission (May 1977): Justice J.C. Shah unmasked administrative abuses, illegal detentions, and extra-constitutional excess.
  • 44th Amendment (1978): Made Article 20 & 21 non-suspendable; replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion"; restored 5-year term.
Janata 295 Seats 44th Amendment 1978
Analytical Core: Emergency demonstrated that Indian democracy cannot be destroyed easily, catalyzing the modern civil liberties movement (PUCL).
