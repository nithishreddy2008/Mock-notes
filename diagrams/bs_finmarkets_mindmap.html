Financial Market — Concept & Functions

Financial market = the market for the creation and exchange of financial assets (shares, debentures, bills)
Functions: mobilises savings to the most productive uses · helps price the assets · provides liquidity · lowers the cost of transactions
Two halves: money market (≤ 1 year) and capital market (> 1 year)

Stock Exchange — Functions & Trading

Functions: liquidity · continuous pricing of securities · safety of transactions · economic growth · spreads the equity cult · room for healthy speculation
Trading procedure: choose broker (registered) $arrow$ open demat + PAN $arrow$ place order $arrow$ execution on exchange $arrow$ settlement $arrow$ shares reach demat
Settlement today runs on T+1 — trades settled one day after the transaction

Depository Services & Demat

Depository = holds securities in electronic form — like a bank holds money
India has two: NSDL and CDSL
Demat account: the investor's account with the depository through a participant (DP)
Benefits: no fake/forged certificates $arrow$ instant transfer $arrow$ no bad deliveries $arrow$ safety, lower stamp duty

Money vs Capital Market

Money market: short-term funds (up to 1 year) — call money, T-bills, commercial paper, certificates of deposit, commercial bills
Capital market: long-term funds (over 1 year) — shares, debentures, bonds
One-year wall separates the two halves

Primary vs Secondary Market

Primary: securities sold first time — company receives the money (IPO, rights, private placement, e-IPO)
Secondary: existing securities traded on exchanges — money moves investor to investor; company gets nothing
Secondary gives the liquidity that makes primary issues sellable

SEBI — Watchdog of the Market

Objectives: protect investors · regulate and develop the securities market · prevent malpractices and fraud
Protective: stop fraudulent practices, check price rigging, ban misleading statements, control insider trading
Regulatory: register brokers and intermediaries, regulate takeovers, conduct inquiries and audits
Developmental: investor education, training intermediaries, promoting fair practices

Traps that catch most students

Treasury bills are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value — the discount is the return
Call money = banks lending overnight; commercial paper = firms borrowing unsecured
Secondary market never pays the company — it only moves shares between investors
SEBI is a regulator, not a participant — it polices the market, it does not trade in it
