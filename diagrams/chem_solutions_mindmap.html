Unit 1: Solutions

Liquid Solutions & Colligative Behavior

1. Expressing Concentration

Units & T-Dependence
  • Mass-Based (T-Independent): Molality (m = mol/kg solvent), Mole Fraction (x), Mass % (w/w), ppm.
  • Volume-Based (T-Dependent): Molarity (M = mol/L solution), Normality (N), Volume % (v/v). Thermal expansion lowers M.
  • Parts per Million (ppm): Expresses trace impurities (air/water pollution): ppm = (w_solute / w_total) × 10⁶.
Core Relation: m = [1000 · M] / [1000·d - M·M₂] (d = density in g/mL)

2. Solubility & Henry's Law

Gas Dissolution
  • Henry's Law Equation: Partial pressure p = K_H · x. Higher K_H implies *lower* solubility at fixed p.
  • Temperature Effect: Gas dissolution is exothermic (ΔH < 0); solubility drops with rising T (aquatic life prefers cold water).
  • Real-World Applications: High pressure in soda packaging; Helium-diluted air for scuba divers (prevents 'bends'); Anoxia at high altitude.
Exam Rule: K_H increases with T; K_H(He) > K_H(N₂) > K_H(O₂)

3. Raoult's Law & Vapour Pressures

Volatile Liquids
  • Raoult's Law Formula: p₁ = p₁° x₁ and p₂ = p₂° x₂. Total P = p₁° + (p₂° - p₁°) x₂.
  • Vapour Phase Mole Fraction (Dalton): y₁ = p₁ / P_total = (p₁° x₁) / P_total; y₂ = p₂ / P_total.
  • Henry as Special Case: Involatile solutions obey Raoult's law where solute constant equals pure vapour pressure p₁°.
Vapour Enrichment: Vapour phase is always richer in the more volatile component (Konovalov Rule).

4. Deviations & Azeotropes

Mixing Thermodynamics
  • Ideal (ΔH=0, ΔV=0): f_AB = f_AA = f_BB (Benzene + Toluene, n-Hexane + n-Heptane).
  • Positive (ΔH>0, ΔV>0): f_AB < f_AA, f_BB. Forms *Minimum Boiling Azeotrope* (95% Ethanol + 5% H₂O).
  • Negative (ΔH<0, ΔV<0): f_AB > f_AA, f_BB via H-bonding. Forms *Maximum Boiling Azeotrope* (68% HNO₃ + 32% H₂O).
Constant Boiling: Azeotropes distil without composition change; separation requires chemical drying.

5. The Four Colligative Laws

Particle-Number Laws
  • RLVP: Δp/p₁° = i · x₂ (independent of temperature).
  • Boiling Point Elevation: ΔT_b = i · K_b · m (K_b(H₂O) = 0.52 K·kg·mol⁻¹).
  • Freezing Point Depression: ΔT_f = i · K_f · m (K_f(H₂O) = 1.86 K·kg·mol⁻¹).
  • Osmotic Pressure (Π): Π = i · C · R · T (best method for polymers/proteins).
Proportionality: Colligative Property ∝ Total Number of Solute Particles (i · m).

6. Van 't Hoff Factor (i)

Abnormal Masses
  • Factor Definition: i = Normal Molar Mass / Observed Molar Mass = Actual Particles / Initial Formula Units.
  • Dissociation (i > 1): i = 1 + (n - 1)α. For NaCl (i=2), K₂SO₄ (i=3), Al₂(SO₄)₃ (i=5). M_exp < M_normal.
  • Association (i < 1): i = 1 + (1/n - 1)β. Benzoic acid in benzene dimerises: i ≈ 0.5, M_exp ≈ 244.
Freezing Depression Order: 0.1 M Al₂(SO₄)₃ > K₂SO₄ > NaCl > Glucose.
