Types of Solutions

Binary solution: just two components — the solvent (more) and the solute (less)
9 possible combos: solute and solvent can each be solid, liquid or gas
Everyday set: O2 in water (gas in liquid), salt water (solid in liquid), brass = Cu + Zn (solid in solid)
Saturation ladder: unsaturated → saturated → supersaturated (more solute than the limit)

Concentration Units

Mass %: (mass of solute × 100) / mass of solution
Mole fraction x: moles of one part ÷ total moles — has no units
Molarity M: mol solute per litre of solution — changes with temperature
Molality m: mol solute per kg of solvent — temperature-independent (no volume involved)
ppm: parts per 106 — trace amounts like lead or chlorine in water

Solubility & Henry's Law

Solid in liquid: if dissolving absorbs heat, raising temperature raises solubility
Gas in liquid: solubility falls as temperature rises — boiled water tastes flat
Henry's law: p = KH · x — higher pressure, more gas dissolves
Higher KH = less soluble — that is why KH rises with temperature
Scuba: deep = high pressure = extra N2 dissolves; rush up and it bubbles out (the bends)

Raoult's Law & Deviations

Raoult: partial pressure = mole fraction × pure vapour pressure (p1 = x1p₁°)
Ideal: obeys Raoult at all x; ΔHmix = 0, ΔVmix = 0 (benzene + toluene)
Positive deviation: A–B pulls weaker than A–A, B–B — ethanol + acetone
Negative deviation: A–B pulls stronger — chloroform + acetone, phenol + aniline
Azeotropes: constant-boiling mixtures that distil unchanged (min-boiling ethanol–water 95%)

Colligative Properties

Depend only on the NUMBER of solute particles — not their identity
Relative lowering of vapour pressure: Δp/p₁° = x₂
Elevation of boiling point: ΔTb = Kbm — salt raises water's boiling point
Depression of freezing point: ΔTf = Kfm — salt on icy roads
Osmotic pressure: π = CRT — semipermeable membrane, solvent flows in; reverse osmosis purifies water

Abnormal Mass & van't Hoff i

Why abnormal: colligative formulas assume particles never combine or split
Dissociation: NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ — particles double, i > 1, molar mass reads LOW
Association: benzoic acid pairs up in benzene — particles halve, i < 1, molar mass reads HIGH
Fix every formula: ΔTb = i·Kbm, ΔTf = i·Kfm, π = iCRT, Δp/p₁° = i·x₂
Osmotic method wins: it works at room temperature, so i corrections stay reliable

WATCH-OUTS THAT SET MCQ TRAPS

Molarity vs molality: temperature changes M (volume) but never m (mass)
KH direction: bigger KH = LOWER solubility, not higher
Osmosis direction: solvent always moves from dilute → concentrated side
Association makes i < 1 — the molar mass then comes out higher than the true one
