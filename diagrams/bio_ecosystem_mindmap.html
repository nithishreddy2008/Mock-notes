1. Structure & Productivity

Energy Input
Stratification: Vertical distribution of different species occupying different vertical strata (e.g., Trees $arrow$ Shrubs $arrow$ Herbs/Grasses in a mature forest).
Productivity Equations:
• GPP (Gross Primary Productivity): Total rate of organic matter synthesis.
• NPP (Net Primary Productivity): Available biomass for heterotrophs. $NPP = GPP - R$ (where $R$ is respiratory loss).
• Secondary Productivity: Rate of new organic matter formation by consumers.
Biosphere NPP Numbers: Entire biosphere NPP is 170 billion tons (dry weight). Oceans occupy 70% of surface but produce only 55 billion tons; land produces 115 billion tons!
Trap: Secondary productivity belongs to consumers (herbivores/carnivores), NOT plants!

2. Ecological Pyramids

Trophic Geometry
Pyramid of Numbers: Upright in grasslands; Inverted/Spindle-shaped in a tree ecosystem (1 tree hosts thousands of insects and hyper-parasites).
Pyramid of Biomass: Upright in terrestrial forests; Inverted in oceans (standing crop of phytoplankton is far smaller than zooplankton and fishes).
Pyramid of Energy: ALWAYS UPRIGHT, NEVER INVERTED in any ecosystem. Follows Lindeman's 10% law (energy is lost as metabolic heat at every transfer).
Standing Crop vs State: Standing Crop = Living biomass at a trophic level; Standing State = Amount of inorganic nutrients in soil.
Trap: Ecological pyramids ignore food webs, omnivores, and decomposers (saprophytes)!

3. Decomposition & Food Chains

Nutrient Recycling
The 5 Steps of Decomposition:
1. Fragmentation: Earthworms break detritus.
2. Leaching: Water-soluble nutrients seep into soil as unavailable salts.
3. Catabolism: Fungal/bacterial enzymes degrade detritus.
4. Humification: Forms dark, amorphous, colloidal humus (microbial resistant).
5. Mineralization: Slow release of inorganic ions ($CO_2, Ca^{2+}, PO_4^{3-}$).
GFC vs DFC Energy Flow: Grazing Food Chain (GFC) dominates in oceans; in terrestrial ecosystems, far more energy flows via Detritus Food Chain (DFC)!
Trap: Decomposition is slow if detritus is rich in lignin/chitin; fast if rich in nitrogen/sugars!

4. Succession & Nutrient Cycles

Community & Biosphere
Ecological Succession:
• Hydrarch: In water (Phytoplankton pioneer $arrow$ submerged $arrow$ floating $arrow$ reed-swamp $arrow$ marsh-meadow $arrow$ scrub $arrow$ Forest climax).
• Xerarch: On bare rock (Crustose lichens pioneer $arrow$ bryophytes $arrow$ herbs $arrow$ shrubs $arrow$ Forest). Both culminate in intermediate mesic climax!
Carbon vs Phosphorus Cycle:
• Carbon: Gaseous; 71% global carbon in oceans, 1% in atmosphere.
• Phosphorus: Sedimentary; rock weathering reservoir; zero respiratory release.
Ecosystem Services: Robert Costanza (1997) valued nature at US $33 trillion/year (Soil formation = ~50%, Recreation = 10%, Climate = 6%).
Trap: Both hydrarch and xerarch successions progress towards the exact same state: mesic!
