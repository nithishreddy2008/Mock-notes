FIGURE 1 · CORE THEMATIC COMPARISON
1. The Soviet Socialist Model (1917–1985)
A. State-Owned Economy & Gosplan
Zero Private Property: Land, heavy factories, and mineral reserves were owned exclusively by the socialist state.
State Planning: 5-Year Plans (Gosplan) fixed production quotas, domestic prices, and labor allocations from Moscow.
B. Guaranteed Social Minimum
Zero Unemployment: Right to work was constitutionally guaranteed for every citizen.
Universal Subsidies: Heavily subsidized bread, municipal housing, free healthcare, public transport, and free education for all children.
C. Authoritarian CPSU Stranglehold
Single-Party Monopoly: Communist Party of Soviet Union (CPSU) tolerated zero opposition; dissent punished by gulag exile.
Bureaucratic Nomenklatura: Entrenched party elite enjoyed special privileges while consumer markets suffered severe shortages.
D. Geopolitical Burden
Arms Race Exhaustion: Huge diversion of GDP into nuclear weaponry and subsidies to Eastern European satellite states depleted civilian reserves.
2. Gorbachev's Reform Drive (1985–1991)
A. Perestroika (Economic Restructuring)
Decentralization Attempt: Introduced limited enterprise self-financing and wage incentives to modernize production.
Caught in Transition: Dismantled central controls without establishing free markets, causing food rationing and consumer goods blackouts.
B. Glasnost (Open Public Discussion)
Censorship Lifted: Intellectuals and media exposed Stalinist atrocities, state corruption, and economic stagnation.
Nationalist Awakening: Popular transparency allowed suppressed ethnic nationalities to openly challenge Soviet imperial domination.
C. Non-Intervention in Eastern Europe
Sinatra Doctrine: Abandoned the Brezhnev Doctrine; refused military force to suppress democratic revolts in Poland, Hungary, and East Germany.
Berlin Wall (9 Nov 1989): Citizens dismantled the wall, culminating in rapid German reunification in October 1990.
D. Terminal Disintegration (1991)
August 1991 Coup: Hardline coup failed; Boris Yeltsin emerged as hero.
Belavezha Accords: Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus dissolved the USSR on 8 Dec 1991.
3. Shock Therapy & Market Transition (1990s)
A. The IMF / World Bank Blueprint
Abrupt Capitalist Transition: Instantaneous price decontrol, currency convertibility, trade opening, and total privatization without regulatory phases.
B. "Largest Garage Sale in History"
Mass Disinvestment: Over 90% of state industries auctioned off at throwaway prices.
Voucher Corruption: Citizens sold privatization vouchers to criminal oligarchs and mafia cartels due to immediate poverty.
C. Economic Collapse & Ruble Crash
Hyperinflation: Prices soared dramatically; life savings wiped out.
Ruble Devaluation: Russian currency crashed against US dollar; GDP shrank by 40% between 1989 and 1998, worse than the Great Depression.
D. Social Catastrophe & Oligarchy
Safety Nets Abolished: Elimination of state food and housing subsidies plunged millions into poverty; life expectancy plummeted.
Mafia Capture: Criminal syndicates and banking oligarchs seized control of Russian oil, gas, and metallurgical conglomerates.
Core Constitutional & Geopolitical Anchor: Russia alone inherited the USSR's UN Security Council permanent seat & nuclear arsenal; Baltic states (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania) joined NATO & EU (2004).
Strategic Bond: 2000 India-Russia Strategic Partnership (BrahMos, Kudankulam, Sakhalin-1).
