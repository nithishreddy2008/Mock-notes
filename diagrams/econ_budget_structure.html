THE BUDGET'S SKELETON & THE THREE DEFICITS THAT MEASURE IT

Budget structure — every rupee has a bucket

Two tests sort everything: does it create a liability or reduce an asset? → capital; otherwise → revenue
GOVERNMENT BUDGET (Annual Financial Statement)
REVENUE BUDGET
Revenue Receipts
Tax: GST, income tax, excise
Non-tax: interest, fees, profits, grants
Revenue Expenditure
Salaries, subsidies, interest payments
No asset born, no liability cleared
CAPITAL BUDGET
Capital Receipts
Borrowings (create liability)
Loan recovery, disinvestment (asset down)
Capital Expenditure
Roads, schools, buildings (asset born)
Loan repayment (liability cleared)
Read it once: the two questions — "did the government OWE anyone new?" (liability) and "did it GAIN something lasting?" (asset) — sort every single line of the budget into its bucket.

The three deficits — one worked budget

Given: RR ₹1,400 cr · RE ₹1,520 cr · capital receipts ₹600 cr (borrowings ₹560 + recoveries ₹25 + disinvestment ₹15) · CE ₹480 cr · interest payments ₹320 cr
Revenue deficit RD = RE − RR = 1,520 − 1,400 = ₹120 cr
Government must BORROW just to run its daily bills — consumption financed by debt
Fiscal deficit FD = Total expenditure − Total receipts (excl. borrowings) = 2,000 − 1,440 = ₹560 cr
= the borrowing requirement itself (₹560 borrowed ✓). Feeds the debt trap and crowds out private credit
Primary deficit PD = FD − interest payments = 560 − 320 = ₹240 cr
THIS year's own overspending, stripped of inherited interest — shows the fiscal stance cleanly
Read it once: FD is the WHOLE borrowing need; PD subtracts the interest inherited from PAST deficits; RD is the revenue budget's own shortfall. Three lenses, one budget.

TRAP 1

The two tests decide everything: capital receipt = creates liability OR reduces assets; capital expenditure = creates asset OR reduces liability. Revenue = neither.

TRAP 2

FD equals the borrowing requirement — by definition. If a numerical's borrowings don't match FD, recheck your subtraction.

TRAP 3

Interest payment is REVENUE expenditure (it clears no asset, and repaying principal is the capital part) — and it is subtracted AGAIN inside primary deficit.
