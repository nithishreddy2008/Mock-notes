Nucleophilic Aromatic Substitution (SNAr) in Haloarenes

Meisenheimer Stabilization
Why Chlorobenzene is Inert to Simple Substitution4 Core Factors
1. Resonance Effect: Lone pairs of Cl delocalize over the ring, giving C-Cl partial double bond character (shorter, 1.69 Å vs 1.78 Å in alkyl chloride, and harder to break).
2. Hybridisation: Ring carbon is sp² (33% s-character) → more electronegative, holds C-Cl tighter than sp³ (25% s).
3. Instability of Phenyl Cation: SN1 is ruled out as phenyl cation cannot be stabilized by resonance.
4. Electronic Repulsion: Approaching electron-rich nucleophile is repelled by ring's π-electron cloud.
Activating Effect of -NO₂ (o/p Regioselectivity)Carbanion Delocalization
Electron-withdrawing nitro group (-NO₂) activates the ring by pulling negative charge into the nitro oxygens during the Meisenheimer intermediate. Crucial rule: Negative charge appears only at ortho and para positions during resonance; hence, -NO₂ at meta positions exerts negligible activating effect on SNAr!

■ Electrophilic Substitution in Haloarenes

  • Paradoxical Behaviour: Halogen (-Cl, -Br) is deactivating overall due to strong electron-withdrawing inductive effect (-I > +R), making haloarenes react slower than benzene.
  • Orientation: However, the +R (resonance) effect delocalizes lone pairs and directs incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions (negative charge concentrates at o/p).
  • Major Product: Para-isomer is always the major product because steric hindrance is minimized and crystal packing is symmetrical (higher melting point).
  • Typical Reactions: Halogenation (Cl₂/FeCl₃), Nitration (HNO₃/H₂SO₄), Sulphonation (conc. H₂SO₄/Δ), and Friedel-Crafts Alkylation & Acylation (CH₃Cl or CH₃COCl with anhy. AlCl₃).
