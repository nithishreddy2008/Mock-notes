1. Mutualism

+ / + (Both Benefit)
Ecological Mechanism: Obligate or facultative reciprocal interaction where both co-existing species derive substantial physiological or reproductive fitness benefits. Co-evolution is mandatory.
NCERT Benchmark Examples:
• Lichens: Alga (phycobiont: food) + Fungus (mycobiont: shelter/water).
• Mycorrhizae: Fungi (_Glomus_) absorb phosphorus + plant roots supply sugar.
• Fig & Wasp: Wasp oviposits in fig fruit & pollinates; developing larvae eat seeds.
• Ophrys Orchid (Sexual Deceit): Petal mimics female bee (_Colletes_) in shape, color & odor. Male pseudocopulates and transfers pollen!
Trap: If female bee morphology changes during evolution, orchid pollination collapses unless orchid co-evolves!

2. Competition

- / - (Both Suffer)
Ecological Mechanism: Rivalry between individuals for common limiting resources (food, space, light). Fitness of both species ($r$) is significantly lower in each other's presence.
NCERT Benchmark Examples:
• Abingdon Tortoise (Galapagos): Became extinct within a decade after goats were introduced due to goats' superior browsing efficiency.
• Gause's Exclusion: Two closely competing species for identical limiting resources cannot coexist indefinitely; inferior is eliminated.
• Connell's Barnacles: Superior _Balanus_ excludes smaller _Chthamalus_.
• MacArthur's Warblers: 5 species coexist via resource partitioning.
Trap: Competition does NOT always require limiting resources or related species (e.g., flamingos & fish for zooplankton).

3. Predation

+ / - (Predator / Prey)
Ecological Mechanism: Predator captures, kills, and consumes prey. Acts as a vital conduit for energy transfer across trophic levels and keeps prey populations under control.
NCERT Benchmark Examples:
• Prickly Pear Cactus (Australia 1920s): Invasive weed covered millions of hectares; controlled by introducing natural moth predator (_Cactoblastis_).
• Pisaster Starfish (Pacific Coast): Keystone predator. Removal caused extinction of >10 invertebrate species due to unchecked competition.
• Prey Defenses: Monarch butterfly stores toxic cardiac glycoside; camouflage.
Trap: Predators in nature are "prudent"—they do NOT overexploit prey, or else both would face extinction.

4. Parasitism

+ / - (Parasite / Host)
Ecological Mechanism: Parasite derives free nutrition and shelter from living host, causing biological harm, reduced vigor, lower reproduction, or increased vulnerability to predators.
NCERT Benchmark Examples:
• Ectoparasites: Lice on humans, ticks on dogs, marine copepods on fish, _Cuscuta_ on hedge plants (lost chlorophyll and leaves).
• Endoparasites: Human liver fluke (uses 2 intermediate hosts: snail & fish).
• Brood Parasitism: Cuckoo (Koel) lays eggs in crow's nest; parasitic eggs evolved to mimic host eggs in size and color to avoid ejection.
Trap: Female mosquito is NOT a parasite—it needs human blood solely as a protein source for egg development, not shelter!

5. Commensalism

+ / 0 (Beneficiary / Neutral)
Ecological Mechanism: One species derives substantial physical, positional, or nutritional advantage, while the host species remains completely unaffected (neither harmed nor benefited).
NCERT Benchmark Examples:
• Orchid on Mango: Epiphytic orchid gains sunlight/space without taking water/sap from mango tree branch.
• Barnacles on Whale: Barnacles gain mobile plankton-rich feeding substrate.
• Cattle Egret & Grazing Cattle: Cattle stir up vegetation, flushing out insects that egrets forage effortlessly.
• Clownfish & Sea Anemone: Clownfish gains shelter from stinging tentacles.
Trap: The mango tree, whale, cattle, and sea anemone receive strictly ZERO harm and ZERO benefit (Score: 0).

6. Amensalism

- / 0 (Inhibited / Neutral)
Ecological Mechanism: One species is inhibited, poisoned, or severely harmed by the presence or chemical secretions of another, while the second organism derives zero benefit and experiences zero harm.
NCERT Benchmark Examples:
• Antibiosis (_Penicillium_ vs Bacteria): Mold _Penicillium notatum_ secretes penicillin antibiotic into substrate, killing neighboring bacteria; mold gains nothing directly.
• Allelopathy (Black Walnut Tree): Roots of _Juglans nigra_ secrete toxic juglone into soil, inhibiting germination of nearby herbaceous seedlings.
• Algal Blooms: Dense cyanobacteria poison fish via microcystins.
Trap: Antibiosis is amensalism (-/0), NOT predation (+/-) or competition (-/-), because the producer is unaffected!
