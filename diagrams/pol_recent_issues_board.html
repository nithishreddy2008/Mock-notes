CONTEMPORARY CHALLENGES & SOCIAL TRANSFORMATIONS — THE SYSTEM MATRIX

FOUR CORE PILLARS OF CONTEMPORARY INDIAN POLITICS

1. Globalisation & LPG (1991)
Balance of payments crisis → Rupee devalued, gold pledged → Privatisation, delicensing, and trade openness.
2. Rise of OBCs in North India
Mandal mobilization → Emergence of SP, RJD, and JD(U) → Power shifted from upper-caste elites to backward castes.
3. Dalit Political & Social Assertion
Electoral (BAMCEF, DS-4, BSP under Kanshi Ram) & Non-Electoral (Dalit Panthers direct resistance, Marathi literature).
4. Communalism & Secularism
Ayodhya dispute (1986–92) → Demolition of Babri structure → 2002 Gujarat riots → Debates on principled distance.

Dalit Mobilization: Electoral & Non-Electoral

BAMCEF (1978): Backward and Minority Communities Employees Federation founded by Kanshi Ram as a non-political union.
DS-4 (1981): Dalit Shoshit Samaj Sangharsh Samiti formed as a mass agitational platform against caste exploitation.
Bahujan Samaj Party (1984): Kanshi Ram established BSP to contest elections; united SCs, STs, OBCs, and minorities (Bahujans).
Dalit Panthers (1972): Militant youth organization formed in Maharashtra by Namdeo Dhasal and J.V. Pawar.
Literary Revolution: Dalit Panthers generated powerful Dalit poetry and literature; forced passage of 1989 Atrocities Act.

The Great Debate: Models of Secularism

Western Secularism: Strict, mutual exclusion / wall of separation between church and state; state does not fund or regulate religion.
Indian Secularism: Principled distance. State respects all religions equally (Sarva Dharma Sambhava) without establishing any.
Legitimate State Reform: State can intervene to eradicate social evils (banning untouchability, sati, child marriage).
Minority Protection: Articles 29 and 30 grant minorities rights to establish and administer educational institutions.
Criticisms & Debates: Critics allege "minority appeasement" or vote-bank politics; defenders cite constitutional equality.

CHRONOLOGY OF MAJOR SOCIAL MOVEMENTS & COMMUNAL FLASHPOINTS

1972

Dalit Panthers: Militant group formed in Bombay slums; launched direct action against atrocities and caste violence.

1978–1984

Kanshi Ram's Trio: BAMCEF (1978) → DS-4 (1981) → Bahujan Samaj Party founded on 14 April 1984.

Feb 1986

Ayodhya Locks: Faizabad court ordered locks opened for Hindu prayers; sparked counter-mobilization by BMAC & VHP.

1991

LPG Reforms: Rao & Manmohan Singh rolled out New Economic Policy; trade unions and farmers staged nationwide protests.

6 Dec 1992

Babri Demolition: Structure razed by Karsevaks; Kalyan Singh govt resigned; Centre imposed Art. 356 in 4 BJP states.

Feb–Mar 2002

Gujarat Riots: Sabarmati Express coach burned at Godhra (57 killed); mass communal violence followed; SC monitored cases.

CHALLENGE OF AND RESPONSES TO ECONOMIC GLOBALISATION (POST-1991)

Economic Discontents & Labour Left trade unions (AITUC, CITU) opposed disinvestment of PSUs and labor contractization. Public sector strikes resisted the retreat of the welfare state and job cuts.
Agrarian & Farmer Grievances Farmer groups (BKU, Karnataka Rajya Raitha Sangha) resisted WTO intellectual property regimes, seed patenting by MNCs, and withdrawal of agricultural power/fertilizer subsidies.
Cultural & Social Critiques Both Left and Right cultural organizations resisted Westernization and vulgar consumerism (e.g., protests against Valentine's Day celebrations and beauty pageants in the 1990s).

PITFALL 1: BAMCEF vs DS-4 vs BSP

Do NOT confuse: BAMCEF (1978) was a non-political government employees' union. DS-4 (1981) was an agitational front. The Bahujan Samaj Party (1984) was the actual political party.

PITFALL 2: Dalit Panthers Geography

The Dalit Panthers was founded in Maharashtra (1972) by Namdeo Dhasal and J.V. Pawar, NOT in UP or Bihar. Their main medium of expression was Marathi literature and direct street action.

PITFALL 3: Demolition of Babri Masjid

The demolition occurred on 6 December 1992. The Chief Minister of Uttar Pradesh at that time was Kalyan Singh (BJP), who resigned immediately after the demolition.
