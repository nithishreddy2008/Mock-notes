1. Perfect Competition Features & Revenue
Price-Taker Model
  • Core Assumptions: Very large number of buyers and sellers; Homogeneous (identical) product; Free entry and exit; Perfect knowledge; Zero transport costs.
  • Price Taker: Industry determines market price via demand and supply. The individual firm has zero market power.
  • Horizontal Demand Curve: Demand facing the firm is infinitely elastic ($e_d = \infty$).
  • The Golden Revenue Identity: P = AR = MR. Every extra unit sold adds exactly price $P$ to Total Revenue.
  • Total Revenue ($TR$): $T R = P \times Q$. A straight line through the origin with constant slope equal to market price $P$.
CUET Rule: A price-taking firm never chooses price; it ONLY chooses the output quantity that maximizes profit!
2. Profit Maximization & Equilibrium Rules
MR = MC Framework
  • The 3 Golden Equilibrium Conditions:
    • Condition 1 (Necessary): $P = M R = M C$.
    • Condition 2 (Sufficient): $M C$ must be rising at equilibrium (cuts MR from below).
    • Condition 3 (Short Run): $P \ge A V C_{\min}$ (Shutdown condition).
    • Condition 3 (Long Run): $P \ge A T C_{\min}$ (Exit condition).
  • Short-Run Profit Possibilities:
    • Supernormal Profit: If $P > A T C$ ($TR > TC$).
    • Normal Profit (Break-Even): If $P = A T C_{\min}$ ($TR = TC$, zero economic profit).
    • Economic Loss: If $A V C_{\min} \le P < A T C$ (Operates to cover fixed overheads).
    • Immediate Shutdown: If $P < A V C_{\min}$ (Loss exceeds $TFC$).
Shutdown Logic: At shutdown point $P = A V C_{\min}$, the firm loses exactly $TFC$ whether it produces or shuts down!
3. Supply Curve Derivation & Shifts
Supply = Rising MC
  • Short-Run Supply Curve: That portion of the Short-Run Marginal Cost ($S M C$) curve that lies on or above the minimum $A V C$. Output is zero for $P < A V C_{\min}$.
  • Long-Run Supply Curve: That portion of the Long-Run Marginal Cost ($L M C$) curve that lies on or above the minimum $L A C$. Output is zero for $P < L A C_{\min}$.
  • Market Supply: Obtained by horizontal summation of all individual firms' supply curves at each price level.
  • Supply Shift Drivers:
    • Rightward (Increase): Fall in input prices ($w, r$), technological improvement, government production subsidies.
    • Leftward (Decrease): Rise in factor prices, imposition of per-unit sales tax, technological setbacks.
Unit Tax Impact: A unit tax $t$ shifts the $SMC$ curve vertically upward by $t$, causing supply to shift leftward!
4. Elasticity of Supply & CUET Exam Traps
Es & Trap Matrix
  • Percentage Formula: $e_s = \frac{\% \Delta Q}{\% \Delta P} = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$.
  • Geometric Intercept Rules:
    • Through Origin: $e_s = 1.0$ at every point, regardless of slope angle!
    • Cuts Price (Vertical) Axis: $e_s > 1$ (Elastic supply).
    • Cuts Quantity (Horizontal) Axis: $e_s < 1$ (Inelastic supply).
  • Long-Run Zero Profit Fallacy: Normal profit is an implicit cost. In long-run equilibrium, $P = \min LAC \implies$ Zero Economic Profit, but Positive Accounting Profit!
  • Time Horizon & Elasticity: Supply is perfectly inelastic in the momentary period ($e_s = 0$), more elastic in short run, and highly elastic in long run ($e_s > 1$).
Trap: A firm makes economic losses between min AVC and min ATC, yet still produces in the short run to minimize losses!
