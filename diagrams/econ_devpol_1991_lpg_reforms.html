The 1991 Economic Reforms (NEP): Crisis & The LPG Architecture

Macroeconomic Emergency, Stabilization vs Structural Reforms, Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation & WTO

CLASS 12 · INDIAN ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
1. The 1991 Crisis & Emergency
BoP & Fiscal Emergency
Macroeconomic Triggers
Fiscal Deficit: Unsustainable 8.4% of GDP driven by non-developmental expenditure.
Forex Reserves Depletion: Dropped below USD 1 billion (barely 2 weeks of essential imports).
Double-Digit Inflation: Annual inflation climbed above 12-14%.
Gulf War Shock (1990-91): Spike in crude oil prices & sudden halt in Gulf remittances.
Emergency Rescue Measures
Gold Pledged: 47 tonnes shipped to Bank of England & UBS to avoid sovereign default.
IMF-World Bank Bailout: USD 7 billion structural adjustment loan with reform conditions.
Two-Pronged Strategy:
• Stabilisation Measures: Short-term (contain inflation, rebuild forex, restore BoP balance).
• Structural Reform Measures: Long-term (dismantling licensing, opening trade & capital).
2. The Three LPG Reform Pillars
July 1991 Policy Shift
Pillar 1: Liberalisation (Freeing Enterprise)
Industrial De-licensing: Abolished licensing for all except 5-6 strategic/hazard sectors.
Financial Sector: RBI shifted from "Regulator" to "Facilitator"; private/foreign banks entered.
Tax Reforms: Direct tax slabs simplified & reduced; indirect taxes rationalized.
Trade Deregulation: Import quotas abolished by 2001; tariffs scaled down.
Pillar 2: Privatisation (PSU Transformation)
Disinvestment: Selling equity stakes in PSUs to private sector & public.
Managerial Autonomy: Status awards (Maharatna, Navratna, Miniratna) granting financial autonomy.
Pillar 3: Globalisation (World Integration)
Exchange Rate: 1991 Rupee devaluation → 1993 LERMS market floating.
Outsourcing Hub: India became global IT/BPO powerhouse (skills + low wage).
WTO Membership: Joined as founding member on Jan 1, 1995 (successor to GATT 1948).
3. Critical Appraisal & Modern Milestones
Achievements vs Deficits
Major Achievements
GDP Growth: Accelerated past the "Hindu rate of growth", led by Service sector.
Forex Reserves: Rose from USD 1 billion (1991) to over USD 600 billion.
FDI & FPI Expansion: Massive foreign capital inflows transformed infrastructure & tech.
Structural Deficits & Criticisms
Neglect of Agriculture: Public capital investment in irrigation plunged; subsidy cuts raised costs.
Jobless Growth: GDP gains concentrated in capital-intensive services; formal factory jobs lagged.
Disinvestment Flaws: Undervaluation of prime public sector assets.
Key Modern Milestones
• Demonetisation (8 Nov 2016): ₹500 & ₹1000 notes scrapped (86% currency) to hit black money.
• GST (1 July 2017): 101st Amendment, single unified indirect tax ("One Nation, One Market").
Reforms Axiom: Stabilization tackled immediate liquidity; LPG structural reforms transformed India from autarkic stagnation to an open, globalized emerging market.
