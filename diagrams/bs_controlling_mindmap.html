CONTROLLING
UNIT VIII · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Meaning & Importance

Controlling = comparing actual performance with planned standards, finding deviations and taking corrective action
Importance: accomplishes goals · judges accuracy of standards · efficient use of resources · improves motivation · ensures order & discipline · facilitates coordination

Planning $arrow$ Controlling — The Twins

Planning is the basis of controlling: the plan supplies the standards that control measures against
Controlling completes planning: deviations found by control feed the next plan
Without planning, control has no benchmark; without controlling, planning is blind — the two are one loop
Both look forward and backward: standards look ahead, comparison looks back

Steps 3 & 4 — The Deciding Moves

Compare: actual vs standard — percentage, cost, units — deviations found
Critical Point Control: monitor KRAs (key result areas) — not everything equally
Management by Exception: tolerate small deviations; escalate only the significant ones
Corrective action: fix the process, revise the standard, or retrain — nothing is left unattended

Control Process (4 Steps)

1 Setting performance standards — benchmarks from the plan (units, cost, time, quality)
2 Measuring actual performance — accurate, objective, on time
3 Comparing with standards — find and size the deviations
4 Analysing deviations + corrective action — critical points, exceptions, then act
The loop: corrective action feeds the next cycle of standards

Standards — Two Kinds

Quantitative: in numbers — 10,000 units, ₹5 lakh cost, 48-hour delivery
Qualitative: in words and judgments — courtesy of staff, quality of service, brand image
Qualitative standards are hard to measure — firms convert them into proxies (complaint counts, survey scores)

Why Controlling Needs Everything Else

Planning gives the standards; organising gives the structure to fix accountability; staffing gives capable people to measure
Directing gets the work running; controlling closes the loop and restarts planning
Controlling is the last function of one cycle and the first of the next

Traps that catch most students

Controlling ≠ bossing — it is measuring against standards, not scolding people
Standards come from planning — a control without a plan has nothing to measure against
Management by exception = only significant deviations climb up — small misses are handled below
Controlling is not the end — corrective action restarts the planning cycle
