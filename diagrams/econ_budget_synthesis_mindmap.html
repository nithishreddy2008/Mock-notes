MACROECONOMICS: GOVERNMENT BUDGET & THE ECONOMY

FIG 3: MASTER SYNTHESIS MINDMAP
PILLAR 1
Constitution & Objectives
Article 112 Foundation
Presented as the Annual Financial Statement of estimated receipts and expenditures for the upcoming fiscal year (April 1 – March 31).
Six Cardinal Objectives
1. Reallocation of Resources: Taxing demerit goods, subsidizing clean tech.
2. Reducing Inequalities: Progressive taxation & pro-poor subsidies.
3. Economic Stability: Counter-cyclical stabilization.
4. Management of PSUs: Funding public utilities.
5. Economic Growth: Capital Capex in infrastructure.
6. Employment Generation: Public works & programs.
PILLAR 2
The Revenue Budget
The Revenue Rule
Neither creates a liability NOR alters government assets. Regular operational cash flows.
Revenue Receipts (RR)
• Tax Revenue: Direct Taxes (Income Tax, Corp Tax) vs Indirect Taxes (GST, Customs).
• Non-Tax Revenue: Interest on loans given, PSU dividends, RBI surplus, Fees, Fines, Escheats, Grants.
Revenue Expenditure (RE)
Routine running costs: Salaries, Pensions, Administrative services, Subsidies, and Interest payments on past debt.
PILLAR 3
The Capital Budget
The Capital Rule
Creates/Reduces a liability OR Creates/Reduces government assets. Long-term balance sheet items.
Capital Receipts (CR)
• Debt Receipts (Liability Created): Market borrowings, RBI loans, external commercial loans.
• Non-Debt Receipts (Assets Reduced): Recovery of loans given to States, Disinvestment of PSU equity.
Capital Expenditure (CE)
• Asset Created: Building highways, dams, schools, defense aircraft.
• Liability Reduced: Repayment of borrowed loan principal.
PILLAR 4
Deficits & Fiscal Reform
The Three Deficit Formulas
RD = RE − RR
FD = Tot Exp − Receipts (excl Borrowings)
PD = FD − Interest Payments
Borrowing Equivalence
Fiscal Deficit = Total Gross Borrowings. Reflects the exact borrowing requirement of the government.
FRBM Act 2003 Targets
Institutional framework to eliminate revenue deficit and bring fiscal deficit down to 3% of GDP. Debt target: 60% of GDP.
Alert 1: Direct vs Indirect Taxes
Direct Tax: Impact and incidence fall on the same person; burden cannot be shifted (e.g. Income Tax). Indirect Tax: Burden is shifted to consumer (e.g. GST).
Alert 2: Interest vs Principal Repayment
Interest Payment is REVENUE expenditure (services past debt). Repayment of Loan Principal is CAPITAL expenditure (reduces debt liability).
Alert 3: Zero Primary Deficit Meaning
When PD = 0, Fiscal Deficit equals Interest Payments exactly. The government borrows only to pay interest on old accumulated debt.
