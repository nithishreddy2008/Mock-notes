1. Shielding Hierarchy Failure

Orbital screening ability follows: s > p > d > f. The 14 electrons entering the highly diffuse 4f subshell screen each other and outer 5d/6s electrons very poorly.

2. Steady Zeff Escalation

With each step from 58Ce to 71Lu, nuclear charge increases by +1 without commensurate shielding. The effective nuclear charge (Zeff) relentlessly pulls outer electron shells closer.

2. Basicity Trend of Ln(OH)₃ Hydroxides

As ionic radius decreases from La³⁺ (103 pm) to Lu³⁺ (86 pm), the covalent character of the Ln–OH bond increases (Fajan's rule). Therefore:

La(OH)₃ (Most Basic)   >   Ce(OH)₃   >   ...   >   Lu(OH)₃ (Least Basic)
3. Anomalous Oxidation States (+2 and +4)
Ce⁴⁺ (4f⁰) Strong Oxidiser

Stable noble-gas [Xe] 4f⁰ configuration, but easily reduced to the common +3 state. (E° = +1.74 V; analytical reagent).

Eu²⁺ (4f⁷) Strong Reducer

Attains half-filled [Xe] 4f⁷ stability. Readily oxidises back to Eu³⁺ by releasing an electron to act as a reducing agent.

Tb⁴⁺ (4f⁷) Oxidising Agent

Formed by losing 4 electrons to attain half-filled 4f⁷ shell; strong tendency to revert to Tb³⁺.

Yb²⁺ (4f¹⁴) Reducing Agent

Has completely filled 4f¹⁴ shell; readily loses an electron to attain the dominant trivalent Ln³⁺ state.

4. Massive Density Jump in 5d Metals

Because atomic radii of 5d metals remain nearly equal to 4d metals while atomic masses increase by ~80%, densities roughly double (e.g. Osmium & Iridium have d ≈ 22.6 g/cm³).
