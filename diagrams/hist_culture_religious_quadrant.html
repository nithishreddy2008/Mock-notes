Early Religious Traditions & Philosophies (c. 600 BCE – 600 CE)

Quadrant 1 · Jainism (Tirthankaras & Severe Asceticism)
Animist & Ascetic Ethic
  • Universal Animism: The entire cosmos is animated; even stones, rocks, water, and fire possess living souls (*jiva*).
  • Strict Ahimsa: Extreme non-injury to humans, animals, plants, and insects forms the bedrock of Jain ethics.
  • Karma & Asceticism: Cycle of rebirth is shaped by karma; the soul can only be liberated from karma through severe penance, world-renunciation, and self-starvation (*Sallekhana*).
  • Five Great Vows (*Mahavratas*): Barred killing, lying, stealing, property ownership (*aparigraha*), and mandated celibacy (*brahmacharya*); split into Digambaras and Shvetambaras.
Quadrant 2 · Early Buddhism (Theravada / The Lesser Vehicle)
The Middle Path
  • The Middle Path (*Majjhima Patipada*): Rejected both extreme self-indulgence and extreme self-mortification practiced by Jains and ascetics.
  • Cosmic Realities: The world is transient (*anicca*), devoid of an immortal eternal soul (*anatta*), and sorrow (*dukkha*) is universal.
  • Self-Effort & Nibbana: The Buddha was a mortal teacher, not a god; liberation from rebirth (*nibbana* - extinction of ego and desire) achieved strictly by personal meditation and moral conduct.
  • Aniconic Art & Canon: Buddha was never depicted as a human statue, but through symbols (tree, wheel, stupa); teachings preserved in Pali *Tripitaka* (Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma).
Quadrant 3 · Mahayana Buddhism (The Great Vehicle)
Theistic & Bodhisattva Cult
  • Deification of the Buddha: The Buddha was transformed from a human guide into a supreme, transcendent divine savior who responded to devotional prayer.
  • The Bodhisattva Ideal: Cult of deeply compassionate spiritual beings who achieved enlightenment but deliberately postponed entering nirvana to stay behind and rescue suffering humanity.
  • Image Worship: Elaborate human statues of the Buddha and Bodhisattvas (Avalokiteshvara, Maitreya) sculpted in Gandhara and Mathura schools, worshipped with incense and flowers.
  • Sectarian Terminology: Followers styled their tradition Mahayana ("Great Vehicle") and dismissed older conservative monks as Hinayana ("Lesser Vehicle" / Theravadins).
Quadrant 4 · Puranic Hinduism & Early Temples
Bhakti & Temple Architecture
  • Bhakti & Theistic Cults: Salvation through intense personal loving devotion (*bhakti*) to a chosen personal deity, primarily within Vaishnavism (10 avatars of Vishnu) and Shaivism (Shiva as linga and dancer).
  • Puranas: Composed in accessible Sanskrit by Brahmanas to integrate local folk deities, female goddesses, and lower castes excluded from formal Vedic rites.
  • Temple Architectural Evolution: Began as small square rock-cut cells (Garbhagriha housing the deity) → topped by tall ornate towers (Shikhara) → pillared assembly halls (Mandapa).
  • Kailashnatha at Ellora: 8th c. CE monolithic architectural marvel carved entirely out of a single living basalt hillside from top to bottom.
Philosophical Rule: The mid-first millennium BCE witnessed a global intellectual turning point across Greece (Socrates), Iran (Zarathustra), China (Kong Zi), and India (Buddha/Mahavira).
