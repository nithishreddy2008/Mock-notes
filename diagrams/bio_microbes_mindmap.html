Household Products

Curd: Lactobacillus turns milk sugar into lactic acid; also adds vitamin B₁₂
Dosa & idli: bacteria + yeasts ferment the batter; CO₂ puffs it spongy
Bread: baker's yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae raises the dough
Cheese: Swiss holes = CO₂ of Propionibacterium; Roquefort veins = fungi

Industrial Products

Beverages: yeast ferments malted cereals + fruit juices into ethanol
Antibiotics: penicillin from Penicillium — Fleming 1928; Chain & Florey 1942
Enzymes: lipases scrub clothes in detergents; pectinases clear bottled juice
Bioactive: Cyclosporin A shields transplants; statins cut cholesterol

Sewage Treatment

Primary (physical): screening → grit removal → sedimentation
Secondary (biological): flocs grow in the aeration tank; BOD falls
Settling tank sinks the flocs as activated sludge → anaerobic digester
Effluent is released only when BOD is low enough

Biogas

Made by methanogens — microbes that work without any oxygen
Mix: methane 50–70% + CO₂ + traces of other gases
Methanobacterium lives in cattle rumen → gobar gas from dung
Burns without smoke; spent slurry is a good fertiliser

Biocontrol Agents

Bacillus thuringiensis: spores kill caterpillars; its genes built Bt cotton
Baculovirus (nucleopolyhedrovirus): species-specific, fits organic farming
Trichoderma (a fungus) fights root pathogens
Ladybirds eat aphids; dragonflies hunt mosquitoes

Biofertilisers

Rhizobium: lives in legume root nodules, fixes N₂ (symbiosis)
Azotobacter + Azospirillum: free-living fixers in the soil
Mycorrhiza (Glomus): better phosphorus + water uptake; drought shield
Cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc): fix nitrogen in paddy fields

COMMON ERRORS & TRAPS

Swiss cheese holes come from Propionibacterium CO₂ — not from yeasts
Flocs are masses of bacteria + fungi — not pure bacteria
BOD goes down as treatment succeeds; methanogens work without oxygen
