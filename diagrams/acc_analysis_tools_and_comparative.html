Schedule III reporting framework, comparative horizontal analysis, common-size vertical models, and analytical limitations

UNIT V · FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
Schedule III Part I & II Reporting Framework Statutory Structure
Balance Sheet (Part I)
1. Equity & Liabilities: Shareholders' Funds, Share App Pending Allotment, Non-Current Liab, Current Liab.
2. Assets: Non-Current Assets (Property, Plant, Equip, Intangibles, Non-Current Inv), Current Assets (Inv, TR, Cash).
Statement of P&L (Part II)
Revenue: Revenue from Operations + Other Income = Total Revenue.
Expenses: Materials, Stock purchases, Change in inventory, Employee benefits, Finance costs, Depr/Amort, Other exp.
CUET Trap: Loose Tools and Stores/Spares are part of Inventory in Balance Sheet, but are EXCLUDED while computing Quick Ratio!
Comparative Statements: Horizontal (Dynamic) Analysis Time-Series Trend
Comparative statements examine two or more successive years to reveal directions, magnitudes, and rates of operational and financial growth.
Column C
Absolute Change = Current Year (B) − Previous Year (A)
Column D
% Change = [ Absolute Change (C) / Base Year (A) ] × 100
Zero/Negative Base Rule: If the previous year (Base Year A) figure is Zero or Negative, the percentage change cannot be calculated (mathematically undefined)!
Dynamic Nature: Shows movement over time across successive balance sheets or income statements.
Common-Size Statements: Vertical (Static) Analysis Component % Ratio
Common-size statements convert each individual line item into a percentage of a common base, enabling cross-sectional and intra-firm sizing.
Balance Sheet Base = 100%
% = [ Individual Asset or Liab / Total of Balance Sheet ] × 100
Income Statement Base = 100%
% = [ Individual Expense or Tax / Revenue from Operations ] × 100
Base Trap: In Common-Size Income Statement, the base is ALWAYS Revenue from Operations (Net Sales), NEVER Total Revenue!
Limitations of Financial Statement Analysis Analytical Constraints
1
Historical Cost Bias: Financial statements reflect historical money values; they ignore the impact of price level changes and inflation.
2
Ignores Qualitative Elements: Fails to measure human resource caliber, management competency, employee morale, and labor relations.
3
Window Dressing Vulnerability: Figures can be manipulated to project an artificially favorable liquidity or solvency standing at year-end.
4
Accounting Policy Divergence: Varying methods of depreciation (SLM vs WDV) or inventory valuation (FIFO vs Weighted Avg) hinder comparability.
CUET Exam Note: Financial analysis is merely a diagnostic tool — it does not provide definitive management decisions on its own.
