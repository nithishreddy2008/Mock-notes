Core Chronology & Geographic Extent
  • Date Bracket: Mature Harappan phase dated to c. 2600 – 1900 BCE (Early Harappan before 2600, Late post-1900).
  • Geographic Boundaries: Manda (North/J&K), Daimabad (South/Maharashtra), Sutkagen Dor (West/Makran), Alamgirpur (East/UP).
  • Nomenclature: Named after Harappa (first excavated site, 1921 by Daya Ram Sahni). Also known as Indus Valley Civilisation.
  • Key Pioneer Archaeologists: Alexander Cunningham (first DG of ASI, missed date), John Marshall (announced 1924), R.E.M. Wheeler (stratigraphy, 1944).
1
Urban Planning & Sanitation
  • Dual Settlement: Raised Citadel (west, mud-brick platforms, public buildings) and larger Lower Town (east, residential quarters).
  • Standardized Bricks: Sun-dried and baked bricks in strict 1:2:4 ratio (thickness : width : length) across all regional sites.
  • Grid Drain Network: Streets laid in grid pattern cutting at right angles; domestic drains connected directly into covered street mains with sumps.
  • Monumental Citadel Structures: Great Bath at Mohenjodaro (bitumen-sealed waterproof tank) and the massive Warehouse / Granary.
2
Subsistence & Agriculture
  • Crops & Flora: Wheat, barley, lentils, chickpea, sesame; millets prominent in Gujarat; rice rare (found at Lothal/Rangpur).
  • Faunal Remains: Cattle, sheep, goat, buffalo, pig (domesticated); wild boar, deer, gharial; fish and fowl consumed.
  • Agricultural Tech: Terracotta plough models at Cholistan & Banawali; ploughed field with criss-cross furrows at Kalibangan.
  • Irrigation Works: Canals identified at Shortughai (Afghanistan); rock-cut water reservoirs at Dholavira (Gujarat).
3
Craft Production & Material Culture
  • Specialized Craft Centres: Chanhudaro (< 7 hectares, exclusively devoted to bead-making, seal-carving, weight-making); shell-work at Balakot & Nageshwar.
  • Raw Material Sources: Carnelian (Bharuch), Lapis Lazuli (Shortughai), Steatite (South Rajasthan/North Gujarat), Copper (Khetri mines, Rajasthan & Oman).
  • Weight System: Cubical chert weights; lower denominations binary (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32... up to 12,800); higher denominations decimal.
  • Luxury vs Utilitarian: Faience pots (luxury, rare); querns, pottery, needles, flesh-rubbers (utilitarian, widely distributed).
4
Trade Networks & Epigraphy
  • Oman Connection: Chemical nickel tracing links Harappan copper artifacts to Omani copper; black-clay Harappan jars found at Omani sites.
  • Mesopotamian Texts: Refer to Magan (Oman) and Meluhha ("land of seafarers", Harappa); mention carnelian, lapis, copper, gold.
  • Harappan Script: Undeciphered, written right-to-left (boustrophedon evidenced), 375 to 400 distinct signs; longest inscription has 26 signs.
  • Seals & Sealings: Steatite seals with animal motifs (unicorn, humped bull, elephant) used to stamp clay tags on tied goods to verify uncompromised transit.
5
Society, Religion & Theories of Decline
  • Burial Practices: Extended pits; grave goods modest (pottery, copper mirrors, beads); lack of massive wealth indicates no royal pyramid-style hoarding.
  • Religious Beliefs: "Pashupati" seal (seated proto-Shiva surrounded by animals), terracotta "Mother Goddess" figurines, conical stones (lingas), sacred pipal tree.
  • Authority Debate: Single state hypothesis (standardized bricks, planned grids, uniform weights) vs multiple city-states (Mohenjodaro, Harappa).
  • Decline (c. 1900 BCE): Climate change, tectonic shifts/flooding, drying of Ghaggar-Hakra; abandonment of core cities; Wheeler's "Aryan invasion" thesis disproven.
