DIRECTING
UNIT VII · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Meaning & Importance

Directing = instructing, guiding, motivating and leading people so they act willingly toward goals
Importance: initiates action · integrates efforts · means of motivation · helps realise potential · supports change · brings stability & balance
Directing is continuous and flows from every manager to every subordinate

Supervision & Motivation

Supervisor = the link between workers and management — oversees work, guides, feeds back, settles grievances
Motivation = the process of stimulating people to act to accomplish goals
Maslow: physiological $arrow$ security $arrow$ social $arrow$ esteem $arrow$ self-actualisation — a satisfied need no longer motivates

Incentives

Financial: pay and allowances, bonus, profit-sharing, co-partnership/stock options, perquisites, retirement benefits
Non-financial: status, career growth, job enrichment, job security, recognition, employee participation, delegation/autonomy
Money feeds the lower Maslow rungs; recognition and growth feed the upper rungs

Leadership — Three Styles

Authoritative: leader decides alone, orders flow down — fast, but kills initiative
Democratic (participative): decisions with the group — commitment rises, decisions slower
Laissez-faire (free-rein): full freedom to the team — experts thrive, coordination suffers
Leadership = influencing people to work willingly toward group goals

Communication

Communication = exchange of ideas, facts and feelings so both sides understand alike
Formal: official channels, planned networks — accountability clear, but slow
Informal (grapevine): social, fast, no record — good feedback, but rumours spread
Process: sender $arrow$ message $arrow$ encoding $arrow$ medium $arrow$ receiver $arrow$ feedback

Barriers & Remedies

Semantic: words with many meanings, jargon $arrow$ use simple, clear language
Psychological: premature judgment, distrust, emotions $arrow$ listen, be patient
Organisational: rigid rules, long chains $arrow$ shorten lines, allow two-way flow
Personal: fear of authority, lack of confidence $arrow$ build trust, openness

Traps that catch most students

Directing is not just ordering — it includes guiding, motivating, leading, communicating
Satisfied needs stop motivating — the unmet rung is the motivator
Laissez-faire is not absence of leadership — the leader still sets goals and watches results
Grapevine = informal communication — it can carry both truth and rumour
