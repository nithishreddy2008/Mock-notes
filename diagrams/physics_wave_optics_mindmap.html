1. Wavefronts & Huygens' Theory
Wave Geometries
Point: Spherical | Slit: Cylindrical | Distant: Plane
  • Wavefront: Surface passing through all particles vibrating in identical phase; rays are always perpendicular.
  • Secondary Wavelets: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary disturbance spreading at speed v.
  • Envelope: Tangential surface touching secondary wavelets in forward direction forms the new wavefront.
2. Proof of Reflection & Snell's Law
Geometric Proofs
Reflection: i = r | Refraction: sin i / sin r = v₁/v₂ = μ
  • Reflection Proof: Congruence of right triangles ΔABC ≅ ΔAEC (hypotenuse AC, side BC = AE = vt) yields i = r.
  • Snell's Law: Kinematic wavelet ratio: (v₁t/AC) / (v₂t/AC) = v₁/v₂ = n₂/n₁ = μ.
  • Invariance: Frequency ν and phase remain unchanged; wavelength compresses: λ₂ = λ₁ / μ.
3. Interference & Coherence
Superposition
I = I₁ + I₂ + 2√(I₁I₂)cos ϕ | I_max = (√I₁ + √I₂)²
  • Coherence: Two sources must have identical frequency and a constant, time-invariant phase difference.
  • Constructive Interference: Path diff Δx = nλ, phase ϕ = 2nπ ⟹ I_max = 4I₀ (for identical slits).
  • Destructive Interference: Path diff Δx = (n - ½)λ, phase ϕ = (2n-1)π ⟹ I_min = 0 (perfect darkness).
4. Young's Double Slit (YDSE)
Fringe Width
β = λ·D / d | θ = λ / d | Δy = (D/d)(μ - 1)t
  • Fringe Width: Uniform across all fringes; proportional to wavelength λ and screen distance D, inversely to d.
  • Liquid Medium: Wavelength reduces to λ/μ, reducing fringe width: β' = β / μ.
  • Thin Film Shift: Introducing transparent plate of thickness t shifts entire pattern by Δy = (D/d)(μ - 1)t.
5. Single-Slit Diffraction
Central Maxima
Minima: a·sin θ = nλ | Central Width: β₀ = 2λD / a = 2β
  • Central Maximum: Broad, intense peak with angular width 2λ/a; twice the width of secondary maxima!
  • Secondary Maxima: Occur at a·sin θ = (n + ½)λ with rapidly decaying brightness (I₀/22, I₀/61).
  • Aperture Limit: Noticeable diffraction requires slit width a to be comparable to wavelength λ (a ~ λ).
6. Polarization & Brewster
Malus & Brewster
Malus: I = I₀·cos² θ | Brewster: tan i_p = μ (i_p + r_p = 90°)
  • Transverse Evidence: Only transverse waves can be polarized; longitudinal acoustic waves cannot.
  • Malus's Law: Linearly polarized light passing through analyzer tilted at angle θ transmits intensity I = I₀ cos²θ.
  • Brewster's Angle: Light reflected at tan i_p = μ is 100% polarized perpendicular to plane of incidence.
