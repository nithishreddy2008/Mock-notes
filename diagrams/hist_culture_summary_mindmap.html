1. Mid-1st Millennium BCE Ferment
64 Sects & Debates
  • Global Awakening: Parallel philosophical surge across Greece (Socrates/Plato), Iran (Zarathustra), China (Kong Zi), and Gangetic India.
  • Kutagarashala: Pointed-roof debate huts in groves where mendicant teachers questioned Vedic authority and animal sacrifices.
  • Heterodox Skepticism: Fatalists (Ajivikas under Makkhali Gosala) and Materialists (Lokayatas under Ajita Kesakambalin) rejected karma.
  • Linguistic Shift: Teachers discarded elite Vedic Sanskrit, communicating directly in vernacular Prakrit dialects.
★ Core Axis: Renunciation & Ethical Reform
Core Tenets
  • Vedic Critique: Buddhism and Jainism rejected infallible Vedic authority, hereditary varna supremacy, and bloody rituals.
  • Universal Empathy: Rebirth driven by moral conduct; liberation accessible to all castes, women, and householders through ethical living.
  • Monastic Orders: Institutionalized wanderers into structured communities governed by democratic communal consensus.
  • Canon Conservation: Codification of oral wisdom into written canonical pitakas and agamas upon master's demise.
2. Jain Philosophy & Mahavira
Tirthankara Path
  • 24 Tirthankaras: Vardhamana Mahavira was the 24th teacher, preceded by Rishabhanatha (1st) and Parshvanatha (23rd).
  • Cosmic Jiva: Entire world is animate; stones, water, wind, and fire have souls (*jiva*); extreme non-violence (*ahimsa*).
  • Ascetic Penance: Karma purged only through rigorous monastic self-denial, world-renunciation, and voluntary fasting death (*Sallekhana*).
  • Five Mahavratas & Schism: Non-injury, truth, non-stealing, non-possession, celibacy; split into Digambara and Shvetambara.
3. The Buddha, Dhamma & Sangha
The Middle Path
  • Four Great Sights: Siddhartha Gautama encountered old man, sick man, corpse, and ascetic; attained enlightenment under Bodhi tree.
  • Core Dhamma: World is transient (anicca), soulless (anatta), filled with sorrow (dukkha); avoided Jain extremes via Middle Path.
  • The Sangha: Monastic order of bhikkhus; women admitted as bhikkhunis through stepmother Mahapajapati Gotami at Ananda's urging.
  • Tripitaka ("Three Baskets"): Vinaya Pitaka (monastic rules), Sutta Pitaka (Buddha's discourses), Abhidhamma Pitaka (philosophical analysis).
4. Stupas: Architecture & Preservation
Sanchi vs Amaravati
  • Sacred Mounds: Reared over bodily relics of the Buddha; 4 sacred sites: Lumbini (birth), Bodh Gaya (enlightenment), Sarnath (first sermon), Kusinara (parinirvana).
  • Anatomy of Stupa: Anda (hemispherical dome), Harmika (balcony of gods), Yashti (central mast), Chhatri (triple umbrellas), Toranas (gateways).
  • Sanchi Miracle: Preserved in situ due to Shahjehan Begum and Sultan Jehan Begum funding plaster casts, museum, and John Marshall's volumes.
  • Amaravati Tragedy: Stripped of exquisite limestone friezes by Colin Mackenzie, Walter Elliot, and British officials; ruined by in-situ neglect.
5. Mahayana & Structural Temples
Image & Bhakti
  • Rise of Mahayana: Deification of Buddha, worship of compassionate Bodhisattvas who postpone personal nirvana to save all living beings.
  • Sculptural Schools: Anthropomorphic Buddha images created at Gandhara (Greco-Roman realistic drapery) and Mathura (fleshy indigenous sandstone).
  • Puranic Hinduism: Vaishnavism (10 *avataras* restoring cosmic order) and Shaivism (linga and anthropomorphic Shiva); accessible Puranas.
  • Temple Evolution: Small square cell (Garbhagriha) → tall towering spire (Shikhara) → assembly halls (Mandapa); Ellora rock-cut monolith.
