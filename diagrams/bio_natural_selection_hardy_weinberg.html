POPULATION GENETICS & MECHANISMS OF EVOLUTION
Part 1: Three Operation Modes of Natural Selection
Phenotypic Distribution Shifts
Stabilising
Peak gets higher & narrower
More individuals acquire mean character value. Eliminates peripheral extremes.
E.g. Human birth weight (2.5–3.5 kg has highest survival).
Directional
Peak shifts in one direction
More individuals acquire value other than mean. Directional replacement.
E.g. Industrial melanism (dark moths), DDT resistance in mosquitoes.
Disruptive
Two distinct peaks form
More individuals acquire peripheral character values at both ends of distribution curve.
Can lead to polymorphism and sympatric speciation.
Classic Case Study: Industrial Melanism in Biston betularia (England) Directional Selection
Pre-Industrialisation (1850s):
• Lichens grew thick on tree trunks (no smoke/pollution).
• White-winged moths camouflaged against light bark.
• Dark melanic moths (carbonaria) easily spotted & eaten by predatory birds.
Post-Industrialisation (1920s):
• Coal smoke & soot covered tree trunks; lichens died off.
• Dark melanic moths camouflaged on dark soot-covered bark.
• White moths stood out and were predated → Dark moth population surged.
Key NCERT Insight: No variant was completely wiped out. Selection is proportional, reversible, and acts on existing genetic variations!
Part 2: Hardy-Weinberg Principle & Evolutionary Forces
Genetic Equilibrium
p2 (AA) + 2pq (Aa) + q2 (aa) = 1   |   p + q = 1
p = Frequency of Allele A q = Frequency of Allele a 2pq = Heterozygotes (Aa) p2 + 2pq = Dominant Phenotype
Five Factors Known to Affect Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium Disturbance leads to Evolution
1. Gene Migration
When migration of a section of population happens to another place, gene frequencies change in both original and new populations. Repeated movement is gene flow.
2. Genetic Drift
Change in allele frequency occurring purely by chance in small populations. Extreme form: original drifted individuals become founders → Founder Effect.
3. Mutation
Advantageous mutations are rare, but critical for long-term raw material. Hugo de Vries proposed Saltation (single-step large mutation) drives sudden speciation.
4. Recombination
Crossing over during pachytene of meiosis produces novel combinations of parental alleles without changing total gene pool frequencies.
5. Natural Selection
Differential reproduction: heritable variations that enable better survival produce more progeny. Selection shifts gene pool toward higher adaptive fitness.
CUET UG Exam Trap Alert:
• If homozygous recessive (aa) frequency is given as 16% (0.16), then q = √0.16 = 0.4, p = 0.6.
• Heterozygotes (Aa) = 2pq = 2(0.6)(0.4) = 0.48 (48%), NOT 0.24!
• Total Dominant Phenotype = p2 + 2pq = 0.36 + 0.48 = 0.84 (84%).
