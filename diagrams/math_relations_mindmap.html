RELATIONS & FUNCTIONS
CUET UG MATHEMATICS

Relations & Their Types

Relation: any set of ordered pairs from A × A
Reflexive: every (a, a) present — no exceptions
Symmetric: arrows come in mirror pairs
Transitive: chains close: a→b→c forces a→c
Equivalence: all three — sorts A into classes

One-One, Onto, Bijective

One-one: no two inputs share an output
Onto: range = codomain, nothing unused
Bijective: both at once — perfect pairing
x² test: not one-one on ℝ; 2x + 3 is bijective ℝ → ℝ

Composition & Inverse

(f∘g)(x) = f(g(x)) — inside first, order matters
g's output must fit f's domain — the range check
Inverse needs bijection — swap and solve: y = 2x + 3 → f⁻¹(y) = (y−3)/2
f⁻¹ ≠ 1/f — undo machine, not reciprocal

Why Principal Branches Exist

sin, cos, tan repeat — not one-one on their natural domains
Restrict to one clean branch — then the inverse is a function
Principal values: sin⁻¹ ∈ [−π/2, π/2], cos⁻¹ ∈ [0, π], tan⁻¹ ∈ (−π/2, π/2)
Sum rules: sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2; tan⁻¹x + cot⁻¹x = π/2

Domain & Range (inverse trig)

sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹: domain [−1, 1] — inputs are sine/cosine values
tan⁻¹, cot⁻¹: domain ℝ — slopes/roots go anywhere
sec⁻¹, cosec⁻¹: domain |x| ≥ 1
Brackets matter: open vs closed ends are the MCQ

Checking a Relation (workflow)

Step 1: list every (a, a) for reflexive — one miss = fail
Step 2: mirror-scan every pair for symmetry
Step 3: walk every 2-step chain for transitivity
Verdict: 3/3 → equivalence; name the failing test otherwise

TOP TRAPS
