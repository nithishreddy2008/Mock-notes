The Punjab Drama: Resolution to Healing

Linguistic Suba (1966): Punjabi-speaking state created by separating Haryana and Himachal Pradesh.
Anandpur Resolution (1973): Akali Dal demanded political decentralization and redrawing of federal boundaries.
Tragic 1984: Operation Blue Star (June); Indira Gandhi assassinated (Oct); horrific anti-Sikh violence in Delhi.
Rajiv-Longowal Accord (July 1985): Agreed to Chandigarh transfer, Ravi-Beas tribunal; Longowal killed by extremists.

The North-East: Three Core Demands

Demands for Autonomy: Non-Assamese hill tribes rejected Assamese language imposition; created separate states.
Secessionist Armed Struggles: Mizo National Front (Laldenga) and Naga National Council (A.Z. Phizo).
Anti-"Foreigner" Agitations: Assam movement led by AASU against unchecked migration from Bangladesh.
Democratic Integration: Accords transformed rebel leaders into democratically elected Chief Ministers (Laldenga, Mahanta).

THE THREE HISTORIC RAJIV GANDHI ACCORDS (1985–1986)

JULY 1985

Punjab Accord: Signed with Harchand Singh Longowal. Agreed to transfer Chandigarh to Punjab and adjudicate water disputes.

AUGUST 1985

Assam Accord: Signed with AASU leaders (P.K. Mahanta). All foreigners entering after 24 March 1971 to be detected & deported.

JUNE 1986

Mizo Peace Accord: Signed with Laldenga (MNF). Mizoram granted full statehood with special protections; rebels surrendered arms.

FOUR CORE LESSONS OF DEMOCRATIC INTEGRATION IN INDIA

1. Regional Aspirations are Legitimate Regional and linguistic identities are integral components of federal democracy, not signs of disloyalty or anti-national intent.
2. Accommodation Over Suppression Armed force can manage security, but long-term peace requires negotiated political accords, dialogue, and democratic rehabilitation.
3. Flexible Power Sharing Asymmetrical federalism (Art 370, 371A-J, 6th Schedule Autonomous Councils) accommodates regional distinctiveness within one nation.
4. Balanced Regional Development Economic grievances, backwardness, and feelings of exploitation often trigger regional militancy; shared prosperity cements unity.

TRAP 1: The Assam Accord Cutoff Date

The baseline cutoff date agreed in the Assam Accord is 24 March 1971 (midnight before the Bangladesh war started). Distractor options always test 1947, 1951, or 1965.

TRAP 2: Naga Movement vs Mizo Movement Leaders

A.Z. Phizo led the Naga National Council (NNC). Laldenga led the Mizo National Front (MNF). Do not swap these two legendary rebel leaders in MCQ options.
