Central Monarchy & Council (Mantriparishad)
Pataliputra Apex
  • King at the Centre: Absolute executive, legislative, and judicial fountainhead; advised by Chanakya's ministerial council.
  • Pataliputra Capital: Directly administered imperial core surrounded by a massive wooden palisade with 570 towers and 64 gates.
  • Imperial Cadres: High-ranking officials (*Mahamattas*), revenue collectors (*Samaharta*), and imperial treasurers (*Sannidhata*).
Four Strategic Provincial Viceroyalties (Kumaramatyas)
Regional Nodes

Headed by royal princes (*Kumaras*) along long-distance overland and maritime trade arteries:

Military Committee Coordination — 30 Members across 6 Operational Sub-Committees
Megasthenes' Indica Account
Branch I
Navy
Maintained riverine and coastal flotillas along the Ganga, Son, and maritime gateways.
Branch II
Transport & Supply
Procured bullock-carts, forage, rations for soldiers, and hired mechanical artisans/drummers.
Branch III
Infantry
Oversaw foot-soldiers armed with broadswords, bamboo longbows, and javelins.
Branch IV
Cavalry
Managed war-horses imported from Kamboja and Bactria; saddles and bridle upkeep.
Branch V
War Chariots
Maintained fighting chariots carrying archers and charioteers on flat plains.
Branch VI
War Elephants
Procured, tamed, and armored wild elephants from the dense forests of Jharkhand/Kalinga.
Pataliputra Municipal Board (30 Members)
6 Civic Committees
  • Industrial & Artisan Oversight: Regulated craft wages, guild rules, and material authenticity.
  • Care of Foreigners: Managed foreign travellers, lodgings, medical care, and burial of deceased.
  • Vital Statistics & Trade: Compulsory birth/death registration; monitored weights, measures, and 10% sales tax collection.
Dhamma Mahamattas & Moral Ideology
Ashokan Policy
  • Special Imperial Cadre: Appointed in Ashoka's 14th regnal year to spread *Dhamma* across all sects and classes.
  • Non-Sectarian Principles: Obedience to parents/elders, generosity to Brahmanas and ascetics, humane treatment of slaves/servants.
  • Epigraphic Propagation: Inscribed on living rocks and polished monolithic sandstone pillars across the empire.
Key Administrative Rule: Mauryan state power was uneven — intense around the Pataliputra core and provincial nodes, limited along peripheral forest tracts.
