FOUR CHALLENGES FACING THE INDIAN ECONOMY — ONE BOARD

1. HUMAN CAPITAL FORMATION

How people become a resource: spending on education, health, training converts humans into productive capital
Sources of HCF: expenditure on education, health, on-the-job training, study programmes, migration (cost counts as investment), information
Why it pays: skilled people earn more, innovate more, and pass gains to children — growth with equity
Human vs physical capital: knowledge cannot be separated from its owner; human capital uses BOTH consumption and investment spending
Education sector growth: literacy climbed (18% → 74%+), elementary/secondary/higher institutes multiplied; mid-day meal and universalisation drives
The problems: spending deficit relative to needs, teacher vacancies, poor quality outcomes, educated unemployment — skills don't match jobs

2. RURAL DEVELOPMENT

Lifting farming and village life: credit, marketing, diversification
Rural credit needs: seeds and inputs before harvest, land improvement, consumption between harvests
Credit sources: INSTITUTIONAL (cooperatives, commercial banks, RRBs, government, SHGs) vs NON-INSTITUTIONAL (moneylenders, landlords, traders — costly, exploitative)
Marketing hurdles: too many intermediaries, poor storage, distress sales, no grading, thin roads
Marketing fixes: regulated markets (APMC), MSP guarantee, cooperative marketing, warehousing, grading and standardisation
Diversification: crop shift + allied activities (dairy, poultry, fisheries, horticulture) + non-farm jobs (food processing, rural tourism)
Organic farming: chemical-free, eco-friendly, premium prices — but lower early yields, certification costs, short shelf life

3. EMPLOYMENT

Who works, on what terms — and the formal-informal divide
Types of workers: SELF-EMPLOYED (largest share), REGULAR SALARIED (fewest but best paid), CASUAL WAGE (insecure, daily rates)
Formal vs informal: written contract + benefits + security vs none of these — most Indian workers live in the informal world
Participation: workforce participation rate (WPR) — worker per 1,000 people; women's rates lag sharply
The worry: growth without matching jobs ("jobless growth") — output rises faster than employment
Policies: MGNREGA 2005 — 100 days of guaranteed wage work per rural household; self-employment and skill programmes on top

4. ENVIRONMENT & SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT

The environment as provider, sink and life-support — and the duty to protect it
Four functions: supplies resources (air, water, soil, minerals), absorbs waste (the SINK), sustains life (biodiversity, climate), enhances quality of life (scenery, calm)
Carrying capacity: resource extraction beyond renewal and waste beyond absorption = carrying capacity breached
India's state: air pollution in cities, contaminated rivers, deforestation, soil erosion, falling groundwater — development's unplanned bill
Sustainable development: meeting PRESENT needs without destroying FUTURE generations' ability to meet theirs (Brundtland idea)
Strategies: solar and wind power, CNG and LPG, bio-composting, mini-hydel projects, traditional knowledge and practices, organic methods
