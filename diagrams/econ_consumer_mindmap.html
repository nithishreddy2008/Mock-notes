Micro & Central Problems

Micro studies one buyer, one firm, one market; macro studies economy-wide totals
Scarcity (limited resources, unlimited wants) forces every economy to answer what / how / for whom
Opportunity cost = the next best thing given up; PPF shows the max combos, bowed out because resources are not equally suited to both goods

The Utility Engine

MU = ΔTU (extra utility from one more unit); TU = sum of MUs
Law of diminishing MU: each extra unit gives less extra satisfaction — the 3rd ice cream never beats the 1st
MU in money: divide utils by utils-per-rupee to compare with price in ₹

Consumer's Equilibrium (Utility Approach)

One good: keep buying while MUx > Px (in money terms); stop at MUx = MU of money
Two goods: shift spending until MUx/Px = MUy/Py = MU of money, with the budget exactly spent
Budget line: PxX + PyY = M; slope = Px/Py; income shifts it parallel, a price change pivots it

Demand & Its Behaviour

Demand = desire + ability + willingness to pay — miss one and it is only a wish
Law of demand: own price ↓ → quantity demanded ↑ (other things equal)
Determinants: income (normal vs inferior), prices of substitutes & complements, tastes, expectations, population
Market demand = horizontal sum of all individual demands

Movement vs Shift

Own price changes → movement along the same curve (expansion ↓price, contraction ↑price)
Any non-price factor → whole curve shifts right (increase) or left (decrease)
Words carry marks: "change in quantity demanded" = movement; "change in demand" = shift

Price Elasticity of Demand

Percentage method: ep = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP (drop the minus sign)
Total expenditure method: P ↑ and TE same → e = 1; TE rises → e > 1; TE falls → e < 1
Degrees: 0 (perfectly inelastic) < e < 1 (inelastic) < e = 1 (unitary) < e > 1 (elastic) → ∞
More elastic when: many substitutes, luxury, big budget share, many uses, longer time

Traps that catch most students

Own price never shifts the demand curve — it only moves you along it
Demand ≠ desire — all three conditions (want + money + willingness) must hold
TE method only classifies (>1, =1, <1) — it can never give the exact elasticity value
Inferior goods flip the income rule — income rises, demand falls; Giffen goods even break the law of demand
