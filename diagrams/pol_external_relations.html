INDIA'S EXTERNAL RELATIONS — PEACE TALK, THREE WARS, ONE BOMB
1962

The Sino-Indian War

Background: Tibet absorbed by China (1950); Panchsheel signed (1954); Dalai Lama flees to India (1959); disputed borders — Aksai Chin in Ladakh (China built a road through it) and the McMahon Line in NEFA.

October-November 1962: China attacks simultaneously in NEFA and Ladakh; Indian defences collapse; V.K. Krishna Menon resigns as Defence Minister.

End: unilateral Chinese ceasefire (21 Nov 1962); Aksai Chin stays Chinese-held. "Hindi-Chini bhai-bhai" is over — the 1962 defeat became the national benchmark of shock.

1965

The Second Indo-Pak War

Background: Lal Bahadur Shastri is PM; spring skirmishes in the Rann of Kutch; Pakistan's Operation Gibraltar infiltrates guerrillas into Kashmir (August).

September 1965: full war — Indian armour drives toward Lahore; Pakistan's Patton tanks meet India's Centurions at Khem Karan.

End: Tashkent Agreement (10 January 1966) brokered by the USSR restores the status quo; Shastri dies in Tashkent the next day. Kashmir's ceasefire line stays what it was.

1971

The Bangladesh War

Background: Pakistan's military cracks down on East Pakistan's Awami League (March 1971); nearly 80 lakh refugees flood into India.

August 1971: Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace and Friendship — 20 years; the alliance shift that deterred US-China moves.

3-16 December 1971: two-front war; Dhaka falls; Bangladesh is born (16 Dec 1971). Then the Shimla Agreement (1972): Indira Gandhi and Z.A. Bhutto convert the ceasefire line into the Line of Control.

Panchsheel — the five principles (29 April 1954)

1

Mutual respect for each other's territorial integrity and sovereignty

2

Mutual non-aggression

3

Mutual non-interference in each other's internal affairs

4

Equality and mutual benefit

5

Peaceful co-existence

The irony to remember: signed with CHINA in 1954 — eight years before China's 1962 attack buried its first principle in Ladakh.

Non-Alignment — Nehru's policy spine

The meaning: join NEITHER Cold War bloc — keep independent judgment on each issue, not neutrality on every issue.
The founding five: Nehru (India), Nasser (Egypt), Tito (Yugoslavia), Sukarno (Indonesia), Nkrumah (Ghana).
The milestones: Bandung Afro-Asian conference 1955; first NAM summit Belgrade 1961.
The practice: arms and aid from BOTH blocs — steel plants and MiGs from the USSR, PL-480 wheat from the USA.
The exam phrase: non-alignment is a policy of independent decision-making — it does NOT mean isolation or silence.

The nuclear programme

Beginnings: Atomic Energy Commission 1948; Homi Bhabha's vision of peaceful atomic power.
Pokhran-I, 18 May 1974: "Smiling Buddha" — India's first test, announced as a PEACEFUL NUCLEAR EXPLOSION.
The refusals: India declined to sign the NPT (1968) — it locks "have" and "have-not" nations into permanent tiers — and later the CTBT too.
The line to quote: no-first-use posture, universal disarmament demanded — but the option kept.

THE ALLIANCE SHIFT — from solo non-alignment to the Soviet treaty

1950s: strict non-alignment, both blocs court India — Pakistan joins US pacts (SEATO 1954, CENTO), pushing India closer to Moscow for arms. 1962: the US and UK airlift emergency arms during the China war. 1965: the US arms embargo hits India harder than Pakistan (already Soviet-equipped); the USSR mediates Tashkent. 1971: the Indo-Soviet Treaty (20 years) converts friendship into commitment — the clearest "shifting alliance" the syllabus line means. Read the shift as response, not abandonment: each move answered a rival's move.

TRAP 1

Non-alignment ≠ neutrality. India judged every issue on merits and voted independently — it even took sides (Bangladesh 1971). Neutrality would have meant standing aside on ALL issues.

TRAP 2

Tashkent (1966) ≠ Shimla (1972). Tashkent followed 1965 with Shastri; Shimla followed 1971 with Indira Gandhi and created the Line of Control.

TRAP 3

1974's test was "peaceful nuclear explosion" — the exact official phrase. The word "test" in the question stem usually hunts the year 1974, not 1998.
