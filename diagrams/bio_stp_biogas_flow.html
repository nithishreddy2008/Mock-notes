Sewage Treatment Plant (STP) & Biogas Architecture

Sequential Physical & Biological Purification, Microbial Flocs, BOD Degradation, and Anaerobic Methanogenesis

CUET UG Biology · Unit VIII
1. Primary (Physical) Stage 1

Sequential Filtration

Raw municipal sewage passes through coarse mesh screens to physically intercept and remove large floating debris (plastic, paper, rags).

Physical Separation
▼

Grit Chamber

Sedimentation of small pebbles, coarse sand, and grit particles based on gravitational density settling.

Gravity Settling
▼

Primary Settling Tank

All solids that settle form the Primary Sludge. The supernatant liquid forms the Primary Effluent.

Primary Sludge vs Effluent
2. Secondary (Aeration) Stage 2

Massive Aeration Tank

Primary effluent is pumped in. Constant mechanical agitation + continuous air pumping fuels aerobic metabolism.

Aerobic Chamber
▼

Flocs Formation

Masses of aerobic bacteria associated with fungal hyphae form mesh-like structures called Flocs.

Flocs = Bacteria + Fungi
▼

BOD Reduction

Flocs rapidly consume organic pollutants. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) drops dramatically, signaling clean water.

Sharp BOD Decline
3. Settling & Sludge Stage 3

Settling Tank

Effluent enters settling tank where bacterial and fungal flocs naturally sediment to form Activated Sludge.

Activated Sludge
▼

Inoculum Recirculation

A small portion of activated sludge is pumped back into the aeration tank to act as the starting inoculum.

Recycled Inoculum
▼

Clean Effluent Release

Treated secondary effluent (low BOD) is safe for discharge into natural water bodies (rivers, lakes).

River Release Safe
4. Anaerobic Biogas Stage 4

Anaerobic Sludge Digester

Major activated sludge is pumped into closed anaerobic digesters without oxygen.

Anoxic Digestion
▼

Methanogen Digestion

Anaerobic methanogens (Methanobacterium) break down bacterial/fungal mass and cellulose.

Methanobacterium
▼

Biogas Gas Mixture

Produces combustible Biogas: CH4 (50–70%), CO2 (30–40%), and trace H2S/H2. Used as rural fuel.

CH4 + CO2 + H2S

BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) Rule

BOD measures the amount of O2 required by microbes to oxidize all organic matter in 1 L of water. Higher BOD = Higher Polluting Potential. Secondary treatment continues strictly until BOD is reduced.

Cattle Rumen & Biogas Plant (Gobar Gas)

Cattle dung (gobar) is rich in methanogens and cellulosic fiber. The Indian IARI & KVIC floating-dome biogas plant produces inflammable biogas used for cooking and lighting, leaving rich mineral manure.

National River Conservation Plans

Initiated by the Ministry of Environment and Forests: Ganga Action Plan and Yamuna Action Plan build large-scale municipal STPs to prevent untreated sewage from poisoning holy rivers.
