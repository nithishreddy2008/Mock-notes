The Human Development Paradigm: Pillars, Approaches & Measurement

Conceptual architecture of Dr. Mahbub-ul-Haq and Prof. Amartya Sen: capability expansion, welfare, and global indexation

CUET UG Geography · Analytical Matrix
The Four Structural Pillars of Human Development
Core Dimensions
1 Equity
Equal, fair access to opportunities available to everybody, regardless of gender, race, income, caste, or social status. Removes institutional barriers.
2 Sustainability
Continuity in the availability of opportunities for future generations. All environmental, financial, and human resources must be utilized sustainably.
3 Productivity
Human labor productivity enhanced through building continuous capabilities. People are the true wealth of a nation; investing in health and skills raises productivity.
4 Empowerment
The freedom and capability to make real choices. Demands good governance, democratic decentralization, and empowerment of marginalized groups.
The Four Methodological Approaches
Evolution of Thought
1 Income Approach
The oldest approach; human development is linked directly to income. Higher income reflects higher freedom and greater access to opportunities.
2 Welfare Approach
Views humans as passive beneficiaries. Proposes government maximizes public expenditure on education, healthcare, and amenities.
3 Basic Needs Approach
Pioneered by the International Labour Organisation (ILO). Emphasizes 6 non-negotiable basic needs: health, education, food, water, sanitation, and housing.
4 Capability Approach
Associated with Nobel Laureate Amartya Sen. Focuses on building human capabilities in health and education to unlock true human agency and freedom.
HDI Measurement Dimensions (1/3 Weight Each)
UNDP Methodology
1. Longevity & Health Weight 1/3
Indicator: Life Expectancy at Birth
Higher life expectancy indicates a longer, healthier life with robust healthcare infrastructure and disease protection.
2. Knowledge & Education Weight 1/3
Indicators: Mean Years & Expected Years of Schooling
Adult literacy rate and combined gross enrolment ratio reflect a society's access to knowledge and skill formation.
3. Decent Standard of Living Weight 1/3
Indicator: Gross National Income (GNI) per capita in PPP US$
Purchasing Power Parity adjusts for international price differences to evaluate real command over economic resources.
