1. Basic Macroeconomic Concepts
Foundations
  • Stock vs. Flow:
    • Stock: Measured at a specific point in time (Capital, Wealth, Money Supply).
    • Flow: Measured over a period of time (Income, Investment, GDP, Depreciation).
  • Final vs. Intermediate Goods:
    • Final Goods: Out of production boundary for consumption or investment.
    • Intermediate Goods: Used as raw materials or resold in same year. Excluded to avoid double counting!
  • Gross vs. Net Investment: Net Investment = Gross Investment - Depreciation.
  • Normal Residents: Resides in economic territory for ≥ 1 year and center of economic interest lies there.
End-Use Classification: A good is intermediate or final based on its end-use, NOT its physical nature! Sugar used by bakery is intermediate; sugar used at home is final.
2. Circular Flow & 3 Conversion Pillars
Conversion Machinery
  • Triple Equivalence: Value of Production ≡ Total Factor Incomes Generated ≡ Total Final Expenditure.
  • Pillar 1 (Gross ↔ Net): Net = Gross - Depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital).
  • Pillar 2 (Domestic ↔ National): National = Domestic + NFIA
    • $\text{NFIA} = \text{Factor Income from Abroad} - \text{Factor Income to Abroad}$.
  • Pillar 3 (Market Price ↔ Factor Cost): Factor Cost = Market Price - NIT
    • $\text{NIT} = \text{Indirect Taxes (GST)} - \text{Subsidies}$.
  • Core Identities:
    • Domestic Income: NDP_FC
    • National Income: NNP_FC = NDP_FC + NFIA
Depreciation Trap: Normal wear/tear and expected obsolescence are Depreciation; sudden natural disasters or riots are Capital Losses, NOT depreciation!
3. The Three Measurement Methods
Estimation Machinery
  • Value Added Method: $GVA_{MP} = \text{Value of Output} - \text{Intermediate Consumption}$. Sum across primary, secondary, tertiary sectors $= GDP_{MP}$.
  • Income Method: $NDP_{FC} = \text{Compensation of Employees} + \text{Operating Surplus} + \text{Mixed Income}$.
    • $\text{Operating Surplus} = \text{Rent} + \text{Royalty} + \text{Interest} + \text{Profit}$.
    • $\text{Profit} = \text{Corporate Tax} + \text{Dividend} + \text{Retained Earnings}$.
  • Expenditure Method: $GDP_{MP} = C + G + I + (X - M)$.
    • $I = \text{Gross Domestic Capital Formation} = \text{GFCF} + \Delta\text{Stock}$.
Exclusion Rule: Transfer payments (pensions, scholarships), sale of second-hand goods, and financial securities are strictly EXCLUDED from all 3 methods!
4. Real vs. Nominal GDP & Welfare Limits
GDP Deflator & Welfare
  • Nominal GDP: Output valued at current year market prices ($Q_t \times P_t$). Can rise purely due to inflation!
  • Real GDP: Output valued at constant base year prices ($Q_t \times P_0$). True indicator of physical output growth!
  • GDP Deflator (Price Index): GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100. Comprehensive measure of economy-wide inflation.
  • Why GDP is an Incomplete Index of Welfare:
    • Distribution of GDP: High GDP with rich-poor inequality does not mean mass welfare.
    • Non-Monetary Exchanges: Barter, household unpaid labor (housewives) excluded.
    • Externalities: Pollution, ecological degradation are negative externalities not deducted from GDP!
Green GDP: Measures economic growth adjusted for environmental degradation and depletion of natural resource capital.
