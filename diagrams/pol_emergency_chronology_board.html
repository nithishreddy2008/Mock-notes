CRISES OF THE CONSTITUTIONAL ORDER — THE EMERGENCY ENGINE (1973–1977)

THE THREE-ACT CRISIS — from judicial clash to democratic restoration

1973–74: Resistance Erupts
Kesavananda Bharati clash → CJI supersession; Gujarat Navnirman; JP leads Bihar Total Revolution
12–25 June 1975: The Spark
Allahabad HC unseats Indira; SC grants conditional stay; JP calls Ramlila rally; Emergency declared
1975–77: 21 Months of Emergency
MISA arrests (1.11 lakh), press censorship, Sanjay Gandhi's excesses, 42nd "Mini-Constitution" amendment
March 1977: Democratic Rebound
Congress wiped out in North; Janata Party takes 295 seats; Morarji Desai sworn in as first non-Cong PM

The Extra-Constitutional Dimensions

Sanjay Gandhi's Caucus: Ran executive policy without holding any formal cabinet or constitutional post.
Forced Sterilisations: Coercive family planning targets enforced on poor, rural labourers and slum dwellers.
Turkman Gate Demolitions: Bulldozers flattened centuries-old Delhi settlements overnight; protests shot down.
The 42nd Amendment (1976): Stripped Fundamental Rights; barred judicial review of amendments; extended Lok Sabha tenure to 6 years.

The Resistance & Democratic Response

Clandestine Press: Underground newsletters; Himmat, Sadhana, Seminar closed rather than submit.
Award Refusals: Kannada writer Shivarama Karanth and Hindi novelist Phanishwarnath Renu returned state honours.
Legal Defiance: Courageous habeas corpus pleas; HC judges resisted before Supreme Court folded in ADM Jabalpur (1976).
The Ballot Box Verdict: Indian electorate decisively proved that democracy cannot be suspended by executive decree.

THE JUNE 1975 COUNTDOWN — from court verdict to midnight decree

12 JUNE 1975

Justice J.L. Sinha of Allahabad HC invalidates Indira's 1971 election for corrupt campaign practices (Raj Narain petition).

24 JUNE 1975

Supreme Court vacation judge V.R. Krishna Iyer grants conditional stay: Indira can remain PM, but cannot vote in Parliament.

25 JUNE 1975

JP leads massive rally at Ramlila Maidan; calls for civil disobedience and asks armed forces to disobey unconstitutional orders.

MIDNIGHT 25 JUNE

President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed signs Emergency under Art 352 ("internal disturbance"); electricity to newspapers cut off.

THE TWO KEY POST-EMERGENCY COMMISSIONS & CONSTITUTIONAL SAFEGUARDS

Shah Commission of Inquiry (May 1977) Headed by former CJI J.C. Shah. Disclosed that Emergency was declared without consulting Cabinet; documented custodial torture, illegal detentions, and administrative subversion.
The 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) Passed by Janata government. Replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion"; mandated written Cabinet advice; made Articles 20 and 21 non-suspendable even during Emergency.
Rise of Civil Liberties Bodies The trauma of Emergency led to the birth of human rights watchdog organizations: People's Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL) and People's Union for Democratic Rights (PUDR).

TRAP 1: The Grounds of Emergency

1975 Emergency was proclaimed under "internal disturbance" (Article 352). It was the 44th Amendment in 1978 that replaced this vague phrase with "armed rebellion".

TRAP 2: ADM Jabalpur (1976) vs Kesavananda Bharati (1973)

Kesavananda Bharati (1973) established the Basic Structure doctrine. ADM Jabalpur (1976) notoriously ruled that citizens had no right to life (Art 21) during Emergency (Justice H.R. Khanna dissented).
