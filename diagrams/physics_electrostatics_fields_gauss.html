1. Coulomb Force & Charges

Point Charges
Charge Quantization & Invariance Basic Postulates

Quantization: Total charge is integral multiple of elementary unit: q = ±n·e where e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

Relativistic Invariance: Charge magnitude is strictly independent of speed or frame of reference (unlike relativistic mass).

Coulomb's Law (Vector Form) Inverse Square
F₁₂ = [1 / (4πε₀)] · [q₁ q₂ / r²] r̂₂₁

Properties: Central, conservative, obeys Newton's 3rd Law (F₁₂ = -F₂₁). ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²·N⁻¹·m⁻².

Dielectric Medium: F_med = F_vac / K. Force decreases by factor K (relative permittivity ε_r ≥ 1).
Superposition & Distributions Multi-Body

Superposition: Net force on charge is vector sum: F_net = Σ F_i. Two-body interaction is unaffected by other charges.

Charge Densities: Linear λ = dq/dL [C/m], Surface σ = dq/dA [C/m²], Volume ρ = dq/dV [C/m³].

2. Electric Field & Dipole

Vector Field
Field Definition & Field Lines Force / Charge
E = lim(q₀→0) [F / q₀] = [1 / (4πε₀)] · [q / r²] r̂

Field Line Rules: Originate on +q, terminate on -q; never form closed loops; tangents show direction; density indicates field intensity.

Electric Dipole (p = q · 2a) Dipole Fields

Axial Line (End-on): E_axial = [2 k p / r³] along p̂ (for r >> a).

Equatorial Line (Broadside): E_equat = [k p / r³] antiparallel to p̂.

Critical Ratio: E_axial = 2 · E_equat at same distance r. Both fall off as 1/r³ (faster than point charge 1/r²).
Dipole in External Field (E) Torque & Energy

Uniform Field: Net force F_net = 0; Torque τ = p × E = p E sin θ.

Potential Energy: U = -p · E = -p E cos θ.

Equilibria: θ = 0° (Stable, U_min = -pE); θ = 180° (Unstable, U_max = +pE); Work done from θ₁ to θ₂ = pE(cos θ₁ - cos θ₂).

3. Gauss's Law & Planar / Linear

Flux Theorem
Gauss's Theorem Formulation Total Flux
Φ_E = ∮ E · dA = q_enclosed / ε₀

Flux Invariance: Net flux depends solely on net enclosed charge; independent of shape, size, or position within closed Gaussian surface.

Infinite Straight Wire (Linear λ) Cylindrical Symmetry
E = λ / (2 π ε₀ r)

Gaussian Surface: Coaxial cylinder of radius r, length L. Flat end caps contribute zero flux (E ⊥ n̂). Field is purely radial: E ∝ 1/r.

Infinite Plane Sheet of Charge (σ) Planar Symmetry
E = σ / (2 ε₀)

Independence of Distance: Field is uniform everywhere perpendicular to sheet, regardless of distance r from surface.

Oppositely Charged Sheets (±σ): Outside plates: E = 0; Between plates: E = σ/ε₀ (foundation of parallel plate capacitor).

4. Spherical Shell & Conductors

Spherical Symmetry
Thin Spherical Shell (Radius R) Total Charge q

Outside (r > R): E_out = [1 / (4πε₀)] · [q / r²] = (σ R²)/(ε₀ r²).

Surface (r = R): E_surf = [1 / (4πε₀)] · [q / R²] = σ / ε₀ (maximum).

Inside (r < R): q_enc = 0 ⟹ E_in = 0 everywhere.

E-Discontinuity: Field jumps abruptly from 0 to σ/ε₀ at shell boundary r = R.
Conductor Electrostatics Equilibrium Rules

Inside Conductor: Net electrostatic field E = 0; net interior volume charge ρ = 0.

Surface Field: E = (σ / ε₀) n̂ (strictly normal to surface).

Constant Potential: Electrostatic potential V is uniform throughout volume and equal to surface value (equipotential body).

Electrostatic Shielding Faraday Cage

Field inside any cavity in a conductor is zero, shielded from external electrical disturbances. Used in sensitive coaxial cables & instruments.
