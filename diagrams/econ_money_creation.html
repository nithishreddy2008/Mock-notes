HOW BANKS CREATE MONEY — AND HOW THE RBI STEERS IT

Credit creation — the ₹10,000 that became ₹1,00,000

CRR = 10%. Every bank keeps 10% as reserves and lends the rest — the same money hops from bank to bank, shrinking each hop
DEPOSIT ₹10,000
→
reserve ₹1,000
→
LOAN ₹9,000
re-deposit ₹9,000
→
reserve ₹900
→
LOAN ₹8,100
re-deposit ₹8,100
→
reserve ₹810
→
LOAN ₹7,290
... and on, and on ...
TOTAL deposits → ₹1,00,000
Read it once: the banking system MULTIPLIES the initial deposit by 1/CRR (here 1 ÷ 0.10 = 10). Lower the CRR, steeper the curve — the reserve requirement is the brake pedal on money creation.

The RBI's control panel — tools that move money supply

Three quantitative levers change the volume of money; repo changes its price
CRR Cash Reserve Ratio — the share banks MUST park with the RBI. CRR ↑ → lendable funds ↓ → money supply ↓
SLR Statutory Liquidity Ratio — the share banks must hold as liquid assets (cash, gold, approved securities). SLR ↑ → loan capacity ↓
Repo rate Rate at which the RBI LENDS to banks. Repo ↑ → borrowing costlier → credit ↓. Reverse repo: RBI BORROWS from banks, absorbing liquidity.
OMO Open Market Operations — RBI SELLS securities → money leaves the system; BUYS securities → money enters.
Margin req. The gap between a loan's value and its collateral — raising it shrinks credit (selective/qualitative tool).
Read it once: every tool answers one question — "how much money, at what price?" The RBI tightens (raises CRR/SLR/repo, sells securities) to cool inflation; loosens to fight recession.

TRAP 1

Money multiplier = 1/CRR — but only banks together create this money; a SINGLE bank cannot, because its loan may leave to another bank.

TRAP 2

Repo vs reverse repo are opposite doors: repo = RBI lends (injects); reverse repo = RBI borrows (absorbs). Read the door before answering.

TRAP 3

M1 is the exam's default "money supply": currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI. Time deposits join only in M2/M3.
