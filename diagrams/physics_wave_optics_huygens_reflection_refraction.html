1. Wavefronts & Huygens
Secondary Wavelets
Wavefront: Locus of all points having identical phase
Rays ⊥ Wavefronts | Forward Envelope = New Wavefront
  • Wavefront Topologies: Point source produces spherical wavefronts ($I \propto 1/r^2$); line slit produces cylindrical wavefronts ($I \propto 1/r$); infinitely distant source produces plane wavefronts ($I = \text{const}$).
  • Secondary Wavelets: Each point on a primary wavefront emits secondary wavelets spreading out in all directions with wave velocity $v$.
  • Forward Envelope: The forward tangential envelope to these secondary wavelets defines the shape and position of the new wavefront at time $t + \Delta t$.

2. Proof of Reflection
i = r Proof
BC = AE = v·t (Common hypotenuse AC)
ΔABC ≅ ΔAEC (RHS Congruence) ⟹ ∠i = ∠r
  • Geometric Construction: Time taken for ray at $B$ to reach $C$ is $t = BC / v$. In same time $t$, secondary wavelet from $A$ spreads to radius $AE = v t$.
  • RHS Congruence: Right triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle AEC$ share hypotenuse $AC$ and side $BC = AE = v t$, proving $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle AEC$.
  • Law of Reflection: Corresponding angles are equal: $\angle BAC = \angle ECA \implies i = r$. Incident ray, normal, and reflected ray lie in same plane.

3. Proof of Refraction
Snell's Law Proof
sin i = BC / AC = v₁·t / AC | sin r = AD / AC = v₂·t / AC
sin i / sin r = v₁ / v₂ = n₂ / n₁ = μ [Snell's Law]
  • Wavelet Speed Ratio: In time $t$, incident point $B$ travels $v_1 t$ to reach $C$, while wavelet from $A$ travels $v_2 t$ to reach $D$ in medium 2.
  • Snell's Law: $\sin i / \sin r = (v_1 t / AC) / (v_2 t / AC) = v_1 / v_2 = n_2 / n_1$.
  • Wavelength Compression: Frequency $\nu$ remains strictly unchanged ($E = h\nu$ constant); wavelength compresses: $\lambda_2 = \lambda_1 / n$.
