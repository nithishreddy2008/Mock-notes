1. CONDUCTANCE METRICS

Fundamental Laws
Conductance (G) & Cell Constant (G*)
G = 1 / R = κ · (A / l) | G* = l / A
• Units: Conductance in Siemens (S) or Ω⁻¹. Cell constant G* in cm⁻¹ or m⁻¹.
• Conductivity formula: κ = G × G* = (1 / R) × (l / A).
Molar Conductivity (Λm)
Λm = (1000 × κ) / C [S cm² mol⁻¹]
Conductance of all ions produced by dissolving 1 mole of electrolyte in solution volume V. (In SI units: Λm = κ / (1000 × C) in S m² mol⁻¹).
CRITICAL EXAM DISTINCTION ON DILUTION
1. Conductivity (κ) DECREASES on Dilution:
Dilution increases total volume, so the number of conducting ions per unit volume (per cm³) decreases.

2. Molar Conductivity (Λm) INCREASES on Dilution:
Because Λm = κ × V. The increase in volume V upon dilution overwhelmingly outweighs the decrease in κ!
Strong vs Weak Electrolyte Behaviour
• Strong (KCl, NaCl): Fully ionised (α = 1). Dilution only weakens interionic attractions; Λm increases slowly.
• Weak (CH₃COOH): Partially ionised. Dilution drastically shifts equilibrium to products (α → 1); steep rise in Λm near infinite dilution.

2. DEBYE-HÜCKEL-ONSAGER & KOHLRAUSCH

High-Yield Graphs
Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration
Λ°m = ν₊ λ°₊ + ν₋ λ°₋
*"At infinite dilution, each ion migrates independently and makes a definite contribution to total molar conductivity, irrespective of the co-ion."*
APPLICATION: WEAK ELECTROLYTE Λ°m CALCULATION
Λ°m(CH₃COOH) = Λ°m(CH₃COONa) + Λ°m(HCl) − Λ°m(NaCl)
Degree of dissociation: α = Λm / Λ°m
Dissociation constant: Ka = (C · α²) / (1 − α)

3. FARADAY'S LAWS & ELECTROLYSIS

Quantitative Stoichiometry
Faraday's 1st & 2nd Laws of Electrolysis
w = Z · I · t = (M · I · t) / (n · F)
• 1st Law: Mass deposited w ∝ charge Q (w = Z·Q).
• Electrochemical Equivalent: Z = M / (n · F) = Equivalent Mass / 96500.
• 2nd Law: For same charge Q: w₁ / w₂ = E₁ / E₂.
• 1 Faraday (F): Charge of 1 mole of e⁻ = 96,487 C ≈ 96,500 C.

THE O₂ OVERPOTENTIAL TRAP
Thermodynamically, oxidation of water to O₂ (E° = 1.23 V) is favoured over Cl⁻ to Cl₂ (E° = 1.36 V). However, oxidation of water to O₂ is kinetically very sluggish and requires a high *overpotential*. Hence, Cl₂(g) discharges preferentially at the anode!
