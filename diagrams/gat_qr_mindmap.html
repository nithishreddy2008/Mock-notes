The Application Method

Four steps: read the scene → name the tool (formula) → compute → check units and size
Words to math: "is" means =, "of" means ×, "per" means ÷, "more" means +
Sanity size: a courtyard costing ₹56,000 cannot measure 4 sq cm — absurd sizes expose slips

Number Theory & Arithmetic

Divisibility: digit-sum rules for 3 and 9; 6 = even + 3-rule
HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers — rescue formula
Speed trio: distance = speed × time; km/h → m/s = ÷ 3.6
SI = P × R × T ÷ 100 — simple interest never compounds

Algebra: Words to Equations

Name the unknown — "let the number be x" is half the answer
One line per fact — translate each sentence into its own equation
Consecutive numbers: x, x+1, x+2 — the sum trick settles them

Geometry Essentials

Triangle angles sum to 180°; ratio splits divide it in parts
Pythagoras: a² + b² = c² in RIGHT triangles only
Triple sight-reading: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25
Circle: area πr², circumference 2πr — radius vs diameter first

Mensuration Toolkit

2D: rectangle l×b · square s² · triangle ½bh · circle πr²
3D: cube s³ · cuboid lbh · cylinder πr²h
Which one fires? Carpet = area; fence = perimeter; fill = volume

Scoring Habits

Tool first: pick the formula BEFORE computing — no blind arithmetic
Units ride along: convert once, at the start (km/h to m/s)
Marking maths: +5 right, −1 wrong — computed-and-checked attempts

Traps that catch most students

Area when perimeter is asked — "cost of fencing" ≠ "cost of carpeting"
πr² with the diameter — halve the diameter before squaring
Pythagoras on non-right triangles — check the right angle first
Skipping the unit conversion — 72 km/h vs 20 m/s decides the answer
