MACROECONOMICS: INCOME, EMPLOYMENT & THE MULTIPLIER

FIG 3: MASTER SYNTHESIS MINDMAP
PILLAR 1
AD, AS & Propensities
Components of AD & AS
• AD: $C + I$ (two-sector planned expenditure).
• AS: $Y = C + S$ (45° line from origin).
• Investment: Autonomous ($I = \bar{I}$), independent of income level.
Consumption Function
C = c̄ + bY (b = MPC)
• Autonomous $c̄$: Survival spending at $Y=0$.
• $APC = C/Y$ (falls as $Y$ rises; $>1$ before breakeven; $=1$ at breakeven).
Saving Function & Rules
S = -c̄ + (1 - b)Y
• $MPC + MPS = 1$ (always).
• $APC + APS = 1$ (always).
PILLAR 2
Equilibrium Output
Two Theoretical Approaches
Approach 1: AD = AS (C + I = Y)
Approach 2: S = I (Leakage = Injection)
Both approaches produce identical equilibrium income: $Y^* = (c̄ + \bar{I}) / MPS$.
Dynamic Restoring Force
• AD > AS: Inventories deplete $\implies$ firms hire and expand output.
• AS > AD: Inventories accumulate $\implies$ firms cut production until $AD = AS$.
Effective Demand
The point where the AD curve intersects the 45° AS line. Output is determined by effective demand, not potential capacity.
PILLAR 3
The Multiplier Engine
Multiplier Formula
K = ΔY / ΔI = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS
Direct relationship with $MPC$; inverse relationship with $MPS$.
Limits & Operation
• Minimum: $K = 1$ (if $MPC = 0$).
• Maximum: $K = \infty$ (if $MPC = 1$).
• Forward Multiplier: $\Delta I \uparrow \implies \Delta Y \uparrow$.
• Backward Multiplier: $\Delta I \downarrow \implies \Delta Y \downarrow$.
Paradox of Thrift
If all citizens attempt to save a higher proportion of income ($MPS \uparrow$), aggregate demand collapses, leaving total realized national savings unchanged or lower!
PILLAR 4
Gaps & Policy Tools
Inflationary Gap (Excess AD)
• Planned $AD > Y_F$ (at full employment).
• Causes pure demand-pull inflation (output pinned at $Y_F$).
• Remedy: Contractionary fiscal ($G\downarrow, T\uparrow$) & Dear money policy.
Deflationary Gap (Deficient AD)
• Planned $AD < Y_F$ (at full employment).
• Leads to involuntary unemployment.
• Remedy: Expansionary fiscal ($G\uparrow, T\downarrow$) & Cheap money policy.
Underemployment Equilibrium
An economy can settle into a stable equilibrium ($AD = AS$) with persistent involuntary unemployment if aggregate demand is deficient.
Alert 1: Multiplier Bounds
The investment multiplier $K$ can never be less than 1 and can never be negative. Its strict mathematical domain is $1 \le K \le \infty$.
Alert 2: Output at Full Employment
Under Excess Demand, national real output cannot increase because all resources are already 100% utilized. Only nominal prices and wages rise.
Alert 3: APC vs MPC Distinction
APC ($C/Y$) compares aggregate levels and can exceed 1 (below breakeven). MPC ($\Delta C / \Delta Y$) measures marginal slope and is strictly bounded: $0 < MPC < 1$.
