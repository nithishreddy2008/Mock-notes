Government Intervention Mechanics: Maximum Legal Price (Ceiling) vs. Minimum Support Price (Floor) & Market Distortion

Government Control Ceiling < P* (Shortage) Floor > P* (Surplus)
Price Ceiling (Maximum Price Legislation)
Imposed Below P*
Price Floor / Minimum Support Price (MSP)
Imposed Above P*

Price Ceiling (Upper Legal Limit)

  • Target Beneficiary: Protects low-income consumers against price gouging for essential goods (wheat, rice, medicines, urban house rent).
  • Operational Condition: Must be set strictly BELOW equilibrium price ($P_c < P^*$) to be binding.
  • Direct Consequence: $Q_D > Q_S \implies$ Shortage. Necessitates Rationing / Dual Pricing / PDS.

Price Floor / MSP (Lower Legal Limit)

  • Target Beneficiary: Protects producers (farmers via Minimum Support Price) or workers (statutory Minimum Wage).
  • Operational Condition: Must be set strictly ABOVE equilibrium price ($P_f > P^*$) to be binding.
  • Direct Consequence: $Q_S > Q_D \implies$ Surplus. Government must purchase the entire unsold output to maintain the floor!

The Ineffective Intervention Trap

Fatal CUET Trap: If government imposes a Price Ceiling ABOVE equilibrium ($P_c > P^*$), it is non-binding and ineffective—market clears at $P^*$! Similarly, a Price Floor BELOW equilibrium ($P_f < P^*$) is completely non-binding and has zero market effect!
