NATION-BUILDING AND ITS PROBLEMS
POLITICS IN INDIA SINCE INDEPENDENCE · CH. 1

Nehru's Approach

Two tasks at once: shape one nation out of diversity AND build democracy — simultaneously, not one after the other
Unity in diversity: accommodation of languages and cultures instead of forced uniformity
Secular promise: after Partition's violence, Muslims who stayed = equal citizens by choice
Hesitation accepted: Nehru resisted linguistic states, then bowed to democratic pressure — and unity held

Partition Legacy & Refugees

The violence: lakhs killed; the border cut through Bengal and Punjab communities
About 80 lakh refugees: camps in Delhi, West Bengal, Punjab; rehabilitation ministries
Identity loss: refugees rebuilt homes, trades and citizenship from zero
Gandhi's last fast: Calcutta 1947 against riots — assassinated Jan 1948

Integration of 565 States

Patel + V.P. Menon: States Department; persuasion + pressure; Instrument of Accession
Most joined by 15 Aug 1947 — defence, external affairs, communications to the Union
Junagadh: plebiscite 1948 · Hyderabad: police action Sept 1948 against Nizam + Razakars
Manipur, Tripura, Nagaland belt: mergers negotiated 1947-49; Nagaland becomes a state only in 1963

The Kashmir Problem

Oct 1947: tribal invasion backed by Pakistan; Maharaja Hari Singh signs the Instrument of Accession
Sheikh Abdullah + National Conference backed accession to India
War 1947-48: ceasefire line (later LoC) splits the state; UN involvement
Article 370: special status from the accession terms; Delhi Agreement 1952; wars 1965, 1971 keep the dispute alive

Reorganisation of States

First refusals: Dhar Commission 1948 + JVP Committee — language rejected as the basis
Potti Sriramulu's fast: 58 days; dies 15 Dec 1952; Andhra State 1953 follows
SRC (Fazl Ali, Panikkar, Kunzru): 1953-55; language accepted with unity safeguards
1956 Act: 14 states + 6 UTs; then Bombay split 1960, Nagaland 1963, Punjab split 1966

Language Conflicts

Constitution: Hindi official with a 15-year English transition; 14 languages in the Eighth Schedule
Nehru's 1959 assurance: English remains as long as non-Hindi states want
Official Languages Act 1963: English continues beyond 1965
Madras 1965: anti-Hindi agitation; three-language formula defuses the duel

Traps that catch most students

Andhra State ≠ Andhra Pradesh: 1953 carved Andhra from Madras; 1956 added Telangana to make Andhra Pradesh
Who accepted language? The SRC — not Dhar or JVP, which both rejected language as the basis
Accession subjects are limited: defence + external affairs + communications — not total surrender of powers
Kashmir's ally: the National Conference supported accession — do not credit the Congress party for it
