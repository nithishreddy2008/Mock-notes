Double Fertilization & Post-Fertilization Ontogeny Flow FIGURE 2

Six-phase sequential ontogenetic pathway from chemotropic tube guidance to syngamy, triple fusion, endosperm nutrition, and seed fate

CUET UG Biology
Angiosperm Landmark
S.G. Nawaschin (1898)
STEP 1 Recognition & Chemotropism
Pollen-Pistil Dialogue & Tube Growth
Pistil recognizes compatible pollen via continuous chemical dialogue between pollen proteins and stigma receptors. Pollen hydrates; intine pushes through a germ pore forming a pollen tube. The tube grows down the style, carrying 2 male gametes at its tip under chemotropic guidance.
Incompatibility Barrier: Self-incompatible pollen is blocked either by inhibition of germination on stigma or inhibition of tube elongation in the style.
STEP 2 Embryo Sac Entry
Micropylar Entry & Synergid Penetration
Pollen tube reaches ovule and enters strictly through the Micropyle (Porogamy). The Filiform Apparatus of synergids directs the tube into the cytoplasm of one degenerating synergid. The tube tip ruptures, releasing both non-motile male gametes.
Synergid Fate: One synergid degenerates upon tube penetration; the second synergid degenerates soon after syngamy is initiated.
STEP 3 Generative Fertilization
Syngamy: Zygote (2n) Formation
One male gamete ($n$) migrates toward the egg cell and fuses with its nucleus, completing Syngamy. Results in a diploid single-celled Zygote (2n) at the micropylar pole. Zygote normally remains dormant until some endosperm is formed.
Genetic Equation: Male Gamete ($n$) + Egg Cell Nucleus ($n$) → Diploid Zygote ($2n$). Embryo progenitor.
STEP 4 Vegetative Fertilization
Triple Fusion: PEN (3n) Formation
Second male gamete ($n$) moves to central cell and fuses with the 2 Polar Nuclei (n + n). Because three haploid nuclei fuse, it is called Triple Fusion. Forms the triploid Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN, 3n), and central cell becomes PEC.
Nawaschin's Landmark: Syngamy + Triple Fusion = Double Fertilization, a universal signature unique to Angiosperms.
STEP 5 Nutritive Engine
Endosperm Ontogeny & Seed Types
Endosperm development strictly precedes embryo development. PEN divides mitotically to form free nuclei (Free-nuclear endosperm, e.g. coconut water). Later, cytokinesis forms walls (Cellular endosperm, e.g. white coconut meat).
Seed Classification: • Non-albuminous: Endosperm fully consumed (Pea, Groundnut, Bean).
• Albuminous: Endosperm persists in seed (Castor, Wheat, Maize).
STEP 6 Morphogenesis & Organ Fate
Embryogeny & Floral Fate Map
Zygote develops: Proembryo → Globular → Heart-shaped → Mature Embryo. Surrounding floral organs undergo coordinated post-fertilization metamorphosis:
Ovary → Fruit (Pericarp)
Ovule → Seed
Integuments → Seed Coat (Testa/Tegmen)
Nucellus → Perisperm (e.g. Beet, Pepper)
DOUBLE FERTILIZATION FORMULA: Syngamy (1 sperm + 1 egg = 2n Zygote) + Triple Fusion (1 sperm + 2 polar nuclei = 3n PEN) 2 Synergid Discharges
CHRONOLOGICAL RULE: Endosperm develops before embryo to secure nutritional survival Essential Adaptation
SEED COAT: Outer Integument → Testa; Inner → Tegmen; Micropyle remains as pore Protective Envelope
