1. Responses & Adaptations

Physiology & Ecology
Four Organismic Responses:
• Regulate: Maintain homeostasis (endothermy/osmoregulation; mammals, birds).
• Conform: Internal state varies with ambient environment (>99% animals, plants).
• Migrate: Keoladeo National Park (Bharatpur) hosts migratory birds from Siberia.
• Suspend: Hibernation (bears in winter), Aestivation (snails/fish in summer), Diapause (suspended development in zooplankton).
Morphological & Physiological Adaptations:
• Kangaroo Rat: Meets 100% water requirement via internal fat oxidation; concentrated urine.
• Desert Plants (CAM): Sunken stomata, thick cuticle; _Opuntia_ flattened green stem (phylloclade) with spine leaves.
• Allen's Rule: Mammals in colder climates possess shorter ears and limbs to minimize heat loss.
• Altitude Sickness: Acclimation via elevated erythropoietin $arrow$ increased RBC count, higher breathing rate, reduced hemoglobin binding affinity.
Trap: Allen's rule applies to extremities (ears/limbs); Blubber in seals provides thermal insulation.

2. Population Attributes

Demography
Individual vs Population Parameters:
• Individuals have births/deaths; Populations have Natality ($B$) and Mortality ($D$) rates (per capita).
• Sex Ratio: Percentage of males and females in a population.
Age Pyramids (Shape & Status):
• Expanding (Triangular): High proportion of pre-reproductive individuals.
• Stable (Bell-shaped): Pre-reproductive and reproductive cohorts are equal.
• Declining (Urn-shaped): Pre-reproductive cohort smaller than reproductive cohort.
Population Density Estimation:
• Direct biomass/percent cover (e.g., single massive Banyan tree vs 200 _Parthenium_ weeds).
• Indirect census: Pugmarks and fecal pellets used for Tiger census in National Parks.
Trap: Absolute numerical count is NOT always meaningful (Banyan tree percent cover dominates!).

3. Population Growth Dynamics

Mathematical Models
Density Fluctuation Equation:
$N_{t+1} = N_t + [(B + I) - (D + E)]$
Natality ($B$) & Immigration ($I$) increase density; Mortality ($D$) & Emigration ($E$) decrease density.
Exponential Growth (J-Curve):
• Condition: Unlimited resources.
• Equation: $d N / d t = r N$ | Integral: $N_t = N_0 e^(r t)$
• $r$ = Intrinsic rate of natural increase (Flour beetle $0.12$; Norway rat $0.015$). Unrealistic in nature.
Logistic Growth (Verhulst-Pearl S-Curve):
• Condition: Limited resources with Carrying Capacity ($K$).
• Equation: $d N / d t = r N ((K - N) / K)$
• Phases: Lag $arrow$ Acceleration $arrow$ Deceleration $arrow$ Asymptote ($N = K$). Realist model.
Trap: When $N$ reaches $K$, environmental resistance $((K-N)/K)$ becomes zero and growth stops!

4. Interspecific Interactions

Ecological Web
Interaction Matrix & Classic Examples:
• Mutualism (+/+): Lichens, Mycorrhizae, Fig-Wasp, _Ophrys_ sexual deceit.
• Competition (-/-): Abingdon tortoise & goats; Gause's exclusion; Connell's barnacles (_Balanus_ vs _Chthamalus_); MacArthur's resource partitioning (5 warbler species).
• Predation (+/-): _Pisaster_ keystone starfish; _Cactoblastis_ moth on prickly pear; Monarch butterfly cardiac glycoside defense.
• Parasitism (+/-): Ectoparasites (_Cuscuta_, ticks, lice, copepods); Endoparasites (liver fluke); Brood parasitism (cuckoo & crow).
• Commensalism (+/0): Orchid on mango; barnacle on whale; cattle egret & grazing cattle; clownfish & sea anemone.
• Amensalism (-/0): _Penicillium_ antibiosis against bacteria; black walnut allelopathy.
Trap: Female mosquito is NOT considered a parasite because it does not live on/in host for shelter!
