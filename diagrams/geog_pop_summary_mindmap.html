People: Population & Development

Fundamentals · Unit II
1. Distribution & Density Spatial Patterns
  • George Cressey: "Asia has many places where people are few and few places where people are very many."
  • Top 10 Populous Nations: Account for ~60% of humanity; 6 located in Asia (China, India, Indonesia, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Russia).
  • Arithmetic Density: Total Population / Total Land Area (persons/km²).
  • High vs Low Density: High: NW Europe, East/South Asia; Sparse: Polar biomes, hyper-arid deserts, high altitudes.
2. Distribution Determinants Drivers
  • Geographical: Fresh water availability, flat river plains (Ganga, Nile), moderate Mediterranean climate, fertile loamy soils.
  • Minerals: Katanga-Zambia copper belt (Africa) attracting mining settlements.
  • Urban & Industrial: Kobe-Osaka region (Japan) and Tokyo megalopolis offering employment, services, and agglomeration.
  • Socio-Cultural: Religious centers (Varanasi, Jerusalem) and political stability attracting in-migration.
3. Components of Change Demodynamics
  • Natural Growth: Births minus Deaths; Actual Growth: (Births - Deaths) + (In-migrants - Out-migrants).
  • Crude Birth/Death Rates: Live births or deaths per 1,000 population per year (CBR, CDR).
  • Push Factors: Unemployment, poor living standards, political turmoil, droughts, famines.
  • Pull Factors: Better job opportunities, higher wages, peace, stable climate, educational amenities.
4. Demographic Transition Theory
  • Progression: Rural, agrarian, illiterate stage to urban, industrial, literate stage.
  • Stage 1 (High Fluctuating): High birth & death rates, low life expectancy, primitive agriculture (rainforest tribes).
  • Stage 2 (Expanding): High birth rate + plunging death rate due to sanitation/medicine (Kenya, Nigeria, Sri Lanka).
  • Stage 3 (Low Fluctuating): Both rates low, aging society, zero/slow growth (Japan, Canada, Western Europe).
5. Composition & Pyramids Age-Sex
  • Triangular Pyramid (Expanding): Wide base, rapid fertility, youthful population (Nigeria, Bangladesh, Mexico).
  • Bell-Shaped Pyramid (Constant): Tapered top with equal birth and death rates (Australia).
  • Narrow-Base Pyramid (Declining): Low birth rates and bulging top, high aging dependency (Japan).
  • Sex Ratio: World (Males per 1,000 females); India (Females per 1,000 males).
6. Human Development Paradigms
  • Pioneers: Dr. Mahbub-ul-Haq (expanding choices) & Prof. Amartya Sen (capability expansion).
  • 4 Pillars: Equity, Sustainability, Productivity, Empowerment.
  • 4 Approaches: Income, Welfare, Basic Needs (ILO: 6 needs), Capability.
  • 3 Dimensions (1/3 each): Life expectancy, Schooling (Mean/Expected), GNI per capita (PPP$).
  • Global Tiers: Very High (>0.800), High (0.700–0.799), Medium (0.550–0.699), Low (<0.550).
Core Conceptual Synthesis: Population is the true dynamic resource of the Earth; human development transforms mere numbers into empowered, healthy, and productive citizens.
