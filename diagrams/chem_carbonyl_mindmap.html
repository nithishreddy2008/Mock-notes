ALDEHYDES, KETONES & CARBOXYLIC ACIDS

The Carbonyl Family & Names

C=O: sp² carbon, ~122 pm, strongly polar — C is the electrophile, O the nucleophile
Names: -al (ethanal), -one (propanone), -oic acid; the aldehyde C is always C-1
Aldehyde < ketone in reactivity: two alkyl groups crowd and feed the ketone C; aldehyde has only one (+H)
Acid family: -COOH is resonance-stabilised; its OH is acidic, its OH carbon is NOT a carbonyl target

Preparation Board

Aldehydes: 1° alcohol oxidation (PCC stops at CHO); Rosenmund (acid chloride + H₂/Pd-BaSO₄); Etard (toluene + CrO₂Cl₂); Gattermann-Koch (CO + HCl/AlCl₃)
Ketones: 2° alcohol oxidation; acid chloride + dialkylcadmium; from nitriles; ozonolysis; alkyne + water (HgSO₄)
Acids: oxidation of 1° alcohols/aldehydes, nitrile hydrolysis, Grignard + CO₂, HVZ halogenation, ester/amide hydrolysis

Aldehyde & Ketone Chemistry

Additions: HCN (cyanohydrin), NaHSO₃ (bisulphite adduct), Grignard, ROH → acetals; NH₃ derivatives (oxime, hydrazone, 2,4-DNP)
Reductions: LiAlH₄/NaBH₄ → alcohols; Clemmensen (Zn-Hg/HCl); Wolff-Kishner (N₂H₄/KOH)
Oxidation: aldehydes easy (Tollen's, Fehling's); ketones resist
α-chemistry: haloform (methyl ketones), aldol (α-H), cross-aldol, Cannizzaro (no α-H)

Physical & the H-Bond Story

Boiling: alkane < carbonyl (polar) < alcohol (H-bonds to itself)
Solubility: small carbonyls dissolve (O accepts water H-bonds); big chains sink; benzaldehyde barely mixes
Acids boil highest of all — two-point H-bonding pairs molecules into dimers
Smell: aldehydes sweet (vanillin, cinnamaldehyde); ketones solvents (acetone)

Acid Chemistry

Acidity: stronger than phenol/alcohol; EWG groups push it up (ClCH₂COOH < Cl₂CHCOOH < Cl₃CCOOH); alkyl groups pull it down; F > Cl > Br > I
Neutralisations: NaOH, NaHCO₃ (CO₂ fizz — the test), Na₂CO₃
Derivatives: esterification (alcohol C–O intact), acid chloride (SOCl₂), anhydride (P₂O₅), amide (NH₃, heat)
Special: HVZ α-halogenation (needs red P), decarboxylation (soda lime), LiAlH₄ → 1° alcohol

Uses That Anchor Questions

Formalin (40% HCHO): disinfectant, preservative of biological specimens; bakelite via phenol + HCHO
Acetone: nail-polish remover, solvent
Acetic acid: vinegar (5-6%), cellulose acetate, aspirin synthesis
Benzaldehyde: almond scent, perfumes, dyes

WATCH-OUTS THAT SET MCQ TRAPS

Rosenmund stops at CHO — BaSO₄ poisons the catalyst; aldehyde cannot over-reduce. HCHO needs a different route
Fehling's says no to benzaldehyde — aromatic aldehydes pass Tollen's but fail Fehling's
HCOOH is both an acid AND an aldehyde — passes Tollen's and Fehling's
Aldol needs α-H, Cannizzaro needs NO α-H — the two disproportionation routes are mutually exclusive
