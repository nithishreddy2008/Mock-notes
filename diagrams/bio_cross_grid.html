1. Classical Mendelian Crosses

PUNNETT MATRICES
Monohybrid Cross ($T t \times T t$) Cross studying a single gene locus (Plant height in Pisum sativum).
• Phenotypic Ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf (3:1)
• Genotypic Ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1:2:1)
TEST CROSS: 1:1
Dihybrid Cross ($R r Y y \times R r Y y$) Simultaneous segregation of two unlinked seed traits (Shape & Color).
• Phenotypic: 9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green
• Dihybrid Genotypic: 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1 (9 distinct genotypes)
DIHYBRID TEST CROSS: 1:1:1:1
Mendelian Postulates 1. Law of Dominance: Discrete factors occur in pairs; one dominates.
2. Law of Segregation: Alleles separate cleanly in anaphase I (purity of gametes; universal with no exceptions).
3. Law of Independent Assortment: Unlinked gene pairs assort independently.
Test Cross Diagnostic: Unknown dominant phenotype ($T\_$) crossed with homozygous recessive ($t t$) to determine zygosity.

2. Non-Mendelian Extensions

ALLELIC INTERACTIONS
Incomplete Dominance (Snapdragon / Dog flower) In Antirrhinum majus & Mirabilis jalapa, red ($R R$) $\times$ white ($r r$) yields pink ($R r$). Neither allele completely dominates.
PHENOTYPIC = GENOTYPIC = 1:2:1
Co-Dominance (ABO Blood Groups in Humans) Controlled by gene I with 3 alleles: $I^A, I^B, i$. Both $I^A$ and $I^B$ express distinct surface sugar antigens equally in heterozygote $I^A I^B$ (Blood Group AB).
• Population has 6 genotypes and 4 phenotypes (A, B, AB, O).
Pleiotropy (Single Gene $\to$ Multiple Traits) A single mutated gene influences multiple unrelated phenotypic traits.
• Phenylketonuria (PKU): Defective phenylalanine hydroxylase causes mental retardation, hair & skin hypopigmentation.
• Sickle-Cell Anemia: $\beta$-globin point mutation causes RBC sickling, anemia, splenic fibrosis, and malaria resistance.
Polygenic Inheritance (Quantitative Genetics) Traits controlled by 3 or more additive genes (e.g., Human skin color: $A, B, C$). Phenotypic distribution shows a smooth bell-shaped Gaussian curve.
Multiple Allelism Rule: Multiple alleles (e.g., ABO blood groups) can be observed only in population studies, not in an individual (who carries at most 2 alleles).

3. Pedigree Analysis Blueprint

CLINICAL INHERITANCE
Standard Pedigree Notation • Square = Male; Circle = Female; Diamond = Sex unspecified.
• Shaded / Solid = Affected; Open = Normal.
• Horizontal bar = Mating; Double bar = Consanguineous mating.
• Arrow pointing to symbol = Proband / Propositus.
Autosomal Dominant Pattern • Trait appears in every generation without skipping.
• Affected child always has at least one affected parent.
• Equal frequency in males and females.
• NCERT Benchmark Example: Myotonic Dystrophy.
Autosomal Recessive Pattern • Trait often skips generations.
• Unaffected carrier parents ($A a \times A a$) produce affected child ($a a$) with 25% probability.
• Consanguinity increases incidence.
• NCERT Benchmark: Sickle-Cell Anemia, Thalassemia.
X-Linked Recessive Pattern • Criss-Cross Inheritance: Carrier mother transmits to 50% of sons. Affected father never transmits directly to sons; passes mutated allele to 100% of daughters (carriers).
• NCERT Benchmark: Hemophilia, Red-Green Color Blindness.
Pedigree Diagnostic Rule: If an unaffected mother and unaffected father have an affected daughter, the trait must be autosomal recessive (never X-linked recessive).
NTA CUET Core Insight: Mendel selected Pisum sativum due to 7 clear pairs of contrasting characters, bisexual self-pollinating flowers, short life cycle, and prolific seed output. Incomplete dominance does NOT support blending inheritance; parental alleles re-emerge in $F_2$.
