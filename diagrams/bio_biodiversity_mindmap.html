BIODIVERSITY & CONSERVATION
Combined diversity at all levels of biological organization: Genetic, Species & Ecological
1. Three Levels of Biodiversity
Organization
• Genetic Diversity: Diversity within a single species over its range. E.g., Rauvolfia vomitoria in Himalayas produces varying potency & concentration of reserpine; India has >50,000 genetically different rice strains and >1,000 mango varieties.
• Species Diversity: Richness of species in a given community. E.g., Western Ghats harbor a greater amphibian species diversity than the Eastern Ghats.
• Ecological Diversity: Ecosystem richness. E.g., India has deserts, rainforests, mangroves, coral reefs, wetlands, and alpine meadows compared to Norway.
2. Global & Indian Species Patterns
Biogeography
• Robert May's Global Estimate: Approximately 7 million species exist globally; only 22% have been scientifically recorded so far.
• Taxonomic Proportions: >70% of recorded species are animals, while plants (including fungi, algae, bryos, gymnos, angios) comprise ~22%.
• Insect Dominance: Insects make up >70% of all animal species (7 out of every 10 animals on Earth are insects!).
• Fungi Dominance: The number of fungi species in the world is greater than the combined total of fishes, amphibians, reptiles, and mammals!
• India (Megadiverse): 2.4% world land area, but holds 8.1% of global species diversity; >45,000 plants and >90,000 animals documented.
3. Species-Area & Ecosystem Stability
Dynamics
• Humboldt's Species-Area Law: S = C · AZ (Rectangular hyperbola) → log S = log C + Z log A (Linear slope Z).
• Slope (Z) Values: 0.1 to 0.2 for small regions regardless of taxonomic group; 0.6 to 1.2 for whole continents; 1.15 for frugivorous birds and mammals in tropical forests.
• David Tilman (Minnesota Plots): Ecosystems with higher species richness showed less year-to-year variation in total biomass and higher overall productivity.
• Paul Ehrlich's Rivet Popper: Airplane = ecosystem; rivets = species; popping rivets from wings = loss of keystone species causing catastrophic crash.
4. Biodiversity Loss & Mass Extinction
Crisis
• IUCN Red List (2004): Documents extinction of 784 species in last 500 years (338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, and 87 plant species).
• Recent Famous Extinctions: Dodo (Mauritius), Quagga (Africa), Thylacine (Australia), Steller's sea cow (Russia), and 3 tiger subspecies: Bali, Javan, Caspian.
• Current 6th Mass Extinction: Extinction rates estimated to be 100 to 1,000 times faster than pre-human background rates, entirely anthropogenic!
• Threatened Taxa: 12% bird, 23% mammal, 31% gymnosperm, and 32% amphibian species (highest vulnerability!) face extinction.
5. The "Evil Quartet" (Causes of Loss)
Extinction Drivers
• 1. Habitat Loss & Fragmentation (#1 Cause): Amazon rainforest cleared for soy & cattle; rainforests dropped from 14% to <6% of land.
• 2. Over-exploitation: Human greed leading to over-harvesting; drove Steller's sea cow and Passenger pigeon to complete extinction.
• 3. Alien Species Invasions: Nile perch caused extinction of >200 cichlid fishes in Lake Victoria; invasive weeds: Parthenium, Lantana, Eichhornia; Clarias gariepinus catfish.
• 4. Co-extinctions: Obligate mutualisms: plant-pollinator mutualisms (fig and fig wasp); host-specific parasites perish when host dies out.
6. Why & How to Conserve Biodiversity
Conservation
• Why Conserve? Narrowly utilitarian: Food, timber, >25% medicines from plants, bioprospecting; Broadly utilitarian: Amazon generates 20% of global O2, pollination, soil; Ethical: Intrinsic value & stewardship.
• In-situ (On-Site): 14 Biosphere Reserves, 90 National Parks, 448 Wildlife Sanctuaries, 34 Hotspots (Western Ghats, Indo-Burma, Himalaya), Sacred Groves.
• Ex-situ (Off-Site): Zoological parks, Botanical gardens, Cryopreservation (-196°C in liquid N2), Seed/Pollen banks, In vitro tissue culture.
• Global Summits: Earth Summit (Rio de Janeiro, 1992); World Summit (Johannesburg, South Africa, 2002; 190 countries pledged reduction).
