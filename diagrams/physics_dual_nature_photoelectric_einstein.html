Apparatus & Key Definitions
Work Function (Φ₀) & Threshold Frequency
Φ₀ = h ν₀ = h c / λ₀
Minimum energy required to liberate an electron from metal surface. Lowest for Cs (2.14 eV), highest for Pt (5.65 eV).
  • Hertz & Hallwachs Observation: Zn plate loses negative charge rapidly when irradiated with UV light due to electron emission.
  • Instantaneous Process: Time lag between incident radiation and emission is < 10⁻⁹ s (~1 ns).
  • Retarding Potential: When A is made negative w.r.t C, electrons are repelled. The potential at which photocurrent drops to zero is Stopping Potential (V₀).
4 Core Characteristic Graphs (CUET High-Yield)
1. Current vs Light Intensity
At fixed frequency > ν₀, number of emitted photoelectrons/sec ∝ light intensity.
2. Current vs V (Var. Intensity, Fixed ν)
Same V₀ for all intensities! Saturation current ∝ Intensity (I₃ > I₂ > I₁).
3. Current vs V (Var. ν, Fixed Intensity)
Higher frequency requires higher retarding potential: V₀₃ > V₀₂ > V₀₁ (ν₃ > ν₂ > ν₁).
4. Stopping Potential (V₀) vs Frequency (ν)
Universal slope = h/e for all metals! Intercept on ν-axis gives threshold frequency ν₀.
Crucial CUET takeaway: The slope of V₀ vs ν graph is independent of metal nature (always h/e). Different metals give parallel lines shifted along frequency axis!
Einstein's Equation & Wave Theory
Einstein's Photoelectric Equation
K_max = h ν - Φ₀ = e V₀
K_max = h (ν - ν₀) = h c (1/λ - 1/λ₀)
Photon Property Value / Relation
Energy (E) E = h ν = h c / λ
Momentum (p) p = E / c = h / λ
Rest Mass (m₀) Strictly zero (m₀ = 0)
Speed in Vacuum c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s
Electric Charge Neutral (not deflected in E, B)
Why Wave Theory Failed:
  • Intensity independence: Wave theory predicted K_max ∝ Intensity. Experiment showed K_max depends solely on frequency ν.
  • Threshold Frequency: Wave theory allowed emission at any frequency given enough intensity. Experiment found zero emission below ν₀.
  • Time Lag: Wave theory calculated minutes to hours to absorb enough wavefront energy. Experiment showed instantaneous emission (<10⁻⁹ s).
Nobel Prize (1921): Albert Einstein was awarded the Nobel Prize specifically for the discovery of the law of photoelectric effect, establishing photon quantization.
