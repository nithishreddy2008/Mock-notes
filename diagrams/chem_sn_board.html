SN2 — Bimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution

Single Concerted Step
Reaction Coordinate
Backside attack opposite to leaving group. Passes through a single pentacoordinate transition state [Nu···C···X]‡. No intermediate.
Rate = k [R-X] [Nu⁻]  ·  Overall Order = 2  ·  Molecularity = 2
Substrate Reactivity
CH₃X > 1° > 2° >> 3° (Steric crowding around α-carbon blocks backside approach. 3° halides are virtually inert).
Stereochemistry
100% Inversion of Configuration (Walden Inversion, umbrella flipping inside out). Chiral carbon inverts from (R) to (S) or vice-versa.
Nucleophile & Solvent
Requires strong, unhindered nucleophile (OH⁻, CN⁻, I⁻, CH₃O⁻) and polar aprotic solvent (Acetone, DMSO, DMF, acetonitrile; do not solvate/cage the nucleophile).
Leaving Group Effect
Reactivity order: R-I > R-Br > R-Cl >> R-F (Governed by C-X bond dissociation enthalpy and conjugate base stability: I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻).

SN1 — Unimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution

Two Distinct Steps
Reaction Coordinate
Step 1 (Slow/RDS): Heterolytic C-X cleavage yields planar sp² carbocation intermediate. Step 2 (Fast): Nucleophile attacks from either face.
Rate = k [R-X]  ·  Overall Order = 1  ·  Molecularity = 1
Substrate Reactivity
3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃X (Stabilized by hyperconjugation and +I effects). Allylic and benzylic halides react rapidly due to resonance-stabilized carbocations.
Stereochemistry
Racemisation (Frontside + backside attack on planar carbocation). Often slight excess of inversion due to leaving-group intimate ion-pair shielding.
Nucleophile & Solvent
Weak, neutral nucleophile is sufficient (H₂O, ROH). Favored by polar protic solvent (Water, Methanol, Formic acid; solvates both carbocation and leaving halide ion).
Rearrangements
Possible! Carbocation intermediate can undergo 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts to form a more stable carbocation before nucleophilic attack.

● Ambidentate Nucleophile Strategy

Cyanide vs Isocyanide: Ionic KCN attacks via carbon → R-CN (alkanenitrile). Covalent AgCN coordinates via nitrogen lone pair → R-NC (isocyanide).
Nitrite vs Nitro: Ionic KNO₂ gives R-ONO (alkyl nitrite) via oxygen. Covalent AgNO₂ gives R-NO₂ (nitroalkane) via nitrogen.

▲ Substitution vs Elimination (E2)

Base vs Nucleophile: Strong, unhindered base (OH⁻) in water favours SN2. Hot alcoholic KOH (C₂H₅O⁻/C₂H₅OH) acts as a strong base, extracting β-hydrogen → E2 dehydrohalogenation.
Substrate effect: 3° halides with hot base undergo exclusive E2 elimination over substitution.

■ Saytzeff (Zaitsev) Regiochemistry

Saytzeff's Rule: Dehydrohalogenation of unsymmetrical alkyl halides preferentially eliminates β-hydrogen from the adjacent carbon with fewer hydrogens, yielding the more substituted, more stable alkene (e.g. 2-bromobutane → but-2-ene (80%) major over but-1-ene (20%)).
