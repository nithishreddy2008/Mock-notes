SN2 vs SN1 — THE TWO WAYS A NUCLEOPHILE KICKS OUT A HALIDE
Same ingredients (R–X + nucleophile), completely different choreography, rate laws and stereochemistry

SN2 — ONE CONCERTED STEP (backside attack)

MechanismNucleophile attacks the carbon from the side opposite to X while C–X breaks — one single step through a pentacoordinate transition state (carbon briefly has 5 neighbours, half-bonds to Nu and X).
Nu⁻ +  R–X → Nu…C…X‫‡‬ → Nu–R + X⁻
Rate lawRate = k[R–X][Nu⁻] — second order overall: both reactants appear in the rate equation, because the slow step IS the only step.
StereochemistryComplete INVERSION of configuration — the umbrella flips inside out (Walden inversion). A chiral R–X gives the opposite enantiomer.
Reactivity orderCH₃X > 1° > 2° >> 3° — backside access decides; bulky groups block the attack path. Sterics rule.
Best conditionsStrong nucleophile, polar APROTIC solvent (acetone, DMF — solvents that do not hydrogen-bond-cage the nucleophile), unhindered substrate.

SN1 — TWO STEPS (carbocation first)

MechanismStep 1 (slow): C–X breaks alone → planar sp² carbocation + X⁻. Step 2 (fast): nucleophile grabs the cation from either face.
R–X → R⁺ + X⁻   (slow, rate-determining)  ;   R⁺ + Nu⁻ → R–Nu
Rate lawRate = k[R–X] — first order: the nucleophile is absent from the rate law because it arrives after the slow step.
StereochemistryAttack on the flat cation from both faces → RACEMISATION (mostly inversion + some retention, because the leaving X⁻ partially shields one face).
Reactivity order3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃X — carbocation stability decides (hyperconjugation, +I). Benzyl and allyl halides race too.
Best conditionsWeaker nucleophile is fine, polar PROTIC solvent (water, ethanol — it stabilises the ions formed in step 1).

Choose the mechanism

3° substrate + weak nucleophile + protic solvent → S⁽1.  Methyl/1° substrate + strong nucleophile + aprotic solvent → S⁽2. 2° can go either way.

Elimination competes

With a BASE (esp. hot, alcoholic KOH), elimination (E2, Saytzeff's more-substituted alkene) beats substitution — bulky bases tip it further.

Ambidentate nucleophiles

CN⁻ attacks through C → R–CN (nitrile); through N → R–NC (isocyanide). NO₂⁻ gives nitro (R–NO₂) via N, nitrite (R–O–N=O) via O.
