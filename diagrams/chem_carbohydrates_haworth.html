1. Glucose Open-Chain Proof Chain
Structure Proof
1. Straight 6-Carbon Backbone: Prolonged heating with HI / P, Δ yields n-hexane, confirming an unbranched six-carbon chain.
2. Carbonyl Functionality: Reacts with HCN to form a cyanohydrin and with NH₂OH to form an oxime, confirming presence of >C=O.
3. Aldehyde Group Location: Mild oxidation with Bromine water (Br₂ / H₂O) oxidises glucose to Gluconic acid (6-carbon monocarboxylic acid) $\implies$ carbonyl is an aldehyde at C-1.
4. Five Hydroxyl (-OH) Groups: Acetylation with (CH₃CO)₂O forms a stable glucose pentaacetate, confirming 5 -OH groups on distinct carbons.
5. Primary Alcohol at C-6: Vigorous oxidation with conc. HNO₃ yields Saccharic acid (dicarboxylic acid), proving a primary terminal -CH₂OH at C-6.
CUET Trap: Bromine water oxidises CHO $\to$ COOH (Gluconic acid), whereas conc. HNO₃ oxidises BOTH CHO and primary CH₂OH $\to$ COOH (Saccharic acid).
2. D/L Configuration Convention
Stereochemistry
Reference Standard: Assigned relative to the lowest asymmetric stereocenter (penultimate carbon, C-5 in hexoses) compared to glyceraldehyde:
  • D-Series: -OH at C-5 lies on the RIGHT in standard Fischer projection.
  • L-Series: -OH at C-5 lies on the LEFT in standard Fischer projection.
Crucial Distinction: D and L designate spatial configuration, NOT optical rotation! Optical activity is denoted by (+) for dextro and (-) for laevo, determined experimentally. Natural glucose is D-(+)-glucose.

4. D-Fructose: The Ketohexose
Ketohexose Furanose
Formula & Nature: C₆H₁₂O₆, a ketohexose possessing a ketone group at C-2 and D-configuration at C-5. Natural fructose is strongly laevorotatory: D-(-)-fructose.
Furanose Ring: Forms a 5-membered cyclic hemiketal ring between C-2 ketone and C-5 hydroxyl.
Reducing Mystery Solved: Fructose contains a ketone yet reduces Tollens' and Fehling's reagents! Under alkaline test conditions, it undergoes keto-enol tautomerism (Lobry de Bruyn-van Ekenstein transformation) via an enediol intermediate to aldoses (glucose/mannose).

Invert Sugar Phenomenon: Sucrose is dextrorotatory ($+66.5^\circ$). Upon acid/invertase hydrolysis, it gives equimolar D-(+)-glucose ($+52.7^\circ$) and D-(-)-fructose ($-92.4^\circ$). Because the negative rotation of fructose is far higher than the positive rotation of glucose, the resulting mixture is laevorotatory ($-39.9^\circ$). Hence termed Invert Sugar!
