POLITICS OF PLANNED DEVELOPMENT
POLITICS IN INDIA SINCE INDEPENDENCE · CH. 2

What "Development" Meant

The Left (Nehru): planning, heavy industry, a socialistic pattern — the state leads growth
The Right (Rajaji, later Swantantra): free economy, small state — planning breeds permits and queues
The Gandhian view: villages, handicrafts and decentralisation before steel mills
Bombay Plan 1944-45: big industrialists THEMSELVES asked for state-led industry — the consensus was wide

The Planning Machinery

Planning Commission 1950: executive resolution, advisory; the PM chairs it — Nehru the first chairman
NDC 1952: PM + Chief Ministers approve plans — the federal seal
Five-year rhythm: targets, outlays, reviews — the plan becomes the state's clock
First Plan 1951-56: agriculture, Harrod-Domar — target ~2.1%, achieved ~3.6%

The Industrial Turn

Avadi 1955: Congress adopts "socialistic pattern of society"
Mahalanobis model: Second Plan 1956-61 — machines that make machines
IPR 1956: Schedule A (17 industries, state exclusive), B (state-led), C (private under licence)
Licence raj begins: every licence = a permit, a queue, an inspector — and a favour

State Sector Expansion

Steel with many flags: Bhilai-USSR, Rourkela-West Germany, Durgapur-UK — non-alignment in concrete
Takeovers: Imperial Bank → SBI (1955); life insurance → LIC (1956)
Public sector becomes the economy's spine: dams, plants, railways, research labs
The costs: shortages, queues, weak competition — inefficiency freezes in

Food Crisis to Green Revolution

1957-58: foreign-exchange crisis — machinery AND food imports together
PL-480: American wheat against rupees keeps the republic fed
Land reforms stall: state governments drag their feet — rural elites block
1966: HYV + irrigation + fertiliser = Green Revolution — famine fear ends in Punjab-Haryana-west UP

New Economic Interests

Big industry: licences made fortunes — then complaints made Swatantra
Rich farmers: irrigation and subsidies build a rural lobby that plans must answer
Organised workers: public-sector unions bargain — and strike — with the plan state
The pattern: every expansion of the state creates a group organised around it

Traps that catch most students

Harrod-Domar vs Mahalanobis: agriculture's First Plan used Harrod-Domar; industry's Second used Mahalanobis — never swap
Planning Commission 1950 ≠ NDC 1952: two bodies, two years, two jobs — commission plans, council approves
IPR 1956 has THREE schedules: A exclusive-state (17 industries), B state-led, C licensed private — not two
Bombay Plan ≠ communist plan: it came from INDUSTRIALISTS (Tata-Birla circle) asking for state-led industry
