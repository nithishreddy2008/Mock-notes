Three Challenges & Partition Trauma
Foundational Trials of Independent India (1947)
Three Foundational Challenges National Goals
  • Challenge 1 (Unity in Diversity): Forge a united nation accommodating enormous continental linguistic, cultural, and religious heterogeneity.
  • Challenge 2 (Democratic Institutionalization): Establish representative parliamentary democracy based on universal adult franchise.
  • Challenge 3 (Egalitarian Development): Ensure welfare and economic development of all citizens, eradicating mass poverty (Directive Principles).
Partition & Displacement Trauma Cataclysm
  • Two-Nation Theory: Propounded by Muslim League (Jinnah); opposed by Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ("Frontier Gandhi").
  • Territorial Bifurcation: Radcliffe Line divided Punjab and Bengal; non-contiguous East and West Pakistan.
  • Over 80 lakh refugees displaced; 5 to 10 lakh lives lost in communal massacres; massive atrocities against women ("honor killings").
Mahatma Gandhi's Martyrdom 30 Jan 1948
  • Undertook fasts unto death in Calcutta (Sept 1947) and Delhi (Jan 1948) to halt communal rioting.
  • Assassinated by Nathuram Godse on 30 January 1948; prompted banning of RSS.
2
Integration of Princely States
Sardar Patel, V.P. Menon & 565 Princely Polities
The Instrument of Accession Legal Framework
  • British lapse of paramountcy created potential "balkanisation" into 565 independent states.
  • Patel (Home Minister) and V.P. Menon persuaded rulers to sign the Instrument of Accession, ceding Defense, External Affairs, and Communications.
Four Complex Integration Case Studies Accords & Police Actions
  • Hyderabad (Operation Polo): Nizam wanted independence; unleashed communal militia (Razakars led by Kasim Razvi). Indian Army entered in Sept 1948 to liberate the state.
  • Junagadh: Nawab acceded to Pakistan despite Hindu majority; popular rebellion forced Nawab to flee; Plebiscite (Feb 1948) ratified merger with India.
  • Jammu & Kashmir: Maharaja Hari Singh signed Instrument of Accession on 26 October 1947 following Pakistani tribal invasion; special status under Article 370.
  • Manipur: Maharaja Bodhchandra Singh held first Indian election based on universal adult franchise in June 1948; merged in Sept 1949.
3
Linguistic Reorganisation of States
From Nagpur 1920 to SRC 1956 & Subsequent Bifurcations
The Andhra Movement & Martyrdom Historic Catalyst
  • Nagpur Congress (1920): Promised linguistic reorganization of provincial Congress committees.
  • Post-1947 leadership feared linguistic states would trigger national balkanisation.
  • Veteran Gandhian Potti Sriramulu died on 15 December 1952 after a 58-day fast; forced Nehru to announce Andhra State in Dec 1952 (carved from Madras).
States Reorganisation Commission (SRC) 1953–1956
  • Appointed in Dec 1953; headed by Justice Fazal Ali, with members K.M. Panikkar and H.N. Kunzru.
  • Recommended linguistic reorganization; led to the States Reorganisation Act, 1956 creating 14 States and 6 Union Territories.
Chronology of Later Statehoods Bifurcations
  • 1960: Bombay bifurcated into Maharashtra and Gujarat.
  • 1963: Nagaland carved out of Assam.
  • 1966: Punjab bifurcated into Punjab, Haryana, and Himachal Pradesh UT.
  • 1972: Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura; 1987: Mizoram, Arunachal, Goa.
  • 2000: Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand; 2014: Telangana.
