Geometric Intercept Elasticity Rules

  • Origin Intercept ($e_s = 1$): Any straight-line supply curve passing through the origin has unitary elasticity ($e_s = 1$) at EVERY point, regardless of its slope angle!
  • Price Axis Intercept ($e_s > 1$): Any supply curve whose straight-line extension cuts the vertical (price) axis is elastic ($e_s > 1$).
  • Quantity Axis Intercept ($e_s < 1$): Any supply curve cutting the horizontal (quantity) axis is inelastic ($e_s < 1$).

Movement Along vs. Shift in Supply

  • Change in Quantity Supplied (Movement Along): Caused strictly by a change in the commodity's OWN price ($P$). Upward movement = Expansion; Downward = Contraction.
  • Change in Supply (Shift of Curve): Caused by non-price parameters:
    • Rightward Shift (Increase in Supply): Drop in input prices, technological progress, government subsidies, reduction in excise tax.
    • Leftward Shift (Decrease in Supply): Rise in input wages, outdated tech, imposition of per-unit tax.

CUET Exam Trap: Slope vs. Elasticity

Fatal Trap: Believing a steeper supply curve is automatically less elastic. Reality: Two supply lines passing through the origin at 30° and 75° angles BOTH have identical elasticity $e_s = 1.0$ at every point! Elasticity is determined by the axis intercept, not the visual steepness!
