Energy Bands in Solids
Band Gap Parameters (at 300 K)
Si: E_g = 1.1 eV  |  Ge: E_g = 0.7 eV  |  GaAs: 1.42 eV
At 0 K, semiconductors behave as perfect insulators (CB completely empty, VB completely full).
  • Valence Band (VB): Contains valence electrons responsible for interatomic covalent bonding.
  • Conduction Band (CB): Band of free mobile states above VB. Electrons here drift under electric fields.
  • Forbidden Gap ($E_g$): Energy difference between bottom of CB ($E_c$) and top of VB ($E_v$). No electron states allowed here!
Intrinsic vs Extrinsic Semiconductors

Mass Action Law (At Constant Temperature)
n_e · n_h = n_i²
Valid for both intrinsic ($n_e = n_h = n_i$) and extrinsic semiconductors in thermal equilibrium!
Common CUET Confusion: A doped semiconductor (n-type or p-type) carries zero net electric charge. Dopant atoms are neutral before substitution; after donation/acceptance, mobile charges balance fixed parent ions!
Conductivity & Temp Scaling
Total Electric Current & Conductivity
I = I_e + I_h = e A (n_e v_e + n_h v_h)
σ = e (n_e μ_e + n_h μ_h) = 1 / ρ
$\mu_e, \mu_h$ are electron & hole mobilities. In Si/Ge, $\mu_e > \mu_h$ always!

Carrier Density Constant ($n \sim 10^{28}\text{ m}^{-3}$) $n_i \propto T^{3/2} e^{-E_g / (2 k_B T)}$
Why Electrons are Faster than Holes:
Electrons move in the empty conduction band freely, while holes move via step-by-step bound valence-electron transitions into adjacent vacancies. Thus $\mu_e \approx 2\text{ to }3 \times \mu_h$.
