COORDINATION COMPOUNDS

Ligand-to-Metal Dative Coordinate Bonding

1. Werner's Coordination Theory

Foundations
Dual Valencies: Metals exhibit Primary (ionisable, oxidation number, non-directional) and Secondary (non-ionisable, coordination number, directional).
Precipitation Logic: Only counter-ions outside square brackets precipitate with AgNO₃. E.g. [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ gives 2 mol AgCl; inner Cl⁻ is bound.
Molar Conductivity: Follows total ion count: [Co(NH₃)₆]Cl₃ (4 ions) > [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂ (3 ions) > [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]Cl (2 ions) > [Co(NH₃)₃Cl₃] (0).
Stereochemical Shapes: Directional secondary bonds define fixed 3D shapes: Octahedral (CN=6), Tetrahedral or Square Planar (CN=4).

2. Nomenclature & Ligands

IUPAC Rules
Ligand Denticity: Monodentate (Cl⁻, NH₃), Bidentate (ox²⁻, en), Polydentate (hexadentate edta⁴⁻). Chelating ligands form stable rings (chelate effect).
Ambidentate Ligands: Can coordinate via two different donor atoms: NO₂⁻ (nitro) / ONO⁻ (nitrito-O); SCN⁻ (thiocyanato) / NCS⁻ (isothiocyanato).
Naming Order: Cation first, then anion. Inside entity: ligands alphabetically before metal. Anionic ligands end in -o (chlorido, cyanido).
Anionic Metal Suffix: If complex sphere is negative, metal name ends in -ate (ferrate, cuprate, cobaltate) + Roman numeral oxidation state in parentheses.

3. Coordination Isomerism

Structural & Stereo
Structural: Ionisation ([Co(NH₃)₅SO₄]Br vs [Co(NH₃)₅Br]SO₄), Hydrate ([Cr(H₂O)₆]Cl₃ vs [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O), Linkage, and Coordination isomers.
Geometrical (Cis/Trans): Cis-platin (anti-cancer) vs trans-platin; octahedral [Ma₄b₂] cis (violet) vs trans (green).
Fac/Mer Isomerism: Seen in [Ma₃b₃] octahedral complexes. Facial = 3 identical ligands on one face (all 90°); Meridional = around a meridian plane.
Optical (Chirality): Dextro/laevo enantiomers without plane of symmetry. [Co(en)₃]³⁺ and cis-[PtCl₂(en)₂]²⁺ are chiral; trans is achiral (meso).

4. Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

Hybridisation
Hybridisation Types: sp³ (Tetrahedral, [NiCl₄]²⁻), dsp² (Square Planar, [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻), sp³d² (Outer Octahedral), d²sp³ (Inner Octahedral).
Inner vs Outer Complex: Strong ligands force electron pairing to free inner (n−1)d orbitals → d²sp³ (low spin). Weak ligands use nd orbitals → sp³d² (high spin).
Magnetic Moment: μ = √(n(n+2)) BM. Diamagnetic when all paired (e.g. [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻); paramagnetic when unpaired (e.g. [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺, μ=4.90 BM).
VBT Failures: Cannot explain why splitting differs between ligands, cannot predict spectra/colours, treats bonding as purely covalent.

5. Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

Orbital Physics
Octahedral Splitting: Axial ligands split 3d into lower t₂g (−0.4 Δo) and higher eg (+0.6 Δo). Gap is Δo (10 Dq).
Tetrahedral Splitting: Inverted: lower e (−0.6 Δt), upper t₂ (+0.4 Δt). Δt ≈ (4/9) Δo. Because Δt < P, complexes are always high spin.
Spin Decision (Δo vs P): If Δo < P → High Spin (weak-field, Hund's rule). If Δo > P → Low Spin (strong-field, pairing favored).
Spectrochemical Series: I⁻ < Br⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < ox²⁻ < H₂O < NCS⁻ < edta⁴⁻ < NH₃ < en < NO₂⁻ < CN⁻ < CO.

6. Stability & Applications

Real-World Chemistry
Colour Origin: d–d transitions absorb light of frequency ν = Δo/h; transmitted complementary colour is seen. d⁰ (Sc³⁺) and d¹⁰ (Zn²⁺) are colourless.
Complex Stability (β): Higher charge and smaller radius of central ion increase stability constant β. Chelate effect strongly elevates stability.
Metallurgy & Analysis: Cyanide leaching of gold/silver ([Au(CN)₂]⁻); Mond process for Ni ([Ni(CO)₄]); EDTA titrations for water hardness (Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺).
Biological Catalysts: Chlorophyll (Mg²⁺ porphyrin), Hemoglobin (Fe²⁺), Vitamin B₁₂ (Co³⁺ cyanocobalamin), Cis-platin (anti-cancer medicine).
