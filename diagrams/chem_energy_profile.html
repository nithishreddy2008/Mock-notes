ACTIVATION ENERGY — THE HILL EVERY REACTION MUST CLIMB
Energy profile of an exothermic reaction: uncatalysed path (blue) vs catalysed path (green). The hill shrinks — the start and end points never move.

Read the picture in three lines

1. Molecules must reach the top of the hill (the transition state) before they can roll down to products.  2. Ea = height of that hill measured from the reactant plateau.  3. A catalyst cuts a lower pass through the hill — it cannot move the start or end points.

Why a catalyst speeds things up

A lower hill means a much bigger fraction of molecules own enough energy to cross. In Arrhenius language, a smaller Ea makes the exponential factor e−Ea/RT larger — exponentially more successful collisions per second, so the reaction finishes sooner.

Exothermic vs endothermic

This sketch shows products lower than reactants: energy released, ΔH < 0 (exothermic). For an endothermic reaction the product plateau sits higher — but the hill-crossing story is exactly the same.
