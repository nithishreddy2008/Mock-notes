Globalisation & LPG Reforms
1991 Macroeconomic Crisis & Multi-Sectoral Resistance
1991 Balance of Payments Crisis Forex Crunch
  • Forex Depletion: Foreign reserves fell to $1.2 billion in June 1991, barely sufficient for 2 weeks of essential imports.
  • Emergency Measure: Caretaker government pledged 67 tonnes of gold to Bank of England and UBS.
  • Structural Reforms: PM Narasimha Rao & Finance Minister Manmohan Singh launched LPG (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation).
  • WTO Accession: India joined the World Trade Organization (WTO) as an original founding member in 1995.
2 Weeks Reserves LPG 1991
Multi-Sectoral Opposition Resistance
  • Trade Unions (CITU/AITUC): General strikes protesting worker retrenchment, PSU sell-offs, and casualisation.
  • Farmers' Movements: KRRS (Prof. M.D. Nanjundaswamy) and BKU opposed WTO TRIPS seed patents and subsidy cuts.
  • Cultural Resistance: Left and Swadeshi Jagran Manch opposed Western commercialism and consumerist ethos.
KRRS (Nanjundaswamy) TRIPS Protests
Core Fact: 1991 reforms dismantled the "Licence Raj", lowering peak tariffs and opening FDI to private/foreign enterprise.

Dalit & Backward Assertion
Electoral Power (BSP) & Militant Rights (Dalit Panthers)
Dalit Panthers (May 1972) Non-Electoral Arena
  • Origins: Formed in Bombay slums by young radical writers: Namdeo Dhasal (author of Golpitha) and J.V. Pawar.
  • Ideological Stance: Inspired by Black Panther Party; militant resistance against rural untouchability and upper-caste violence.
  • Legislative Climax: Direct struggles contributed to passing the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989.
Maharashtra 1972 Namdeo Dhasal
Kanshi Ram & The BSP Electoral Arena
  • BAMCEF (1978): Employees' federation providing financial, intellectual, and organisational backbone.
  • DS-4 (1981): Direct agitational forum before launching electoral party.
  • BSP (1984): Formed to capture state power for the 85% Bahujan majority; Mayawati became UP CM in 1995.
  • OBC Regional Formations: SP (Mulayam), RJD (Lalu), and JD(U) consolidated intermediate agrarian power.
BAMCEF 1978 BSP (1984)
CUET Distinction: BAMCEF (1978) was a non-electoral employee federation; BSP (1984) was the formal political party.

Communalism & Secularism
Ayodhya Timeline, Gujarat 2002 & Principled Distance
Ayodhya Dispute & Fallout Chronology
  • Feb 1986: Faizabad District Court ordered locks opened for Hindu worship, reigniting dispute.
  • 1990 Rath Yatra: L.K. Advani from Somnath to Ayodhya; Advani arrested in Bihar; V.P. Singh govt fell.
  • 6 Dec 1992: Demolition of Babri Masjid; CM Kalyan Singh resigned; President's Rule in 4 BJP states.
  • S.R. Bommai (1994): Supreme Court upheld dismissals, declaring secularism part of the Basic Structure.
  • Inquiry: Liberhan Commission investigated demolition; submitted report in 2009.

Indian vs Western Secularism Model
  • Western Model: Strict mutual exclusion ("wall of separation"); state neither aids nor intervenes in religion.
  • Indian Model: "Principled Distance" (Rajeev Bhargava); equal respect (Sarva Dharma Sambhava) and reform.
  • Gujarat Riots (2002): Godhra train carnage; Best Bakery case transferred by SC to Maharashtra for fair trial.
Principled Distance Best Bakery Retrial
Philosophical Core: Indian secularism protects minority educational rights (Articles 29/30) while allowing state social reform.
