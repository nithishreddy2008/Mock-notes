ANALYSIS OF FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
UNIT V · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Financial Statements of a Company

Schedule III format: company statements follow fixed major headings — the skill is placing each item under the right one
Balance Sheet heads: Shareholders' funds · Non-current liabilities · Current liabilities; Assets: Non-current · Current
Statement of P&L: revenue from operations → other income → expenses → profit before tax → tax → profit after tax
Statements show the position, not the reason — analysis supplies the why

Why Analyse? Meaning & Limits

Meaning: working through the figures systematically to judge profitability, efficiency and solvency
Significance: earning capacity · short-term & long-term solvency · operating efficiency · easy comparison
Users: management, investors, banks & lenders, employees, government
Limitations: historical figures · window dressing · ignores price changes · ignores qualitative facts

Comparative Statements

Four columns: previous year · current year · absolute change · % change
% change = absolute change ÷ previous-year figure × 100 — the base is always the earlier year
Two forms: comparative income statement (profit trend) · comparative balance sheet (position trend)
Rupee changes ignore size; % changes make years comparable

Common-Size Statements

Every item restated as a % of revenue from operations (P&L) or of the balance-sheet total
Each column totals 100 — the size of the firm stops mattering
Read vertically: which head eats the biggest slice of revenue or assets
The best tool for comparing two firms of different sizes

Accounting Ratios

Four families: Liquidity · Solvency · Activity (turnover) · Profitability
Ideal marks: current ratio 2:1 · quick ratio 1:1 (commonly quoted benchmarks)
Every ratio is one relationship: a part ÷ a whole, or a flow ÷ a balance
Turnover ratios use the average of opening & closing figures when both are given

Cash Flow Statement

Three activities: Operating · Investing · Financing
Indirect method: net profit before tax → add back non-cash items → adjust working-capital changes → tax paid
Class 12 convention: interest & dividend paid → financing; interest & dividend received → investing
Check line: net change in cash = closing cash − opening cash

COMMON ERRORS & TRAPS

Closing inventory ≠ average inventory — turnover ratios need (opening + closing) ÷ 2
Quick assets exclude inventory & prepaid expenses — the quick ratio drops them; the current ratio does not
% change on a negative base is meaningless — a profit of ₹1,00,000 to a loss of ₹50,000 is a 150% fall? No — state the reversal in words
