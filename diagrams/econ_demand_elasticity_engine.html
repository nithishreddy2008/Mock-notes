Demand Axioms
1. Law of Demand & Determinants
The Law of Demand
Pₓ ↑ ⟹ Qₓ ↓ (Ceteris Paribus)
Inverse relationship between own price and quantity demanded, assuming other factors (income, tastes, related goods prices) remain constant.
Why Demand Curve Slopes Downward
1. Law of Diminishing MU: Extra units valued less $\implies$ Bought only at lower prices.
2. Substitution Effect: Good becomes relatively cheaper than substitutes.
3. Income Effect: Real purchasing power increases as price falls.
4. New Buyers: Consumers previously priced out enter the market.
5. Multiple Uses: Put to less urgent uses as price falls.
Giffen Goods Exception
Highly inferior staple goods where negative Income Effect overwhelms positive Substitution Effect. Demand curve slopes upward! ($P \uparrow \implies Q \uparrow$).
Curve Dynamics
2. Movement along vs Shift of Demand
Movement (Change in Quantity Demanded)
Caused strictly by change in OWN PRICE of the good ($P_x$):
• Expansion of Demand: Downward movement along same curve as $P_x$ falls.
• Contraction of Demand: Upward movement along same curve as $P_x$ rises.
Shift (Change in Demand)
Caused by factors OTHER THAN own price ($P_x$ constant):
• Rightward Shift (Increase in Demand): Income rises (Normal good), Substitute price rises, Complement price falls, Favorable tastes.
• Leftward Shift (Decrease in Demand): Income falls (Normal good), Income rises (Inferior good), Substitute price falls, Complement price rises.

Measurement
3. Three Price Elasticity Methods
1. Percentage / Proportionate Method
e_d = - (ΔQ / ΔP) · (P / Q)
Exact mathematical calculation. Minus sign reflects the inverse law of demand (elasticity is quoted in absolute value).
2. Point (Geometric) Method on Linear Curve
e_d = Lower Segment / Upper Segment
• Vertical intercept: $e_d = \infty$ (Perfect elastic).
• Above midpoint: $e_d > 1$ (Elastic).
• Exactly at midpoint: $e_d = 1$ (Unitary).
• Below midpoint: $e_d < 1$ (Inelastic).
• Horizontal intercept: $e_d = 0$ (Perfect inelastic).
3. Total Outlay / Expenditure Method (Marshall)
• e_d > 1 (Elastic): Price & Total Outlay move in OPPOSITE directions ($P \uparrow \implies TE \downarrow$).
• e_d = 1 (Unitary): Total Outlay remains CONSTANT when price changes.
• e_d < 1 (Inelastic): Price & Total Outlay move in SAME direction ($P \uparrow \implies TE \uparrow$).
Income Elasticity Classifications
• Normal Luxury Goods: Income Elasticity $e_y > 1$.
• Normal Necessity Goods: $0 < e_y \le 1$.
• Inferior Goods: Income Elasticity is strictly negative ($e_y < 0$). Demand falls as income rises!
Rectangular Hyperbola Demand Curve
• Form: $P \cdot Q = \text{Constant}$.
• At every point along this curve, the price elasticity of demand is strictly unitary ($e_d = 1$).
• Total expenditure ($P \cdot Q$) is identical at all price levels!
Slope vs Elasticity Distinction
• Slope of demand curve $= \Delta P / \Delta Q$ (constant on linear curve).
• Elasticity $= (\Delta Q / \Delta P) \cdot (P / Q) = (1 / \text{Slope}) \cdot (P / Q)$.
• Flatter demand curve is more elastic at any given intersection point!
