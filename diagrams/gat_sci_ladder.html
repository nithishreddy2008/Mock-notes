THE SCIENTIFIC-LITERACY LADDER + THE CLAIM CHECKLIST
CUET UG · GENERAL APTITUDE TEST · SCIENTIFIC LITERACY & GENERAL MENTAL ABILITY
1

Observe — collect what is actually there

Facts first: what is given, what changed, what stayed fixed. No guessing yet — just the scene.

2

Question — say what you need to find

Turn the puzzle into one clear question: "Why did X happen?" or "Which claim fits the data?"

3

Explain — propose a mechanism

A good explanation says HOW it works, step by step — not just that it works.

4

Test — match the explanation to evidence

Does the mechanism fit every given fact? Does it predict what you observe? Extra assumptions = weaker claim.

5

Conclude — answer exactly what was asked

Pick the option the ladder supports — not the most dramatic or the most familiar one.

The claim checklist — before believing ANY "science" claim

Evidence: is real data offered, or only big promises?
Mechanism: can it say HOW it works in plain steps?
Source: who gains if you believe it — expertise or profit?
Alternatives: could something simpler explain the same result?
Framing: "instant", "cures everything", "no side effects" are alarm words

Worked through the ladder

Claim: "This herbal pill cures any fever in one dose, with no side effects."

Evidence? None offered — only the promise. Mechanism? Missing: fevers have many different causes, so one dose curing ALL of them has no plausible how. Framing? "Any" + "one dose" + "no side effects" — three alarm words at once. Verdict: reject until real evidence appears.

Traps that catch most students

Together ≠ because of — two things rising together is not causation
Memory over mechanism — recall the HOW, not a memorised verdict
Dramatic answers — the ladder's quiet conclusion beats the loud option
