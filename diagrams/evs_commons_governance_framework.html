PHASE 1 · HARDIN'S DILEMMA
1. Open-Access Tragedy
  • Garrett Hardin (1968): Unregulated pasture grazed by herdsmen.
  • Rational Utility Trap: Positive utility (+1) of adding another animal accrues solely to the individual herdsman.
  • Externalized Cost: Fractional negative utility (-1/n) of overgrazing is shared across the entire community.
  • Systemic Collapse: Freedom in an open unmanaged commons brings ruin to all.
CUET Trap: Hardin conflated unowned Open-Access regimes with communal Common Property Resources.
PHASE 2 · OSTROM'S DIAGNOSTICS
2. CPR Economic Taxonomy
  • Elinor Ostrom (1990): Nobel Laureate 2009 for empirical commons research.
  • High Subtractability / Rivalry: Consumption by one appropriator subtracts from available resource stock.
  • High Exclusion Cost: Extremely difficult or prohibitively expensive to fence or exclude users.
  • Empirical Evidence: Swiss alpine grazing, Spanish acequia canals, Indian irrigation tanks.
Core Invariant: Neither state top-down control nor total privatization is universally superior to community tenure.
PHASE 3 · INSTITUTIONAL DESIGN
3. Ostrom's 8 Design Principles
  • 1. Defined Boundaries: Clear delineation of users and resource boundaries.
  • 2. Local Congruence: Rules tailored to ecological conditions.
  • 3. Collective Choice: Users participate in modifying rules.
  • 4. Accountable Monitors: Audited by users themselves.
  • 5. Graduated Sanctions: Penalties escalate with repeat offenses.
  • 6. Low-Cost Conflict Resolution: Rapid local mediation.
  • 7. Minimal External Recognition: State respects autonomy.
  • 8. Nested Enterprises: Layered polycentric tiers.
Ostrom Rule: Self-organizing users sustain CPRs for centuries through robust polycentric governance.
PHASE 4 · ECOLOGICAL STEADY-STATE
4. Limits to Growth & Equity
  • Herman Daly: Steady-State Economy with constant physical capital and population stocks.
  • Biophysical Limits: Raw material throughput held strictly within ecosystem regenerative capacity.
  • Waste Assimilation: Pollution output cannot exceed biospheric absorption sinks.
  • Dual Equity Dimensions: Intergenerational (future generations) & Intragenerational equity (Global North vs South).
Brundtland Baseline: Meets present needs without compromising future generations' capacity to meet their own.
