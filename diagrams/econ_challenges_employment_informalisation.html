Employment, Labour Force & Informalisation Dynamics

Workforce Participation, Types of Workers, Unemployment Dimensions, and the Formal vs Informal Sector Divide

CLASS 12 · INDIAN ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
1. Labour Metrics & Worker Categories
Employment Structure
Worker-Population Ratio (WPR)
$WPR = (\text{Total Workers} / \text{Total Population}) \times 100$.
• Rural WPR is higher than Urban WPR (necessity to work; lower schooling years).
• Male WPR is significantly higher than Female WPR due to uncounted domestic labour.
Three Employment Categories
1. Self-Employed (~52%): Farmers, shopkeepers, own-account business owners who own and operate their enterprises.
2. Regular Salaried (~24%): Fixed monthly salaries, written employment contracts, leave & social security.
3. Casual Wage Labourers (~24%): Daily or piece-rate wages with zero job security, medical, or pension benefits.
Casualisation Trend: Progressive migration of workers away from self-employment towards daily-wage casual contract labour.
2. Unemployment & "Jobless Growth"
Structural Inefficiencies
Disguised Unemployment
Situation where more workers are engaged in an activity than actually required. The Marginal Product of Labour ($MP_L = 0$). If surplus workers are withdrawn, total output remains completely unchanged. Heavily prevalent in Indian family farming.
Other Forms of Unemployment
Seasonal: Agricultural workers idle between planting & harvesting seasons.
Open: Able and willing individuals actively seeking work at current wages but finding zero employment.
Educated: Degree holders lacking market-relevant technical skills.
The "Jobless Growth" Paradox
A trajectory where real GDP expands steadily via capital-intensive technology and skill-heavy tertiary sectors without generating a proportional expansion in formal factory jobs.
3. Formal vs Informal Sector Divide
10-Worker Legal Threshold
Formal / Organised Sector (< 10% Workers)
Criteria: All public sector enterprises + private establishments hiring 10 or more hired workers.
Protections: Guaranteed social security (Provident Fund, gratuity, pensions, maternity leave) & trade union collective bargaining rights.
Informal / Unorganised Sector (> 90% Workers)
Criteria: All agricultural labourers + private enterprises hiring fewer than 10 workers (small workshops, street vendors, domestic help).
Realities: Zero social security, hire-and-fire vulnerability, low wages, hazardous working conditions.
Informalisation Phenomenon: Even within formal corporate enterprises, permanent regular jobs are increasingly outsourced to informal contract labour to evade pension and social security liabilities!
CUET Legal Benchmark: Formal Sector = 10 or more hired workers (with social security) · Informal Sector = < 10 workers (over 90% of Indian workforce).
