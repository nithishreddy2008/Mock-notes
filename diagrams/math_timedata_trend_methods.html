TIME SERIES — COMPONENTS & TREND METHODS

CUET UG · MATHEMATICS · TIME BASED DATA
T

Trend (Secular)

Long-run direction over years — growth, decline, flat
Ex: smartphone users rising for a decade
S

Seasonal

Fixed-calendar repeats within a year
Ex: ice-cream every summer, umbrellas every monsoon
C

Cyclical

Multi-year waves, no fixed calendar — booms and recessions
Ex: business cycle up-down over 5–10 years
I

Irregular

One-off shocks — unpredictable spikes
Ex: a strike, a flood, a viral trend

1 · Freehand (Graphical)

Do: plot the points, draw a smooth line by eye through the middle.
Pros: instant, no maths. Cons: subjective — two people draw two lines.
Fits: a first look, quick reports.

2 · Semi-Averages

Do: split the data into two equal halves, average each, plot the two means at each half's midpoint, join.
Trend line = the straight line through those two points.
Care: needs an even number of periods (odd → drop the middle year).

3 · Moving Averages

Do: average each span of 3 (or 4) consecutive years, write it at the middle year, slide along.
Effect: smooths seasonal/irregular wiggles into a clean trend ribbon.
Care: a 4-point MA has no centre year — centre it with a second 2-point MA.

4 · Least Squares (best fit)

Model: y = a + bx with b = Σxy / Σx², a = Σy / n (coded x, Σx = 0).
Why "least squares": the line minimising the squared vertical misses.
Care: code odd n as −2…2; even n as half-steps −2.5…+2.5.

TRAPS THE OPTIONS ARE BUILT AROUND

Seasonal vs cyclical: calendar-fixed = seasonal; multi-year wave = cyclical
Semi-averages need TWO equal halves — an odd count must drop the middle
3-year MA sits on a year; 4-year MA sits BETWEEN two years
Least squares needs Σx = 0 — forget the coding and the arithmetic explodes
Trend is the long run — one festive spike is seasonal/irregular, not trend
Moving averages kill the endpoints — first and last spans have no MA
