Comparative Paradigm: PR vs. Advertising vs. Publicity vs. Propaganda

Systematic Structural Differentiation for CUET UG Mass Media Studies · Unit 7

FIGURE 1 · CONCEPT MATRIX
Core Dimension Public Relations (PR) Advertising Publicity Propaganda
Primary Objective MUTUAL GOODWILL
Build long-term sustained trust, understanding & reputational equity between org & publics.		 SALES CONVERSION
Directly induce purchase of commercial goods, services, or branded propositions.		 MEDIA EXPOSURE
Generate immediate news buzz and public visibility via third-party media coverage.		 IDEOLOGICAL CONTROL
Manipulate mass beliefs, dogmas, and political allegiances, often subverting logic.
Financial Cost & Media Space EARNED & OWNED MEDIA
No payment for media placement; time/editorial space is earned through news value.		 PAID MEDIA SPACE
Explicit financial payment for print column cm, TV airtime, or digital ad inventory.		 ZERO MEDIA COST
Unpaid editorial coverage resulting from organic events, leaks, or press releases.		 STATE/SPONSORED
Funded heavily by political regimes, covert interest groups, or state apparatus.
Control Over Content & Timing LOW-TO-MODERATE
Gatekeepers (journalists, editors) decide if, when, and how information is reported.		 TOTAL CONTROL
Advertiser dictates exact creative layout, copy, duration, placement, and run frequency.		 NO EDITORIAL CONTROL
Media outlets can frame, critique, distort, or dismiss the story entirely.		 COERCIVE CONTROL
Censorship or monopolistic message repetition suppressing opposing narratives.
Perceived Credibility HIGH (3RD-PARTY ENDORSEMENT)
Perceived as independent, objective editorial reportage rather than sales pitch.		 LOW-TO-MODERATE
Consumers exhibit commercial skepticism; recognized as self-serving promotion.		 VERY HIGH
Regarded as genuine organic news discovered independently by journalists.		 SUSPECT / DOGMATIC
Highly polarizing; relies on emotional bias, selective truth, or disinformation.
Direction of Communication TWO-WAY SYMMETRICAL
Encourages dialogue, stakeholder feedback loops, social audits, and responsiveness.		 ONE-WAY PERSUASIVE
Monologue directed at target audience to stimulate commercial buying actions.		 ONE-WAY TRANSMISSION
Information dissemination to public channels without formal feedback channels.		 ONE-WAY INDOCTRINATION
Authoritarian messaging designed to stifle dissent and enforce conformity.
