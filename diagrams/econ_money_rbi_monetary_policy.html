RBI MONETARY POLICY ARCHITECTURE: QUANTITATIVE & QUALITATIVE INSTRUMENTS

FIG 2: CREDIT CONTROL TOOLS & TRANSMISSION MATRIX
QUANTITATIVE (GENERAL) Regulates Overall Volume & Cost of Credit
Repo Rate
Rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against government securities.
Mechanism: Repo ↑ → Bank borrowing cost ↑ → Lending rates ↑ → Credit demand ↓.
Rev. Repo / SDF
Rate at which RBI absorbs surplus liquidity from banks. (SDF = collateral-free window).
Mechanism: Reverse Repo ↑ → Banks park more funds with RBI → Market lending capacity ↓.
Bank Rate
Rate at which RBI rediscounts first-class bills & lends long-term without collateral.
Mechanism: Bank Rate ↑ → Commercial banks raise base lending rates → Long-term borrowing ↓.
CRR
Cash Reserve Ratio: Percentage of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) kept in cash with RBI.
Mechanism: CRR ↑ → Commercial banks' lendable cash balances fall directly.
SLR
Statutory Liquidity Ratio: Percentage of NDTL maintained by banks in liquid assets (cash, gold, G-Secs).
Mechanism: SLR ↑ → More funds locked in safe liquid assets → Commercial credit pool shrinks.
OMO
Open Market Operations: Outright purchase or sale of government securities in open market.
Mechanism: RBI Sells G-Secs → Mopped up bank cash reserves ↓; RBI Buys G-Secs → Injects cash reserves ↑.
QUALITATIVE (SELECTIVE) Regulates Flow & Direction of Credit to Sectors
Margin Req.
Margin Requirement: Difference between market value of security offered and loan sanctioned.
Example: Collateral = ₹1,00,000; Margin = 20% → Loan = ₹80,000.
Mechanism: Margin ↑ to 40% → Loan shrinks to ₹60,000 → Discourages speculative borrowing.
Credit Rationing
Rationing of Credit / Quotas: Fixing statutory credit ceilings for specific commercial sectors or limits on bill rediscounting.
Mechanism: Directs funds towards priority sectors (agriculture, MSMEs) while curbing hoarding and speculation.
Moral Suasion
Persuasion, Appeals & Guidelines: RBI holds periodic meetings and issues advisories urging banks to conform to credit policies.
Mechanism: Voluntary compliance without invoking statutory penal enforcement.
Direct Action
Penal Enforcement: Imposing statutory monetary penalties, refusing rediscounting facilities, or cancelling banking licences.
Mechanism: Exercised strictly against errant commercial banks defying directives.
TRANSMISSION MATRIX Monetary Policy Actions: Inflation (Excess Demand) vs Deflation (Deficient Demand)
COMBATING INFLATION / EXCESS DEMAND (DEAR MONEY / CONTRACTIONARY POLICY)
Repo Rate: ↑ RAISE Rev. Repo: ↑ RAISE Bank Rate: ↑ RAISE CRR & SLR: ↑ RAISE OMO: SELL G-Secs Margin: ↑ INCREASE
Impact: Cost of credit increases + Lendable bank cash reserves drain out → Credit creation plummets → Money Supply (M1/M3) falls → Aggregate Demand (AD) contracts, arresting inflation.
COMBATING DEFLATION / DEFICIENT DEMAND (CHEAP MONEY / EXPANSIONARY POLICY)
Repo Rate: ↓ REDUCE Rev. Repo: ↓ REDUCE Bank Rate: ↓ REDUCE CRR & SLR: ↓ REDUCE OMO: BUY G-Secs Margin: ↓ DECREASE
Impact: Cost of credit falls + Lendable bank cash reserves surge → Commercial banks expand lending aggressively → Money Supply (M1/M3) rises → Aggregate Demand (AD) expands, reviving output.
