Foundations of NAM & Article 51
Nehru's Triad, Bandung & Constitutional Mandate
Nehru's Three Objectives Core Pillars
  • 1. Preserve Hard-Won Sovereignty: Refusal to join military alliances (NATO/SEATO/CENTO or Warsaw Pact).
  • 2. Protect Territorial Integrity: Defending national borders against foreign aggression.
  • 3. Rapid Economic Development: Securing economic and technical assistance from both Cold War blocs.
Afro-Asian Solidarity & Bandung 1947–1961
  • Asian Relations Conference (March 1947): Hosted in New Delhi under Nehru before independence.
  • Bandung Conference (1955): Historic Afro-Asian summit in Indonesia; laid foundation for NAM.
  • 1st NAM Summit (Belgrade, 1961): Founded by the 5 Titans: Nehru, Tito (Yugoslavia), Nasser (Egypt), Sukarno (Indonesia), Nkrumah (Ghana).
Constitutional Directive (Article 51) DPSP
  • Mandates that the State shall promote international peace and security, maintain just and honorable relations, and foster respect for international law and treaty obligations.
2
Sino-Indian Relations & 1962 War
Panchsheel, Tibet Crisis & Boundary Trauma
The Panchsheel Agreement (1954) 29 April 1954
  • Signed by Nehru and Chinese Premier Zhou Enlai.
  • Five Principles: Mutual respect for sovereignty, non-aggression, non-interference, equality & mutual benefit, peaceful coexistence.
  • India recognized Chinese sovereignty over Tibet.
The Tibet Crisis & Boundary Dispute 1959 Flashpoint
  • Chinese military occupation of Tibet (1950); armed Tibetan uprising crushed in 1959.
  • Dalai Lama fled to India in March 1959; granted political asylum in Dharamsala, infuriating Beijing.
  • Disputed Frontiers: Aksai Chin in Ladakh (China built strategic road) and NEFA (Arunachal Pradesh) across the McMahon Line.
The 1962 War & Aftermath Military Shock
  • China launched massive attacks in Ladakh and NEFA in Oct–Nov 1962; declared unilateral ceasefire.
  • Resignation of Defense Minister V.K. Krishna Menon; first No-Confidence Motion against Nehru.
  • Diplomatic ties restored in 1976; Rajiv Gandhi's visit in 1988 restored normalized ties.

Wars with Pakistan & Nuclear Policy
1965 Tashkent, 1971 Shimla & Pokhran Deterrence
1965 War & Tashkent Declaration Shastri's Leadership
  • Pakistan launched Operation Desert Hawk (Rann of Kutch) and Operation Grand Slam (Kashmir).
  • Shastri gave slogan "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan"; ordered army to march toward Lahore.
  • Tashkent Agreement (Jan 1966): Mediated by Soviet Premier Alexei Kosygin; Shastri died suddenly in Tashkent hours after signing.
1971 Bangladesh War & Shimla Dec 1971 Triumph
  • Indo-Soviet Treaty (Aug 1971): 20-year pact counteracted US-China-Pakistan axis.
  • 13-day war ended on 16 Dec 1971 with surrender of 93,000 Pakistani troops under Gen Niazi.
  • Shimla Agreement (July 1972): Indira Gandhi and Z.A. Bhutto agreed to resolve disputes bilaterally; converted Ceasefire Line into LoC.
India's Nuclear Trajectory Pokhran I & II
  • Initiated by Dr. Homi J. Bhabha; rejected NPT (1968) and CTBT (1996) as discriminatory.
  • Pokhran-I (May 1974): Peaceful nuclear explosion under Indira Gandhi.
  • Pokhran-II (May 1998): Operation Shakti tests under Vajpayee; established nuclear triad & No First Use (NFU) doctrine.
