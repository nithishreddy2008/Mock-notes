THE FIRM'S EQUILIBRIUM & THE MARKET'S EQUILIBRIUM PRICE

The firm: price taker, profit maximiser

Horizontal price line (AR = MR = P) meets the U-shaped MC — the firm stops where MC = MR on the RISING branch
MC (rising after its dip) AVC P = AR = MR
Read it once: the price line is flat because the firm is too small to move the market. Produce every unit whose MC is below the price; stop at E where MC = MR on the RISING arm — and only if the price covers AVC.

The market: where the price is decided

Buyers and sellers together set equilibrium price P*; a demand or supply shift re-prices the market
Demand Supply D after increase
Read it once: above P* supply beats demand (excess supply pushes price DOWN); below P* demand beats supply (excess demand pushes price UP). Any shift of D or S re-prices the market — demand right: P↑ Q↑; supply right: P↓ Q↑.

TRAP 1

The supply curve's own price NEVER shifts it. Own price slides you ALONG the S curve; input costs, technology and taxes shift it.

TRAP 2

MC = MR is not enough. Equilibrium needs the RISING branch — and the shutdown check: P below AVC's minimum means produce nothing at all.

TRAP 3

Market supply adds QUANTITIES, not prices — at each price, sum every firm's quantity (horizontal summation), exactly like market demand.
