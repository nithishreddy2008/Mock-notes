The Central Dogma, Step by Step
How information stored in DNA becomes a working protein — the storyline of the whole chapter
1

Replication

DNA copies itself before a cell divides. Semi-conservative: each daughter duplex gets one old + one new strand; the template strand rules the sequence.

2

Transcription

One gene → pre-mRNA. RNA polymerase starts at the promoter and stops at the terminator, reading the template strand 3'→5'.

3

Splicing

Introns out, exons joined. A methyl-guanosine cap goes on the 5' end and a poly-A tail on the 3' end (eukaryotes).

4

Translation

mRNA is read in codons on the ribosome; tRNAs bring matching amino acids. AUG starts; UAA, UAG, UGA stop.

5

Polypeptide → protein

The finished chain folds into its working 3-D shape — the gene product the cell actually uses.

Side card — Lac operon in one line

Lactose present → repressor lifts → transcription ON. With no lactose the repressor sits on the operator and blocks RNA polymerase — an inducible, negative-control switch.

Side card — Mutations

One base change (point mutation) alters a single codon; an insertion or deletion can shift every codon after it (frameshift) and change the whole protein.
