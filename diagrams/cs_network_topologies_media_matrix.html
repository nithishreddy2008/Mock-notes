Computer Networks: Topologies & Transmission Media Matrix

Comparative Architecture of Topologies, Physical Guided Cables, and Wireless Communication Channels

OurMocks Networks Guide
TOPOLOGY Physical Topologies
Star TopologyHub / Switch
All nodes connect to a central hub/switch. Easy fault isolation. Single point of failure: central hub. Most popular LAN layout.
Bus TopologyBackbone Cable
Single central backbone wire with terminators at ends. Low cable cost. Central cable break disables entire network. Hard to isolate faults.
Tree & Mesh TopologiesHierarchical / Full
Tree: Hierarchical star-bus hybrid. Mesh: Point-to-point links between every node ($n(n-1)/2$ links). Maximum fault tolerance, highest cost.
GUIDED MEDIA Physical Wired Cables
Twisted Pair (UTP / STP)RJ-45 Jack
Pairs twisted to minimize crosstalk and electromagnetic interference (EMI). Cheap, flexible, max run 100m. Cat5e/Cat6 LANs.
Coaxial CableBNC / F-Type
Central copper conductor with dielectric insulator and braided metallic shielding. Higher bandwidth than twisted pair. Cable TV/broadband.
Optical Fiber CableLight Waves
Glass/plastic core transmits light via Total Internal Reflection. Immense bandwidth, immune to EMI, lowest attenuation over long distances.
UNGUIDED MEDIA Wireless Channels
Radio WavesOmni-directional
3 kHz to 1 GHz. Penetrates walls and solid obstacles easily. Used in AM/FM radio, Wi-Fi, cordless phones. Subject to interference.
MicrowavesUnidirectional Line-of-Sight
1 GHz to 300 GHz. Cannot penetrate obstacles; requires line-of-sight parabolic dish alignment. Repeaters every 25–50 km. Cellular towers.
Infrared & SatelliteShort Range / Global
Infrared: Cannot penetrate walls (high security, TV remotes). Satellite: Geostationary orbits (36,000 km) for transcontinental WAN links.
