What a Time Series Is

Chronological data: one variable, recorded in time order
Univariate: only the value + its time stamp
Order is the data — shuffling destroys the meaning
Examples: yearly rainfall, monthly sales, daily temperature

The Four Components

T — trend: the long-run direction
S — seasonal: calendar-fixed repeats within a year
C — cyclical: multi-year booms and slumps
I — irregular: one-off shocks
Models: additive Y = T + S + C + I; multiplicative Y = T·S·C·I

Secular Trend

Long-term tendency — decades, not months
Reads: growth, decline or plateau
Driver: population, technology, income — slow forces
Isolating it is the whole measuring-trend business

Method 1 & 2: Freehand, Semi-Averages

Freehand: smooth line by eye — fast, subjective
Semi-averages: two equal halves, two means, join them
Needs an even count — odd means drop the middle year
Line passes both midpoints — that IS the trend line

Method 3: Moving Averages

Average each 3-year span, park it at the middle year, slide
Smooths seasonal and irregular noise away
4-point MA lands between years — centre it again
Cost: the ends of the table lose their averages

Method 4: Least Squares

y = a + bx with coded x so Σx = 0
a = Σy/n, b = Σxy/Σx² — two divisions, no algebra
"Least squares" = smallest total squared miss
Odd n: code −2…2 · Even n: half-steps ±2.5

TOP TRAPS

Seasonal ≠ cyclical — the calendar is the divider
4-year MA centring — the forgotten second pass
Coding skipped — least squares without Σx = 0 invites errors
