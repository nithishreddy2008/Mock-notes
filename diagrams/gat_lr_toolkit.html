THE SEVEN-FAMILY REASONING TOOLKIT
CUET UG · GENERAL APTITUDE TEST · LOGICAL & ANALYTICAL REASONING · ONE METHOD PER FAMILY
FAMILY 1

Analogies

LOOKS LIKE
A : B :: C : ? — "Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?"
THE METHOD
Name the relationship in words first ("works at"), then apply it to C.
THE TRAP
Options that match the category, not the relationship.
FAMILY 2

Sequences (Series)

LOOKS LIKE
2, 6, 12, 20, ? or A, C, F, J, ? — find the next term.
THE METHOD
Differences, then ratios, then positions. Three checks, in order.
THE TRAP
A pattern that fits one gap but not all — verify every step.
FAMILY 3

Coding–Decoding

LOOKS LIKE
CAT → DBU; now code DOG the same way.
THE METHOD
Compare letter by letter; find the shift (+1 here); apply to the new word.
THE TRAP
Assuming a uniform shift when the code reverses or swaps.
FAMILY 4

Syllogisms

LOOKS LIKE
"All pens are books; some books are red." What must follow?
THE METHOD
Draw the Venn circles — never argue by feel. Conclusions must be true in EVERY diagram.
THE TRAP
A conclusion true in your favourite diagram but not all possible ones.
FAMILY 5

Blood Relations

LOOKS LIKE
"Pointing to a photo, she said, 'He is my mother's only son.'" Who is he?
THE METHOD
Draw the tree — males as squares, females as circles — one clue at a time.
THE TRAP
The speaker's gender hiding inside the sentence; "only son" ≠ "only child".
FAMILY 6

Direction Sense

LOOKS LIKE
Walks 3 km north, 4 km east. How far from start?
THE METHOD
Sketch the path with a compass cross; displacement via 3-4-5 right triangle.
THE TRAP
Total distance walked vs straight-line displacement — two different answers.
FAMILY 7

Puzzles (Seating & Order)

LOOKS LIKE
Five friends in a row facing north; clues fix their positions.
THE METHOD
Build a slot table; fix the definite clue first, chain the rest, re-read all clues at the end.
THE TRAP
Left/right flips when people face south — mark the facing direction first.

Traps that catch most students

Arguing syllogisms by feel — "sounds logical" is not "must be true"; the Venn decides
Reading relations from the listener's side — relations always belong to the SPEAKER
Trusting one-step patterns — a series answer must survive every term, not just the last gap
