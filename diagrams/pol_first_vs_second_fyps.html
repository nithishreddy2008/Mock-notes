Planning Architecture & Mixed Model
Bombay Plan, Planning Commission & State Ideology
The Bombay Plan (1944) Industrialist Pact
  • Joint memorandum signed by big industrialists (J.R.D. Tata, G.D. Birla, Purshotamdas Thakurdas).
  • Demanded state intervention: the state must make massive capital investments in heavy industry and infrastructure.
  • Established national consensus that market forces alone could not industrialize post-colonial India.
The Planning Commission (March 1950) Advisory Body
  • Set up via cabinet resolution (extra-constitutional, non-statutory body).
  • Prime Minister as ex-officio Chairman; formulated Five-Year Plans (FYPs).
  • Replaced on 1 January 2015 by NITI Aayog under Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
The Mixed Economy Consensus Public + Private
  • Refused extremes of pure capitalism (US) and total state collectivization (USSR).
  • State commanded heavy industries, mining, power, transport; private sector dominated agriculture, trade, consumer goods.
2
First vs Second Five-Year Plans
K.N. Raj (Agrarian) vs P.C. Mahalanobis (Heavy Industry)
First FYP (1951–1956) Agrarian Consolidation
  • Drafted by young economist K.N. Raj; mantra: India must "hasten slowly".
  • Prioritized agriculture, massive irrigation networks, and power projects (e.g., Bhakra Nangal Dam).
  • Emphasized raising national savings rates; aimed to pull the country out of post-partition agrarian poverty.
Second FYP (1956–1961) Heavy Industrialisation
  • Architect: P.C. Mahalanobis (founder of Indian Statistical Institute, ISI).
  • Promoted structural transformation via rapid heavy industrialization (capital goods, machinery, steel).
  • Built three massive public steel plants: Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur.
  • Imposed heavy import tariffs to protect domestic industries (import substitution).
Third FYP & Plan Holidays 1961–1969
  • Third Plan (1961–66) derailed by 1962 China War, 1965 Pak War, and two successive monsoon failures.
  • Led to three annual "Plan Holidays" (1966–1969).
3
Debates & Agricultural Revolutions
Charan Singh, Green Revolution & Operation Flood
Major Developmental Debates Ideological Cleavages
  • Agriculture vs Industry: Chaudhary Charan Singh argued industrial planning devastated villages; J.C. Kumarappa proposed Gandhian rural decentralization (Economy of Permanence).
  • Land Reforms: Abolition of Zamindari succeeded; land ceilings and tenancy laws were subverted by big landlords.
The Green Revolution High-Yielding Varieties
  • Mid-1960s food crisis & Bihar famine forced end of US PL-480 import dependence.
  • Pioneered by M.S. Swaminathan using Norman Borlaug's Mexican dwarf wheat strains, chemical fertilizers, tubewell irrigation, and guaranteed MSPs.
  • Concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, Western UP; created rich peasant class ("kulaks"); heightened regional disparities.
The White Revolution (Operation Flood) Dairy Co-ops
  • Launched in 1970 by National Dairy Development Board (NDDB); led by Dr. Verghese Kurien ("Milkman of India").
  • Centered in Anand (Gujarat); transformed dairy farming via rural milk cooperatives (Amul).
