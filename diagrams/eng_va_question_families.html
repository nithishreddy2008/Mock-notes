THE FOUR VERBAL-ABILITY QUESTION FAMILIES
CUET UG · ENGLISH · VERBAL ABILITY · METHOD + TRAP FOR EACH FAMILY
FAMILY 1

Rearranging the Parts

LOOKS LIKE
Sentences marked P, Q, R, S come scrambled — put them back in order so the paragraph flows.
THE METHOD
Find the opener first (no pronoun dangling, introduces the scene), then link pairs, then check the full flow.
THE TRAP
An option starting with "He/It/They..." is almost never the opening sentence.
FAMILY 2

Match the Following

LOOKS LIKE
Two columns — words to meanings, books to authors, animals to group names — pair every item.
THE METHOD
Lock the pairs you are sure of first; strike them out; match the leftovers from what remains.
THE TRAP
One wrong pair ruins the whole answer — verify every four, not just the first two.
FAMILY 3

Choosing the Correct Word

LOOKS LIKE
A sentence with a blank (or a confusing word pair) — pick the word that fits meaning AND grammar.
THE METHOD
Run three checks: sense (does it fit the sentence?), grammar (noun/verb?), partners (usual collocation?).
THE TRAP
Confusables — affect/effect, advice/advise, principal/principle — sound-alike pairs with fixed roles.
FAMILY 4

Synonyms & Antonyms

LOOKS LIKE
One word in a short sentence — pick the closest meaning (synonym) or the opposite (antonym).
THE METHOD
Read the sentence, predict your own word, then match. For antonyms, flip your prediction — and read the question twice.
THE TRAP
In antonym questions, a perfect synonym sits in the options waiting to be picked by mistake.

The universal 3-check ritual — before marking ANY verbal answer

1
Sense check
Say the sentence (or paragraph) with your choice inside. Does the meaning survive?
2
Grammar check
Right job for the blank — noun, verb, adjective? Right agreement with the subject?
3
Question-type check
Did it ask for synonym or ANTONYM? Match the following done in full? Every sentence used in the jumble?

Traps that catch most students

Answering by ear — "sounds nice" is not a rule; run the three checks
Stopping after two matches — match-the-following needs all four verified
Ignoring sentence links — pronouns (he, it, this) and connectors (but, then) are the jumble's glue
