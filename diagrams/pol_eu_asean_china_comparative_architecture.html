ALTERNATIVE CENTERS OF POWER: COMPARATIVE ARCHITECTURAL MATRIX

CUET UG Political Science · Regional Blocs, Economic Superpowers & Diplomatic Mechanisms

FIGURE 1 · COMPARATIVE REGIONAL MATRIX
1. European Union (EU) · Supranational Model
A. Evolution & Treaties
Marshall Plan (1948): OEEC created for post-war recovery.
Treaty of Rome (1957): Established European Economic Community (EEC) with 6 founding nations.
Maastricht Treaty (1992): Created the European Union (effective 1993); single currency Euro launched in 1999/2002.
B. Economic & Military Power
Global Economic Stature: World's 2nd largest economy after US; single market with free movement of goods, capital, and labor.
Diplomatic Leverage: Permanent veto seat on UN Security Council (France); combined military second only to US.
C. Structural Limitations
Euro-Skepticism: National resistance to common constitution (rejected in 2005 by France/Netherlands); Brexit (2020).
Foreign Policy Discord: Deep divisions over US foreign wars (e.g. UK joined 2003 Iraq invasion; Germany/France opposed).
2. ASEAN · Consensus & Intergovernmental
A. Genesis & Membership
Bangkok Declaration (8 Aug 1967): Founded by 5 nations: Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand.
Expansion to 10: Added Brunei (1984), Vietnam (1995), Laos & Myanmar (1997), and Cambodia (1999).
B. The 'ASEAN Way' & 3 Pillars (2003)
The 'ASEAN Way': Informal, non-confrontational, sovereign-respecting consensus diplomacy with NO supranational parliament.
Three Pillars: Security Community (ARF 1994 for dialogue), Economic Community (AEC free trade area), Socio-Cultural Community.
C. India's Engagement
Look East (1991) to Act East (2014): Signed FTAs on goods (2009) and services (2014); strategic partner in defense and maritime security.
3. Rise of China · Gradual Market Socialism
A. Maoist Model vs Reforms
Maoist Economy (1949–1978): State ownership, self-reliant command model, basic social guarantees, but low industrial productivity.
Deng Xiaoping (1978): Abandoned Shock Therapy; launched "Socialism with Chinese Characteristics" & Open Door Policy.
B. Phased Modernization Strategy
Four Modernizations (1973): Zhou Enlai proposed agriculture, industry, science/tech, defense.
Stepwise Privatization: Agriculture privatized in 1982; Industry privatized in 1998; Special Economic Zones (SEZs); WTO entry (2001).
C. India-China Relations
Conflict & Thaw: 1962 Border War severed ties; restored by Rajiv Gandhi's 1988 Beijing visit; massive trade surplus despite LAC border disputes.
East Asian Economic Dynamism: Japan (G7 member, 1% defense ceiling, global tech giant: Sony, Toyota, Honda); South Korea (OECD member, Chaebol giants: Samsung, Hyundai, LG).
Projection: China projected to surpass US economy by 2040.
