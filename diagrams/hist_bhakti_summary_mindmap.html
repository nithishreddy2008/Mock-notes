1. Integration of Cults & Tantra
Great & Little Traditions
  • Two Processes: Dissemination of Brahmanical texts via accessible Puranas; assimilation of local folk and tribal deities into the pantheon.
  • Jagannatha at Puri: Local wooden tribal deity identified as a form of Vishnu; worshipped alongside Balabhadra and Subhadra.
  • Goddess Cults: Regional stone deities smeared with vermilion integrated as consorts of major gods (Lakshmi, Parvati).
  • Tantric Practices: Open to all men and women regardless of varna; rejected Vedic authority; influenced both Shaivism and Buddhism.
★ Core Axis: Personal Devotion & Social Equity
Bhakti & Tasawwuf
  • Rejection of Vedic Exclusivism: Direct personal emotional bond with the Divine, bypassing sacrificial rituals and priestly monopoly.
  • Vernacular Medium: Composed songs in spoken languages (Tamil, Kannada, Punjabi, Braj, Hindavi, Dakhani) rather than elite Sanskrit.
  • Gender & Subaltern Empowerment: High visibility of female mystics (Andal, Karaikkal Ammaiyar, Akka Mahadevi, Mirabai) and low-caste saints.
  • Syncretic Cosmopolitanism: Exchange between Sufi mysticism and Yogic/Bhakti devotionalism across regional khanqahs and shrines.
2. Alvars & Nayanars (Tamil South)
c. 6th–9th Century CE
  • Alvars & Nayanars: Alvars worshipped Vishnu; Nayanars worshipped Shiva; traveled from shrine to shrine singing ecstatic Tamil hymns.
  • Textual Canons: Nalayira Divyaprabandham (composed by 12 Alvars, praised as the "Tamil Veda"); Tevaram (compiled Nayanar songs).
  • Women Mystics: Andal (saw herself as Vishnu's divine bride); Karaikkal Ammaiyar (renounced beauty, lived as ascetic demoness).
  • Imperial Chola Patronage: Rulers (Parantaka I, Rajaraja I) built monumental stone temples (Thanjavur, Chidambaram) and consecrated Nataraja bronzes.
3. Virashaivas / Lingayats
12th Century Karnataka
  • Founding Leader: Initiated by Basavanna, a Brahmana minister in the Kalachuri kingdom, with Allama Prabhu and Akka Mahadevi.
  • Sacred Linga: Worshipped Shiva exclusively as a linga; wore a small silver-encased linga looped over the left shoulder (*Lingayats*).
  • Radical Social Reform: Fiercely rejected the caste hierarchy, Brahmanical ritual pollution, rebirth, child marriage, and idol worship.
  • Burial & Vachanas: Buried their dead without cremation (believing they united with Shiva); composed lyrical Kannada prose (*Vachanas*).
4. Sufi Silsilas & Chishti Khanqahs
Mystic Hospices
  • Silsila Lineage: Spiritual genealogy tracing back to Prophet Muhammad; Chishti order introduced by Muinuddin Chishti of Ajmer (1192 CE).
  • Hospice Ecosystem (*Khanqah*): Community kitchen (*langar*), musical audition (*sama'* featuring qawwals), and pilgrimage (*ziyarat*) on death anniversary (*urs*).
  • Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya: Master of Delhi (d. 1325 CE); maintained spiritual independence from Sultans; disciple Amir Khusrau pioneered Hindavi verse.
  • Vernacular Genres: Composed Sufi romances (*Premakhyans*, e.g. Jayasi's *Padmavat*) and household songs (*Lurinama*, *Charkhanama*).
5. Northern Sants: Kabir, Nanak & Mira
Saguna vs Nirguna
  • Kabir (c. 14th–15th c.): Nirguna monotheism; challenged both Vedic and Islamic orthodoxies; renowned for Ulatbansi; compiled in *Bijak* and *Adi Granth*.
  • Baba Guru Nanak (1469–1539 CE): Preached formless God (*Nirankar*); established *Sangat* (congregation) and *Langar* (free kitchen); poems compiled in *Adi Granth* (1604).
  • Khalsa Panth (1699): Transformed by Guru Gobind Singh with the *Five Ks* into a community of saint-soldiers.
  • Mirabai: Rajput princess who rejected palace luxury, dedicated her life to Krishna (*Giridhar Gopal*), and chose untouchable leather-worker Raidas as guru.
