Direct Side-by-Side Comparison: Zero Order vs First Order Kinetics & Pseudo-First-Order Mechanics

Unit III: Chemical Kinetics Zero vs First Order Half-Life Dynamics

ZERO ORDER KINETICS

Rate = k · [R]⁰ = k
Differential & Integrated Equations
−d[R]/dt = k ⟹ [R] = [R]₀ − k·t
• Concentration decreases linearly with time.
• Rate of reaction is completely independent of reactant concentration.
• Units of k: mol · L⁻¹ · s⁻¹ (identical to rate units).
Half-Life & Completion Time
t₁/₂ = [R]₀ / (2k) | t₁₀₀% = [R]₀ / k = 2 · t₁/₂
• Half-life is directly proportional to initial concentration [R]₀ (doubling [R]₀ doubles t₁/₂).
• Reaction goes to 100% completion in finite time: $t_{100\%} = 2 \times t_{1/2}$.
CLASSIC EXAM EXAMPLES
1. Decomposition of gaseous ammonia on hot platinum catalyst: 2NH₃(g) → N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) at high pressure.
2. Thermal decomposition of HI on gold (Au) surface.

FIRST ORDER KINETICS

Rate = k · [R]¹
Differential & Integrated Equations
k = (2.303 / t) · log([R]₀ / [R]) | [R] = [R]₀ · e^(−kt)
• Concentration decays exponentially with time.
• Plotting $\ln[R]$ vs $t$ yields slope $= -k$; plotting $\log[R]$ vs $t$ yields slope $= -k / 2.303$.
• Units of k: s⁻¹ or min⁻¹ (independent of concentration units).
Half-Life & Progression Fractions
t₁/₂ = ln 2 / k = 0.693 / k (Independent of [R]₀)
• t₇₅% = 2 × t₁/₂ (75% completed, 25% remaining).
• t₈₇.₅% = 3 × t₁/₂ | t₉₉.₉% ≈ 10 × t₁/₂.
• A first order reaction theoretically takes infinite time ($t = \infty$) for 100% completion!
CLASSIC EXAM EXAMPLES
1. All natural and artificial radioactive decays (e.g. Radium decay, Carbon-14 dating).
2. Thermal decomposition of N₂O₅: 2N₂O₅ → 4NO₂ + O₂.

PSEUDO-FIRST ORDER & TRAPS

High-Yield Concepts
Pseudo-First Order Reactions
Rate = k' · [A] · [B] ⟹ Rate = k · [A]
A bimolecular reaction made first order by taking one reactant in large excess, so its concentration remains practically constant throughout the reaction:
k = k' · [B]ₑₓ𝚌ₑₛₛ
TWO MANDATORY CUET EXAMPLES
1. Acid-catalysed Hydrolysis of Ethyl Acetate:
CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O(excess) ⟶ CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH
Rate = k [CH₃COOC₂H₅] (Water conc. is constant ≈ 55.5 M).

2. Inversion of Cane Sugar:
C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O(excess) ⟶ C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose) + C₆H₁₂O₆ (Fructose)
Rate = k [C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁].
Order vs Molecularity Matrix
• Order: Experimental sum of powers in rate law. Can be zero, fractional, or negative. Applies to elementary and complex reactions.
• Molecularity: Theoretical number of colliding species in an elementary step. Can only be a positive whole integer (1, 2, or 3). Never zero or fractional!
PRESSURE KINETICS EQUATION
For A(g) → B(g) + C(g):
k = (2.303 / t) · log[pi / (2pi − pt)]
pi = initial pressure of A | pt = total pressure at time t.
