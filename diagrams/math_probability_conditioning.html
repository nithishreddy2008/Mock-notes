THE CONDITIONING TOOLKIT — FROM P(A|B) TO BAYES

CUET UG · MATHEMATICS · PROBABILITY

Conditional probability

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B),   P(B) > 0
Read: "given B happened, how likely is A?" — the universe shrinks to B
Reflex: cards — given the card is red, P(king) = 2/26 = 1/13

Multiplication theorem

P(A ∩ B) = P(B) · P(A | B) = P(A) · P(B | A)
Chains: three events multiply along the tree — draw it, never memorise blind
Independent events: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B) — knowledge of one changes nothing

Total probability

P(A) = Σ P(Eᵢ) · P(A | Eᵢ)
Setup: events Eᵢ partition the sample space (mutually exclusive, exhaustive)
Use: A can arrive through several doors — add the door probabilities weighted by their sizes

Bayes' theorem

P(Eᵢ | A) = P(Eᵢ) · P(A | Eᵢ) / Σ P(Eⱼ) · P(A | Eⱼ)
Flips the arrow: you saw the effect, you want the causing door
Example: defective bulb found — which machine most likely made it?

THE BAYES TREE — "DEFECTIVE BULB, WHICH MACHINE?"

Machine share
A: 25% of output, defect rate 5%
B: 35% of output, defect rate 4%
C: 40% of output, defect rate 2%
Door weights
P(A)·P(D|A) = .25 × .05 = .0125
P(B)·P(D|B) = .35 × .04 = .0140
P(C)·P(D|C) = .40 × .02 = .0080
Total
P(D) = .0125 + .0140 + .0080 = .0345 — the denominator is the whole tree added up
→
Answer
P(A | D) = .0125 / .0345 = 25/69 — effect observed, cause asked
→

TRAPS THE OPTIONS ARE BUILT AROUND

P(A|B) vs P(B|A) — the arrow direction is the entire question
Independence ≠ mutually exclusive — disjoint events with positive probability are dependent
Bayes numerator is one door, denominator is all doors — forgetting the sum kills it
"At least one" = 1 − P(none) — direct chains overcount and waste time
With replacement keeps probabilities equal; without shifts them — read the words
P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B) — the subtracted overlap is not optional
