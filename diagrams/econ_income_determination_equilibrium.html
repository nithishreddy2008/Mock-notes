CORE MECHANISM Equilibrium Conditions & Restoring Dynamics
1. The Two Equilibrium Conditions
• AD = AS Approach: Aggregate demand (planned expenditure) matches aggregate output ($Y = C + I$).
• S = I Approach: Planned leakage from income stream (saving) equals planned injection (investment).
Y* = (c̄ + Ī) / (1 − MPC) = (c̄ + Ī) / MPS
2. When AD > AS (Planned Spending > Output)
• Buyers demand more goods than currently produced.
• Unplanned inventories fall below desired levels (inventory depletion: $\Delta \text{Stock} < 0$).
• Producers hire more labor and expand production until output reaches $Y^*$.
3. When AS > AD (Output > Planned Spending)
• Output exceeds total planned purchases.
• Unsold goods pile up; unplanned inventories accumulate ($\Delta \text{Stock} > 0$).
• Producers slash output and lay off workers until supply contracts back to $Y^*$.
4. Effective Demand Principle
• Effective Demand is the exact point on the AD curve where aggregate demand is supported by aggregate output ($AD = AS$).
• Coined by J.M. Keynes: output and employment are determined by effective demand, not by potential supply alone.
45-Degree Line Significance
The 45° line represents all points where aggregate expenditure equals national output ($AS = Y = C + S$). Every point on this line is a potential equilibrium locus.
Breakeven vs Macro Equilibrium
At Breakeven Point ($B$), $C = Y$ and $S = 0$. At Macro Equilibrium ($E$), $AD = AS$ ($C + I = Y$) and $S = I$. They coincide only if autonomous investment is zero.
Inventory Adjustment Mechanism
The macroeconomic restoring force is the unplanned change in inventories. When $AD \neq AS$, involuntary inventory shifts force firms to adjust production towards $Y^*$.
