Population & Sample

Population: the whole group of interest
Sample: the studied subset — must REPRESENT it
Representative: mirrors the population's spread
Non-representative: convenience picks — biased results

Sampling Designs

Simple random: lottery / random numbers — equal chance
Systematic: k = N/n, random start, every k-th unit
Start 7, k = 10: 7, 17, 27, … — spread with one draw
WR vs WOR: replace the unit or not before the next draw

Parameter vs Statistic

Parameter: population constant — μ, σ
Statistic: sample number — x̄, s; wobbles by sample
Relation: the statistic estimates the parameter
Limitation: one sample ≠ the whole truth — sampling error

Central Limit Theorem

Sampling distribution of x̄: centred at μ
SE = σ/√n — bigger samples, tighter means
Shape: turns normal as n grows, whatever the population
Worked: μ = 60, σ = 12, n = 36 → SE = 2

Hypotheses

H₀ (null): status quo — "no difference", carries equality
H₁ (alternate): the doubt — ≠, >, or <
Verdict language: reject / fail to reject — never "prove true"
Significance: the pre-set risk of a false alarm (5%)

One-Sample t-Test

t = (x̄ − μ₀)/(s/√n) — small samples, σ unknown
df = n − 1 — one degree spent on the mean
Compare |t| to the table at 5% for that df
Beyond → reject H₀; inside → keep
