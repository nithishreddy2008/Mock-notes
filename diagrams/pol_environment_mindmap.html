ENVIRONMENT & NATURAL RESOURCES
CONTEMPORARY WORLD POLITICS · CH. 6 (TOPIC 15)

1. Evolution of Global Green Norms

Limits to Growth (1972): Club of Rome landmark publication warning against industrial resource exhaustion.
Our Common Future (1987): Brundtland Commission report popularizing the concept of sustainable development.
Rio Earth Summit (1992): 170 nations adopted Agenda 21, Rio Declaration, and UNFCCC on climate change.
Kyoto Protocol (1997): Legally binding greenhouse gas reduction targets for developed states (Annex-I).

2. CBDR: North vs South Cleavage

Principle 7 of Rio: Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR) recognizes historic emissions by the North.
Global North's View: Demands universal emission caps on all states; wants developing giants to cut emissions.
Global South's Stance: Economic development is paramount; per-capita emissions are low; North must provide funds/tech.
Paris Accord (2015): Voluntary Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) aiming for well below 2°C warming.

3. The Global Commons & CPRs

Four Global Commons: Earth's Atmosphere, Antarctica, the Ocean Floor, and Outer Space outside sovereign reach.
Key Treaties: 1959 Antarctic Treaty, 1987 Montreal Protocol (ozone protection), 1982 UNCLOS (Law of the Sea).
Common Property Resources: Village pastures, forests, and water bodies managed collaboratively by local communities.
Tragedy of the Commons: Encroachment, privatization, and commercial overuse destroying communal ecosystems.

4. Resource Geopolitics: Oil & Water

Oil as Power: Middle East holds roughly 64% of global oil reserves; control over pipelines drives military interventions.
Gulf Conflicts: 1990–91 Gulf War & 2003 Iraq War fundamentally shaped by global energy security imperatives.
Water Wars: Upstream vs downstream riparian disputes: Euphrates-Tigris, Jordan River, and the River Nile (GERD).
Pollution of Shared Waters: Agricultural runoff and industrial toxins polluting cross-border rivers and aquifers.

5. Rights of Indigenous People

Definition: Descendants of original inhabitants prior to conquest; roughly 300 million indigenous people worldwide.
Core Grievance: Forced dispossession of ancestral lands, commercial mining, logging, and large-scale dam building.
Global Solidarity: UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP 2007); World Council of Indigenous Peoples.
Indian Stand: India uses the constitutional term Scheduled Tribes (Adivasis) (8.6% of population); asserts all Indians are indigenous.

6. India's Stand in Climate Debates

Equity & CBDR: Staunch defender of historical emissions equity; ratified Kyoto Protocol in August 2002.
NAPCC (2008): National Action Plan on Climate Change launched with 8 National Missions (Solar, Energy Efficiency, etc.).
International Solar Alliance (2015): Co-founded with France at COP21 Paris; global headquarters in Gurugram, India.
Net-Zero 2070: Panchamrit climate commitments unveiled at Glasgow COP26 (2021); massive renewable energy rollout.

EXAM PITFALLS — DATES, TREATIES & COMMONS

Kyoto Protocol Ratification: India signed and ratified Kyoto in August 2002, NOT in 1997!
Montreal vs Kyoto: Montreal Protocol (1987) protects the Ozone Layer; Kyoto Protocol (1997) targets Greenhouse Gases!
Brundtland Report: Published in 1987 titled Our Common Future (defined sustainable development).
