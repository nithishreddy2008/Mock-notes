INCOME & EMPLOYMENT DETERMINATION
COURSE II MACRO · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Aggregate Demand & Components

Two-sector AD = C + I; full model adds G and (X − M)
C: household spending — the biggest, steadiest component
I: firms' NEW capital + inventory change — volatile, autonomous
Ex-ante (planned) values are what the model uses

Consumption & Propensities

C = c̄ + b·Y — autonomous spending (c̄) + MPC × income
S = −c̄ + (1 − b)·Y — dissaving below breakeven
MPC + MPS = 1 always; APC + APS = 1 too
Breakeven: C = Y, S = 0, APC = 1

Two-Sector Equilibrium

AD = AS (spending view) — where C + I crosses the 45° line
S = I (leakage view) — planned saving exactly funds investment
Solve: Y = (c̄ + I) ÷ (1 − MPC)
Equilibrium ≠ full employment — gaps can sit either side

The Multiplier

K = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS — MPC 0.8 → K = 5
ΔY = K × ΔI — ₹100 of new investment lifts income by ₹500
Why it ripples: one person's spending is another's income — round after round
Higher MPC → bigger K — the leak (saving) is smaller

The Two Gaps

Excess demand: AD beyond full-employment output → inflationary gap → prices climb, output can't
Deficient demand: AD short of full-employment output → deflationary gap → idle machines, unemployment
Both are measured against Y(FE), the full-employment income

Correction Levers

Deficient demand → raise government spending, cut taxes, expand money supply
Excess demand → cut government spending, raise taxes, contract money supply
Fiscal levers move G and T; monetary lever (RBI) moves money supply and credit

Traps that catch most students

Multiplier inverts: K = 1/MPS — MPC 0.75 gives K = 4, not 1.33
S = I is about PLANNED values — unplanned inventory swings are the signal that Y is off equilibrium
Inflationary gap ≠ rising output — beyond full employment only PRICES rise
Equilibrium income can sit below full employment — the Keynesian lesson: markets need policy help
