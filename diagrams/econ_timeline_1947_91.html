1947 → 1991 — THE ROAD THAT ENDED AT THE CRISIS

Four decades of policy in one line

Above the lane: the event · below the lane: why it matters for the exam
1947

Eve of independence

Stagnant agriculture, de-industrialised trade, 16% literacy, life expectancy 32 years.

1950-51

Planning begins

Planning Commission set up (1950); First Five Year Plan (1951-56) — agriculture, Harrod-Domar model.

1956

The industrial turn

Second Plan (1956-61) — Mahalanobis model; IPR 1956 puts the state at the command post.

1966

Green Revolution

HYV seeds + irrigation + fertilisers break the food deadlock — wheat and rice lead.

1969

Banks nationalised

14 big banks taken over — credit steered to priority sectors; plan holiday already passed (1966-69).

1980s

Slow loosening

Growth picks up but deficits and imports balloon — the fiscal strain builds silently.

1991

The crisis

Reserves down to about two weeks of imports; gold flown out as collateral; IMF conditions.

Exam hook: the four features of the inherited economy — ask "what kind of country did planners inherit?"

Exam hook: goals of planning — growth, modernisation, self-reliance, equity — memorise all four.

Exam hook: IPR 1956 = "industrial policy spectrum" — Schedule A/B/C industries; licence raj begins.

Exam hook: the middle path on subsidies — adoption vs misuse, both sides needed in answers.

Exam hook: SSI reservation and employment — the small-scale debate links here.

Exam hook: inward-looking trade — tariffs and quotas to substitute imports.

Exam hook: NEP = stabilisation + structural reform = LPG. Know all three words' features.

LIBERALISATION

Delicensing of industry · deregulation of prices · freedom to import/export · rupee devalued (1991) · financial-sector reform. The state retreats from day-to-day control.

PRIVATISATION

Disinvestment of PSU equity · fewer industries reserved for the public sector · autonomy packages (MoUs, Navratna-style status) to make PSUs compete.

GLOBALISATION

Tariff cuts peak rates · quantitative restrictions phased out · FDI and FII invited · WTO membership (1995) locks openness in.

Appraisal in one line: reforms sped up growth, rebuilt reserves and revived competition — but critics count jobless growth, neglected agriculture and rising inequality; the debate is never one-sided.

TRAP 1

Eve of independence ≠ empty economy. Railways and some modern industry existed — but backward, foreign-owned and low-capacity.

TRAP 2

Green Revolution = 1966, not the Second Plan. The Second Plan (1956) is the INDUSTRY turn — pairing the wrong decade is the classic MCQ.

TRAP 3

1991 crisis → NEP = LPG. Stabilisation (short-run) + structural adjustment (long-run) — know which parts belong to which.
