DETECT THE CARBONYL — THE FOUR CLASSIC TESTS
Each reagent asks a different question: is it a carbonyl? an aldehyde? a methyl ketone? — and answers with a colour or a precipitate

2,4-DNP TEST — is it a carbonyl at all?

Reagent2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine (Brady's reagent) in methanol + acid
PositiveYellow, orange or red precipitate (the 2,4-DNP hydrazone derivative)
Who passesBoth aldehydes and ketones — any C=O compound; carboxylic acids and esters fail

TOLLEN'S TEST — aldehyde or ketone?

ReagentAmmoniacal silver nitrate — the silver mirror reagent, a mild oxidant
PositiveShiny silver mirror or grey precipitate: RCHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + 2Ag + 4NH₃ + 2H₂O
Who passesAldehydes only (and formic acid, alpha-hydroxy ketones); ketones are immune

FEHLING'S TEST — aliphatic aldehyde check

ReagentFehling A (CuSO₄) + Fehling B (alkaline sodium potassium tartrate), warmed
PositiveRed-brown Cu₂O precipitate: RCHO + 2Cu²⁺ + 5OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + Cu₂O + 3H₂O
Who passesAliphatic aldehydes only — aromatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde) and ketones give nothing

IODOFORM TEST — the CH₃CO– fingerprint

ReagentI₂ + NaOH (warm)
PositiveYellow CHI₃ crystals with antiseptic smell — the methyl ketone is chopped to iodoform + carboxylate
Who passesMethyl ketones (RCOCH₃), ethanal, and any alcohol oxidisable to them (ethanol, 2° alcohols with CH₃CH(OH)–)
The decision tree: 2,4-DNP first (carbonyl or not) → Tollen's (aldehyde?) → Fehling's (aliphatic?) → iodoform (methyl ketone?). Four tests, complete identification.
Why ketones resist oxidation: breaking a C–C bond would be needed, so only brutal oxidants (KMnO₄, hot HNO₃) cleave them — which is exactly why the mild Tollen's/Fehling's reagents distinguish the two families.
