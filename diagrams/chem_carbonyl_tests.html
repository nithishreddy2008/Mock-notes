CARBONYL CHEMICAL DISTINCTIONS & CARBOXYLIC ACID ACIDITY

Tollens', Fehling's, Iodoform, Schiff's Reagent, Carboxylate Resonance, and Substituent pKa Hierarchies

Tollens' Silver Mirror
Aldehyde Check
Reagent & Chemistry
Ammoniacal Silver Nitrate: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ OH⁻. Mild oxidant in basic medium.
Observation
Shiny Silver Mirror (Ag↓) coating tube wall RCHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + 2Ag↓ + 4NH₃ + 2H₂O
Diagnostic Scope
• Positive: All aldehydes (aliphatic & aromatic) + Formic Acid (HCOOH).
• Negative: Ketones are completely unreactive.
Fehling's Solution Test
Aliphatic Specific
Reagent Composition
Fehling A: aq. CuSO₄ (blue).
Fehling B: alkaline Rochelle salt (sodium potassium tartrate + NaOH).
Observation
Red-Brown Precipitate of Cuprous Oxide (Cu₂O↓) RCHO + 2Cu²⁺ + 5OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + Cu₂O↓ + 3H₂O
CUET Distinction Trap
• Positive: Aliphatic aldehydes (CH₃CHO, etc.).
• Negative: Benzaldehyde (C₆H₅CHO) fails! Ketones fail.
Iodoform Test (CHI₃)
CH₃-C=O Fingerprint
Reagent & Mechanism
I₂ + NaOH (generates NaOI). Triiodinates the α-methyl carbon followed by base cleavage.
Observation
Bright Yellow Precipitate (CHI₃↓, mp 119°C) Antiseptic hospital-like odour.
Eligible Substrates
• Ethanal (CH₃CHO) is the ONLY aldehyde.
• All methyl ketones (R-CO-CH₃).
• Ethanol & 2° alcohols with CH₃-CH(OH)- unit.
Schiff's & NaHCO₃ Tests
Functional Proof
Schiff's Reagent Test
Rosaniline hydrochloride decolourised with SO₂. Aldehydes restore intense pink/magenta colour instantaneously; ketones do not.
Sodium Bicarbonate Effervescence
Carboxylic acids react with sat. NaHCO₃ releasing brisk effervescence of CO₂↑ gas: R-COOH + NaHCO₃ → R-COONa + H₂O + CO₂↑ Phenols (pKa ~10) fail this test; only picric acid responds!

Carboxylate Resonance vs Phenoxide Resonance Truth

Why Carboxylic Acids are more acidic than Phenols:
• In the carboxylate ion (R-COO⁻), the negative charge is delocalized over two identical, highly electronegative Oxygen atoms. Both resonance structures are completely equivalent with identical C-O bond lengths (127 pm).
• In phenoxide (C₆H₅O⁻), negative charge delocalizes onto less electronegative Carbon atoms (ortho/para). Non-equivalent resonance structures are less stable.
Special Behavior of Formic Acid (HCOOH):
Formic acid contains both a carboxyl group (-COOH) and a pseudo-aldehyde group (-CHO)! Hence, HCOOH uniquely reduces Tollens' and Fehling's reagents.

Substituent Inductive & Resonance Effects on pKa CUET Ranking

• Electron-Withdrawing Groups (-I, -M): Disperse negative charge, stabilizing RCOO⁻ → Increases Acidity (Decreases pKa).
• Electron-Donating Groups (+I): Intensify negative charge, destabilizing RCOO⁻ → Decreases Acidity (Increases pKa).
• Position Factor: Inductive effect is distance-dependent (α-halo > β-halo > γ-halo).
ACIDITY RANKING (HIGHEST TO LOWEST):
CF₃COOH > CCl₃COOH > CHCl₂COOH > NO₂CH₂COOH > NC-CH₂COOH > FCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > BrCH₂COOH > HCOOH > C₆H₅COOH > CH₃COOH
*Benzoic acid (pKa 4.20) is stronger than acetic acid (pKa 4.76) due to sp² phenyl ring (-I), but weaker than formic acid (pKa 3.75).
