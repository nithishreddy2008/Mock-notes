1947'S THREE FRONTS — PARTITION, PRINCELY STATES, LANGUAGE
FRONT 1

Partition's Legacy & Refugees

The human cost: lakhs killed in communal violence; about 80 lakh refugees crossed into India from Pakistan — the largest migration of the century.

The challenge: shelter, food, jobs and identity for ruined families — Punjabis and Sindhis in the west, Bengalis in the east — while cities like Delhi and Calcutta overflowed.

The response: refugee camps, rehabilitation ministries, citizenship guarantees; the trauma also shaped India's secular promise — Muslims who stayed were equal citizens.

FRONT 2

565 Princely States

The players: Sardar Patel + V.P. Menon (States Department) used persuasion, pressure and the Instrument of Accession — defence, external affairs, communications to the Union.

Most signed by 15 Aug 1947. Three holdouts became legends:

Junagadh: Muslim ruler, Hindu majority — plebiscite (1948) joined India · Hyderabad: Nizam + Razakars — "police action" Sept 1948 · Kashmir: tribal invasion Oct 1947 — Hari Singh acceded, war followed.

FRONT 3

Language & the States Map

The demand: draw state borders on language — the Congress itself had promised it (1921 linguistic provinces).

The hesitation: after Partition, language-based division looked dangerous — Dhar Commission (1948) and JVP Committee said NO to language as the basis.

The turning point: Potti Sriramulu's 58-day fast for a Telugu state — he died on 15 Dec 1952; Andhra State (1953) followed, then the SRC map of 1956: 14 states + 6 UTs.

The reorganisation ladder — five rungs to remember

1

1948 — Dhar Commission: favours administrative convenience, rejects language as the basis for states.

2

1948-49 — JVP Committee (Jawaharlal, Vallabhbhai, Pattabhi): reconfirms the rejection — but public feeling keeps building.

3

1952 — Potti Sriramulu dies after a 58-day fast; riots force the Centre's hand; Andhra State, Oct 1953 — the first linguistic state.

4

1953-55 — States Reorganisation Commission: Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru — language accepted WITH unity safeguards.

5

1956 — States Reorganisation Act: 14 states + 6 union territories; later splits: Bombay → Maharashtra + Gujarat (1960), Nagaland (1963), Punjab → Haryana (1966).

Language politics — the long duel

Hindi's status: the Constitution made Hindi in Devanagari the official language — with a 15-year transition from English.
Nehru's guarantee (1959): English stays as long as non-Hindi states want it — a promise that calmed the south.
Official Languages Act 1963: English continues beyond 1965 — the legal fix.
Jan 1965, Madras: anti-Hindi agitation erupts anyway; deaths, self-immolations — Hindi never imposed.
Three-language formula: Hindi + English + a regional language — the compromise schools still carry.

TRAP 1

Instrument of Accession = limited subjects. Rulers ceded only defence, external affairs and communications — NOT full legislative power. That is why Article 370's special status grew from the accession terms.

TRAP 2

Andhra STATE 1953 ≠ Andhra PRADESH 1956. 1953 carved Andhra out of Madras; the 1956 Act rebuilt it as Andhra Pradesh by adding Telangana (Hyderabad region).

TRAP 3

The Commission that accepted language was the SRC (1953-55) — NOT the Dhar Commission or JVP Committee, which REJECTED language as the basis. Pair the right body with the right verdict.
