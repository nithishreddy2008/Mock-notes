1. Democratisation in Pakistan & Bangladesh
A. Pakistan: Army-Democracy Pendulum
Military Dictators: Ayub Khan (1958), Yahya Khan (1969), Zia-ul-Haq (1977), Pervez Musharraf (1999 coup).
Civilian Interludes: Z.A. Bhutto (1971–77); 1988–1999 party competition between Benazir Bhutto (PPP) & Nawaz Sharif (PML-N).
Institutional Drag: Dominance of military, intelligence (ISI), orthodox clergy, and landlord elites undermines stability.
B. Bangladesh: From Mukti Joddha to Multi-Party
Birth of the Nation: Dec 1971 liberation war; Sheikh Mujibur Rahman introduced 1972 secular socialist constitution.
Military Rule: Mujib assassinated in 1975; military regimes of Ziaur Rahman and H.M. Ershad (ousted in 1990).
Democratic Era: Continuous democratic elections since 1991, contested between Awami League and BNP.
2. Himalayan Kingdoms & Island Republics
A. Nepal: From Hindu Monarchy to Republic
1990 Movement: King Birendra accepted constitutional monarchy.
Triangular Struggle: Royalist forces vs Parliamentary parties vs Maoist communist insurgents (1996–2006).
Abolition of Monarchy: April 2006 mass democracy uprising stripped King Gyanendra of power; Constituent Assembly abolished monarchy in 2008.
B. Sri Lanka: Ethnic Conflict & LTTE
Majoritarian Strain: Sinhala majority dominance alienated Sri Lankan Tamils.
Armed Militancy: LTTE (Velupillai Prabhakaran) demanded separate Tamil Eelam; Indian Peace Keeping Force (IPKF, 1987–90); LTTE military defeat in May 2009.
C. Maldives & Bhutan
Maldives: Sultanate until 1968; presidential rule under Gayoom; multi-party democracy legalized in June 2005.
Bhutan: Constitutional monarchy; pioneered Gross National Happiness (GNH); first multi-party elections in 2008.
3. Bilateral Ties & Multilateral Integration
A. India-Pakistan Friction
Nuclear Tests: Pokhran-II & Chagai Hills (May 1998); Kargil War (1999); cross-border terror disputes vs CBMs.
B. Bilateral Accords & Gujral Doctrine
Water Treaties: Indus Waters Treaty (1960); Ganga Water Sharing Accord (Farakka Barrage, 1996).
1950 Nepal/Bhutan Treaties: Open borders and free movement.
Gujral Doctrine (1996): Non-reciprocal concessions to small neighbors without demanding immediate quid pro quo.
C. SAARC & SAFTA Framework
SAARC (Dec 1985): Founded in Dhaka by 7 nations; Afghanistan joined as 8th member in 2007; Secretariat in Kathmandu.
SAFTA (2004/2006): Aims to lower regional tariffs by 20%; hampered by Indo-Pak political gridlock.
Core Definition of South Asia: Seven core nations (India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Maldives) + Afghanistan (joined SAARC 2007). China is NOT part of South Asia!
Peace Accords: Operation Cactus (1988 Maldives rescue); Indo-Sri Lanka Accord (1987).
