Vijayanagara Empire: Politics, Economy and Architecture (c. 1336 – 1565 CE)

1. Foundation & Colin Mackenzie
1336 & 1800 CE
  • Origins: Founded in 1336 CE by brothers Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama dynasty on the Tungabhadra river.
  • Sacred Mythology: Capital established near sacred hills linked to monkey kingdom (Kishkindha) and shrine of local mother goddess Pampadevi.
  • Rediscovery: Ruins of Hampi brought to light in 1800 by Colonel Colin Mackenzie (surveyor and first Surveyor General of India).
  • Foreign Travellers: Documented by Nicolo de Conti (1420), Abdur Razzaq (1440s), Duarte Barbosa, Domingo Paes, and Fernao Nuniz (16th c.).
★ Core Axis: Imperial Power & Cultural Synthesis
Statecraft & Divine Kingship
  • Hindu Suratrana: Rulers claimed divine delegation to rule on behalf of guardian deity Virupaksha; styled themselves "Sultans among Hindu Kings".
  • Raichur Doab Wars: Continuous geopolitical contest against Bahmani Sultanate and Deccan Sultanates for control of fertile river basins and ports.
  • Mahanavami Legitimacy: Rulers asserted ritual sovereignty during the 10-day Autumn Navaratri festival through royal inspection and tribute.
  • Architectural Synthesis: Blended traditional South Indian Dravidian stone temple traditions with Indo-Islamic vaulted arches and domes.
2. Dynasties & Krishnadeva Raya
Zenith to Talikota
  • Four Dynasties: Sangama (1336–1485), Saluva (1485–1505), Tuluva (1505–1570), and Aravidu (1570–1646).
  • Krishnadeva Raya (r. 1509–1529): Greatest Tuluva ruler; captured Raichur doab (1512); authored political treatise Amuktamalyada in Telugu.
  • Patronage & Townships: Built the suburb Nagalapuram (named after mother); constructed the hall and Eastern Gopuram of Virupaksha temple.
  • Battle of Talikota (1565): Allied Deccan Sultans (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar) routed Rama Raya's army at Rakshasi-Tangadi; city destroyed.
3. Amaranayaka Feudal System
Military Administration
  • Amaranayaka Framework: Derived from Sultanate *Iqta* system; military chiefs (*nayakas*) administered designated territories (*amaram*).
  • Taxes & Obligations: Collected revenue from peasants; maintained stipulated contingent of cavalry, war horses, and war elephants.
  • Imperial Control: Sent annual cash tribute; personally attended court at Mahanavami with gifts; king exercised power to transfer them.
  • War Horse Trade: Critical reliance on imported Arabian horses; managed by local merchants (kudirai chettis) and Portuguese traders.
4. Hydraulic & Defensive Grid
7 Concentric Walls
  • Tungabhadra Basin: Natural amphitheater surrounded by granite boulder hills; seasonal rainwater streams dammed into reservoirs.
  • Canals & Tanks: Kamalapuram tank irrigated fields and piped water to Royal Centre; Hiriya canal watered the central valley.
  • Seven Lines of Forts: Abdur Razzaq noted walls encircling not just the citadel, but agricultural fields, orchards, and forests to resist sieges.
  • Dry Masonry: Built with wedge-shaped granite stones using mortice-and-tenon joints without any mortar or cementing paste.
5. Sacred & Royal Architecture
Temples & Palaces
  • Virupaksha Temple: Ancient Shaivite shrine; pillared hall used for marriage of deities; central painted ceilings.
  • Vitthala Temple: Dedicated to Vishnu; iconic monolithic Stone Chariot and 56 musical acoustic pillars.
  • Mahanavami Dibba: Massive 40-ft high stone platform (11,000 sq ft) with carved bas-reliefs of horse riders, dancers, and elephants.
  • Secular Palaces: Lotus Mahal (council chamber with cusped arches), vaulted Elephant Stables, and Hazara Rama temple.
