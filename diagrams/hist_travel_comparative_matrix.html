Themes in Indian History Part II · Chapter 5
Through the Eyes of Travellers: Comparative Matrix of Accounts
Systematic comparative analysis of Al-Biruni, Ibn Battuta, and François Bernier across origin, textual methodology, and societal perceptions

1. Al-Biruni (c. 973 – 1048 CE)
11th Century · Khwarizm
Text:
Kitab al-Hind (Arabic, 80 chapters)
Context:
Brought to Ghazni by Sultan Mahmud (1017)
Primary Method:
Textual Indology (Sanskrit texts, Vedas, Puranas)
Key Observations & Perspectives
  • Three Barriers to Understanding: Sanskrit language complexity, religious insularity/prejudices of Brahmanas, and psychological self-absorption of local society.
  • Geometric Textual Method: Each chapter began with a question, followed by a description based on Sanskrit traditions, and concluded with a comparison to other civilizations (ancient Greece/Persia).
  • Critique of Pollution: Rejected the Brahmanical concept of untouchable ritual pollution, arguing that natural law ensures that anything impure strives to regain original purity (sun cleanses air, salt purifies sea).
  • Social Divisions: Noted 4 varnas and 8 categories of crafts-people (*Antyaja*) outside the varna system, who rendered essential civic services.
2. Ibn Battuta (1304 – 1377 CE)
14th Century · Tangier (Morocco)
Text:
Rihla (Arabic, "Travels")
Context:
Qazi of Delhi under Muhammad bin Tughlaq
Primary Method:
Empirical observation, ethnographic interviews
Key Observations & Perspectives
  • Encounter with Unfamiliar: Astonished by the Coconut (compared to a human head with eyes and brain) and Paan / Betel (leaf cultivated like vine, chewed with areca nut and lime).
  • Vibrant Urban Network: Described Delhi as a vast city with 28 massive gates (*Darwaza*); highlighted Daulatabad's bustling market for musicians (*Tarababad*).
  • Efficiency of Postal System: Documented the state communication grid: horse post (uluq, every 4 miles) and faster foot post (dawa, 3 stations per mile; news reached Delhi in 5 days instead of 50).
  • Slaves & Domestic Labor: Noted widespread domestic slavery, female singers, concubines, and slave couriers employed for royal surveillance and service.
3. François Bernier (1620 – 1688 CE)
17th Century · France
Text:
Travels in the Mogul Empire (French)
Context:
Physician to Prince Dara Shukoh & Danishmand Khan
Primary Method:
Binary Eurocentric comparative political theory
Key Observations & Perspectives
  • Absence of Private Property in Land: Postulated that the Mughal Emperor was the sole proprietor of all land, claiming this ruined agriculture and impoverished peasants.
  • Binary Opposition: Systematically compared India unfavorably to France; depicted India as having only "beggars and barbarians" ruled by a "king of kings".
  • Urban Critique ("Camp Towns"): Dismissed flourishing Mughal cities as transient "camp towns" dependent entirely upon royal imperial military movements.
  • Global Historiographical Impact: His flawed thesis of crown ownership influenced Montesquieu's concept of Oriental Despotism and Karl Marx's Asiatic Mode of Production.
Historiographical Takeaway: Travellers did not passively document India; their perceptions were filtered through distinct linguistic, religious, and political paradigms.
