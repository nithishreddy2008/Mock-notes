Rural Resistance & Ecological Transitions (1780–1878)
Chronological trajectory: the battle of the hoe and plough in Rajmahal, the Santhal Hul, and the Deccan peasant uprising

CUET UG History · Unit 9
1780s
Paharias & The Hoe in Rajmahal Hills
• Francis Buchanan documented the Paharias living around the Rajmahal hills. • Practiced shifting cultivation using the hoe (kural); gathered mahua and silk cocoons. • Augustus Cleveland (1780s) proposed a pacification policy granting monthly allowances to chiefs.
Ecological Symbol
The Hoe (Shifting Forest Cultivation)
1832
Demarcation of Damin-i-Koh
• British invited Santhals to settle the Rajmahal valleys to practice settled plough agriculture. • Demarcated Damin-i-Koh ("skirts of the hills") with masonry boundary pillars. • Santhal villages surged from 40 in 1838 to over 1,473 by 1851.
Ecological Symbol
The Plough (Settled Agrarian Frontier)
1855–56
The Santhal Hul (Rebellion)
• Moneylenders (dikus) charged exorbitant interest (up to 500%); state demanded heavy cash revenue. • Led by brothers Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu claiming divine inspiration from Thakurji. • British crushed rebellion with military force; subsequently carved out Santhal Pargana district.
Administrative Outcome
Creation of Santhal Pargana (5,500 sq mi)
1861–65
American Civil War & Cotton Boom
• Outbreak of American Civil War cut off raw cotton supplies to British textile mills in Lancashire. • Britain turned to Bombay Deccan; triggered massive Cotton Boom. • Sahukars extended lavish credit to ryots, financing export cultivation.
Credit Condition
Booming Export Advances & Speculation
May 1875
The Deccan Riots (Supa & Poona)
• End of Civil War collapsed cotton prices; moneylenders abruptly stopped credit. • The Limitation Law of 1859 (bonds valid for only 3 years) led sahukars to manipulate debts. • Uprising began at Supa (Poona district); peasants burned bahikhatas (account books).
Peasant Action
Seizure & Burning of Debt Bonds
1878
Deccan Riots Commission Report
• Bombay Government set up an official commission of inquiry. • Produced voluminous report presented to the British Parliament in 1878. • Exonerated colonial revenue demands; laid entire blame on usurious moneylenders.
Colonial Verdict
Blamed Moneylenders, Absolved State
The Battle of the Hoe and the Plough
The conflict between the Paharias (the hoe) and Santhals (the plough) symbolized the colonial state's drive to transform shifting forest hunters into settled, taxable revenue-paying subjects.
Debt Manipulation & The 1859 Limitation Law
Enacted to protect borrowers by limiting loan bonds to three years, the law backfired: sahukars forced ryots to sign new bonds every three years, compounding accrued interest directly into principal.
