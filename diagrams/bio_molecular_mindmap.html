1. DNA Structure & Chromatin Packaging

Duplex & Nucleosome
  • Chargaff's Equimolar Rules: In dsDNA, $[A]=[T]$ and $[G]=[C]$, hence $[A+G]=[T+C]$ (Purines = Pyrimidines). Base ratio $(A+T)/(G+C)$ is species-specific.
  • B-DNA Helix Dimensions: Pitch = 3.4 nm (34 Å); 10 base pairs per turn; distance between adjacent base pairs = 0.34 nm (3.4 Å); right-handed helix.
  • Nucleosome Core: Histone octamer of $(H2A, H2B, H3, H4)_2$ wrapped by ~200 bp of negatively charged DNA; sealed by external linker histone H1.
  • Chromatin Architecture: Euchromatin is loosely coiled, light-staining, and transcriptionally active. Heterochromatin is densely packed, dark-staining, and inactive.

2. The Search for Genetic Material

Classic Proofs
  • Griffith Transformation (1928): Heat-killed virulent Smooth ($S$) Streptococcus pneumoniae transformed living non-virulent Rough ($R$) into virulent $S$-strain in mice.
  • Avery, MacLeod & McCarty (1944): Biochemical characterization proved only DNase abolished transforming activity (Proteases and RNases had no inhibitory effect).
  • Hershey & Chase Blender Experiment (1952): Unequivocal proof using bacteriophage T2 in E. coli: 32P labeled DNA entered bacterial pellet, whereas 35S labeled protein coats remained in supernatant.
  • RNA World Hypothesis: RNA was the first genetic material and biocatalyst (ribozymes); DNA evolved chemically with 2'-deoxyribose and thymine for greater stability.

3. Semi-Conservative DNA Replication

Enzymatic Engine
  • Semi-Conservative Proof: Meselson & Stahl (1958) using 15N/14N in E. coli via CsCl equilibrium density centrifugation; Taylor using 3H-thymidine in Vicia faba.
  • Replication Fork Geometry: Leading Strand synthesized continuously 5'→3' toward fork. Lagging Strand synthesized discontinuously away from fork as Okazaki fragments.
  • Enzymatic Engine: Helicase unwinds; Gyrase relaxes supercoils; Primase provides RNA primer; DNA Pol III polymerizes at ~2,000 bp/s; DNA Pol I removes primers; Ligase seals nicks.
  • Substrate Energetics: Deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs) serve dual roles: active monomer substrates and ATP-equivalent pyrophosphate energy donors.

4. Transcription & Post-Transcriptional Processing

Central Dogma
  • Transcription Unit: Defined by Promoter (RNA Pol binding), Structural Gene (template $3' \to 5'$, coding $5' \to 3'$), and Terminator. RNA Pol requires σ factor for initiation, ρ factor for termination.
  • Eukaryotic RNA Polymerases: Pol I synthesizes 28S, 18S, 5.8S rRNAs; Pol II synthesizes heterogeneous nuclear RNA (hnRNA/mRNA precursor); Pol III synthesizes tRNA, 5S rRNA, snRNA.
  • Post-Transcriptional Processing of hnRNA: (1) Capping: Methyl guanosine triphosphate ($m^7G_{ppp}$) added to 5'-end. (2) Tailing: Polyadenylation of 200–300 adenylate residues at 3'-end. (3) Splicing: Spliceosomes excise non-coding introns and ligate coding exons.

5. Genetic Code & Protein Biosynthesis

Triplet & Ribosome
  • Salient Features of Genetic Code: Triplet (64 codons); 61 sense codons specify 20 amino acids; Degenerate (1 amino acid coded by multiple codons); Unambiguous; Universal.
  • Initiator & Terminators: AUG has dual function (initiator codon & specifies Methionine). UAA (ochre), UAG (amber), UGA (opal) are non-sense stop codons.
  • tRNA (Adapter Molecule): Secondary cloverleaf structure; tertiary compact inverted L-shape. Possesses anticodon loop complementary to mRNA codon and 3'-CCA amino acid acceptor end.
  • Translation Cascade: Aminoacylation of tRNA → Initiation → Elongation (Peptidyl transferase 23S rRNA in bacteria ribozyme) → Termination via Release Factors.

6. Gene Regulation, Genomics & DNA Fingerprinting

Operon & HGP
  • Lac Operon (Jacob & Monod): Negative inducible system. Active repressor binds Operator ($O$) → operon OFF. Inducer (Allolactose) inactivates repressor → operon ON, transcribing $z$ (β-gal), $y$ (permease), $a$ (transacetylase).
  • Human Genome Project (1990–2003): Genome contains 3,164.7 million bp; ~30,000 genes; average gene has 3,000 bp (dystrophin largest at 2.4 million bp); Chromosome 1 has most genes (2,968), Y has fewest (231); <2% codes for proteins.
  • DNA Fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys): Identifies individual genomic uniqueness utilizing Variable Number Tandem Repeats (VNTRs) / minisatellites via Southern blot hybridization.
