Elections & The Congress System
Electoral Hegemony in a Competitive Democracy
1st General Election (1951–52) Sukumar Sen
  • EC set up in Jan 1950; 1st CEC: Sukumar Sen.
  • 17.32 crore voters, 85% illiterate; separate ballot box for each candidate with party symbol.
  • Hailed as the "biggest gamble in history"; proved democracy could flourish in a poor, illiterate society.
First Three Lok Sabha Tallies Sweeping Wins
  • 1952: Congress won 364 / 489 seats (45% vote share); CPI second with 16 seats.
  • 1957: Congress won 371 / 494 seats; CPI won 27 seats.
  • 1962: Congress won 361 / 494 seats; CPI won 29 seats.
Rajni Kothari's "Congress System" Coalition Nature
  • Social Coalition: Accommodated peasants, industrial tycoons, urban elites, dalits, minorities.
  • Ideological Coalition: Encompassed socialists, capitalists, conservatives, radicals.
  • Factional Balancing: Internal factions served as an internal opposition, maintaining ideological equilibrium.
2
Socialist & Communist Formations
CSP, PSP, CPI & The 1957 Kerala Historic Milestone
The Socialist Party (1948) Democratic Socialism
  • Originated as Congress Socialist Party (CSP, 1934) inside Congress; separated in 1948.
  • Founding Leaders: Acharya Narendra Deva, Jayaprakash Narayan (JP), Ram Manohar Lohia, Asoka Mehta.
  • Later evolved into Praja Socialist Party (PSP) and Samyukta Socialist Party (SSP); split over alliance with Congress.
Communist Party of India (CPI) Marxist-Leninist
  • Inspired by 1917 Bolshevik Revolution; early 1920s origin; organized armed Telangana Peasant Rebellion (1948–51).
  • Abandoned armed insurgency in 1951 to enter parliamentary electoral path.
  • Key Leaders: A.K. Gopalan, S.A. Dange, P.C. Joshi, E.M.S. Namboodiripad.
The Kerala 1957 Milestone First Non-Congress Govt
  • CPI won 60 of 126 seats in Kerala assembly; formed government under E.M.S. Namboodiripad (first democratically elected communist government in the world).
  • Dismissed in 1959 under controversial use of Article 356.
  • Split in 1964 into CPI (pro-Soviet) and CPI(M) (pro-China/independent).

Right-Wing, Liberal & Dalit Formations
Bharatiya Jana Sangh, Swatantra Party & Ambedkar's RPI
Bharatiya Jana Sangh (BJS, 1951) Cultural Nationalism
  • Founded in October 1951 by Dr. Shyama Prasad Mukherjee; ideological roots in RSS and Hindu Mahasabha.
  • Core Ideology: "One Country, One Nation, One Culture" (Akhand Bharat); Hindi as official language; opposition to cow slaughter; pro-nuclear weapons.
  • Deendayal Upadhyaya: Propounded the philosophy of Integral Humanism (indigenous holistic development).
The Swatantra Party (1959) Free Enterprise
  • Formed in August 1959 by C. Rajagopalachari (Rajaji), Minoo Masani, N.G. Ranga, and K.M. Munshi.
  • Ideology: Classic economic liberalism; opposed state-led planning, public sector monopolies, land ceiling, and "License-Permit Raj."
  • Advocated pro-Western foreign policy; supported by rich landlords, princes, and industrialists.
Dr. Ambedkar & Dalit Politics Social Justice
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar founded the Independent Labour Party (1936) and Scheduled Castes Federation (SCF, 1942).
  • Laid foundation for the Republican Party of India (RPI); embraced Buddhism with thousands of followers in Oct 1956.
