Agriculture I — GM Crops & Bt Cotton

GMO: an organism carrying a gene placed there by human effort — the gene works in its new host
Bt source: Bacillus thuringiensis, a soil bacterium that makes cry-protein crystals
cryIAc kills cotton bollworms · cryIIAb kills corn borer
Protoxin sits as inactive crystals until the insect's alkaline gut switches it on

Agriculture II — RNAi & Tissue Culture

RNAi: complementary dsRNA binds a specific mRNA and silences it — no protein is made
NCERT story: Meloidogyne incognita in tobacco — nematode genes in via Agrobacterium → sense + antisense RNA → parasite dies
Totipotency: one living cell rebuilds the whole plant — explant → callus → plantlets (micropropagation)
Extras: virus-free plants from meristems · somatic hybrids by protoplast fusion (pomato)

Medicine I — Human Insulin

Structure: chain A (21 amino acids) + chain B (30), joined by disulphide bonds
Made as proinsulin first — the extra C-peptide is cut out during maturation
rDNA route: A and B chains built separately in E. coli, then joined — product: humulin
Old route: pancreas of slaughtered cattle/pigs — costly, and caused allergies

Medicine II — Gene Therapy & Diagnosis

ADA deficiency: adenosine deaminase enzyme missing → immune system fails
1990, first gene therapy: patient's lymphocytes + functional ADA gene (retroviral vector), grown outside, returned
Not permanent — lymphocytes die; the marrow-cell route can cure for life
Early detection: PCR (amplifies pathogen DNA) · ELISA (antigen–antibody) · tagged probe + autoradiography

Transgenic Animals

Definition: DNA manipulated to carry and express a foreign gene; about 95% are mice
Uses: gene regulation study · disease models (cancer, cystic fibrosis) · vaccine safety (polio) · faster toxicity tests · biologicals
Rosie (1997): transgenic cow with human alpha-lactalbumin in milk — 2.4 g per litre

Ethics, Biopiracy & Patents

GEAC (Genetic Engineering Appraisal Committee): Indian body that clears GM research and GM releases
Biopiracy: bio-resources or traditional knowledge used without authorisation or payment
Cases: Basmati (RiceTec, 1997 — claims cut after India objected) · turmeric patent revoked 1997 · neem revoked 2000
India's shield: Biological Diversity Act, 2002

COMMON ERRORS & TRAPS

Bt toxin is NOT pre-activated — it switches on only in the insect's alkaline gut; our acidic gut + no receptor keeps us safe
RNAi silences mRNA, never DNA — and it needs a matching (complementary) dsRNA
Rosie's protein is alpha-lactalbumin, not insulin — and GEAC is an Indian committee, not an international one
