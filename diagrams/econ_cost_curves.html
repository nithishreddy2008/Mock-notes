PRODUCT CURVES & COST CURVES — THE TWO MANDATORY DIAGRAMS

Product curves — one variable input (labour)

Top: Total Product (TP)  ·  Bottom: MP and AP, with the three phases of the law of variable proportions
TP (total product) MP (marginal product) AP (average product)
Read it once: TP rises slowly, then steeply, then flattens where MP = 0. MP rises first, peaks, falls, and cuts AP exactly at AP's maximum — then dives below zero (Phase III: too many workers on the same land).

Cost curves — short run (U-shaped family)

AFC always falls; AVC, AC and MC are U-shaped; MC cuts AVC and AC at their minimum points
AFC AVC AC MC
Why the U-shape: falling AFC + spreading fixed cost pull AC down at first; eventually diminishing returns push MC up hard, and MC drags AVC and AC back up. MC is the fastest mover — it bottoms first, then AVC, then AC.

TRAP 1

MC crosses AVC and AC at their MINIMUM points — exactly there, not before, not after. Any diagram showing otherwise is wrong.

TRAP 2

AFC never touches the x-axis. TFC spread over more and more units shrinks forever but never reaches zero.

TRAP 3

A rational producer stays in Phase II of the law of variable proportions — Phase III means MP is negative: fewer workers would produce MORE.
