d & f BLOCK ELEMENTS
UNIT IV · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Position & Configuration

d-block: groups 3–12, filling (n−1)d — three series: 3d (Sc–Zn), 4d (Y–Cd), 5d (La, Hf–Hg)
Config: (n−1)d¹⁷⁵ ns¹⁷²; exceptions Cr = 3d⁵4s¹, Cu = 3d¹⁵4s¹ (half/full-filled stability)
Transition element (strict): an atom/ion with a partly filled d subshell — Zn, Cd, Hg are d-block but usually not "transition"
f-block: lanthanoids (4f, Ce–Lu) and actinoids (5f, Th–Lr), placed below the table

Size, Contraction & Ionisation

Atomic size dips mid-series then rises slightly (e–e₂ repulsion in d subshell wins at the end)
Ionic size shrinks with oxidation state: Fe³⁺ < Fe²⁺ — more charge pulls harder
Lanthanoid contraction: poor 4f shielding shrinks the 5d series to near-4d sizes — hence Zr ≈ Hf twins
Ionisation enthalpy rises across; second-row (4d) metals are bigger and more easily oxidised

Oxidation States & Potentials

Variable states from ns + (n−1)d sharing: Mn spans +2 → +7; Sc³⁺/Zn²⁺ barely vary
Down a group, higher states stabilise — opposite of p-block: CrO₄²⁻ strong, Mo/W even more so
Cu's E°(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V (positive!) — high atomisation + ionisation costs; Cu refuses dilute acids
M³⁺/M²⁺: only Fe (+0.77) and Mn (+1.57) and Co (+1.97-ish) are strong oxidisers; V is negative (V²⁺ is the stable friend)

Magnetism & Colour

Spin-only moment: μ = √(n(n+2)) BM — n = unpaired electrons; Fe³⁺ (d⁵) ≈ 5.92 BM max
Colour = d–d transitions: Cu²⁺ (d⁵) blue, Ni²⁺ (d⁸) green; Sc³⁺ (d₀) and Zn²⁺ (d¹⁵) are colourless
Charge-transfer colour: MnO₄⁻ and CrO₄²⁻ are intensely coloured with d₀/d⁵ ions — electron hops, not d–d
Complexes: small size + high charge + vacant d orbitals → coordination numbers 4–6 everywhere

Superpowers of the d-Block

Catalysis: Fe (Haber NH₃), V₂O₅ (contact H₂SO₄), Ni (hydrogenation), TiCl₄/AlMe₃ (Ziegler plastics)
Interstitial compounds: small atoms (H, C, N) hide in lattice holes — steel, cast iron: hard, conducting, non-stoichiometric
Alloys: similar radii let metals swap freely — ferrochrome, stainless steel, bronze
Oxides: basic → amphoteric → acidic across the series and as oxidation state climbs (MnO basic, Mn₂O₇ acidic)

The f-Block Twins

Lanthanoids: all +3 mainly; Eu²⁺/Tb⁴⁺ exist (+2 half-filled, +4 half-filled logic); colours from f–f jumps
Contraction cause: 4f electrons shield poorly — each step pulls the shell in; consequences in the 5d row
Actinoids: 5f is bigger and lazier — oxidation states from +3 up to +7; all radioactive
Applications: Mish-metal lighter flints, Nd magnets, Sm–Co magnets, thorium in nuclear fuel, CeO₂ glass polish

WATCH-OUTS THAT SET MCQ TRAPS

"d-block = transition" is FALSE: Zn/Cd/Hg have full d¹⁵ in every common state
Cr and Cu steal the 4s electron — configurations 3d⁵4s¹ and 3d¹⁵4s¹
Copper dissolves in neither dilute HCl nor H₂SO₄ — its positive E° blocks it (oxidising acids like HNO₃ still work)
KMnO₄'s electron count depends on pH: 5 (acid), 3 (mild alkaline), 1 (strong alkaline) — check the medium first
