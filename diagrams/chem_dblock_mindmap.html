d & f BLOCK ELEMENTS

(n−1)d & (n−2)f Electron Filling

1. Electronic Configurations

Foundations
General Formula: (n−1)d1–10 ns1–2. Filling penultimate d-orbitals.
Stability Exceptions: Cr ([Ar] 3d5 4s1) and Cu ([Ar] 3d10 4s1) due to half-filled and fully-filled exchange energy.
Strict Transition Definition: Element with incompletely filled d-subshell in ground state or any stable oxidation state.
Pseudo-Transition: Zn, Cd, Hg have d10 in elemental and ionic states; not typical transition metals.

2. Radii & Physical Trends

Periodic Curves
Atomic Radii Curve: Decreases from Sc to Cr (rising Zeff), remains steady across Fe-Co-Ni, then slight uptick at Cu-Zn (electron repulsion).
High Enthalpy of Atomisation: Arises from strong metallic bonding involving both ns and unpaired (n−1)d electrons. Max at middle (Cr, Mo, W).
Density Jump in 5d: Radii of 4d ≈ 5d due to Lanthanoid Contraction, but mass increases → densities double (Os, Ir ≈ 22.6 g/cm3).
Ionisation Enthalpy: Gradual increase; irregular jumps in IE2 and IE3 due to d0, d5, d10 stable configurations.

3. Oxidation States & E° Couples

Redox Dynamics
Variable States: Close energy of (n−1)d and ns allows both to participate. Maximum states at center (Mn spans +2 to +7).
Heavy Metal Stability: Unlike p-block, heavier 4d/5d congeners favor higher oxidation states (e.g. WVI stable; CrVI strongly oxidising).
E°(M2+/M) Cu Anomaly: Cu has positive E° (+0.34 V); high ΔaH° and IE are not compensated by hydration enthalpy; does not liberate H2 from acids.
E°(M3+/M2+) Trends: High positive for Mn (+1.57 V, d4 → stable d5) and Co (+1.97 V); negative for Cr (−0.41 V, stable t2g3 Cr3+ is reducing).

4. Magnetism, Colour & Complexes

Spectra & Bonding
Spin-Only Moment: μ = √(n(n+2)) BM (n = unpaired electrons). Max for d5 Mn2+, Fe3+ (μ ≈ 5.92 BM).
Origin of Colour: d–d transitions in partially filled d-orbitals split by ligands. d0 (Sc3+, Ti4+) and d10 (Zn2+, Cu+) are colourless.
Charge-Transfer Absorption: KMnO4 (purple) and K2Cr2O7 (orange) have d0 ions; intense colour arises from LMCT, not d–d.
Complex Formation: Small cation size, high charge density, and vacant d-orbitals enable facile coordination (CN = 4, 6).

5. Catalysis, Interstitials & Alloys

Industrial Chemistry
Catalytic Activity: Ability to adopt multiple oxidation states and provide large active surface area. E.g. Fe (Haber), V2O5 (Contact), Ni (Hydrogenation).
Interstitial Compounds: Small atoms (H, C, N, B) trap in crystal interstices. Harder than pure metal, high melting points, retain conductivity, chemically inert.
Alloy Formation: Similar atomic radii (< 15% difference, Hume-Rothery rule) allow mutual lattice substitution. E.g. brass, bronze, stainless steel.
Oxide Nature: Acidity increases with oxidation state (MnO basic → Mn2O3 amphoteric → Mn2O7 strongly acidic covalent oil).

6. Inner Transition (f-Block)

Lanthanoids & Actinoids
Lanthanoid Contraction: Steady decrease in Ln3+ radii from Ce (103 pm) to Lu (86 pm) due to poor 4f shielding. Causes Zr ≈ Hf twin pairing.
Oxidation States: Typical +3 for all Ln. Special +2 (Eu2+: 4f7, Yb2+: 4f14) and +4 (Ce4+: 4f0 strong oxidiser; Tb4+: 4f7).
Basicity of Ln(OH)3: Decreases from La(OH)3 (most basic) to Lu(OH)3 (least basic) as covalent character increases.
Actinoids Comparison: 5f electrons show poorer shielding → greater actinoid contraction. Wider range of oxidation states (+3 to +7) as 5f, 6d, 7s are comparable in energy.
