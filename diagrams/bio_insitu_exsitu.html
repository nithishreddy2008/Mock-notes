In-situ vs Ex-situ Conservation & The Evil Quartet of Biodiversity Loss
NCERT Class 12 Biology · Unit X · Chapter 15
In-situ Conservation (On-Site Protection)
First Priority · Natural Habitat

Conserving the entire ecosystem so that biodiversity at all levels is protected in its natural evolution.

Biosphere Reserves (14 in India): Large protected areas zoning into Core (strictly protected, no human entry), Buffer (research & education), and Transition (sustainable human settlements).
National Parks (90 in India): Strictly reserved for wildlife betterment; strictly zero private ownership, human exploitation, forestry, or grazing allowed.
Wildlife Sanctuaries (448 in India): Protection of wild fauna; regulated human activities (timber harvesting, collecting minor forest produce) permitted without hurting wildlife.
Biodiversity Hotspots (34 Globally): Regions with exceptional species richness and high endemism. 3 cover India: Western Ghats-Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Himalaya. Cover <2% land, cut extinction by ~30%.
Sacred Groves (Tribal Refugia): Khasi & Jaintia Hills (Meghalaya), Aravalli Hills (Rajasthan), Western Ghats (Karnataka/Maharashtra), Chanda & Bastar (MP/Chhattisgarh). Last refuges for rare endemics!
Ex-situ Conservation (Off-Site Protection)
High Urgency · Specialized Care

Threatened animals and plants are taken out of their natural habitat and placed in special settings where they can be protected and given special care.

Zoological Parks & Safari Parks: Captive management of threatened wild animals for assisted breeding, genetic health, and educational awareness.
Botanical Gardens: Ex-situ cultivation and taxonomy of living rare plant collections (e.g., Royal Botanic Gardens Kew; Indian Botanical Garden, Howrah; NBRI, Lucknow).
Cryopreservation (-196°C in Liquid Nitrogen): Advanced preservation of gametes of threatened species in viable and fertile condition for indefinitely long periods.
Seed Banks & Pollen Banks: Desiccation and cold storage of seeds of different genetic strains of commercially and ecologically important plants for decades.
In Vitro Tissue Culture: Micropropagation of rare and endangered plant explants under sterile conditions to mass-produce clonal plantlets.
The "Evil Quartet" — The 4 Major Causes of Biodiversity Loss NCERT Core Concept
IUCN Red List (2004): 784 species extinct in last 500 years (338 vertebrates, 359 invertebrates, 87 plants)
1 Habitat Loss & Fragmentation

The #1 most important cause. Tropical rainforests reduced from 14% to 6% of earth's surface.

• Amazon rainforest ("lungs of the planet") being cleared for soybean cultivation and beef cattle grazing.

• Habitat fragmentation severely hits mammals and birds needing large territories and migratory routes.

2 Over-Exploitation

When human "need" turns into "greed". Over-harvesting beyond population replacement rate.

• Steller's Sea Cow (extinct due to over-hunting for meat and skin in cold Pacific waters).

• Passenger Pigeon (billions extinct due to commercial hunting in North America).

• Marine fish populations severely depleted globally.

3 Alien Species Invasions

Exotic species turning invasive, outcompeting and eliminating native endemic species.

• Nile perch introduced into Lake Victoria caused extinction of >200 native cichlid fish species.

• Invasive weeds: Parthenium (carrot grass), Lantana, and Eichhornia (water hyacinth / Terror of Bengal).

• African catfish (Clarias gariepinus) illegally introduced threatens indigenous catfishes.

4 Co-Extinctions

Obligate symbiotic dependency: When one species becomes extinct, the associated species invariably perishes.

• Host fish extinction: Host-specific monogenean/trematode parasites automatically face extinction.

• Coevolved plant-pollinator mutualism: Extinction of fig wasp leads to extinction of obligate fig tree (Ficus).

International Treaties: Earth Summit (Rio de Janeiro, 1992) · World Summit on Sustainable Development (Johannesburg, 2002; 190 countries) Recent Extinctions: Dodo (Mauritius), Quagga (Africa), Thylacine (Australia), 3 Tiger Subspecies (Bali, Javan, Caspian)
