Foreign Exchange Rate Determination & Exchange Regimes

Market Equilibrium, Currency Valuation Mechanics, and Fixed vs Flexible vs Managed Floating Systems

NCERT CLASS 12 · MACROECONOMICS
1. Market Forces: Demand & Supply
Price of Forex (₹/$)
Demand for Forex ($) Downward Sloping Curve
Demand rises when exchange rate falls (foreign goods become cheaper for Indians).
Imports of Goods & Services: Buying machinery, crude oil, software licenses from abroad.
Tourism & Education Abroad: Indian residents traveling or studying in the USA/Europe.
Unilateral Outflows & Asset Purchases: Gifts abroad, buying foreign real estate or shares.
Supply of Forex ($) Upward Sloping Curve
Supply rises when exchange rate rises (1 USD fetches more Rupees, making Indian goods attractive).
Merchandise Exports: Foreign buyers paying USD for Indian textiles, pharmaceuticals.
Foreign Inflows (FDI & FPI): Global investors bringing USD into Indian capital markets.
Remittances from NRIs: Inward family transfers by Indian diaspora living abroad.
Equilibrium Condition: The exchange rate settles where Quantity Demanded of Forex = Quantity Supplied of Forex.
2. Valuation Shifts: Market Mechanics
Floating System Changes
Currency Depreciation
Exchange Rate Rises ($1 = ₹75 → $1 = ₹83)
Rupee loses value against the Dollar due to higher demand for $ or lower supply of $.
+ Stimulates Exports (Cheaper for foreigners)
- Makes Imports Expensive (Imported inflation)
Currency Appreciation
Exchange Rate Falls ($1 = ₹83 → $1 = ₹75)
Rupee gains value against the Dollar due to higher supply of $ or lower demand for $.
+ Cheaper Imports (Lowers domestic costs)
- Hurts Exporters (Indian goods become dearer)
Critical Distinction: Market vs Official Action
Depreciation / Appreciation: Caused purely by free market forces of demand & supply under a Flexible Exchange System.
Devaluation / Revaluation: Caused by deliberate official government decree under a Fixed Exchange Rate System.
CUET Exam Trap: Do not confuse "exchange rate rises" with "domestic currency appreciates". When exchange rate $e$ (in ₹/$) goes up, the Rupee depreciates (takes more rupees to buy one dollar).
3. The Three Exchange Regimes
Institutional Structure
A. Flexible / Floating Rate System Market Determined
• Exchange rate determined completely by demand & supply without central bank interference.
• Merit: Zero need to maintain huge foreign exchange reserves; automatic BoP correction.
• Demerit: Exchange rate volatility increases trade & foreign investment uncertainty.
B. Fixed / Pegged Rate System Gold / USD Peg
• Government fixes exchange rate to a specific parity value (historical Bretton Woods System).
• Merit: Complete certainty in international contracts and planning.
• Demerit: Central Bank must maintain massive forex reserves to defend the fixed peg.
C. Managed Floating ("Dirty Floating") India's System
• Hybrid regime: Rate is market-determined, but the Central Bank (RBI) actively buys or sells USD in the open market to dampen excessive, speculative swings.
• Prevents sharp shocks to importers and exporters while avoiding rigid pegs.
Key Regimes Rule: Floating = Market Driven (Depreciation/Appreciation) · Fixed = Government Decreed (Devaluation/Revaluation) · Managed Floating = Hybrid with RBI Interventions.
