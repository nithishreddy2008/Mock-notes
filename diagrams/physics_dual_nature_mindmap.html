1. Electron Emission & Work Function
Foundations
Φ₀ = h ν₀ = h c / λ₀  (1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)
  • Emission Modes: Thermionic (heat), Field/Cold emission (strong E ~ 10⁸ V/m), Photoelectric (light), Secondary (impact).
  • Work Function (Φ₀): Minimum energy to pull an electron from metal surface. Sensitive to surface purity.
  • Extremes: Lowest for Alkali metals (Cs = 2.14 eV, K = 2.3 eV); Highest for Platinum (Pt = 5.65 eV).
2. Experimental Study & Laws
4 Empirical Laws
i_photo ∝ Intensity  |  K_max = e V₀ ∝ (ν - ν₀)
  • Photocurrent vs Intensity: Strictly linear at fixed ν > ν₀. Saturation current doubles when intensity doubles.
  • Stopping Potential (V₀): Independent of intensity; strictly linear function of radiation frequency ν.
  • Threshold Frequency (ν₀): No emission occurs if ν < ν₀, regardless of light intensity or illumination duration.
  • No Time Lag: Emission begins within < 10⁻⁹ seconds (instantaneous quantum interaction).
3. Einstein's Equation & Photon Theory
Nobel Prize 1921
h ν = Φ₀ + K_max = h ν₀ + e V₀ ⟹ V₀ = (h/e)ν - Φ₀/e
  • Photon Properties: Energy E = hν, momentum p = h/λ = E/c, speed c in vacuum, rest mass = 0, charge = 0.
  • Collision: 1 photon interacts with exactly 1 electron (all-or-none energy transfer). Photon is absorbed.
  • Millikan's Slope: V₀ vs ν graph has universal slope = h/e (~4.14 × 10⁻¹⁵ V·s) for all metals!
  • Wave Theory Collapse: Wave theory failed on instantaneous emission, threshold frequency, and K_max intensity independence.

DUAL NATURE
OF MATTER & RADIATION
Light exhibits wave (interference, diffraction) and particle (photoelectric, Compton) nature.

Matter particles likewise exhibit wave nature governed by de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p.
4. de Broglie Matter Waves
Particle-Wave
λ = h / p = h / (m v) = h / √(2 m q V)
  • de Broglie Hypothesis: Nature loves symmetry; if radiation has particle nature, matter must have wave nature.
  • Accelerated Electron: λ_e = 12.27 / √V Å = 1.227 / √V nm (e.g. V = 100 V ⟹ λ = 1.227 Å).
  • Heavy Particles: Proton λ_p = 0.286/√V Å; Deuteron λ_d = 0.202/√V Å; Alpha λ_α = 0.101/√V Å.
  • Thermal Neutrons: λ = h / √(3 m k_B T) ≈ 25.2 / √T Å (at 300 K, λ ≈ 1.45 Å).
5. Davisson-Germer Experiment
Experimental Proof
Peak: V = 54 V, ϕ = 50° ⟹ θ = 65° ⟹ λ = 1.65 Å ≈ 1.67 Å
  • Target & Technique: Collimated electron beam scattered from Nickel crystal onto movable Faraday detector.
  • Diffraction Peak: Prominent intensity bump at 54 V and 50° due to constructive interference of electron waves.
  • Bragg Verification: 2d sin θ = nλ with d = 0.91 Å yields λ = 1.65 Å, matching theoretical de Broglie 1.67 Å.
  • G.P. Thomson: Independently verified electron wave diffraction through thin gold foil (shared 1937 Nobel Prize).

Speed Shortcuts
Same V: λ ∝ 1/√(m q)  |  Same E: λ ∝ 1/√m
  • Ratio Trap (p, d, α at same V): λ_p : λ_d : λ_α = 1 : 1/√2 : 1/(2√2) ≈ 2√2 : 2 : 1.
  • Bohr Orbit Standing Wave: Circumference 2πr_n = nλ ⟹ m v r_n = n h / (2π).

  • Photon Flux: Number of photons striking per sec N/t = P / (h ν) = P λ / (h c).
