CRISIS, SPLIT & RESTORATION — THE TRANSFORMATION OF CONGRESS (1964–1971)

THE CHRONOLOGICAL ENGINE — four pivotal turns

1964 & 1966: Succession
Nehru → Shastri (smooth consensus); Shastri dies at Tashkent → Indira defeats Morarji Desai
1967: "Political Earthquake"
Congress loses 8 state assemblies; reduced Lok Sabha majority; SVD coalitions & defections explode
1969: The Historic Split
14 Banks nationalised; Presidential poll revolt (V.V. Giri wins) → Congress (O) vs Congress (R)
1971: The Restoring Landslide
Indira's "Garibi Hatao" sweeps 352 seats; Grand Alliance crushed; Bangladesh war crowns hegemony

The Old Syndicate (Old Guard)

Key Leaders: K. Kamaraj (TN), S. Nijalingappa (Mysore), S.K. Patil (Bombay), Atulya Ghosh (WB), N. Sanjeeva Reddy (AP).
Power Base: Controlled state party machinery, fund allocations, and legislative candidate tickets.
Expectation: Installed Indira Gandhi as PM in 1966 assuming she would act as their pliant proxy (Lohia's "Gungi Gudiya").
Ideology: Centrist-conservative; deeply opposed radical nationalisation and unilateral executive edicts.

Indira Gandhi's Counter-Strategy

Popular Bypass: Bypassed the party bosses to construct a direct emotional and ideological pact with the voters.
Leftist Turn: Pushed the 10-Point Programme (May 1967); nationalised 14 banks (July 1969); abolished Privy Purses.
Presidential Gambit: Defied official nominee Sanjeeva Reddy; endorsed V.V. Giri on a call for a "conscience vote".
New Social Base: Built winning coalition of poor, landless agricultural labour, Dalits, Adivasis, minorities, and women.

THE 1969 SPLIT LADDER — how the party broke apart

MAY 1969

President Zakir Husain dies. Syndicate nominates N. Sanjeeva Reddy as official Congress nominee without Indira's consent.

JULY 1969

Indira promulgates ordinance nationalising 14 major private banks; removes Morarji Desai from Finance Ministry.

AUG 1969

Nijalingappa issues party whip. Indira openly calls for a "conscience vote". Independent candidate V.V. Giri wins.

NOV 1969

Syndicate expels Indira from Congress. Split finalized: Congress (O) under Syndicate and Congress (R) under Indira.

THE SYSTEMIC REINVENTION: NEHRU'S CONGRESS VS INDIRA'S RESTORED CONGRESS

Nehru's "Congress System" (Pre-1967) Broad umbrella coalition of diverse social classes; internal factional bargaining allowed accommodation; powerful regional leaders; high internal democratic tolerance.
Indira's Restored Congress (Post-1971) Centralized, leader-centric command structure; total dependence on Indira's charisma; weak organizational machinery; intolerant of internal dissent; loyalist chief ministers appointed from Delhi.
The Structural Weakness While electorally invincible at the polls, the absence of resilient local dispute-resolution mechanisms left the regime vulnerable to direct street agitations (1973–74 JP Movement → 1975 Emergency).

TRAP 1: The Opposition Slogan vs Indira's Slogan (1971)

The Grand Alliance had only a negative, one-point agenda: "Indira Hatao" (Remove Indira). Indira transformed the narrative into positive socio-economic reform: "Garibi Hatao" (Remove Poverty).

TRAP 2: 1967 Coalitions — The SVD Phenomenon

Samyukta Vidhayak Dal ministries were ideologically contradictory (e.g. Jan Sangh + CPI + Socialists in UP and Bihar). High volatility made defection ("Aaya Ram Gaya Ram") the arbiter of power.
