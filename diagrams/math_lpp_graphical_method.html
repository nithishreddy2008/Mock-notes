THE GRAPHICAL METHOD — CORNERS DECIDE

CUET UG · MATHEMATICS · LINEAR PROGRAMMING

Formulation — the vocabulary that scores

Decision variables: x and y — the quantities you control (units of A, hours of B…)
Objective function: Z = ax + by — the thing to maximise (profit) or minimise (cost)
Constraints: linear inequalities from the story (material, time, budget) + non-negativity x, y ≥ 0
Feasible region: the common shaded area obeying every constraint at once
Feasible vs infeasible solution: a point inside the region vs one outside it
Optimal feasible solution: the feasible point that makes Z best — the exam's destination

The five-step graphical pipeline

Formulate: name x, y; write Z and every constraint (story → inequalities)
Draw each line: replace ≤/≥ with = and plot — the boundary of each half-plane
Shade the right side: test one point (usually the origin) in each inequality; keep the region where ALL overlaps agree
List the corners of the feasible region — including the origin and every axis intercept where boundaries meet
Evaluate Z at every corner: best value wins; for unbounded regions, first confirm the optimum exists by sliding an iso-profit line

CORNER POINT THEOREM + THE THREE REGION MOODS

Bounded region
A closed polygon. Max AND min of Z both exist at corners — evaluate all of them.
Unbounded region
Open forever. A maximum may still exist at a corner — prove it by checking the iso-profit line never improves; otherwise Z grows without limit.
No feasible region
Constraints contradict (no common shade). The LPP is infeasible — no solution exists.

The optimum is a corner — an interior point can always be improved by sliding toward an edge, so vertices are the only candidates. Ties happen when an iso-profit line runs along an edge: every point of that edge is optimal.

The worked classic (memorise its shape)

Max Z = 3x + 2y subject to x + 2y ≤ 10, 3x + y ≤ 15, x, y ≥ 0.
Corners: O(0,0) → Z = 0 · (5,0) → Z = 15 · (4,3) → Z = 18 · (0,5) → Z = 10.
Answer: maximum 18 at x = 4, y = 3 — the intersection of the two constraint lines, found by solving them simultaneously.
Habit: the winning corner is usually where two non-axis boundaries cross — solve that pair of equations, then verify it obeys the other inequalities.

TRAPS THE OPTIONS ARE BUILT AROUND

Shading the wrong side — test the origin before committing any line's side
Skipping the origin corner — O(0,0) is a genuine candidate whenever it is feasible
Unbounded ≠ no maximum — prove existence with an iso-profit slide, don't assume
Evaluating Z at interior points — vertices only, the theorem says so
Dropping x, y ≥ 0 — the region must sit in the first quadrant
Answering the point when the value was asked — "at (4,3)" vs "18": read the stem
