1. Chromosome Theory & Linkage

MORGAN'S FLY LAB
Sutton & Boveri (1902) Parallelism • Chromosomes and Mendelian factors occur in homologous pairs.
• Segregate during anaphase I of meiosis into different gametes.
• Independent pairs assort independently into gametes.
T.H. Morgan's Drosophila Linkage Genes located on the same chromosome physical tend to be inherited together (*linkage*), violating independent assortment.
• Cross A (Tightly Linked): Yellow body ($y$) & white eyes ($w$)
98.7% PARENTAL : 1.3% RECOMBINANT
• Cross B (Loosely Linked): White eyes ($w$) & miniature wings ($m$)
62.8% PARENTAL : 37.2% RECOMBINANT
Alfred Sturtevant Gene Mapping Recombination frequency is directly proportional to physical distance between genes: $1\% \text{ recombination} = 1 \text{ map unit (cM)}$.
Linkage vs Distance: Tighter linkage = lesser physical distance = lower recombination frequency.

2. Sex Determination Systems

HETEROGAMETY AXES
XX–XY System (Humans, Drosophila) • Male Heterogamety: Males produce 50% X and 50% Y sperms.
• Female Homogamety: Females produce 100% X ova.
• Father's sperm strictly determines foetal sex (50:50 ratio).
XX–XO System (Grasshoppers, Insects) • Male Heterogamety: Males have only 1 X-chromosome ($XO$); produce 50% X sperms and 50% sperms without sex chromosomes ($O$).
• Females have two X-chromosomes ($XX$).
ZZ–ZW System (Birds, Fowls) • Female Heterogamety: Female is $ZW$ (produces Z and W eggs).
• Male is homogametic: $ZZ$ (produces only Z sperms).
• Maternal egg determines sex in avian species.
Haplodiploidy (Honeybees) • Females ($2n = 32$) develop from fertilized eggs (diploid).
• Males / Drones ($n = 16$) develop parthenogenetically from unfertilized eggs.
• Males have no father and cannot have sons, but have grandfather and grandsons!
Henking Landmark (1891): Observed X-body in 50% insect sperms; later established as X-chromosome by McClung.

3. Human Genetic Pathology

MENDELIAN VS ANEUPLOIDY
Mendelian Disorders (Point Mutations) • Sickle-Cell Anemia: Autosomal recessive point mutation in $\beta$-globin gene: codon $GAG \to GUG$ ($6^{\text{th}}$ amino acid Glu $\to$ Val).
• Thalassemia: Quantitative defect in globin chain synthesis ($\alpha$ on chr 16, $\beta$ on chr 11).
• Hemophilia: X-linked recessive; missing clotting factor VIII or IX.
• PKU: Defective phenylalanine hydroxylase.
Chromosomal Disorders (Aneuploidy Non-Disjunction) • Down Syndrome ($47, +21$): Trisomy of chromosome 21. Short stature, furrowed tongue, partially open mouth, broad palm with single simian crease, mental retardation.
• Klinefelter Syndrome ($47, \text{"XXY"}$): Sex chromosome trisomy. Overall masculine development with gynaecomastia (breast enlargement); sterile.
• Turner Syndrome ($45, \text{"XO"}$): Sex chromosome monosomy. Female phenotype with rudimentary ovaries, webbed neck, lack of secondary sexual characters; sterile.
Aneuploidy vs Polyploidy: Aneuploidy = gain/loss of individual chromosomes (non-disjunction). Polyploidy = failure of cytokinesis post-telophase yielding entire extra chromosome sets (common in plants).
NTA CUET Golden Trap: Thalassemia is a quantitative defect (too few globin chains synthesized), whereas Sickle-Cell Anemia is a qualitative defect (synthesizes structurally abnormal $\beta$-globin).
