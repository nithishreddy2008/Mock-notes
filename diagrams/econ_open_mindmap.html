Balance of Payments — Structure

BoP = the double-entry account of ALL transactions between residents and the rest of the world
Current account: goods (visibles) + services + income + transfers — items CONSUMED in the period
Capital account: FDI, portfolio flows, loans, banking capital — assets CHANGING hands
Balance of trade = goods only — a small slice of the current account

Autonomous vs Accommodating

Autonomous (above the line): done for PROFIT or personal motive, regardless of BoP — exports, FDI, remittances
Accommodating (below the line): official reserve transactions — exist ONLY to plug the autonomous gap
BoP surplus: autonomous receipts > payments → reserves BUILD
BoP deficit: autonomous payments > receipts → reserves DRAIN to cover it

Exchange Rate Regimes

Fixed: government/central bank pegs the rate — certainty, but reserves burn defending it; devaluation possible
Flexible: market D & S decide — no reserve drain, monetary freedom; but volatile trade flows
Managed floating (India): market decides, RBI intervenes to smooth wild swings

Forex Demand & Supply

Demand for $: imports, investing abroad, remittances out, tourism out — downward-sloping D
Supply of $: exports, FDI/portfolio inflows, remittances in, tourists in — upward-sloping S
Equilibrium: D = S fixes ₹/$ rate; D rises → rupee DEPRECIATES

Depreciation & Appreciation

Depreciation: more rupees per $ — imports costlier, exports cheaper abroad; D-marked fall
Appreciation: fewer rupees per $ — imports cheaper, exports costlier abroad
Triggers of depreciation: import surge, FII exit, higher home inflation

Regime Trade-offs (merits/demerits)

Fixed — merits: certainty for traders, no speculation; demerits: reserves burn, imports may misprice, devaluation shocks
Flexible — merits: auto-adjusting, monetary freedom; demerits: volatility hurts trade planning
Managed floating — the compromise: market efficiency + official shock-absorber

Traps that catch most students

BoT is NOT BoP — goods only vs the whole account book
Depreciation ≠ devaluation — market slide vs deliberate government cut of a peg
Reserve transactions are accommodating — they are the plug, not a profit motive
₹ per $ rising = rupee WEAKENING — more rupees per dollar is depreciation, not strength
