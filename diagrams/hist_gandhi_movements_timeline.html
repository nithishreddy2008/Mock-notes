The Three Great Gandhian Mass Movements (1920–1942)

Comparative structural analysis of catalysts, mobilization techniques, social bases, and outcomes

CUET UG History · High-Yield Timeline
Mass Phase I 1920 – 1922

Non-Cooperation Movement

"Swaraj in One Year" through non-violent non-cooperation

Catalysts & Spark
Rowlatt Act (1919), Jallianwala Bagh massacre, Hunter Report, and Khilafat issue (Ali Brothers).
Methods of Resistance
Boycott of law courts, government schools, titles surrendered; picketing liquor shops; adoption of Charkha and Khadi.
Social Spread
Urban middle class, peasants in Awadh (Baba Ramchandra), tribal revolt in Andhra (Alluri Sitarama Raju), tea-garden workers.

Suspension & Turning Point

  • Chauri Chaura incident (4 Feb 1922): 22 policemen killed in Gorakhpur.
  • Gandhi unilaterally withdrew movement citing lack of discipline in Ahimsa.
  • Sentenced to 6 years imprisonment by Judge C.N. Broomfield.
Mass Phase II 1930 – 1934

Civil Disobedience Movement

Defiance of colonial laws starting with the Salt Satyagraha

Catalysts & Spark
Simon Commission boycott (1928), Nehru Report, Lahore Purna Swaraj declaration (1929), Irwin's refusal of 11 Demands.
Methods of Resistance
Dandi Salt March (12 Mar – 6 Apr 1930; 240 miles, 78 marchers); non-payment of chowkidari tax and land revenue; forest law violations.
Social Spread
Extensive female mobilization (Sarojini Naidu, Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay), business classes (FICCI), Red Shirts (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan).

Diplomacy & Settlement

  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931): CDM suspended; Gandhi attended 2nd RTC.
  • Deadlock in London over separate electorates → Communal Award (1932).
  • Poona Pact (Sept 1932) with Dr. B.R. Ambedkar for reserved seats.
Mass Phase III 1942

Quit India Movement

"Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro) — Orderly British withdrawal

Catalysts & Spark
Failure of Cripps Mission (March 1942), threat of Japanese invasion, wartime inflation, and severe food scarcity.
Methods of Resistance
Gowalia Tank Bombay resolution (8 Aug 1942); disruption of railway lines, telegraphs; underground radio networks (Usha Mehta).
Social Spread
Spontaneous pan-Indian youth uprising; socialist leadership (Jayaprakash Narayan, Aruna Asaf Ali, Ram Manohar Lohia); labor strikes.

Parallel Governments (Prati Sarkar)

  • Ballia (UP) under Chittu Pandey: Released prisoners, formed administration.
  • Tamluk (Midnapore): Jatiya Sarkar with Vidyut Vahinis.
  • Satara (Maharashtra): Nana Patil's Prati Sarkar lasting till 1945.
Key Exam Distinction: NCM was non-cooperation with colonial machinery; CDM was active breaking of colonial laws; QIM was total termination of British rule.
