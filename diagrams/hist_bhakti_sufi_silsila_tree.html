Khanqah: Core Institutional Practices
Community & Devotion
1. The Shaikh & Jama'at Khana
The master (Shaikh, Pir, or Murshid) resided in a large hall (jama'at khana) surrounded by disciples (murids), initiating devotees, assigning tasks, and nominating successors (khalifas).
2. Open Communal Langar
Fed hundreds of visitors daily regardless of faith, caste, or wealth; operated purely on unsolicited charitable gifts (futuh) and distributed all food without hoarding.
3. Sama' & Musical Mysticism
Spiritual audition sessions where qawwals recited mystical Persian and Hindavi poetry; induced intense ecstatic divine love (wajd); pioneered by Amir Khusrau.
4. Ziyarat & Urs Festivities
Pilgrimage (ziyarat) to the tomb-shrine (dargah) on the master's death anniversary (urs), celebrating the soul's divine marriage/union with God.
Key Concept: Silsila means an unbroken spiritual chain connecting the Sufi Shaikh through Ali to the Prophet Muhammad.
