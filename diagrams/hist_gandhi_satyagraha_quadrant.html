The Gandhian Philosophy & Constructive Programme

The philosophical pillars and nation-building agenda underpinning moral and mass resistance

CUET UG History · Conceptual Framework
Pillar I Moral Core & Method

Satyagraha & Ahimsa (Truth & Non-Violence)

Satyagraha is not passive resistance but the active deployment of moral force, willing self-suffering, and insistence on truth.

›
Ahimsa as Creed: Complete absence of malice in thought, speech, and deed against the oppressor.
›
Tapasya (Self-Suffering): Melting the conscience of the opponent rather than inflicting physical injury.
›
Abhaya (Fearlessness): Stripping away the psychological fear of prison, lathis, and colonial coercion.
Exam Context: First formulated during the South African struggles (1906–1914) against discriminatory Asiatic Registration Acts.
Pillar II Economic Blueprint

Swadeshi, Khadi & Gram Swaraj

Economic self-reliance as the foundational basis of political sovereignty and moral emancipation of Indian villages.

›
Charkha as Symbol: Dignity of manual labor, universal national solidarity, and rejection of predatory machine capitalism.
›
Village Republics: Decentralized agro-industrial economy ensuring rural self-sufficiency and full employment.
›
Manchester Boycott: Dismantling British imperial trade balance by reviving domestic handloom weaving.
Exam Context: Charkha placed at the center of the Swaraj flag adopted at the 1931 Karachi Congress session.
Pillar III Social Reformation

Sarvodaya & Eradication of Untouchability

True Swaraj is impossible as long as millions remain subjugated under the curse of untouchability within Hindu society.

›
Harijan (Children of God): Term coined to restore human dignity; founded the All India Anti-Untouchability League (1932).
›
Temple Entry Campaigns: Organized peaceful satyagrahas for common well access, temple entry, and sanitation work.
›
Poona Pact (1932): Compromise with Dr. B.R. Ambedkar replacing separate electorates with joint electorates & reserved seats.
Exam Context: Published the weekly newspaper Harijan (1933) to lead continuous ideological warfare against caste discrimination.
Pillar IV National Solidarity

Communal Harmony & Moral Trusteeship

Unconditional fraternity between religious communities and voluntary stewardship of wealth for the common good.

›
Hindu-Muslim Unity: Considered the primary prerequisite for national freedom; championing the Khilafat cause (1920).
›
Trusteeship Doctrine: Capitalists hold societal wealth as moral trustees of the poor, avoiding violent class conflict.
›
Peace Missions (1946–47): Walking barefoot through riot-torn Noakhali, Bihar, and Calcutta to quell communal frenzy.
Exam Context: Lord Mountbatten famously designated Gandhi as the "One-Man Boundary Force" during the 1947 Bengal riots.
Synthesis: Satyagraha provided the weapon for political battle; the Constructive Programme built the societal infrastructure for lasting Swaraj.
