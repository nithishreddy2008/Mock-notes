BIOTECHNOLOGY: PRINCIPLES & PROCESSES

Principles — the Two Arms

EFB definition: using live organisms or enzymes to make a useful product at scale
Arm 1 — genetic engineering: change the DNA itself — cut, paste and transfer genes
Arm 2 — bioprocess engineering: grow cells in bioreactors to make product in bulk
Why not breeding? Breeding mixes whole genomes randomly; rDNA moves one known gene only

Tools of rDNA Technology

Restriction enzymes: molecular scissors — EcoRI cuts GAATTC into sticky ends
Cloning vectors: plasmids like pBR322 — need ori, selectable marker, cloning sites
Competent host: CaCl₂ + cold shock makes cell walls let rDNA in
Other entry routes: micro-injection, gene gun (biolistics), electroporation

rDNA Process — End to End

Isolate: lysozyme + EDTA open cells; RNase + protease clean the DNA
Separate: agarose gel — fragments run to the + electrode, smallest run fastest
Cut + ligate: same enzyme for gene and vector; DNA ligase seals the paste
Transfer + select: into host; antibiotic plates pick out the transformed cells

PCR — the DNA Photocopier

Denaturation: ~94–95°C — the two DNA strands separate
Primer annealing: ~50–60°C — short primers sit on the strand ends
Extension: 72°C — Taq polymerase (from Thermus aquaticus) builds new DNA
Exponential: 1 copy → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 — each cycle doubles the count

Bioreactors

Job: grow engineered cells in bulk — a 100–1000 L stirred vessel, not a flask
Controls: temperature · pH · oxygen · agitator · foam breaker
Simple stirred-tank: paddles mix; sparged = air bubbled through from below
Raw material: cheap sugars + salts + optimum conditions = more product

Downstream Processing (DSP)

Separation: pull the product out of the spent cells and broth
Purification: strip out water, salts and leftover cell parts
Formulation: add preservatives; shape it into tablets, syrup or injection
Quality control + trials: clinical testing before the product reaches a shelf

COMMON ERRORS & TRAPS

EcoRI leaves sticky ends, not blunt — the overhang is the whole point of pasting
Gel separates by size, not charge — the charge (phosphate) is the same for every fragment
The selectable marker lives in the vector, not in the host cell
