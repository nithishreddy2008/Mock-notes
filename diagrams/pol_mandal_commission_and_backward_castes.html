Mandal Commission & Mobilisation
Social Justice, 11 Indicators & 27% Quota
Commissions on Backward Classes Article 340
  • First Commission (1953): Chaired by Kaka Kalelkar; submitted report in 1955; shelved due to lack of quantifiable criteria.
  • Second Commission (1978): Appointed by Morarji Desai Janata govt under B.P. Mandal; submitted report in Dec 1980.
  • 11 Indicators: Used social, educational, and economic metrics; identified 3,743 castes comprising 52% of India's population.
  • 27% Reservation: Recommended 27% quota in Union civil jobs and public sector to adhere to Supreme Court's 50% ceiling.
Kalelkar (1953) B.P. Mandal (1978)
Implementation & Stir (1990) V.P. Singh
  • 7 August 1990: PM V.P. Singh announced implementation of Mandal recommendations in Parliament.
  • Widespread Agitations: Violent protests, student bandhs, and self-immolations across northern Indian university towns.
  • Political Realignment: Transformed Indian democracy from upper-caste hegemony to OBC/backward caste political dominance.
7 Aug 1990 Decision Anti-Mandal Stir
Core Fact: Mandal estimated OBCs at 52% of population, recommending 27% quota to stay strictly within the 50% constitutional ceiling.
2
Indra Sawhney & Bahujan Assertion
Constitutional Jurisprudence & Subaltern Rise
Indra Sawhney Case (1992) Landmark Bench
  • 9-Judge Bench: Upheld 27% OBC reservation in Central civil services as constitutionally valid.
  • The 50% Ceiling: Capped total affirmative action quotas at 50% across regular recruitments.
  • Creamy Layer Exclusion: Advanced/affluent members among OBCs must be excluded from quota benefits.
  • No Quotas in Promotion: Ruled reservation confined strictly to initial appointments, not promotions.
27% Upheld Creamy Layer Excluded
Bahujan Resurgence & Regional Parties Kanshi Ram
  • BAMCEF (1978) & DS-4 (1981): Non-political employees and agitation forums created by Kanshi Ram.
  • Bahujan Samaj Party (1984): Formed to empower Bahujans (SC, ST, OBC, religious minorities); Mayawati became UP CM in 1995.
  • Agrarian OBC Parties: SP (Mulayam Singh), RJD (Lalu Prasad), and JD(U) (Nitish Kumar) displaced Congress in Hindi heartland.
BAMCEF 1978 BSP (Kanshi Ram)
CUET Distinction: The 1992 Indra Sawhney ruling upheld 27% OBC quota but mandated exclusion of the *creamy layer*.

Four Coalition Regimes (1989–2024)
From Instability to Dominant Coalitions
National Front & United Front 1989–1998
  • National Front (1989–90): V.P. Singh; outside support from BJP & Left; Chandra Shekhar interim (1990–91).
  • United Front (1996–98): 13 regional parties; PMs Deve Gowda & I.K. Gujral backed from outside by Congress; Gujral Doctrine.
National Front (1989) United Front (1996)
NDA & UPA Coalitions 1998–2024
  • NDA (1998–2004): Atal Bihari Vajpayee; Pokhran-II (1998); first non-Congress coalition to complete full 5-year term.
  • UPA (2004–2014): Dr. Manmohan Singh; rights architecture: RTI (2005), MGNREGA (2005), RTE (2009), Food Security (2013).
  • NDA III & IV (2014–2024): Narendra Modi; single-party majority (282 in 2014, 303 in 2019) leading coalition; 3rd Democratic Upsurge.
