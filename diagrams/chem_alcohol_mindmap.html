ALCOHOLS, PHENOLS & ETHERS

Classification & Naming

Alcohols by carbon: 1°, 2°, 3°; by count: mono-, di-, tri-hydric (glycol, glycerol)
Allylic/benzylic (next to C=C or ring, sp³ carbon) vs vinylic (on C=C — hard to react)
Names: -ol, -diol, -triol with lowest locant; ethers = alkoxyalkane (methoxyethane)
Phenol: OH directly on the ring; cresols, catechol, resorcinol, hydroquinone

Preparation Toolkit

Alcohols: alkene + H₂O/H⁺ (Markovnikov) or hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov); reduction of aldehydes/ketones/acids/esters (LiAlH₄, NaBH₄); Grignard + aldehyde/ketone
Phenol: Dow (chlorobenzene + NaOH, 623 K), diazonium + water, cumene process (with acetone bonus)
Ethers: dehydration at 413 K (2 R–OH), Williamson: R–X + R'ONa — use primary halides or elimination wins

Physical Properties

Boiling: H-bonding puts alcohols far above alkanes/ethers of similar mass (ethanol 351 K vs dimethyl ether 248 K)
Solubility: small alcohols mix fully with water; large chains drown the –OH
Phenol: barely soluble (the ring dominates); ortho isomers H-bond intramolecularly, so o-nitrophenol is steam-volatile

Alcohol Chemistry

C–OH cleavage: HX/Lucas, PX₃, SOCl₂; 3° via S⁽1, 1° via S⁽2
Dehydration: 443 K alkene (Saytzeff), 413 K ether; ease 3° > 2° > 1°
Acidity: methanol > 1° > 2° > 3° — still weaker than water
Oxidation: 1° → aldehyde → acid; 2° → ketone; 3° refuses; Cu/573 K dehydrogenates
Esterification: with acids, C–O stays intact (isotope proof)

Phenol Chemistry

More acidic than alcohols/water (pKa ~10): the phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilised; nitro groups boost acidity (picric acid), alkyl groups cut it
Ring reactions, no catalyst needed: bromination → 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt); nitration → o- and p-nitrophenol
Kolbe: phenoxide + CO₂ → salicylic acid (aspirin's parent); Reimer-Tiemann: CHCl₃/NaOH → salicylaldehyde
Oxidation: quinone; test: violet complex with FeCl₃

Ethers & the Commercial Duo

Ethers: inert, good solvents; HI cleaves them — 1 eq gives alcohol + iodide, excess gives 2 iodides; 3°/benzyl go S⁽1
Diethyl ether: low BP, the solvent for Grignard (donates lone pairs to Mg)
Methanol: wood spirit — from CO + H₂; toxic, blinds/kills; never drink
Ethanol: fermentation of sugar; 95.6% azeotrope — absolute alcohol via CuO/CaO; denatured with methanol for industry

WATCH-OUTS THAT SET MCQ TRAPS

Acidity ladders differ: phenol > water > alcohol; within alcohols, methanol > 1° > 2° > 3°
Dehydration temperature decides the product — 413 K ether, 443 K alkene
Hydroboration is anti-Markovnikov — propene + BH₃ gives propan-1-ol, not propan-2-ol
Williamson needs primary halides — with 2°/3° halides the base eliminates instead of substituting
