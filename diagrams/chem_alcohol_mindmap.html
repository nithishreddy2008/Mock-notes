1. Preparation MethodsKey Routes

  • From Alkenes (Hydration): Acid-catalysed hydration follows Markovnikov's rule via carbocation (rearrangements possible).
  • Hydroboration-Oxidation: B₂H₆ followed by alkaline H₂O₂ → overall anti-Markovnikov syn-addition of H and OH. No rearrangement!
  • Reduction of Carbonyls: Aldehydes → 1° alcohols; Ketones → 2° alcohols using NaBH₄ or LiAlH₄. Esters/Acids → 1° alcohols with LiAlH₄.
  • Grignard Addition: HCHO + RMgX → 1°; RCHO + RMgX → 2°; R₂CO + RMgX → 3° alcohols. Cumene auto-oxidation yields Phenol + Acetone.

2. Alcohol Distinction TestsDiagnostics

  • Lucas Test (ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl): 3° instant turbidity; 2° within 5 minutes; 1° only upon heating. Tests S_N1 carbocation rate.
  • Victor Meyer Test: 1° alcohol gives Blood Red (sodium nitrolate); 2° gives Deep Blue (pseudonitrol); 3° gives Colourless (R-B-C rule).
  • Dehydrogenation over Cu / 573 K: 1° → Aldehyde; 2° → Ketone; 3° undergoes dehydration to an Alkene (no dehydrogenation!).
  • Oxidation (PCC vs Jones): PCC halts at aldehyde; acidic KMnO₄ / K₂Cr₂O₇ oxidizes 1° to carboxylic acids directly.

3. Acidity of Alcohols & PhenolspKa Trends

  • Alcohols vs Water: Water (pKₐ = 15.7) is more acidic than all alcohols (pKₐ = 16–18), EXCEPT Methanol (CH₃OH, pKₐ = 15.5)!
  • Alcohol Acidity Order: 1° > 2° > 3° due to destabilizing +I inductive electron donation onto alkoxide oxygen (R-O⁻).
  • Phenol Acidity: Phenol (pKₐ ≈ 10) is 10⁶ times more acidic than alcohols due to resonance stabilisation of phenoxide ion (C₆H₅O⁻).
  • Substituent Effects: Electron-withdrawing groups (-NO₂) at ortho/para elevate acidity (Picric acid pKₐ = 0.71). -CH₃ decreases acidity.

4. Hallmark Phenol ReactionsName Reactions

  • Kolbe's Reaction: Sodium phenoxide + CO₂ (400 K, 4–7 atm) → Salicylic acid (o-hydroxybenzoic acid). Precursor to Aspirin.
  • Reimer-Tiemann Reaction: Phenol + CHCl₃ + aq. NaOH → Salicylaldehyde via neutral dichlorocarbene (:CCl₂) electrophile.
  • Electrophilic Halogenation: Br₂ in CS₂ → o- & p-bromophenol (p major); Br₂ in H₂O → 2,4,6-tribromophenol white precipitate.
  • Nitration: Dilute HNO₃ → o- & p-nitrophenol (separable by steam distillation: o has intra-H bond, p has inter-H bond). Conc. HNO₃ → Picric acid.

5. Ethers: Williamson SynthesisS_N2 Rules

  • Williamson Synthesis: R-O⁻ Na⁺ + R'-X → R-O-R' + NaX via S_N2 mechanism.
  • Strict 1° Halide Rule: Alkyl halide R'-X MUST be primary! With 3° halides, alkoxide behaves as a strong base → exclusive E2 alkene elimination.
  • Preparing tert-Butyl Ethers: Must react Sodium tert-butoxide with Methyl halide ((CH₃)₃C-O⁻Na⁺ + CH₃I), NOT tert-butyl halide with methoxide.
  • Dehydration of Ethanol: H₂SO₄ at 413 K → Diethyl ether (S_N2 intermolecular); at 443 K → Ethene (intramolecular elimination).

6. Ether Cleavage by Halogen Acids (HI)Site Selection

  • Unsymmetrical Ethers (1°/2°): I⁻ attacks the smaller, less sterically hindered alkyl group via S_N2 → smaller alkyl iodide + larger alcohol.
  • Tertiary Ether Cleavage: (CH₃)₃C-O-CH₃ + HI → tert-butyl iodide (S_N1) + methanol (forms stable 3° carbocation).
  • Alkyl Aryl Ethers (Anisole): Cleaves to Phenol + Methyl iodide. Ar-O bond has partial double-bond character (+R effect) and cannot cleave.
  • Electrophilic Substitution of Anisole: -OCH₃ is activating and ortho/para-directing (+R > -I). Para-isomer is major.
