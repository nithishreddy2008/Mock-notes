1. Al-Biruni & Kitab al-Hind
11th Century · Khwarizm
  • Text & Structure: Kitab al-Hind composed in Arabic, structured into 80 chapters using a rigorous geometric framework (question → Sanskrit textual description → cross-cultural comparison).
  • Three Epistemological Barriers: Sanskrit language complexity, religious insularity/aloofness of Brahmanas, and cultural self-absorption.
  • Caste Critique: Refuted ritual pollution by citing nature's laws of purification; compared varna to 4 social classes of ancient Sasanian Persia.
  • Antyaja Category: Documented 8 occupational groups born outside the 4 varnas who provided essential services to towns.
★ Core Axis: The Outsider's Gaze
Analytical Lenses
  • Translation & Textuality: Relying on Sanskrit texts (Al-Biruni) vs empirical eyewitness accounts (Ibn Battuta) vs political polemics (Bernier).
  • Familiar vs Exotic: European travellers sought to prove Western superiority, whereas Islamic world travellers sought trade and cosmopolitan pilgrimage.
  • State Capacity: Observations illuminated taxation, currency, market regulation, and centralized imperial communication.
  • Subaltern Realities: Accounts provide accidental historical glimpses of slaves, artisans, peasant distress, and the horror of Sati.
2. Ibn Battuta & The Rihla
14th Century · Morocco
  • Inveterate Globe-Trotter: Traveled across North Africa, West Asia, India, Maldives, and China; dictated his memoirs (Rihla) to Ibn Juzayy.
  • Delhi Sultanate Career: Arrived 1333 CE; appointed Qazi of Delhi for ~8 years by Sultan Muhammad bin Tughlaq; later dispatched as royal envoy to China.
  • Fascination with the Exotic: Detailed the Coconut (resembling human skull/eyes) and Paan / Betel (cultivated like grapevine, chewed with areca nut and lime).
  • Vibrant Urbanism: Described Delhi as a colossal metropolis with 28 gates; celebrated Daulatabad’s singing market (*Tarababad*).
3. The Imperial Communication Grid
Uluq & Dawa
  • Horse Post (*Uluq*): Operated using royal horses stationed at regular posts every 4 miles (*kurohs*) along trunk roads.
  • Foot Post (*Dawa*): Three stations per mile; runners held rods with ringing brass bells to alert the next runner; faster than horses.
  • Rapid Transmission: Reduced the Sindh-to-Delhi dispatch time from 50 days (caravan) to just 5 days.
  • State Surveillance: Enabled central imperial control, quick intelligence on rebellions, and the delivery of fresh Khurasan fruits for the Sultan.
4. François Bernier & Binary Bias
17th Century · France
  • Physician & Philosopher: Spent 12 years in India (1656–1668); served Prince Dara Shukoh and noble Danishmand Khan; dedicated book to King Louis XIV.
  • Crown Land Ownership Fallacy: Argued the Emperor was the sole proprietor of all land, claiming lack of private property caused ruin and misery.
  • Urban Misconceptions: Labeled flourishing Mughal manufacturing cities as parasitic "camp towns" dependent entirely on royal court camps.
  • Intellectual Legacy: His binary comparison influenced Montesquieu’s theory of Oriental Despotism and Marx’s Asiatic Mode of Production.
5. Urban Economy, Slaves & Sati
Subaltern Life
  • Extensive Slave Markets: Slaves openly traded like cattle; female slaves used for domestic labor, palanquin bearing, and royal spycraft.
  • Commercial Strength: Medieval India exported textiles (muslin, silk, calico) globally; merchants organized into *Mahajans* led by the *Nagarsheth*.
  • Manufacturing Karkhanas: Royal workshops where hundreds of artisans produced luxury silk, embroidery, weapons, and jewelry.
  • The Spectacle of Sati: Travellers (Bernier and Ibn Battuta) documented widow immolation, noting both willing devotion and forced coercion of young girls.
Exam Blueprint: Contrast Al-Biruni's Sanskrit Indology, Ibn Battuta's empirical postal accounts, and Bernier's private land ownership thesis.
