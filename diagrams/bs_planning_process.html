THE PLANNING PROCESS — seven steps from goal to follow-up
1

Setting objectives

State exactly what is to be achieved — for the whole firm and for each unit. Objectives are the destination every later step serves.
2

Developing premises

Build the assumptions about the future — demand, prices, technology, policies. All plans stand on these premises, so all managers must agree on them.
3

Identifying alternatives

List every sensible way to reach the objectives — expand, diversify, outsource, licence. The more honest the list, the better the plan.
4

Evaluating alternatives

Weigh each option for cost, risk, feasibility and fit with the premises — positive and negative points, calculated not guessed.
5

Selecting the best alternative

Choose the option with the best balance of benefit and risk — or a combination of options. This is the moment of decision.
6

Implementing the plan

Put the chosen plan into action — assign tasks, allocate budgets, buy materials, schedule work. A plan on paper produces nothing.
7

Follow-up action

Compare actual results with the plan, find deviations, and correct course — the bridge to controlling and the next plan.

The loop closes

Follow-up feeds controlling, and its lessons become next year's objectives and premises — planning is a cycle, not a line.
Exam anchor: questions describe a situation ("the firm first listed four ways to raise ₹50 crore, then compared their costs") and ask which step it shows — memorise the order and each step's one-line job.
