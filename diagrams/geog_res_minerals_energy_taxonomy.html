Unit 7 Architecture: Ferrous & Non-Ferrous Minerals, Fossil Fuels & Clean Renewable Energy Networks

NCERT Class XII Geography

1. Ferrous Minerals

Iron Base
Iron Ore (Hematite & Magnetite) Peninsular Shields
Geological Ore: High-grade Hematite (60–70% iron content) and Magnetite. India has premier reserves in Asia.
Key Belts:
• Odisha-Jharkhand: Badampahar, Mayurbhanj, Kendujhar; Noamundi, Gua.
• Durg-Bastar-Chandrapur: Bailadila range (super-high grade exported via Vizag).
• Ballari-Chitradurga: Kudremukh pipeline to Mangaluru port; Ratnagiri/Goa.
Manganese Ore Steel Smelting Alloy
Industrial Use: Essential for smelting iron ore and manufacturing ferro-manganese alloys (nearly 10 kg manganese per tonne of steel).
Leading Belts: Odisha (Sundargarh, Kendujhar), Madhya Pradesh (Balaghat), Maharashtra (Nagpur, Bhandara), Karnataka (Ballari).
Chromite Refractory & Chemical
Dominance: Odisha accounts for over 90% of total Indian output (Sukinda ultramafic valley in Jajpur district).
Leading States: Odisha, Karnataka (Hassan), Maharashtra.

2. Non-Ferrous & Non-Metallic

Alloy & Electrical
Bauxite (Aluminium Ore) Residual Weathering
Characteristics: Formed by decomposition of surface rocks rich in aluminium silicates; extreme lightness & electrical conductivity.
Key Belts: Odisha leading (Panchpatmali in Koraput, Kalahandi, Bolangir); Amarkantak plateau (MP/Chhattisgarh); Katni-Jabalpur; Kolhapur (Maharashtra).
Copper Ore Deficit Resource
Properties: Highly malleable, ductile, excellent thermal & electrical conductor. India is critically deficient in domestic reserves.
Key Belts: Balaghat mines (Madhya Pradesh — 52% output), Khetri copper belt (Jharkhand-Rajasthan), Singhbhum (Jharkhand).
Mica (Non-Metallic) Electrical Insulator
Qualities: Easily splits into thin sheets; exceptional dielectric strength, low power loss factor, insulating resistance.
Key Belts: Jharkhand (Koderma, Giridih, Hazaribagh); Andhra Pradesh (Nellore); Rajasthan (Bhilwara, Jaipur).

3. Conventional Energy

Fossil Fuels
Coal (Gondwana vs Tertiary) Primary Energy Backbone
Gondwana Coal (~200 Ma): 98% of reserves; metallurgical bituminous quality in river valleys (Damodar, Son, Mahanadi, Godavari).
Tertiary Coal (~55 Ma): High moisture & sulphur; Assam, Meghalaya, Arunachal; Lignite at Neyveli (Tamil Nadu).
Premier Fields: Jharia (largest coking coal field), Raniganj (oldest mine), Bokaro, Singrauli, Talcher, Korba.
Petroleum (Crude Oil) Liquid Hydrocarbon
Occurrence: Sedimentary basins in anticlines and fault traps.
Key Producing Basins:
• Assam: Digboi (oldest operational refinery in India), Naharkatiya, Moran-Hugrijan.
• Western Offshore: Mumbai High (discovered 1973, largest output), Bassein.
• Gujarat: Ankleshwar, Kalol, Mehsana, Cambay.
Natural Gas Clean Transition Fuel
Distribution: KG Basin (Krishna-Godavari offshore), Mumbai High, Barmer (Rajasthan), Tripura. GAIL HVJ Pipeline network.

4. Non-Conventional Energy

Green & Sustainable
Nuclear / Atomic Energy Strategic Power
Mineral Base: Uranium (Dharwar rocks in Jaduguda, Singhbhum; Udaipur); Thorium derived from Monazite sands of Kerala coast.
Key Power Plants: Tarapur (Maharashtra), Rawatbhata (Rajasthan), Kalpakkam & Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu), Narora (UP), Kakrapar (Gujarat), Kaiga (Karnataka).
Solar & Wind Energy Renewable Surge
Solar: Immense insolation in western dry tracts; Bhadla Solar Park (Rajasthan — one of world's largest), Pavagada (Karnataka).
Wind: Muppandal wind farm cluster (Tamil Nadu to Kanyakumari — premier hub), Jaisalmer (Rajasthan), Gujarat coast.
Policy: International Solar Alliance (ISA), PM-KUSUM.
Tidal, Geothermal & Bio Niche Potential
Geothermal: Manikaran (Himachal Pradesh), Puga Valley (Ladakh).
Tidal: Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kuchchh, Sundarbans.
