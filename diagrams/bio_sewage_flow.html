How a Sewage Treatment Plant Cleans Water
The primary (physical) and secondary (biological) journey of city sewage, stage by stage
1

Primary treatment

Screens trap big objects, a grit remover drops sand, and sedimentation tanks let solids settle as primary sludge.

2

Into the aeration tank

The liquid effluent moves on. Flocs — masses of bacteria with fungal filaments — grow as air is pumped in.

3

Bacteria eat the waste

With plenty of oxygen, the microbes digest most of the organic matter. The BOD of the effluent drops.

4

Settling tank

The flow stops and the flocs sink as activated sludge. A small part is returned to the aeration tank.

5

Anaerobic digester

Most sludge is pumped into a sealed tank where methanogens — microbes that hate oxygen — finish the job.

6

Biogas + clean water

The digester yields biogas (mostly methane); the effluent, now with low BOD, is released into water bodies.

SIDE CARD — BOD, the water-quality meter

BOD = biochemical oxygen demand — the oxygen microbes would need to eat the organic waste in the water. The dirtier the water, the higher the BOD. So a lower BOD = cleaner water.
