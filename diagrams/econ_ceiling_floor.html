PRICE CEILING vs PRICE FLOOR — WHERE THE GOVERNMENT DARES TO BEND THE PRICE

PRICE CEILING — maximum legal price

Set BELOW equilibrium so it binds → chronic SHORTAGE (Qd > Qs), queues and black markets
Demand Supply Ceiling (max price)
Read it once: at ₹40 buyers want 140 units but sellers offer only 70 — a permanent gap of 70. Rationing, queues and black-market premiums fill the space that the missing price used to fill.

PRICE FLOOR — minimum legal price

Set ABOVE equilibrium so it binds → chronic SURPLUS (Qs > Qd) that the government must buy (MSP, buffer stock)
Demand Supply Floor (min price)
Read it once: at ₹2,000 farmers plant more (120) while buyers cut back (80) — 40 units pile up. The government procures them at MSP into buffer stocks; otherwise the unsold grain itself would drag the price back down.

TRAP 1

A ceiling binds only BELOW P*; a floor binds only ABOVE P*. On the wrong side of equilibrium the policy does nothing — the market ignores it.

TRAP 2

Both interventions cost the market something: ceiling → shortage, black marketing, quality fall; floor → surplus, storage cost, government spending.

TRAP 3

Directions are opposite: ceiling = maximum (affordability for buyers); floor = minimum (income for sellers). Mixing the two purposes is the classic MCQ bait.
