Meaning & Nature

Principles = general guidelines for decision-making and behaviour of managers
Universal — usable in every organisation, big or small
General guidelines, not readymade answers — applied with judgment
Formed by practice & experience · flexible · mainly behavioural · show cause & effect · contingent on the situation

Significance

Meeting changing environment — flexible guides that adapt
Training value — principles are taught in management courses
Role of management — shows what managers actually do
Optimum utilisation of resources · scientific decisions — balanced, not hasty · coordination of all activities

Taylor's Techniques

Functional foremanship — 8 specialist bosses (4 planning + 4 production)
Standardisation & simplification — of tools, materials, methods
Method study — find one best way · Motion study — remove wasteful movements
Time study — standard time per task · Differential piece wage — efficient workers earn more per piece

Fayol's 14 Principles (1–7)

1 Division of Work — specialise for speed and accuracy
2 Authority & Responsibility — power must match duty
3 Discipline — obedience to agreements and rules
4 Unity of Command — one boss per employee
5 Unity of Direction — one head one plan per goal-group
6 Subordination of Individual to General Interest
7 Remuneration — fair pay keeps employees satisfied

Fayol's 14 Principles (8–14)

8 Centralisation & Decentralisation — the right balance of power
9 Scalar Chain — formal ladder; gang plank = shortcut between peers
10 Order — a place for everything and everyone
11 Equity — kind, fair, no discrimination
12 Stability of Personnel — avoid frequent transfers
13 Initiative — encourage employees to suggest and act
14 Esprit de Corps — team spirit; 'union is strength'

Taylor's 4 Principles

Science, not rule of thumb — study each task scientifically
Harmony, not discord — complete mental revolution on both sides
Cooperation, not individualism — share suggestions together
Development of each person to their greatest efficiency and prosperity

Traps that catch most students

Unity of command (one boss per person) ≠ unity of direction (one head one plan for a group of activities)
Gang plank is the exception that cuts across the scalar chain — not a new principle
Time study = standard time per task; motion study = removing wasteful body movements
Fayol = general, top-level manager, 14 principles; Taylor = shop-floor, scientific management techniques
