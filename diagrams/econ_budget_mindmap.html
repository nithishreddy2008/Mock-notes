GOVERNMENT BUDGET & THE ECONOMY

Budget — Meaning & Objectives

The Annual Financial Statement: estimated receipts and expenditure of the coming fiscal year (1 April – 31 March)
Objectives: reallocate resources, redistribute income, stabilise the economy, manage public enterprises, encourage growth
Two budget parts: revenue budget and capital budget

Receipts — Revenue vs Capital

Revenue receipts (tax + non-tax): neither create liability nor reduce assets
Direct tax burden can't shift (income tax) · indirect shifts (GST)
Capital receipts: borrowings (liability born) + recovery of loans & disinvestment (assets shrink)
The two tests sort every line — liability or asset, otherwise revenue

Expenditure — Revenue vs Capital

Revenue expenditure: no asset born, no liability cleared — salaries, subsidies, interest, pensions
Capital expenditure: creates assets (roads, buildings, machines) OR reduces liabilities (loan repayment)
Same two tests, spending side

Budget Balance Types

Balanced: receipts = expenditure (the classic textbook ideal)
Surplus: receipts > expenditure — cools an overheated economy
Deficit: expenditure > receipts — the normal modern state, measured three ways

The Deficit Family

RD = RE − RR — borrowing for daily consumption
FD = Total expenditure − Total receipts (excl. borrowings) = the borrowing requirement; fuels debt and crowds out private credit
PD = FD − interest payments — this year's own overspending only
FD ≥ PD always; PD = 0 means borrowing only to pay old interest

Deficit Implications

RD: government dissaving — consumption financed by debt
FD: debt trap risk (interest on interest), inflationary financing, crowding out of private investment
PD: the clean reading of the CURRENT year's fiscal stance

Traps that catch most students

Borrowings are a CAPITAL receipt — they create a liability; and FD = borrowings exactly
Interest payment = revenue expenditure — repaying PRINCIPAL is the capital one
Recovery of loans ≠ borrowing — one shrinks assets (capital receipt), the other grows liabilities
PD can be zero while FD is huge — the government may be borrowing only to pay inherited interest
