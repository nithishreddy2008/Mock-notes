THE CIRCULAR FLOW & THE THREE METHODS OF NATIONAL INCOME

Circular flow — one person's expense is another's income

Two loops, four arrows: real flows of services and goods (green), money flows of payments (red) — the loop never stops
Read it once: every rupee a firm pays out (wages, rent, interest, profit) comes back as somebody's spending on goods. That loop is WHY the three methods of measuring national income must agree — they are three windows on ONE flow.

Three methods — three windows on the same flow

Product: count value added · Income: count factor payments · Expenditure: count final spending
Read it once: value added avoids double counting, incomes collect what value added pays out, and expenditure buys what production creates. Same ₹500 from three directions — pick the method the question hands you.

TRAP 1

Only FINAL goods enter GDP. Flour inside bread, steel inside cars — intermediate goods already live inside the final price. Double counting is the classic error.

TRAP 2

Transfers are not income, old sales are not output. Pensions, gifts, second-hand cars and share deals move money — they create NO new goods.

TRAP 3

Domestic ≠ national. Domestic = within the territory (any nationality); national = by normal residents (any location). NFIA bridges them: GNP = GDP + NFIA.
