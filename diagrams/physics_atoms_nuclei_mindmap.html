1. Rutherford Alpha Scattering
Nuclear Atom
r₀ = (2 Z e²) / (4πε₀ K_α)  |  N(θ) ∝ 1 / sin⁴(θ/2)
  • Geiger-Marsden: Most α-particles pass undeflected (atom mostly empty); only ~ 1 in 8000 deflected > 90°.
  • Closest Approach: Kinetic energy converts to electrostatic potential at b = 0. Upper limit r₀ ~ 10⁻¹⁴ m.
  • Failure: Accelerating orbital electrons radiate energy and spiral into nucleus in ~ 10⁻⁸ s; cannot explain discrete lines.
2. Bohr's Hydrogen Atom Model
Quantization
r_n = 0.529 (n²/Z) Å  |  E_n = -13.6 (Z²/n²) eV
  • Postulates: Non-radiating stationary orbits; Quantized angular momentum L = m v r = n h / (2π); ΔE = h ν.
  • Scaling: Radius r_n ∝ n²/Z; Speed v_n ∝ Z/n; Kinetic K = -E_n; Potential U = 2E_n = -2K.
  • Negative Sign: Indicates bound system; energy required to liberate electron to infinity is +13.6 eV.
3. Hydrogen Line Spectra
Transitions
1/λ = R Z² (1/n₁² - 1/n₂²)  |  R ≈ 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹
  • Lyman (n₁=1): Ultraviolet region; Shortest λ = 1/R ≈ 912 Å, Longest λ = 4/(3R) ≈ 1216 Å.
  • Balmer (n₁=2): Visible region; Only series in visible spectrum; Shortest λ = 4/R ≈ 3646 Å.
  • Paschen (n₁=3), Brackett (n₁=4), Pfund (n₁=5): Infrared region. Total spectral lines: N = n(n - 1) / 2.

ATOMS & NUCLEI
SYNTHESIS
Atomic structure is governed by Coulomb force and Bohr's quantum orbital rules.

Nuclear structure is governed by short-range nuclear strong force, mass defect, and binding energy curve.
4. Nuclear Size & Nuclear Forces
Nuclear Core
R = R₀ A^(1/3)  |  ρ ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³ = const!
  • Radius Law: R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm; Nuclear volume V ∝ A; Nuclear density ρ is identical for all nuclei!
  • Species: Isotopes (same Z), Isobars (same A), Isotones (same N = A - Z).
  • Strong Force: Short range (< 2–3 fm), charge-independent, saturated, repulsive core at r < 0.8 fm.
5. Mass Defect & BE per Nucleon
Stability
Δm = [Z m_p + (A-Z)m_n] - M  |  E_b = Δm × 931.5 MeV
  • Curve Peak: ⁵⁶Fe has maximum BE/nucleon = 8.75 MeV/nucleon (most stable nucleus).
  • Plateau: Broad region A = 30 to 170 has nearly constant BE/nucleon ≈ 8.5 MeV/nucleon.
  • Local Spikes: Alpha-like stable nuclei: ⁴He (7.1 MeV), ¹²C (7.7 MeV), ¹⁶O (8.0 MeV).
6. Nuclear Fission & Fusion
Energy Release
Q = [M_reactants - M_products] c² = ΔBE
  • Fission (A > 200): ²³⁵U splits into medium fragments, releasing ~ 200 MeV per event (~ 0.85 MeV/amu).
  • Fusion (A < 20): 4 ¹H ⟶ ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2ν + 26.7 MeV, releasing ~ 6.7 MeV/amu (~ 8× higher than fission)!
  • Coulomb Barrier: Fusion requires extreme temperatures (T ~ 10⁷–10⁸ K) to overcome nuclear repulsion.
