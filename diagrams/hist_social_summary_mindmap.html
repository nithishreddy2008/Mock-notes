The Mahabharata & The Critical Edition
  • Epic Magnitude: Colossal text composed over ~1,000 years (c. 500 BCE to 500 CE) containing over 100,000 verses attributed to Vyasa.
  • Critical Edition Project (1919–1966): Directed by Sanskrit scholar V.S. Sukthankar; took 47 years, spanning 13,000 printed pages across dozens of regional scripts.
  • Twin Discoveries: 1. Pervasive common narrative elements across all manuscripts; 2. Enormous regional variations (over half the edition devoted to variant readings).
  • Two Broad Sections: Narrative (dramatic story of Kurukshetra feud) and Didactic (moral/social instructions, e.g., Bhagavad Gita added later).
1
Kinship Rules & Marriage Types
  • Patriliny (*Pitrivamsha*): Tracing descent father-to-son; sons inherited father's property and royal throne; daughters had no patrilineal claim.
  • Marriage Types: Endogamy (within group/kin), Exogamy (outside the unit), Polygyny (man with multiple wives), Polyandry (woman with multiple husbands, e.g. Draupadi).
  • Manusmriti's 8 Marriage Forms: First 4 considered praiseworthy (*dharmya* - gift of dowered daughter); last 4 condemned (purchase, abduction).
  • Kanyadana: The ritual gifting of a daughter in marriage at puberty regarded as father's highest religious duty.
2
Gotra System & Satavahana Deviations
  • Brahmanical Gotras (c. 1000 BCE): Named after 7 Vedic sages; women assumed husband's gotra on marriage; marriage within same gotra was banned.
  • Satavahana Matronymics: Rulers of Deccan (c. 2nd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE) identified by mother's name: Gautamiputra, Vashisthiputra.
  • Retained Maiden Gotras: Satavahana queens retained father's gotras (Gautami, Vashisthi) instead of adopting husband's gotra.
  • Endogamous Royal Marriage: Satavahanas married women from within the maternal kin group (cross-cousin endogamy), defying Brahmanical exogamy.
3
Varna Order & Non-Kshatriya Kings
  • The 4 Varnas: Brahmanas (teach/sacrifice), Kshatriyas (warfare/rule), Vaishyas (trade/farming), Shudras (service to upper varnas).
  • Shastric Rule Defied: Shastras claimed only Kshatriyas could be kings; historical reality proved anyone wielding military/fiscal power ruled.
  • Dynastic Varna Realities: Mauryas (low origin according to Brahmanas); Shungas and Kanvas (Brahmanas); Shakas (Central Asian Mlechchhas).
  • The "Unique Brahmana": Gautamiputra Satakarni claimed to be Eka Bamhana, crushed Kshatriya pride, yet entered marriage alliance with Shaka ruler Rudradaman.
4
Jatis, Guilds & Social Margins
  • Jati Multiplicity: Unlike 4 rigid varnas, jatis had no fixed number; occupational groups and forest tribes were assimilated as jatis.
  • Mandasor Silk Weavers: Guild of weavers (*reshama-vanik*) migrated from Gujarat to MP, collectively funding a magnificent Sun temple.
  • Nishadas (Forest Tribes): Seen with suspicion by Brahmanas; story of Ekalavya whose archery thumb was demanded by Drona.
  • Chandalas (Untouchables): Disposed of corpses, wore clothes of the dead; Faxian and Xuanzang documented them forced to clap wood to warn citizens.
5
Property, Stridhana & Social Contract
  • Gendered Property: Paternal estate divided among sons (special share for eldest); women could not claim land, owning only Stridhana (bridal gifts).
  • Prabhavati Gupta: Queen regent of Vakatakas, independently granted land via copper plates, proving royal women commanded elite legal autonomy.
  • Buddhist Critique: Buddhism rejected varna by birth (Majjhima Nikaya dialogue of Kachchana); advocated wealth should be shared with ascetics.
  • The Mahasammata Contract: Buddhist Sutta Pitaka myth: kingship is a human contract (*Mahasammata* / the great chosen) funded by taxes, not a divine hierarchy.
