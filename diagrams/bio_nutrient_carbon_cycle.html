The 5 Sequential Steps of Decomposition

Detrital Breakdown
1
Fragmentation
Detritivores (e.g., earthworms) physically break down crude detritus (dead leaves, twigs, feces) into smaller particles, increasing accessible surface area.
2
Leaching
Water-soluble inorganic nutrients dissolve in percolating rainwater and seep deep into lower soil horizons, precipitating as unavailable mineral salts.
3
Catabolism
Bacterial and fungal extracellular enzymes enzymatically degrade complex detrital compounds into simpler inorganic substances. (Simultaneous with steps 1 & 2).
4
Humification
Forms Humus—a dark, amorphous, colloidal substance that is extremely resistant to microbial action, decomposes very slowly, and serves as a major nutrient bank.
5
Mineralization
Humus is degraded further by specialized microbes, releasing free inorganic ions ($CO_2, H_2O, Ca^{2+}, Mg^{2+}, K^+$) back into the soil for plant root uptake.

Biogeochemical Cycles: Carbon vs. Phosphorus

Reservoir Dynamics
1. The Carbon Cycle (Gaseous Reservoir)
• Ocean Reservoir: 71% of global carbon is dissolved in oceans; regulates atmospheric $CO_2$.
• Atmosphere: Holds only about 1% of total global carbon.
• Annual Biosphere Fixation: Photosynthesis fixes $4 \times 10^{13}\text{ kg}$ of carbon annually.
• Release Route: Cellular respiration (producers/consumers), decomposers, and fossil fuel burning.
2. The Phosphorus Cycle (Sedimentary Reservoir)
• Rock Reservoir: Natural reservoir is the lithosphere (phosphate rocks).
• Weathering: Rains weather rocks, releasing minute phosphates ($PO_4^{3-}$) into soil solution.
• Zero Respiratory Release: Living organisms do NOT respire phosphorus into the air.
• Rainfall Inputs: Atmospheric inputs of phosphorus via rainfall are completely negligible.
Rate Determinants of Decomposition
• Chemical Quality: Slow if rich in lignin and chitin; Fast if rich in nitrogen and sugars.
• Climatic Control: Warmth and moisture accelerate decomposition; low temperature and anaerobiosis inhibit it, leading to peat/coal buildup.
Primary Food Chains Compared
GFC (Grazing Food Chain): Energy flows from living plants to herbivores; dominant in aquatic ecosystems.
The Terrestrial Energy Dominance
DFC (Detritus Food Chain): Begins with dead organic matter; in terrestrial ecosystems, much more energy flows via DFC than GFC!
Ecosystem Valuation
Robert Costanza (1997): Put an average price tag of US $33 trillion / year on nature's life-support services (soil formation = ~50%).
