Cardinal Approach
1. Marshallian Utility Analysis
Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility
MU = ΔTU / ΔQ (MU falls as Q rises)
As more standard units of a commodity are consumed continuously, the marginal utility derived from each successive unit decreases.
Single Commodity Equilibrium
MUₓ / Pₓ = MUₘ (or MUₓ = Pₓ)
Consumer maximizes satisfaction when marginal utility per rupee spent on commodity $X$ equals marginal utility of money ($MU_m$).
Two Commodity Equilibrium
MUₓ / Pₓ = MUᵧ / Pᵧ = MUₘ
• If $MU_x / P_x > MU_y / P_y \implies$ Consumer buys more $X$ and less $Y$. By LDUM, $MU_x$ falls and $MU_y$ rises until equality is restored.
• If $MU_x / P_x < MU_y / P_y \implies$ Buys more $Y$ until equalized.
Ordinal Approach
2. Indifference Curve (IC) Analysis
Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS)
MRSₓᵧ = -Δy / Δx = MUₓ / MUᵧ
Rate at which a consumer is willing to sacrifice units of $Y$ to obtain one additional unit of $X$, keeping total utility constant.
The 4 Golden Properties of IC
1. Downward Sloping: Due to monotonic preferences (more of one good requires less of the other).
2. Convex to Origin: Strictly caused by the Law of Diminishing MRS.
3. Higher IC = Higher Utility: Monotonic preference (greater bundle preferred).
4. ICs Never Intersect: Transitivity axiom prevents intersection.
Monotonic Preferences
A consumer always strictly prefers a bundle that contains more of at least one good and no less of the other good (e.g., $(3, 5)$ preferred over $(2, 5)$).
Optimization
3. Budget Line & Tangency Equilibrium
Budget Equation & Slope
Pₓ·x + Pᵧ·y = M | Slope = -Pₓ / Pᵧ
Locus of all combinations of goods $X$ and $Y$ that exhaust total money income $M$. Slope represents Market Rate of Exchange (MRE).
Dual Conditions of Consumer Equilibrium
Condition 1 (Tangency): Slope of IC = Slope of Budget Line:
MRSₓᵧ = Pₓ / Pᵧ
Condition 2 (Convexity): The IC must be strictly convex to the origin at the tangency point (MRS must be diminishing).
Budget Line Dynamics
• Income Shift: Parallel shift outward (Income rises) or inward (falls).
• Price Pivot: Change in $P_x$ rotates line along $x$-axis; change in $P_y$ rotates along $y$-axis.
Disequilibrium Restoration in Ordinal Theory
• If $MRS_{xy} > P_x / P_y$: Consumer values $X$ higher than market price $\implies$ Consumes more $X \implies MRS_{xy}$ falls until $MRS_{xy} = P_x / P_y$.
• If $MRS_{xy} < P_x / P_y$: Consumes less $X$, more $Y \implies MRS_{xy}$ rises to equilibrium.
Cardinal vs Ordinal Utility Assumptions
• Cardinal (Marshall): Utility measurable in absolute numbers (*utils*); $MU$ of money is assumed constant.
• Ordinal (Hicks-Allen): Utility can only be ranked/ordered; avoids unrealistic assumption of constant $MU$ of money.
Boundary & Extreme IC Shapes
• Perfect Substitutes: Linear straight-line IC ($MRS$ constant) $\implies$ Corner solution.
• Perfect Complements: L-shaped (Right-angled) IC ($MRS$ is $0$ or $\infty$) $\implies$ Equi-proportional consumption (e.g., left and right shoes).
