The Three Stages of Production

  • Stage I (Increasing Returns): From origin to AP max ($L_2$). Both AP and TP rise. Fixed factor under-utilized.
  • Stage II (Diminishing Returns): From AP max to TP max ($L_3$). Both AP and MP decline, but MP > 0. Rational zone of production!
  • Stage III (Negative Returns): Beyond TP max ($L_3$). MP < 0, TP falls. Excessive variable factor clogs fixed factor.

Core Mathematical & Geometric Theorems

  • Derivative Relation: $M P_L = d(T P) / d L$ (Slope of TP curve); $A P_L = T P / L$ (Slope of ray from origin to TP).
  • The AP-MP Intersection Rule: When $M P > A P \implies A P$ rises; when $M P = A P \implies A P$ is at its maximum; when $M P < A P \implies A P$ falls.
  • Zero Marginal Product: When $M P = 0 \implies T P$ reaches its mathematical absolute global maximum ($d(T P)/dL = 0$).

CUET Exam Trap Alert

Trap: Students mistakenly assume a rational firm operates where TP is highest or MP is highest. Correction: A rational producer NEVER operates in Stage I (wasting fixed factor) or Stage III (negative MP). A firm operates exclusively in Stage II, where exact output depends on factor prices!
