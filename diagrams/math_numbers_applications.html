MODULO & CONGRUENCE

a mod n = remainder on dividing by n; a ≡ b (mod n) ⇔ n | (a − b)
Rules: reduce before you multiply — (a·b) mod n = ((a mod n)(b mod n)) mod n
Congruence: 29 ≡ 5 (mod 12) because 24 is a multiple of 12 — clock logic
Powers: 7¹⁰ mod 5 → 2¹⁰ mod 5 → 1024 mod 5 = 4

ALLEGATION & MIXTURE

cheap : dear = (dear − mean) : (mean − cheap)
Mean price sits between the two; the ratio is the criss-cross of gaps
Check: ₹30 and ₹45 mixed at ₹36 → (45−36):(36−30) = 9:6 = 3:2 cheap:dear
Verify: (3·30 + 2·45)/5 = 180/5 = ₹36 ✓

RATES THAT COMBINE

work rate adds; empty pipes subtract
Pipes: A fills 1/6 per h, B 1/4, C empties 1/12 → net (2+3−1)/12 = 1/3 → 3 h
Boats: u = b − s (upstream), d = b + s → b = (u+d)/2, s = (d−u)/2
Time = work / rate — always in per-hour (or per-day) fractions

Races & Games — reading the handicap

"A gives B 10 m in a 100 m race" = when A finishes 100 m, B has run 90 m — a *start* of 10 m or a 10 m beat.
"A gives B 10 seconds" = B may start 10 s late; compare speeds on the shared clock.
Killer ratio: speed of A : speed of B = 100 : 90 in the first case — distances in the SAME time carry the speed ratio.
Games of skill: convert "wins 2 of 3" language into probabilities or score ratios before computing.

Numerical inequalities — the flip rule

Add/subtract anything on both sides: inequality keeps its direction.
Multiply/divide by a POSITIVE number: direction keeps.
Multiply/divide by a NEGATIVE number: direction FLIPS — the entire topic in one line.
Example: a > b ⇒ −a < −b; 3 > 2 but −3 < −2.
Reciprocal care: for positives, a > b ⇒ 1/a < 1/b.

TRAPS THE OPTIONS ARE BUILT AROUND

Remainder of a product ≠ product of remainders without re-reducing — mod the result again
Congruence needs the DIFFERENCE divisible, not the numbers equal
Allegation ratio order — cheap:dear attaches to (dear−mean):(mean−cheap), reversed
Boat formulas: b = (u+d)/2 — averaging speeds without halving is the slip
Empty pipes must be subtracted, never added
Negative flip: multiplying an inequality by −1 without flipping is the classic
