Weak-Field Ligand (Δo < P) High-Spin

The crystal field splitting Δo is smaller than the electron pairing energy (P). Electrons prefer to occupy higher eg orbitals rather than pair up in t2g. Maximum unpaired electrons (paramagnetic).
Example (d⁴): t2g³ eg¹ (e.g. [Cr(H₂O)₆]²⁺, [CoF₆]³⁻).

Strong-Field Ligand (Δo > P) Low-Spin

The splitting Δo exceeds the pairing energy P. Electrons are forced to overcome repulsion and pair up in lower t2g orbitals before occupying eg. Minimum unpaired electrons (often diamagnetic).
Example (d⁶): t2g⁶ eg⁰ (e.g. [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻).

The Fundamental Tetrahedral Law

Because ligands point between orbitals rather than directly at them, and there are only 4 ligands (not 6), the splitting energy is very small: Δt ≈ (4/9) Δo. As a result, Δt is virtually never large enough to exceed the pairing energy P. Thus, tetrahedral complexes are almost exclusively high-spin.

Colour Origin in CFT

Absorption of visible light promotes a d-electron across the crystal field gap (Δo): ΔE = hν = hc/λ. The perceived colour is the exact optical complement of the absorbed wavelength.
