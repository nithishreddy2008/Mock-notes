CRYSTAL FIELD SPLITTING IN AN OCTAHEDRAL COMPLEX — THE d ORBITALS BREAK RANKS
Six ligands approach along the axes: orbitals pointing AT ligands (e₂g) rise, orbitals between them (t₂g) sink. The gap is Δ₀.

WEAK-FIELD LIGAND (e.g. F⁻) → HIGH SPIN

Story: the gap is too small to force pairing. Electrons obey Hund's rule first — 4 unpaired electrons in a d⁵ case → paramagnetic, weakly coloured.

Classic pairs: [CoF₆]³⁻, [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺, [MnF₆]³⁻ — "outer orbital" sp³d² in VBT language.

STRONG-FIELD LIGAND (e.g. CN⁻) → LOW SPIN

Story: the gap beats the pairing cost. Electrons cram into t₂g first — 1 unpaired electron in a d⁵ case → less paramagnetic, deeper colour, extra CFSE.

Classic pairs: [Co(CN)₆]³⁻, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻, [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ — "inner orbital" d²sp³ in VBT language.

THE SPECTROCHEMICAL SERIES — memorise the order, not the whole list

I⁻ < Br⁻ < SCN⁻ < Cl⁻ < S²⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < C₂O₄²⁻ < H₂O < NCS⁻ < edta⁴⁻ < NH₃ < en < bipy < phen < NO₂⁻ < CN⁻ ≈ CO  —  left side = weak field (high spin) · right side = strong field (low spin)
