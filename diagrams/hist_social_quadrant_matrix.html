Quadrant 1 · Marriage Systems & The 8 Forms
Conjugal Framework
Dharmashastric Norm
Exogamy Mandatory: Marrying outside the unit; Kanyadana (gifting daughter at puberty) is father's supreme religious duty. Manusmriti lists 8 marriage types: first 4 praised (dowered gifts), last 4 condemned (abduction, purchase).
Documented Reality
Endogamy & Polyandry: Cross-cousin marriage was celebrated in southern India; Satavahana kings practiced polygyny (multiple wives); the central epic hero Draupadi engaged in polyandry (five Pandava husbands).
Quadrant 2 · The Gotra System & Rulers' Identities
Lineage Rules
Brahmanical Rule (c. 1000 BCE)
Two Strict Principles: 1. Women must abandon father's gotra and adopt husband's gotra upon marriage; 2. Persons sharing the same Vedic gotra (clan ancestor) could never marry each other (*sagotra* taboo).
Satavahana Counter-Evidence
Retained Gotra & Matronymics: Satavahana queens retained father's gotras (Gautama and Vashistha); rulers used matronymics (*Gautamiputra*, *Vashisthiputra*); royal marriages occurred within the same gotra clan.
Quadrant 3 · Gendered Access to Property & Wealth
Economic Rights
Manusmriti Legal Injunctions
Patrilineal Devolution: Paternal estate divided strictly among sons upon father's death (special share for eldest); women had no claim on ancestral land; entitled only to Stridhana (bridal gifts) which husbands could not touch.
Royal Women's Exceptions
Prabhavati Gupta's Land Grants: Inscriptions show queen regent Prabhavati Gupta granted land directly to Brahmanas, demonstrating that elite royal women exercised property agency barred to common women.
Quadrant 4 · Divine Creation vs The Social Contract
Legitimizing Ideology
Vedic / Shastric Theory
Cosmic Divine Ordination: Social rank is ordained by birth in the cosmic Purusha Sukta; kings rule by divine mandate; poverty or low varna status is justified as karmic retribution for past deeds.
Buddhist Sutta Pitaka Myth
The Mahasammata Contract: Kingship was human-made! In a golden age, human greed led people to elect a leader (*Mahasammata* / "the great chosen") to maintain order in exchange for 1/6th rice. Kingship is contractual, not divine.
Key Exam Distinction: Dharmashastras represented ideal prescriptive norms written by Brahmanas, not uniform social practice across the subcontinent.
