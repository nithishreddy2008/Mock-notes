PEOPLE: INDIA
(PEOPLE & ECONOMY)

Census 2011 · Unit 5 Architecture

1. Distribution & Spatial Density

382 persons/km²
  • Uneven Spatial Dispersion: High concentration in fertile Northern Plains and coastal deltas; sparse in Himalayas & western desert.
  • Density Hierarchy: Bihar (1,106) & West Bengal (1,028) highest; Arunachal Pradesh (17) lowest among states.
  • Pressure Metrics: Physiological density (Pop / Net Cultivated Area) and Agricultural density reflect real agricultural land stress.
Core Exam Point: Delhi UT density soars to 11,320 persons/km².

2. Four Population Growth Phases

1901 – 2011+
  • Phase I (1901–21): Stagnant; high birth & death rates; negative growth in 1911–21 (-0.31%); 1921 Year of Great Divide.
  • Phase II (1921–51): Steady growth; mortality drops via sanitation & transport; modest 1.0–1.3% annual growth.
  • Phase III (1951–81): Population explosion; Five-Year plans, healthcare; decadal growth peaked at 24.8% (1961–71).
  • Phase IV (1981–2011+): Decelerating growth; decadal slowed to 17.64% (2001–11); family planning & literacy surge.
Adolescents: 20.9% (10–19 yrs) forming core demographic dividend.

3. Regional Growth & Policies

NPP 2000 & NYP
  • North-South Growth Divide: Southern states (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka) below 16% decadal growth.
  • High Growth Belt (EAG States): Bihar (25.4%), UP (20.2%), Rajasthan (21.3%), MP (20.3%) maintain high natural growth.
  • National Population Policy (2000): Free compulsory education up to age 14, reduce IMR < 30/1,000, universal immunization.
  • National Youth Policy (2014): Defined youth as 15–29 years; focus on skill acquisition, digital empowerment & employment.
Policy Focus: Addressing adolescent anemia, school dropouts & child marriage.

4. Linguistic & Religious Composition

Plural Mosaic
  • 4 Language Families: Indo-Aryan (73%), Dravidian (20%), Austroasiatic/Nishada (1.38%), Sino-Tibetan/Kirata (0.85%).
  • Scheduled Tongues: 22 languages in 8th Schedule; Hindi (43.6%) largest, Bengali (8.03%) 2nd largest speaker base.
  • Religious Breakdown: Hindus (79.8%), Muslims (14.2% — largest minority), Christians (2.3%), Sikhs (1.7%), Buddhists (0.7%), Jains (0.4%).
Minority Concentrations: Border states, valleys, and specific coastal districts.

5. Rural-Urban Matrix & Migration

68.8% R · 31.2% U
  • Rural Concentration: 83.3 Crore in 6.4 lakh villages; Himachal Pradesh (89.97%) and Bihar (88.71%) most rural.
  • Urban Concentration: 37.7 Crore; Goa (62.2%) most urbanized major state; Maharashtra has largest absolute urban pop.
  • Census Urban Criteria: Municipality/Cantt; ≥5,000 pop; ≥75% male workers in non-agriculture; density ≥400/km².
  • Migration Streams: Rural-to-Rural (female-dominated for marriage); Rural-to-Urban (male-dominated for employment).
Metropolitan Pull: Greater Mumbai, Delhi, and Kolkata mega-clusters.

6. Occupational Structure & Workers

Work Rate: 39.8%
  • Economic Classification: Main Workers (≥183 days, 77.8%), Marginal Workers (<183 days, 22.2%), Non-Workers (60.2%).
  • 4 Standard Categories: Cultivators (24.6%), Agri Labourers (30.0%), Household Industry (3.8%), Other Workers (41.6%).
  • Primary Sector Shift: Agriculture accounts for 54.6% of workforce (down from 66.8% in 1991); Tertiary sector rising.
  • Gender Participation: Male (53.3%) vs Female (25.5%); higher female work rate in tribal & mountain agrarian belts.
Structural Dependency: 60% dependent population highlights economic imperative.
