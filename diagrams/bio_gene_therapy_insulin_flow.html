Pathway 1: Genetically Engineered Insulin (Eli Lilly, 1983)

Recombinant Therapeutic
1
Human Proinsulin Structure & The C-Peptide Problem
Natural proinsulin contains three chains: Chain A (21 aa), Chain B (30 aa), and an extra stretch called C-peptide (33 aa). In mammalian cells, C-peptide is proteolytically excised to produce mature 51 aa insulin. Bacteria cannot excise C-peptide!
2
Separate Chemical DNA Synthesis (Chains A & B)
In 1983, American company Eli Lilly chemically synthesized two separate DNA sequences corresponding to Chain A and Chain B of human insulin, bypassing the C-peptide coding sequence entirely.
3
Independent Expression in Escherichia coli Vectors
The two synthetic DNA sequences were inserted into plasmids of E. coli downstream of the lacZ promoter. Chain A and Chain B were fermented and expressed independently in separate bacterial bioreactor cultures.
4
Isolation & In Vitro Disulfide Bond Linkage
Chains A and B were extracted, purified via downstream chromatography, and combined in vitro by chemical oxidation to establish covalent disulfide bonds (2 interchain bonds + 1 intrachain bond in Chain A).
5
Active Human Insulin (Humulin) Formation
The reconstituted product, Humulin, is 100% structurally identical to natural pancreatic insulin. Unlike earlier bovine or porcine insulin, Humulin triggers zero immunological rejection or allergic responses.
NCERT Fact-Check: Insulin Structure
• Total Amino Acids: 51 (Chain A = 21 aa, Chain B = 30 aa).
• Mature Insulin lacks C-peptide: C-peptide is present ONLY in pro-insulin.
• Disulfide Bonds: 3 total bonds (two between A and B, one internal within A).

Pathway 2: Clinical Gene Therapy for ADA-SCID (1990)

Ex-Vivo Retroviral Therapy
1
ADA Gene Deletion & SCID Pathophysiology
Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) enzyme is crucial for purine breakdown and immune system function. Inherited deletion/mutation of the ADA gene causes Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID), disabling both T and B lymphocytes.
2
Lymphocyte Extraction & In Vitro Culturing
In 1990, the first clinical gene therapy was administered to a 4-year-old girl. Blood lymphocytes were extracted from the patient's peripheral circulation and grown in laboratory culture vessels.
3
Retroviral Transduction of Functional ADA cDNA
A functional human ADA cDNA construct was introduced into a disarmed retroviral vector. The retrovirus infected cultured lymphocytes, stably integrating the therapeutic gene into host chromosomes.
4
Autologous Infusion of Corrected Cells
The genetically modified, ADA-expressing lymphocytes were washed and re-infused into the young patient's bloodstream, temporarily restoring functional cellular immunity.
5
Periodic Infusion Need vs Permanent Cure
Because mature lymphocytes are mortal cells with finite lifespans, the patient requires periodic re-infusion of corrected cells.
Permanent Cure Strategy: Introducing functional ADA cDNA into bone marrow stem cells at early embryonic stages cures the disease permanently.
Alternative SCID Therapies Compared
• Enzyme Replacement Therapy (ERT): Injections of functional ADA enzyme (not permanent).
• Bone Marrow Transplantation: Requires strict HLA matching; risk of graft-versus-host disease.
• Vector used: Disarmed Retrovirus (infects dividing lymphocytes).
