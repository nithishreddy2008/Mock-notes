THE ALCOHOL REACTION MAP — ONE GROUP, SIX DESTINATIONS
Every S⁽ route attacks the C–OH bond; oxidation attacks the O–H side. Pick the reagent, predict the product.
R–OH
THE ALCOHOL HUB

1. Substitute → alkyl halide

HX (Lucas: ZnCl₂+HCl) or SOCl₂ or PX₃/PX₅
3° bubbles instantly in Lucas, 2° in minutes, 1° only on heating. SOCl₂ gives the purest chloride (gases escape).

2. Dehydrate → alkene or ether

conc. H₂SO₄, 443 K → alkene (Saytzeff wins: but-2-ene > but-1-ene). Milder 413 K → ether (two alcohols join). 3° dehydrates easiest.

3. Esterify (keep the O–H!)

RCOOH + acid, heat → ester + water. The C–O bond of the ALCOHOL stays whole — the oxygen in the ester comes from the alcohol (isotope proof).

4. Act as a weak acid

Na / NaH → alkoxide + H₂ gas. Acidity order: methanol > 1° > 2° > 3° — alkyl groups push electrons and weaken the O–H.

5. Oxidise (the class decides!)

K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺: 1° → aldehyde → acid; 2° → ketone; 3° resists (no C–H on the carbinol carbon). Cu at 573 K dehydrogenates instead: 1° → aldehyde, 2° → ketone, 3° → alkene.

6. With HBr watch the class

3° goes S⁽1 (fast, via carbocation); 1° goes S⁽2 (slow, backside) — the mechanism switch you learned in Unit VI decides everything.
