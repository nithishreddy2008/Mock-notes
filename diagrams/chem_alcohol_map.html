Alcohol Classification, Distinction Tests & Oxidation Pathways

Lucas turbidity kinetics, Victor Meyer colorimetry, Cu/573 K catalytic divergence & relative acidities

CUET Diagnostic Core

Primary (1°) Alcohols

R-CH₂-OH
Lucas Reagent (anhy. ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl)
No turbidity at room temperature. Forms oily alkyl chloride layer only upon vigorous prolonged heating.
Controlled Oxidation (PCC vs Jones)
PCC in CH₂Cl₂: Halts cleanly at Aldehyde (R-CHO).
KMnO₄ / K₂Cr₂O₇ / Jones: Direct oxidation to Carboxylic acid (R-COOH) with same carbon count.
Catalytic Dehydrogenation (Cu / 573 K)
R-CH₂OH → Aldehyde (R-CHO) + H₂↑ (True dehydrogenation; loss of hydrogen without carbon rearrangement).
Relative Acidity & +I Effect
Highest acidity among alcohols (1° > 2° > 3°). Minimal +I inductive destabilization of conjugate alkoxide ion (R-CH₂-O⁻).

Secondary (2°) Alcohols

R₂CH-OH
Lucas Reagent (anhy. ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl)
Turbidity / cloudiness appears within 5 minutes at room temperature due to moderate secondary carbocation stability.
Controlled & Strong Oxidation
Both PCC and strong oxidizers (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄) convert 2° alcohols exclusively to Ketones (R₂C=O) with the same number of carbon atoms. Resists further mild oxidation.
Catalytic Dehydrogenation (Cu / 573 K)
R₂CH-OH → Ketone (R₂C=O) + H₂↑ (Smooth loss of two hydrogens; clean carbonyl formation).
Relative Acidity & +I Effect
Intermediate acidity. Two electron-releasing alkyl groups intensify negative charge on oxygen, weakening proton release.

Tertiary (3°) Alcohols

R₃C-OH
Lucas Reagent (anhy. ZnCl₂ + conc. HCl)
Instant turbidity immediately at room temperature! Highly stable 3° carbocation forms rapidly via S_N1 displacement.
Oxidation Behavior (No α-H)
Resists neutral/alkaline oxidation (lacks α-hydrogen). Under vigorous acidic oxidation (hot HNO₃), C-C cleavage occurs → acid mixture with fewer carbons.
Cu / 573 K (CRITICAL EXAM TRAP)
Does NOT dehydrogenate! Undergoes catalytic dehydration to an Alkene: (CH₃)₃C-OH → 2-Methylpropene + H₂O.
Relative Acidity & +I Effect
Lowest acidity among alcohols. Three +I alkyl groups push electron density onto oxygen, heavily destabilizing tert-butoxide.

● Victor Meyer's Test (Red, Blue & Colourless Rule)

Class Reagent Sequence (P+I₂ → AgNO₂ → HNO₂) + NaOH Treatment Observed Colour
1° Alcohol Forms Nitrolic Acid: R-C(=NOH)-NO₂ Forms soluble sodium nitrolate salt BLOOD RED COLOUR
2° Alcohol Forms Pseudonitrol: R₂C(NO)-NO₂ Insoluble in alkali, stays unchanged DEEP BLUE COLOUR
3° Alcohol No reaction with HNO₂ (no α-H) No reaction occurs COLOURLESS (No Change)
Memory Mnemonic: R-B-C → 1° is Red, 2° is Blue, 3° is Colourless. Classic CUET direct 5-mark match.

■ Acidity Comparison & Esterification Trends

Absolute Acidity Hierarchy:
H₂O (pKₐ = 15.7) > R-OH (pKₐ = 16–18) > Terminal Alkyne > NH₃ > Alkane
Exception: Methanol (CH₃OH, pKₐ = 15.5) is slightly more acidic than water! All other aliphatic alcohols are weaker acids than water (alkoxide R-O⁻ is a stronger base than OH⁻).

Esterification Kinetics (Fischer Esterification):
Rate is controlled by steric hindrance around the reaction center:
  • Reactivity of Alcohols: CH₃OH > 1° > 2° > 3° (tertiary alcohols are slowest).
  • Reactivity of Carboxylic Acids: HCOOH > CH₃COOH > R₂CHCOOH > R₃CCOOH.
