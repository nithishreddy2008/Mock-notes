LEVELS OF MANAGEMENT — who sits where, and what each level owns
TOP LEVEL
Board · CEO
Chairman · MD · CFO · COO
MIDDLE LEVEL
Divisional & Departmental Heads
Operations managers · Plant superintendents
SUPERVISORY (FIRST-LINE) LEVEL
Supervisors · Foremen · In-charges
Office managers · direct link to the workforce
▼
▼

Top level owns

Direction of the whole organisation — vision, objectives and broad policies
Survival and growth — answerable to shareholders and society for everything
Titles: Board of Directors, Chairman, CEO, CFO, COO, MD, VP

Middle level owns

Interpreting policies — turns top's plans into departmental programmes
The link — carries orders down and reports up between top and first-line
Titles: divisional heads, operations management, plant superintendent

Supervisory level owns

Day-to-day running — daily targets and on-the-spot decisions
People care — safety, work conditions, grievances of workers
Titles: supervisor, foreman, section in-charge, office manager

One line to remember

Authority flows down the pyramid; accountability flows up it
More strategy at the top, more operations at the base
Levels exist so that no manager has too many people to supervise directly

Top level — decisions about

Where the organisation is going (objectives, expansion, diversification)
Policies for every department; big money decisions

Middle level — decisions about

How each department meets the top's plan (programmes, budgets)
Matching manpower and machines to the department's share

Supervisory — decisions about

Who does which job today, and how many units must be produced
Fixing small problems before they reach the middle level
