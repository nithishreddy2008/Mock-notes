Framing The Constitution

1946 – 1950 · REPUBLIC FOUNDATION

1. Crucible of Partition & Genesis

Context
  • Cabinet Mission Plan (1946): Assembly indirectly elected by provincial legislatures.
  • Partition Impact: Membership reduced from 389 to 299; Muslim League boycotted.
  • Turmoil: Framing occurred amidst horrific communal riots, refugee crisis, and integration of 565 princely states.
  • Congress Dominance: Congress held 82% of seats, but encompassed a vast plural spectrum.

2. The Architects & Leadership

Leadership
  • Congress Triumvirate: Nehru (democratic vision), Patel (reconciler & state integration), Prasad (Assembly President).
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: Chairman of the Drafting Committee; anchored constitutional morality.
  • Sir B.N. Rau: Constitutional Advisor; synthesized global constitutional frameworks.
  • S.N. Mukherjee: Chief Draftsman; translated complex ideas into precise legal language.

3. Objectives Resolution & Ideals

Vision
  • Moved 13 Dec 1946: Jawaharlal Nehru introduced the defining ideological charter.
  • Sovereign Republic: Proclaimed India an Independent Sovereign Republic (later Socialist, Secular).
  • Core Guarantees: Social, economic, and political justice; equality of status and opportunity.
  • Universal Franchise: Adopted universal adult franchise without property or educational curbs.

4. Rights, Minorities & Social Equity

Debates
  • Separate Electorates Defeated: Opposed by Patel, Pant, and Begum Aizaz Rasul as divisive.
  • Untouchability Abolished: Article 17 outlawed caste untouchability completely.
  • Adivasi Advocacy: Jaipal Singh demanded land protection and tribal representation.
  • Affirmative Action: Legislative and public job reservations for SCs and STs.

5. Federalism & Distribution of Powers

Structure
  • Strong Center: Championed by Nehru and Ambedkar to preserve national unity and planning.
  • Provincial Protest: K. Santhanam warned a financially starved province would cripple governance.
  • Three Lists: Union List (97 items), State List (66 items), Concurrent List (47 items).
  • Emergency Powers: Union given sweeping powers under Article 356 and residuary authority.

6. Language Compromise & Enactment

Adoption
  • Language Conflict: R.V. Dhulekar pushed for Hindi; resisted by South (G. Durgabai).
  • Munshi-Ayyangar Formula: Hindi as Official Language; English continued for official use.
  • Adoption (26 Nov 1949): Signed by 284 members; celebrated as Constitution Day.
  • Enforcement (26 Jan 1950): Inaugurated Republic, commemorating the 1930 Purna Swaraj Day.
