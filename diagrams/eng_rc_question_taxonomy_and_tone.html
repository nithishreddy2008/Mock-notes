1. The 6 Canonical RC Question Archetypes & Solving Protocols
The Four-Step Golden RC Algorithm
Skim Questions (15s) ➔ Read Passage Actively (90s) ➔ Locate Anchors ➔ Eliminate Distractors
Never answer from memory. Always verify the answer by matching the exact line anchor in the passage before committing to an option.
Question Archetype Trigger Stem in CUET Execution Protocol
Central Idea / Theme "The passage primarily discusses..." Look at Paragraph 1 (thesis) & Final paragraph (synthesis). Avoid narrow details.
Direct Factual Recall "According to the passage, why..." Scan for keyword anchors; verify noun-verb relationships. Beware swapped facts.
Inference & Implication "It can be inferred that..." Find what MUST be logically true based on stated lines. Do NOT extrapolate.
Vocabulary in Context "The word 'X' most nearly means..." Substitute candidate options into original sentence; choose best syntactic fit.
Title Selection "The most suitable title is..." Must cover full passage scope; neither too narrow (single para) nor too broad.
Author's Tone & Attitude "The author's tone can be described as..." Examine adjectives, adverbs, and transition markers (however, unfortunately, remarkably).
The Line-Anchor Rule: In CUET UG, 100% of factual and inference questions have an identifiable parent sentence in the text. If an option sounds brilliant and universally true in reality but lacks direct textual warrant, it is an engineered distractor!
CUET Trap Alert (The "Too Extreme" Option): Options containing unconditional universal qualifiers such as always, never, completely, all, impossible, solely are almost universally wrong. Real passages express nuanced, qualified arguments.
2. The Master Author Tone & Mood Matrix
Tone Category Defining Vocabulary & Linguistic Markers Authorial Intent
Objective / Analytical Data, evidence, facts, neutral verbs (states, shows, reports). No emotional bias. Present impartial facts, empirical findings, or scientific descriptions.
Laudatory / Admiring Remarkable, heroic, profound, admirable, visionary, masterclass, exemplary. Praise, celebrate, or highlight positive attributes of a subject.
Critical / Censorious Flawed, negligent, short-sighted, detrimental, catastrophic, reckless. Expose deficiencies, condemn policies, or highlight negative repercussions.
Satirical / Ironical Mocking, tongue-in-cheek, paradoxical exaggeration, sarcastic rhetorical questions. Ridicule folly, hypocrisy, or absurd societal practices through humor.
Didactic / Pedagogical Should, must, ought to, vital lesson, one must realize, imperatives. Teach, instruct, advise, or impart a moral/ethical perspective.
Nostalgic / Melancholic Yearning, bygone days, wistful, golden era, mourn, departed, vanished. Reflect fondly or sorrowfully on the past and what has been lost.
Tone Decryption Diagnostic Steps
Identify Value-Judgments ➔ Is Attitude Positive, Negative, or Neutral? ➔ Eliminate Mismatches
If the author uses words like "regrettably", "alas", or "tragic", eliminate all positive/neutral tones immediately. Match the emotional valence first!
Tone vs Mood Distinction: - Tone: The *author's* attitude toward the subject matter or audience. - Mood: The *emotional atmosphere* created for the reader (e.g. suspenseful, somber, tranquil).
CUET Trap Alert (Confusing Tone with Topic): A passage about war is not automatically "violent" or "angry" in tone; if the author lists casualties and treaty dates dispassionately, the tone is strictly Objective / Informative.
