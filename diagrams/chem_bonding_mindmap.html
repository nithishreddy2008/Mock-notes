Ionic Bonding

Metal × non-metal: electrons transferred, ions held by electrostatic force
Octet rule drives formation — stable noble-gas configuration
Lattice enthalpy ↑ ⇒ melting point ↑, hardness ↑, solubility in water
Conduct electricity only when molten or aqueous (ions free to move)

VSEPR Theory

Repulsion order: lone pair–lp > lp–bond pair > bp–bp
Lone pairs compress angles: CH₄ 109.5° → NH₃ 107° → H₂O 104.5°
Shapes by electron pairs: linear → trigonal planar → tetrahedral → trigonal bipyramidal → octahedral
Count both bonded atoms and lone pairs around the central atom

Molecular Orbital Theory

Bond order = (N₀ − N*) / 2 — bonding minus antibonding electrons
BO ↑ ⇒ stability ↑, bond length ↓ (N₂ BO 3 > O₂ BO 2)
O₂ is paramagnetic — 2 unpaired electrons in π* orbitals
Fill σ1s → σ*1s → σ2s → σ*2s → π2p = σ2p → π*2p → σ*2p

Covalent Bonding & Parameters

Electrons shared; best Lewis structure = lowest formal charge
Bond length ↓ as bond order ↑; resonance = one real averaged structure
Dipole moment = bond polarity + geometry ⇒ CO₂ is net zero
Fajan's rules: small cation, big anion ⇒ more covalent character

Hybridisation

sp 180° (BeCl₂, C₂H₂) · sp² 120° (BF₃, C₂H₄) · sp³ 109.5° (CH₄, NH₃)
sp³d (PCl₅, trigonal bipyramidal) · sp³d² (SF₆, octahedral)
Shortcut: H = ½(V + M − C + A) — valence + monovalent − cation + anion
Sigma bonds use hybrids; free p-orbitals make π bonds

Hydrogen Bonding

H on F, O, N → attracted to a lone pair on a neighbouring molecule
Intermolecular (H₂O, HF, NH₃) vs intramolecular (o-nitrophenol)
Explains: ice floats, H₂O boils highest among hydrides of group 16
o-nitrophenol is steam-volatile — intramolecular H-bond kills clustering

EXCEPTIONS & TRAPS

Octet broken: BeCl₂, BF₃ (incomplete) · PCl₅, SF₆ (expanded)
O₂ paramagnetic — Lewis fails, MOT explains
CO₂ has polar bonds but zero dipole — linear symmetry
