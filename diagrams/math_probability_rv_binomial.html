Discrete Distributions Mean & Variance Bernoulli B(n, p)
Discrete Variables
Random Variable Mapping
Formal Definition
X: S → ℝ
A real-valued function whose domain is the sample space $S$ of a random experiment. Assigns a unique real numerical score to each elementary event.
X = xᵢ x₁ x₂ … xₙ
P(X = xᵢ) p₁ p₂ … pₙ
Normalization Invariant
pᵢ ≥ 0 and ∑ pᵢ = 1
Every probability must be non-negative, and the sum of all probabilities over the complete range must identically equal $1$. Used to solve for unknown parameter $k$.
Statistical Moments
Expectation & Variance
Mean (Expectation)
μ = E(X) = ∑ xᵢ · pᵢ
Weighted average of the values that $X$ takes. Represents the long-run theoretical center of the distribution.
Variance of X
σ² = Var(X) = E(X²) - [E(X)]²
Measures dispersion around the mean: $\text{Var}(X) = \sum x_i^2 p_i - \mu^2$. Always strictly non-negative ($\text{Var}(X) \ge 0$).
Standard Deviation & Linear Scaling
• Standard Deviation: $\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)}$.
• Expectation Scaling: $E(aX + b) = a E(X) + b$.
• Variance Scaling: $\text{Var}(aX + b) = a^2 \text{Var}(X)$. (Constant $b$ has zero variance!).
Bernoulli Process
The Binomial Model B(n, p)
Bernoulli Trial Axioms
1. Number of trials $n$ is finite and fixed.
2. Trials are mutually independent.
3. Each trial has exactly two outcomes: Success ($p$) and Failure ($q = 1 - p$).
4. Probability of success $p$ remains constant across all trials.
Binomial PMF
P(X = r) = ⁿCᵣ · pʳ · qⁿ⁻ʳ
Gives probability of obtaining exactly $r$ successes in $n$ independent trials ($r = 0, 1, 2, \dots, n$).
Binomial Parameters & Invariant
Mean = np, Var = npq (Var < Mean)
Since failure probability $q < 1$, the variance $npq$ of a Binomial distribution is always strictly less than its mean $np$.
Solving for Parameter k
• Standard CUET question gives $P(X = x)$ in terms of $k$.
• Use $\sum p_i = 1$ to form an algebraic equation and solve for $k$.
• Discard any root that makes any individual $p_i < 0$ or $> 1$!
At Least vs At Most r Successes
• "At least 1 success": $P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - q^n$.
• "At most 1 success": $P(X \le 1) = P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) = q^n + n p q^{n-1}$.
• Always use the complement when calculating "at least one"!
Replacement vs Without Replacement
• With Replacement: Trials are independent $\implies$ Follows Binomial Distribution.
• Without Replacement: Probabilities change after each draw $\implies$ Trials are dependent! Use Hypergeometric / sequential multiplication.
