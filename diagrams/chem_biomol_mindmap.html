CARBOHYDRATES: STRUCTURE & STORAGE ENERGY & SKELETON

  • Classification: Monosaccharides (Glc, Fru, Gal), Disaccharides (Sucrose, Maltose, Lactose), Polysaccharides (Starch, Cellulose, Glycogen).
  • Reducing Criterion: Free anomeric C-1/C-2 hemiacetal reduces Fehling's/Tollens'. All monosaccharides reduce; Sucrose is NON-reducing (1,2-bond).
  • D/L vs (+/-): D/L assigned by C-5 configuration in Fischer projection. D-glucose is dextrorotatory D-(+)-glucose.
  • Evidence Chain: HI $\to$ n-hexane; HCN $\to$ cyanohydrin; Br₂/H₂O $\to$ Gluconic acid; conc. HNO₃ $\to$ Saccharic acid; Ac₂O $\to$ Pentaacetate.
  • Mutarotation Equilibrium: α-D-glucopyranose (+112°) $\rightleftharpoons$ Equilibrium (+52.7°) $\rightleftharpoons$ β-D-glucopyranose (+19°).
  • Invert Sugar: Dextro sucrose (+66.5°) hydrolyses to D-(+)-glucose (+52.7°) + D-(-)-fructose (-92.4°), yielding laevorotatory mixture (-39.9°).
  • Polysaccharides: Amylose ($\alpha$-1,4 unbranched) vs Amylopectin ($\alpha$-1,4 + $\alpha$-1,6 branched) vs Cellulose ($\beta$-1,4 linear) vs Glycogen (animal starch).

PROTEINS & AMINO ACIDS CELLULAR WORKFORCE

  • Monomers: 20 standard $\alpha$-amino acids. All are L-amino acids. All are chiral and optically active EXCEPT Glycine (achiral).
  • Essential Amino Acids (10): Val, Leu, Ile, Lys, Met, Phe, Thr, Trp, His, Arg. Must be supplied through diet.
  • Zwitterion: Dipolar ion ⁺H₃N-CH(R)-COO⁻. At Isoelectric Point (pI), net charge is zero; minimal solubility.
  • Peptide Bond (-CO-NH-): Planar trans amide linkage formed by condensation. Partial double bond character prevents free rotation.
  • Four Structural Floors:
    ▪ 1° (Primary): Linear sequence of amino acids (covalent peptide bonds).
    ▪ 2° (Secondary): Regular coils: $\alpha$-helix (intramolecular H-bonds) & $\beta$-sheet (intermolecular H-bonds).
    ▪ 3° (Tertiary): 3D folding into Fibrous vs Globular. Stabilized by -S-S- bridges, H-bonds, salt bridges, hydrophobic forces.
    ▪ 4° (Quaternary): Multi-subunit arrangement (e.g. Haemoglobin: $2\alpha + 2\beta$).
  • Denaturation: Physical/chemical stress destroys 2°, 3°, 4° structures. Globular unfolds; biological activity is lost. 1° structure NEVER breaks!
  • Fibrous vs Globular: Fibrous (Keratin, Myosin, Collagen — insoluble, structural) vs Globular (Albumin, Haemoglobin, Insulin — soluble, functional).

ENZYMES & VITAMINS CATALYSIS & DEFENCE

  • Enzymes: Highly specific globular protein catalysts that drastically lower the activation energy of biochemical reactions.
  • Catalytic Mechanism: Lock-and-key model and induced-fit hypothesis. Highly sensitive to optimum pH (~7.4) and body temperature (~310 K).
  • Enzyme Naming: Substrate + suffix '-ase' (e.g. Maltase, Invertase, Urease, Pepsin, Zymase).
  • Vitamins Classification:
    ▪ Fat-Soluble: A, D, E, K (stored in liver and adipose; danger of hypervitaminosis).
    ▪ Water-Soluble: B-complex, C (excreted in urine; daily requirement; Exception: B₁₂ stored in liver).
  • High-Yield Deficiency Pairings:
    ▪ Vit. A (Retinol) $\to$ Xerophthalmia & Night Blindness.
    ▪ Vit. B₁ (Thiamine) $\to$ Beriberi.
    ▪ Vit. B₂ (Riboflavin) $\to$ Cheilosis.
    ▪ Vit. B₁₂ (Cyanocobalamin) $\to$ Pernicious Anaemia.
    ▪ Vit. C (Ascorbic Acid) $\to$ Scurvy.
    ▪ Vit. D (Calciferol) $\to$ Rickets & Osteomalacia.
    ▪ Vit. K (Phylloquinone) $\to$ Delayed blood clotting.

NUCLEIC ACIDS & HORMONES HEREDITY & SIGNALS

  • Chemical Composition: Pentose Sugar + Heterocyclic Nitrogenous Base + Phosphoric Acid.
  • Structural Units:
    ▪ Nucleoside: Base + Sugar (C-1' glycosidic linkage).
    ▪ Nucleotide: Base + Sugar + Phosphate (C-5' phosphoester linkage).
  • Polynucleotide Backbone: Alternating sugar and phosphate units linked via 3',5'-phosphodiester bridges with $5' \to 3'$ polarity.
  • DNA vs RNA:
    ▪ DNA: $\beta$-D-2-deoxyribose; Bases: A, T, G, C; Double helix; A=T (2 H-bonds) & G≡C (3 H-bonds).
    ▪ RNA: $\beta$-D-ribose; Bases: A, U, G, C (Uracil replaces Thymine); Single-stranded (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA).
  • Chargaff's Rules: $[A] = [T]$, $[G] = [C]$, and $\text{Purines} (A+G) = \text{Pyrimidines} (T+C)$.
  • Hormones: Intercellular endocrine chemical regulators:
    ▪ Steroids: Testosterone, Estrogen, Progesterone, Cortisone.
    ▪ Peptides / Proteins: Insulin (controls blood glucose), Glucagon, Oxytocin.
    ▪ Amine Derivatives: Epinephrine (adrenaline), Thyroxine (iodine-containing).
  • Hormone Deficiencies: Insulin $\to$ Diabetes mellitus; Thyroxine $\to$ Hypothyroidism/Goitre.

EXAM QUICK-FIRE TRAPS

1. Sucrose Linkage: $\alpha$-D-Glc-(1$\to$2)-$\beta$-D-Fru. Both anomeric carbons are locked $\implies$ non-reducing.

2. Invert Sugar: Rotation flips from +66.5° to -39.9° due to fructose's high negative specific rotation (-92.4°).

3. Denaturation: Boiled egg or curdling destroys 2°, 3°, 4° structures, but 1° peptide bonds are completely preserved.

4. Glycine: The ONLY standard amino acid lacking a chiral center (optically inactive).

5. Base Pairing: A=T (2 H-bonds) and G≡C (3 H-bonds). DNA with higher GC content has higher melting temperature ($T_m$).

6. Vitamin Storage: Water-soluble vitamins are excreted in urine, but Vitamin B₁₂ is stored in liver.
