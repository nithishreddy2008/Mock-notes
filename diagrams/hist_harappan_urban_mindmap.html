HARAPPAN URBAN ANATOMY & SETTLEMENTS
THEMES IN INDIAN HISTORY PART I · THEME 1 (CUET UG)

1. The Dual-Town Model (Mohenjodaro)

Citadel (West): Elevated on mud-brick platforms; fortified by perimeter walls; reserved for civic, ceremonial & public governance.
Lower Town (East): Vastly larger residential area; also walled; divided into sectors by planned streets.
Standardized Bricks: Uniform ratio of 1 : 2 : 4 (thickness : width : length) across every Harappan site from Gujarat to Punjab.
Settlement Variations: Dholavira features a unique tripartite division (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town) with massive stone fortifications.

2. Monumental Public Architecture

The Great Bath: Rectangular tank on Citadel; watertight with gypsum mortar; flights of steps on north & south sides.
Colonnaded Corridors: Great Bath surrounded by rooms on 3 sides; one room contained a large brick well; water drained into street canal.
Warehouse / Granary: Massive brick base survived at Mohenjodaro; upper timber superstructures decayed over millennia.
Ritual Significance: Scholars unanimously link the Great Bath to exclusive ritual purification and state ceremonies.

3. Street Grid & Advanced Drainage

Gridiron Layout: Streets intersected at precise right angles (90°); houses aligned along straight street grids.
Covered Drains: Street drains constructed of kiln-fired bricks or dressed stone slabs; easily removable for cleaning.
Domestic Connection: Every house had at least one wall bordering a street; waste water flowed into street drains via soak pits.
Drain Sump Basins: Solid refuse settled in private sump pits; only clarified water entered the municipal channels.

4. Domestic Architecture & Privacy

Courtyard-Centric: Rooms arranged around an open central courtyard; courtyard served as hub for cooking, spinning & craft.
Strict Visual Privacy: No ground-floor windows facing exterior streets; main entrance never gave a direct view into courtyard.
Private Bathrooms: Paved with waterproof bricks; individual drains ran directly through outer wall into municipal drain.
Public & Private Wells: Mohenjodaro boasted ~700 wells, many accessible from street for passing travelers.

5. Agricultural Base & Water Management

Crops Cultivated: Wheat, barley, lentil, chickpea, sesame, and mustard; millets found in Gujarat; rice was exceptionally rare.
Ploughed Field: Kalibangan (Rajasthan) yielded early Harappan ploughed field with two perpendicular furrow sets (double cropping).
Irrigation Systems: Canals traced at Shortughai (Afghanistan); stone water reservoirs discovered at Dholavira (Gujarat).
Animal Traction: Terracotta models of plough found at Cholistan and Banawali (Haryana); humped zebu oxen used for draught.

6. Specialized Craft Production Centers

Chanhudaro: Tiny settlement (~7 hectares) exclusively devoted to craft: bead-making, seal-cutting, shell-working & weights.
Coastal Outposts: Nageshwar and Balakot specialized in maritime shell objects (bangles, ladles, inlay work).
Lothal: Harbor port city in Gujarat; specialized in carnelian bead factories and copper metallurgy; brick dockyard basin.
Raw Material Outposts: Shortughai established in Badakhshan near best source of prized dark blue lapis lazuli.

⚠ CRITICAL CUET EXAM TRAPS & COMMONLY CONFUSED SITES

Kalibangan vs Banawali: Ploughed field is at Kalibangan; terracotta plough models are at Banawali and Cholistan.
Shortughai vs Dholavira: Canals found at Shortughai (Afghanistan); stone water harvesting reservoirs at Dholavira (Gujarat).
Chanhudaro: Only Harappan settlement with NO fortified citadel; 100% craft-oriented industrial town.
