Part 1: Morphological & Anatomical Evidences
Divergent vs Convergent
Homologous Organs DIVERGENT
Same basic origin/structure · Different functions
Indicates common ancestry with functional diversification into different ecological niches.
Key NCERT Examples:
• Vertebrate Forelimbs: Whale flipper, bat wing, cheetah leg, human arm (same bones: humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges).
• Plants: Thorns of Bougainvillea and Tendrils of Cucurbita (both axillary bud modifications).
• Vertebrate heart and brain anatomy.
Analogous Organs CONVERGENT
Different origin/structure · Similar functions
Indicates unrelated ancestors adapting to identical environmental demands or habitats.
Key NCERT Examples:
• Wings: Butterfly (folds of chitinous exoskeleton) vs Bird (feathered forelimb with bones).
• Eyes: Octopus eye (retina from skin) vs Mammal eye (retina from embryonic brain).
• Flippers: Penguin (bird) vs Dolphin (aquatic mammal).
• Storage: Sweet potato (root) vs Potato (underground stem).
Adaptive Radiation: One Ancestor → Diversified Radiating Lines Geographical Niches
Evolution of different species starting from a point in a geographical area and radiating to other habitats:
1. Darwin's Finches (Galapagos):
Ancestral seed-eating ground finch radiated into vegetarian tree finches, insectivorous finches, and woodpecker finches with altered beak shapes.
2. Australian Marsupials:
A single ancestral marsupial gave rise to diverse forms (koala, wombat, kangaroo, banded anteater) isolated on island continent Australia.
Convergent Evolution parallel: Placental wolf mirrors Tasmanian wolf; Placental mole mirrors Marsupial mole.
Embryological & Biochemical Support
• Embryological (Ernst Haeckel): Proposed biogenetic law ("ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny"). Disproved by Karl Ernst von Baer who noted embryos never pass through adult stages of other animals.
• Biochemical Evidence: Similarities in proteins (e.g., cytochrome c) and DNA sequences establish evolutionary kinship across taxa.
Part 2: Origin & Evolution of Man (Chronological Rungs)
Cranial Capacities & Habits
Dryopithecus
~15 mya (Miocene)
Ape-like
Hairy, walked like gorillas and chimpanzees; arboreal knuckle-walker. Common ancestor stock.
Ramapithecus
~15 mya (Shivalik hills)
More man-like
More erect posture, human-like dental arcade with smaller canines. Walked more upright.
Australopithecines
~3–4 to 2 mya
~450–500 cc
East African grasslands (Tanzania, Ethiopia, "Lucy"). Walked bipedally upright. Hunted with stone weapons; essentially ate fruit.
Homo habilis
~2.0 mya (Pleistocene)
650–800 cc
First human-like being (the hominid). Known as "handy man" / tool maker. Did not eat meat.
Homo erectus
~1.5 mya (Java man 1891)
900 cc
Large brain, upright bipedal posture. Homo erectus probably ate meat. Fire discovered & utilised.
Neanderthal Man
100,000–40,000 ya
1400 cc
Near East & Central Asia. Used animal hides to protect bodies; buried their dead with rituals.
Homo sapiens
75,000–10,000 ya
1350–1450 cc
Arose in Africa, migrated across continents. Prehistoric cave art (~18,000 ya, Bhimbetka); agriculture (~10,000 ya). Modern man.
CUET UG High-Yield Hominid Trap Checklist:
• Brain Capacity Match: H. habilis (650–800 cc) | H. erectus (900 cc) | Neanderthal (1400 cc) | H. sapiens (1350–1450 cc).
• Dietary Distinction: H. habilis did NOT eat meat; H. erectus probably ate meat.
• Cultural Markers: Burying dead + hides = Neanderthal; Cave art (Bhimbetka, MP) + agriculture = Homo sapiens.
