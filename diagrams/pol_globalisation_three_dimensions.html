Political Consequences
State Capacity, Minimalist State & Sovereignty
The Minimalist State Welfare Retreat
  • State withdraws from traditional social welfare functions (subsidies, universal healthcare, public enterprise).
  • State role reduced to core sovereign functions: maintaining law and order, national defense, and property rights.
  • Market forces and multinational corporations (MNCs) dictate socio-economic priorities.
Enhanced State Capacity Digital Surveillance
  • Advanced digital information technology, biometric databases, and big-data analytics dramatically empower the state.
  • States gather comprehensive intelligence on citizens, strengthening administrative efficiency, tax collection, and policing.
Primacy of the Sovereign State Enduring Power
  • State sovereignty is not destroyed; states remain the undisputed primary legal and geopolitical actors.
  • National security rivalries and inter-state power politics remain paramount.

Economic Consequences & India
Four Interconnected Flows, Bretton Woods & 1991 LPG
The Four Transnational Flows Core Drivers
  • Flow of Ideas: Free cross-border dissemination of philosophy, scientific knowledge, and democratic norms.
  • Flow of Capital: Massive electronic movement of investments, FDI, and portfolio capital.
  • Flow of Commodities: Removal of tariff and non-tariff trade barriers under WTO rules.
  • Flow of People: Movement of labor; remains the most restricted due to strict immigration barriers in the Global North.
Unequal Distribution of Gains Inequality
  • Creates a small class of affluent winners (corporates, tech elites) and a large mass of vulnerable losers.
  • Erodes institutional social safety nets, exposing unorganized workers to market volatility.
India's 1991 LPG Paradigm Shift Reforms
  • Shifted from post-1947 autarky/protectionism to Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (LPG) under P.V. Narasimha Rao.
  • Spurred GDP growth, expanded IT/services exports, but widened urban-rural income disparities.

Cultural Dialectics & Resistance
Homogenisation, Heterogenisation & Anti-Globalisation
Cultural Homogenisation Americanisation
  • Rise of a uniform global consumerist monoculture dominated by Western/American cultural icons.
  • Popularly termed "McDonaldization": blue jeans, fast food, Hollywood, pop music.
  • Threatens to erode fragile indigenous cultural heritage and local languages.
Cultural Heterogenisation Creative Fusion
  • External global cultural elements do not simply destroy local culture; they are modified and assimilated.
  • Produces unique hybridization: e.g., wearing a Khadi Kurta over blue jeans, or Indianized fast food (McAloo Tikki burger).
Resistance & World Social Forum Anti-Corporate
  • 1999 Seattle Protests: Protests at WTO Ministerial against non-transparent, anti-labor trade rules.
  • World Social Forum (WSF): Founded in Porto Alegre, Brazil (2001): "Another World is Possible."
  • 4th WSF Meeting (2004): Held in Mumbai, India, bringing together anti-globalisation coalitions.
