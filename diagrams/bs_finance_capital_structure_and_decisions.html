Trading on Equity & EBIT-EPS Mechanics
Favorable Financial Leverage (Trading on Equity) ROI > Cost of Debt
• Occurs when Return on Investment (ROI) exceeds contractual interest rate on debt.
• Adding cheaper debt in capital structure magnifies Earnings Per Share (EPS).
• Shareholders benefit directly from fixed-interest contractual claims.
Condition: ROI (e.g. 15%) > Cost of Debt (10%)
Outcome: EPS increases with higher Debt-Equity ratio
Unfavorable Leverage (Negative Trading on Equity) ROI < Cost of Debt
• Occurs when Return on Investment falls below the contractual cost of debt.
• Fixed interest charges burden operating profits, causing EPS to plummet.
• Heightens financial risk and probability of insolvency.
Condition: ROI (e.g. 6%) < Cost of Debt (10%)
Outcome: EPS collapses; equity value destroyed
Core CUET Takeaway: Debt is a double-edged sword. It is the cheapest source due to interest tax-deductibility ($Cost = I(1 - T)$), but carries mandatory legal financial obligation.
Capital Structure Determinants (NCERT)
1

Cash Flow Position

Adequate operating cash flow required to service fixed debt commitments (interest + principal).

2

ICR & DSCR Ratios

Interest Coverage Ratio ($EBIT / Interest$) & Debt Service Coverage Ratio evaluate cash solvency.

3

Tax Rate & Cost of Debt

Higher corporate tax rate makes debt relatively cheaper because interest is tax-deductible.

4

Control Consideration

Debt financing prevents dilution of voting control; equity issue risks management takeover.

5

Floatation Costs & Flexibility

Public equity entails higher floatation costs (underwriting, broker fees) than private debt loans.

Capital Requirements Determinants
Determinants of Fixed Capital (Long-Term)
  • Nature of Business: Manufacturing > Trading.
  • Scale of Operations: Large enterprises need higher fixed assets.
  • Choice of Technique: Capital-intensive > Labor-intensive.
  • Technology Upgradation: Fast obsolescence demands more capital.
  • Growth Prospects & Diversification: Expands plant assets.
  • Financing / Leasing Alternatives: Leasing reduces fixed capital.
Determinants of Working Capital (Short-Term)
  • Operating Cycle: Longer conversion cycle = Higher working capital.
  • Business Cycle & Seasonal: Peak season / boom requires more WC.
  • Credit Allowed vs Availed: Liberal credit given increases WC.
  • Operating Efficiency: Higher inventory turnover reduces WC.
  • Inflation: Rising raw material prices inflate WC requirement.
OPTIMAL CAPITAL STRUCTURE: That mix of Debt and Equity which minimizes the overall Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) and maximizes the market value of Equity Shares ($P_0$).
