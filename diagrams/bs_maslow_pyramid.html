MASLOW'S HIERARCHY OF NEEDS — five rungs, climbed one at a time
Self-actualisation
realise one's fullest potential
Esteem needs
self-respect, recognition, status
Social needs
belonging, affection, friendship
Safety / security needs
job security, pension, safe conditions
Physiological (basic) needs
food, clothing, shelter — the base salary pays for these first

The climb rule

A satisfied need no longer motivates — once a rung is met, the next rung pulls
People climb bottom to top: pay first, praise later
A manager must find which rung the employee is on and motivate there

How firms feed each rung

Physiological: basic salary, canteen
Security: job security, pension, insurance
Social: team events, friendly workplace
Esteem: titles, awards, recognition
Self-actualisation: challenging work, growth, autonomy

Why it matters for motivation

Money reaches the lower rungs — above them, non-financial motivators work better
Motivating at the wrong rung wastes money and goodwill
The theory explains why both financial and non-financial incentives exist

One line to remember

Unsatisfied need = the motivator; satisfied need = the retired one
The pyramid is hierarchical — lower needs dominate until met
Order to memorise: physiological → security → social → esteem → self-actualisation
