THE THREE ZONES OF GENERAL KNOWLEDGE
CUET UG · GENERAL APTITUDE TEST · GK, CURRENT AFFAIRS & STATIC GK
ZONE 1

Static GK

WHAT LIVES HERE
Facts that do not change: books & authors, awards, important days, firsts, rivers, capitals, fundamental rights.
HOW TO PREPARE
Category tables, not random facts — one table a week, revised on a fixed cycle.
QUESTION STYLE
Direct one-liners: "Who wrote Gitanjali?", "23 March marks which day?"
ZONE 2

Current Affairs

WHAT LIVES HERE
Significant events of the last year — national and international: missions, summits, awards, sports, appointments.
HOW TO PREPARE
A daily 15-minute news diet + a monthly consolidation page — the note grid below.
QUESTION STYLE
"Which country hosted the recent G20 summit?" — the event, the place, or the person involved.
ZONE 3

Everyday Knowledge

WHAT LIVES HERE
The world around you: why it rains, what a vaccine does, who runs your state — living common sense.
HOW TO PREPARE
Curiosity with a notebook: every "why?" you look up today is a question you answer tomorrow.
QUESTION STYLE
Applied: "Which vitamin do we make using sunlight?" — everyday science you already use.

The 5-field note grid — every event, current or static, gets these five lines

1
WHAT
The event or fact in one line — Chandrayaan-3 lands near the Moon's south pole.
2
WHEN
Date / day / year — 23 August 2023.
3
WHERE / WHO
Place or person attached to it — ISRO; India.
4
WHY IT MATTERS
First-of-its-kind? Record? Policy change?
5
STATIC LINK
Tie to an old fact — Chandrayaan-3 links to ISRO's founders and earlier missions.

Traps that catch most students

Reading news without notes — unrecorded reading evaporates within a week
Outdated facts — award names and officeholders change; verify against a current source before the exam
Cramming GK in the last week — GK compounds daily; a month of grids beats a night of panic
