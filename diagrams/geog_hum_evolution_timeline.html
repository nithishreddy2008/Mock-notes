Evolution of Human Geography: The Corridors of Time

Chronological sequence of disciplinary paradigms, epistemological shifts, and core analytical approaches

CUET UG Geography · Chronological Evolution
Early Colonial Period Phase 1

Exploration & Description

Imperial and trade interests motivated exploration of uncharted territories. Encyclopedic collection of travelogues and descriptive inventories of foreign resources.
Focus: Discovering new lands, charting coastlines, and cataloguing flora, fauna, and trade wealth for European empires.
Later Colonial Period Phase 2

Regional Analysis

Detailed, comprehensive investigation of all physical and human aspects of defined geographic units. Premised on the holistic idea that regions constitute interdependent parts of the whole earth.
Focus: Regional synthesis; understanding local balance to extract raw materials and administer colonies systematically.
1930s – Inter-War Period Phase 3

Areal Differentiation

Focus on identifying the unique identity and distinguishing traits of particular places. Emphasized explaining why and how one geographic area differed from neighboring areas.
Focus: Spatial uniqueness (idiographic study), separating distinct physical-cultural landscapes across continents.
Late 1950s – Late 1960s Phase 4

Spatial Organisation (Quantitative Revolution)

Application of computers, statistical modelling, and universal laws of physics to human behaviour. Replaced descriptive accounts with quantifiable spatial patterns and mathematical laws.
Focus: Positivist models of market catchments, transport network geometries, and central place hierarchies.
1970s Radical Shift Phase 5

Humanistic, Radical & Behavioural Schools

Backlash against sterile quantitative models. Three revolutionary schools emerged: Welfare/Humanistic (social well-being: housing, health), Radical (Marxian critique of poverty), and Behavioural (lived perception).
Focus: Re-humanising geography; tackling real-world social deprivation, racial segregation, and economic injustice.
1990s to Present Phase 6

Post-Modernism in Geography

Rejection of all-encompassing universal theories and grand global narratives. Emphasis on local context, plural subjective realities, cultural relativity, and marginalized voices.
Focus: Deconstructing power structures; recognizing historical specificity and diverse spatial experiences.
Exam Note: Direct questions are regularly framed from NCERT Table 1.1 matching historical periods with their respective geographical approaches.
