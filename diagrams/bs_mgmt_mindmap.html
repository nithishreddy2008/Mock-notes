NATURE & SIGNIFICANCE
OF MANAGEMENT
UNIT I · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Concept & Characteristics

Management = process of getting things done with the aim of achieving goals effectively and efficiently
Effectiveness = reaching the goal on time; efficiency = reaching it at minimum cost
Characteristics: goal-oriented · all-pervasive · multidimensional · continuous · group activity · dynamic · intangible force
Multidimensional: manages work, people and operations

Objectives of Management

Organisational: survival, profit, growth
Social: eco-friendly production, basic amenities, opportunities for disadvantaged groups
Personal: competitive salary, peer recognition, growth of each employee
All three sets must be balanced, not traded off

Importance (Why It Matters)

Achieves group goals — directs individual effort toward the common target
Increases efficiency — lowers cost through better planning and organising
Creates a dynamic organisation — helps members accept change
Achieves personal objectives · develops society — quality goods, jobs, new technology

Management as Science, Art, Profession

Science? Yes — systematised body of knowledge, principles from experimentation — but an inexact (social) science
Art? Yes — personal skill, creativity, learned by practice; every manager applies theory in own style
Profession? Partly — body of knowledge & association (AIMA) exist, but no compulsory licence or entry test; not yet a full profession
Conclusion: science + art, developing profession

Levels & Functions

Top (Board, CEO) — vision, policies · Middle (dept heads) — interpret, link · Supervisory (foremen) — day-to-day
Five functions: Planning → Organising → Staffing → Directing → Controlling
Controlling compares performance with the standard set in planning and feeds back
Authority flows down, accountability flows up

Coordination — Essence of Management

Coordination = force that synchronises and integrates group effort to achieve common goals
Nature: integral · continuous · group effort · all-pervasive · duty of every manager · deliberate
Why needed: growth in size, functional differentiation, specialisation
Coordination ≠ cooperation: cooperation is voluntary willingness; coordination is deliberate synchronisation and includes it

Traps that catch most students

Effectiveness is about the end; efficiency is about the cost — do not swap them
Management is not a full profession — no compulsory license or restricted entry
Management is needed in every organisation — schools, clubs, hospitals — not only profit businesses
Cooperation alone cannot guarantee coordination
