REGIONAL ASPIRATIONS AND CONFLICTS
POLITICS IN INDIA SINCE INDEPENDENCE · CH. 6 (TOPIC 7)

1. The Indian Accommodation Model

Democratic accommodation: India rejected European nation-state homogeneity; unity through diversity.
Not anti-national: Regional aspirations are treated as healthy expressions of federal democratic politics.
Flexible constitutionalism: Special provisions (Art 370, 371, 5th & 6th Schedules) accommodate unique local needs.
Dual loyalty accepted: Indian identity does not erase linguistic, cultural or regional pride.

2. Jammu and Kashmir

Accession (Oct 1947): Maharaja Hari Singh signed Instrument of Accession after Pakistani tribal raid.
Article 370: Granted special internal autonomy; Sheikh Abdullah became Prime Minister of J&K.
Instability: Sheikh dismissed in 1953; returned via 1974 Indira-Sheikh Accord; died in 1982.
Insurgency (1989): 1987 election rigging triggered widespread armed militancy backed by cross-border training.

3. Punjab Crisis (1980s)

Anandpur Sahib Resolution (1973): Akali Dal demanded state autonomy, Chandigarh, water rights, and federalism.
Operation Blue Star (June 1984): Military action to flush militants from Golden Temple; Akal Takht damaged.
Indira assassinated (31 Oct 1984): Shot by Sikh bodyguards; sparked horrific anti-Sikh violence in Delhi.
Rajiv-Longowal Accord (1985): Compromise pact signed; peace gradually restored by late 1990s through elections.

4. The North-East: Autonomy Demands

Isolation: Linked to mainland via narrow 22-km Siliguri Corridor ("Chicken's Neck"); vast ethnic diversity.
Statehood reorganisation: Carved out from composite Assam — Nagaland (1963), Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura (1972).
Mizoram & Arunachal (1987): Upgraded from Union Territories to full federal states.
Tribal councils: Sixth Schedule autonomous district councils protect tribal land, forests, and customs.

5. Secession & Accords in North-East

Mizo Insurgency: Laldenga led Mizo National Front (MNF) after 1959 famine; waged 20-year guerrilla war.
Mizo Accord (1986): Laldenga signed historic peace accord with Rajiv Gandhi; Mizoram became a model peaceful state.
Nagaland: Led by A.Z. Phizo (Naga National Council); declared unilateral independence in 1947; armed truce talks.
Assam Accord (1985): AASU student agitation against "foreigners"; 24 March 1971 set as identification cutoff date.

6. Rise of Regional Political Parties

Authentic voices: Regional parties articulate distinct regional grievances: DMK/AIADMK, Akali Dal, AGP, TDP.
Telugu Desam Party (1982): Founded by N.T. Rama Rao on the plank of "Telugu self-respect" (Atma Gauravam).
National impact: Regional parties became indispensable kingmakers in the post-1989 coalition era.
Democratic deepening: Decentralised power from New Delhi to state capitals and grassroots communities.

KEY EXAM PITFALLS — ACCORDS, DATES & LEADERS

Assam Accord Cutoff: 24 March 1971 (eve of Bangladesh war) — not 1947 or 1951!
Key Leaders: Laldenga = Mizoram (MNF); A.Z. Phizo = Nagaland (NNC); Harchand Singh Longowal = Punjab (Akali Dal).
Operation Blue Star: Conducted in June 1984 under Indira Gandhi; Longowal Accord signed in July 1985 under Rajiv Gandhi.
