CONTEMPORARY SOUTH ASIA
CONTEMPORARY WORLD POLITICS · CH. 3 (TOPIC 12)

1. Pakistan: Military vs Democracy

Military Coups: General Ayub Khan (1958) → Yahya Khan (1969) → Zia-ul-Haq (1977) → Pervez Musharraf (1999).
Democratic Windows: Zulfikar Ali Bhutto (1971–77); competitive democracy of Benazir Bhutto and Nawaz Sharif (1988–99).
Obstacles to Democracy: Clout of the military establishment, clergy, landed aristocracy, and external US/China patron funding.
Nuclear Capability: Tested nuclear devices in Chagai Hills (May 1998) in direct response to India's Pokhran-II.

2. Bangladesh: Birth & Democracy

1971 Liberation War: Protested West Pakistani economic domination & Urdu imposition; Awami League led by Sheikh Mujib.
Indian Intervention: India gave refuge to 10 million displaced refugees; defeated Pakistani army (Dec 1971).
Authoritarian Reversals: Mujib assassinated in 1975; military dictatorships of Ziaur Rahman and H.M. Ershad.
Multi-Party Restoration (1990): Massive mass upsurge restored parliamentary democracy; alternating Awami League vs BNP.

3. Nepal: Monarchy to Republic

Constitutional Monarchy (1990): People's movement forced King Birendra to accept democratic constitution.
Triangular Struggle: Bloody civil conflict among the King, parliamentary parties, and Maoist guerrillas (1996–2006).
King Gyanendra Takeover (2002–05): Dissolved parliament and dismissed prime minister; triggered massive protests.
April 2006 Uprising: Seven Party Alliance (SPA) + Maoists forced restoration; monarchy abolished in 2008; 2015 Constitution.

4. Sri Lanka: Ethnic Conflict & Peace

Sinhala Majoritarianism: Post-1948 'Sinhala Only' policies disenfranchised Tamil minority in education and civil jobs.
LTTE Insurgency (1976): Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam led by Velupillai Prabhakaran fought armed war for separate Tamil state.
IPKF Intervention (1987–90): Rajiv Gandhi sent Indian Peace Keeping Force; faced local hostility; withdrawn in 1990.
LTTE Defeat (May 2009): Sri Lankan military crushed LTTE and killed Prabhakaran; high economic growth maintained.

5. Regional Cooperation: SAARC & SAFTA

SAARC Founded (Dec 1985): Established in Dhaka with 7 nations; Afghanistan joined as 8th member in 2007 (New Delhi Summit).
SAFTA Agreement (2004): South Asian Free Trade Area signed at 12th Islamabad Summit; effective from 1 January 2006.
Structural Impediments: Bilateral Indo-Pak discord (Kashmir, terror); summits suspended post-2016 Uri attack.
Bilateral Agreements: India-Sri Lanka FTA (1998); Indus Waters Treaty (1960); 1996 Ganga Water Treaty with Bangladesh.

6. India's Bilateral Neighborhood Matrix

Pakistan: Unresolved Kashmir dispute, Siachen glacier, Sir Creek, cross-border terrorism; 1999 Lahore bus diplomacy.
Bangladesh: Land Boundary Agreement (2015 exchange of enclaves); maritime boundary resolved; transit connectivity.
Maldives: Operation Cactus (1988 Indian paratrooper intervention to foil coup); multi-party democracy adopted in 2005.
Bhutan: Exemplary peaceful bilateral ties; major hydroelectric projects (Chukha, Kurichhu, Tala) exporting power to India.

EXAM PITFALLS — DATES, TREATIES & OPERATIONS

SAARC 8th Member: Afghanistan was admitted in 2007 at the 14th Summit in New Delhi, NOT in 1985!
Operation Cactus (1988): Indian military operation in Maldives against Sri Lankan Tamil mercenaries (PLOTE).
Indus Waters Treaty: Signed in 1960 between Nehru & Ayub Khan, brokered by the World Bank.
