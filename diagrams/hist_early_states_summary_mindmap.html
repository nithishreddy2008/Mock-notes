Core Watershed: The 6th Century BCE
  • Second Urbanization: Rapid emergence of fortified cities, punch-marked coinage, wide iron tool usage, and early states.
  • New Religious Movements: Rise of heterodox thinkers — Gautama Buddha and Vardhamana Mahavira challenging Vedic sacrificial rituals.
  • Political Systems: Coexistence of monarchies (*rajyas*) and oligarchic clan-based republics (ganas / sanghas like Vajji).
  • Dharmasutras: Sanskrit texts composed by Brahmanas codifying royal conduct, tax rates (1/6th of produce), and varna obligations.
1
The 16 Mahajanapadas & Magadha's Rise
  • Key Mahajanapadas: Magadha, Kosala, Kuru, Panchala, Gandhara, Avanti, and the Vajji confederacy (capital: Vaishali).
  • Magadhan Dominance: Emerge as the supreme state (c. 6th–4th cent. BCE) under Bimbisara, Ajatasattu, and the Nandas.
  • Strategic Advantages: Rich iron-ore deposits in Jharkhand; fertile alluvium of Ganga/Son; war elephants; natural river communications.
  • Capitals: First at fortified Rajagriha ("house of the king" ringed by hills); shifted to riverine Pataliputra (Patna) in 4th cent. BCE.
2
The Mauryan Empire & Dhamma
  • Foundation (321 BCE): Chandragupta Maurya overthrowing Nandas; guided by Chanakya (author of Arthashastra).
  • Sources: Megasthenes' Indica, Buddhist/Jaina chronicles, and Emperor Ashoka's monumental rock/pillar edicts.
  • Military Organization: Megasthenes notes a central board of 30 members coordinating 6 sub-committees (Navy, Supply, Infantry, Cavalry, Chariots, Elephants).
  • Ashokan Dhamma: Moral ethos of non-violence, elder respect, religious tolerance; propagated by dedicated Dhamma Mahamattas.
3
Divine Kingship & Regional Kingdoms
  • Kushana Divine Kingship: Colossal statues unearthed at Mat (Mathura) and Surkh Kotal; rulers assumed title Devaputra ("son of God").
  • Gupta Prashastis: Kings praised as divine protectors; court poet Harishena authored the famous Prayag Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar) for Samudragupta.
  • Chiefdoms of the South: Cholas, Cheras, and Pandyas in Tamilakam; patronized early Sangam bardic poetry and maritime trade.
  • Feudatory Ties: Guptas governed through samantas (subordinate lords) who provided military levies and tributes to the sovereign.
4
Agrarian Tech, Land Grants & Gender
  • Productivity Tech: Spread of iron-tipped ploughshares and labour-intensive paddy transplantation in alluvial zones.
  • Hydraulic Engineering: The rock-cut Sudarshana Lake (Gujarat) built by Mauryas, rebuilt by Saka ruler Rudradaman, and restored by Guptas.
  • Agrahara Grants: Tax-free land endowments granted by monarchs to Brahmanas and temples, recorded on copper plates (*tamrapatra*).
  • Prabhavati Gupta: Daughter of Chandragupta II married into Vakatakas; exceptionally granted land despite Dharmashastric restrictions on women.
5
Trade, Coins & Inscriptional Decipherment
  • Numismatic Evolution: Silver/copper punch-marked coins (6th c. BCE) → Kushanas issue first gold coins (1st c. CE) → Guptas issue purest gold dinars.
  • Merchant Guilds (Shrenis): Organized banking, raw material procurement, and manufacture; made collective votive stupa donations.
  • Decipherment Breakthrough: James Prinsep (1838) unlocked Ashokan Brahmi and Kharosthi, identifying king "Piyadassi" as Ashoka.
  • Epigraphic Limits: Weathered stones, missing letters, royal eulogy bias (*prashastis*), and total silence on ordinary peasants.
