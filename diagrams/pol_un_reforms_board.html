UNSC Expansion: Kofi Annan's 1997 Criteria

Economic Capability: Must be a major economic power capable of contributing significantly to world development.
Military Strength: Must be a major military power with operational capability for international peace enforcement.
Financial Contribution: Must be a substantial contributor to the regular United Nations operating budget.
Demographic Weight: A large nation in terms of population, reflecting global humanity.
Democratic Values: A nation that respects democracy, fundamental human rights, and rule of law.
Geographical Balance: Must enhance equitable geographical representation and diverse global civilizations.

India's Justification for Permanent UNSC Seat

Demographic Primacy: World's most populous nation, representing over 1.4 billion people (18% of global population).
Democratic Beacon: World's largest functioning democracy with an unbroken constitutional continuity since 1950.
Economic Dynamism: World's 5th largest economy (and 3rd by PPP), driving global post-pandemic recovery.
Peacekeeping Champion: One of the largest cumulative troop and police contributors to UN Peacekeeping worldwide.
Non-Proliferation Integrity: Impeccable record of voluntary export controls and responsible nuclear doctrine (No First Use).

GLOBAL ECONOMIC GOVERNANCE: WEIGHTED VOTES & TRADE RULES

International Monetary Fund (IMF) Established at Bretton Woods in 1944. Operates on a quota-based weighted voting system where financial contributions dictate voting power. The US possesses an effective veto over major decisions with over 16.5% voting share.
World Bank (IBRD & IDA) Created in 1944 to finance post-war reconstruction. Today it finances infrastructure and antipoverty programs in developing countries. By unwritten convention, the World Bank President is always a US citizen nominated by Washington.
World Trade Organization (WTO) Unlike the IMF, every WTO member theoretically has one vote and decisions are taken by consensus. In practice, however, major trading blocs (US, EU) dominate deliberations via "Green Room" informal consultations.

PITFALL 1: Non-Permanent Term Limits

The 10 non-permanent members of the UNSC are elected for a 2-year term by the General Assembly, and cannot be immediately re-elected for another consecutive term.

PITFALL 2: Trusteeship Council Status

The Trusteeship Council did NOT get abolished; its operations were merely suspended on 1 November 1994 following the independence of Palau, the last UN trust territory.

PITFALL 3: ICJ Election & Term

The International Court of Justice (The Hague) has 15 judges elected for 9-year terms by simultaneous voting in both the General Assembly and the Security Council.
