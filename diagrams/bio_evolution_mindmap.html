1. Origin of Life & Early Earth
Chemical Evolution
• Timeline: Big Bang (~20 bya) → Earth forms (~4.5 bya) → Life appears (~4.0 bya) → Cellular life (~2.0 bya).
• Early Reducing Atmosphere: Water vapour, CH4, CO2, NH3 — no free O2.
• Oparin-Haldane Hypothesis: Inorganic gases → simple organics → macromolecules → coacervates.
• Miller-Urey (1953): Electric discharge (~800°C) with CH4:NH3:H2 + H2O vapour produced amino acids (glycine, alanine, aspartic acid).
• RNA World: RNA was first genetic and biocatalytic material (ribozymes).
2. Theories of Evolution
Historical Foundations
• Lamarckism: Use and disuse of organs; inheritance of acquired traits (disproved by Weismann germplasm).
• Darwinism: Charles Darwin (HMS Beagle, Galapagos) & Alfred Wallace (Malay Archipelago) co-proposed natural selection. Key tenets: branching descent & natural selection.
• Mutation Theory (Hugo de Vries): Worked on Oenothera lamarckiana. Evolution is jerky & sudden via Saltation (single-step large mutation) — random and directionless (contrasting Darwin's slow gradualism).
3. Selection Modes & Population Genetics
Mechanisms
• Three Modes: Stabilising (favours mean, e.g. infant birth weight), Directional (shifts to one extreme, e.g. Industrial melanism, DDT resistance), Disruptive (favours both extremes, splits peak).
• Hardy-Weinberg Law: In large, randomly mating populations: p2 + 2pq + q2 = 1 and p + q = 1.
• Five Disturbing Factors: 1. Gene migration/flow, 2. Genetic drift (Founder effect, bottleneck in small pools), 3. Mutation, 4. Genetic recombination, 5. Natural selection.
4. Geological Milestones & History
Eras & Epochs
• Major Milestones: Invertebrates (500 mya) → Jawless fish (350 mya) → Seaweeds (320 mya) → First amphibians (350 mya).
• Coelacanth (Lobefin): Caught in S. Africa (1938); transitional living fossil between fish & amphibians.
• Reptiles & Dinosaurs: Ichthyosaurs returned to water (200 mya). Tyrannosaurus rex was largest carnivore (20 ft). Dinosaurs disappeared suddenly ~65 mya.
• Mammals: Shrew-like earliest mammals; took over after dinosaur extinction.
5. Comparative Anatomy & Radiations
Evidence Patterns
• Homologous (Divergent): Common origin, differing jobs. Whale flipper, bat wing, human arm. Bougainvillea thorn & Cucurbita tendril.
• Analogous (Convergent): Differing origins, identical jobs. Bird vs butterfly wings; octopus vs mammal eye; sweet potato (root) vs potato (stem).
• Adaptive Radiation: One ancestor radiates into different ecological niches. Darwin's finches (Galapagos); Australian marsupials vs Placental equivalents.
6. Origin & Evolution of Man
Hominid Ladder
• Dryopithecus & Ramapithecus (15 mya): Dryopithecus ape-like; Ramapithecus more man-like.
• Australopithecines (3–4 mya, Lucy): Upright bipedal; stone tools; fruit-eaters.
• Homo habilis (2 mya): 650–800 cc brain; toolmaker; did not eat meat.
• Homo erectus (1.5 mya, Java): 900 cc brain; probably ate meat.
• Neanderthal (100k–40k ya): 1400 cc; used hides; buried their dead.
• Homo sapiens (75k–10k ya): 1350–1450 cc; cave art (18k ya); agriculture (10k ya).
CUET UG Top Traps
Homology vs Analogy: Divergent = Homology (common ancestor). Convergent = Analogy (different origins adapting to same need). Never flip these!
Genetic Drift: Operates ONLY in small isolated populations purely by chance. Bottleneck and Founder effect are subtypes of drift.
Hominid Meat Diet: Homo habilis did NOT eat meat. Homo erectus probably ate meat. Neanderthal buried dead with ceremony.
