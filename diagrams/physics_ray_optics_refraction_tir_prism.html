Snell's Law & Apparent Depth Refraction
Snell's Law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
Apparent Depth: d' = d / μ (where μ = n₂/n₁ > 1)
Normal Shift: Δt = d - d' = d · (1 - 1/μ)
  • Bending Rule: Ray bends towards normal when slowing down (rarer → denser); bends away when speeding up (denser → rarer).
  • Frequency Invariance: Frequency ν remains invariant ($E = hν = \text{const}$); speed and wavelength compress by factor μ.
  • Shift Independence: Normal shift Δt is independent of the object's depth below the slab; it depends strictly on slab thickness d.
Medium Speed: v = c / μ Wavelength: λ' = λ / μ
TIR & Optical Waveguides Critical Angle
Critical Angle: sin θ_c = n_rarer / n_denser = 1 / μ
Glass-Air: μ ≈ 1.50 ⟹ θ_c ≈ 41.8° ≈ 42°
Acceptance Angle: sin θ_a = √(μ₁² - μ₂²) (Numerical Aperture)
  • Dual Mandatory Conditions: Light must travel from denser to rarer medium, and incident angle must exceed critical angle ($i > θ_c$).
  • Zero Loss Propagation: TIR reflects 100% of incident radiant power with zero absorption, unlike metallic mirrors.
  • Prism Periscopes: 45°-90°-45° right-angle isosceles crown glass prisms bend light by 90° or invert by 180° via TIR ($i = 45° > 42°$).
Diamond θ_c ≈ 24.4° (High Sparkle) Water θ_c ≈ 48.6°
Prism Deviation & Dispersion Spectral Dispersion
Prism Angles: A = r₁ + r₂ & δ = i + e - A
Min Deviation: μ = sin((A + δ_m)/2) / sin(A/2)
Thin Prism (A < 10°): δ ≈ (μ - 1) · A
Dispersive Power: ω = (μ_v - μ_r) / (μ_y - 1)
  • Minimum Deviation Symmetry: Occurs strictly when $i = e$ and $r_1 = r_2 = A/2$; the refracted ray travels parallel to the prism base.
  • Cauchy's Dispersion Law: $\mu(\lambda) \approx A_0 + B_0/\lambda^2$; since $\lambda_{\text{red}} > \lambda_{\text{violet}}$, $\mu_{\text{violet}} > \mu_{\text{red}}$, hence violet deviates most.
  • Slab vs Prism: Parallel glass slab causes lateral displacement without net angular dispersion; non-parallel prism faces produce permanent angular dispersion.
Flint Glass: High ω (Dispersion) Crown Glass: Low ω
