1. Linguistic Composition (Grierson Survey)

4 Language Families

Indo-Aryan (Indo-European Family)

73% of Population

Spatial Belt: Northern, Western, and Central India. Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia, Assamese, Urdu, Rajasthani, Maithili.

Dravidian (Dravida Family)

20% of Population

Spatial Belt: Peninsular India. Telugu (largest Dravidian speaker base), Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam, and tribal tongues (Gondi, Kurukh/Oraon).

Austroasiatic / Nishada Family

1.38% of Population

Spatial Belt: Chotanagpur plateau and Meghalaya. Santhali, Mundari, Ho, Birhor, and Khasi (Mon-Khmer branch in Meghalaya).

Sino-Tibetan / Kirata Family

0.85% of Population

Spatial Belt: Himalayan borderlands and Northeast. Tibeto-Himalayan (Bhotia, Kinnauri), North Assam (Mishi, Dafla), and Assam-Myanmarese (Bodo, Naga, Manipuri).

Constitutional Benchmark: 22 Languages in 8th Schedule Largest: Hindi (~43.6%)

2. Religious Composition & Spatial Clusters

Census 2011 Data

Hindus (Majoritarian Distribution)

79.8% · 96.6 Crore

Majoritarian presence across most states; exceeds 80–90% in Himachal Pradesh, Odisha, and Chhattisgarh. Less than 50% in J&K, Punjab, and Northeast.

Muslims (Largest Religious Minority)

14.2% · 17.2 Crore

Concentrated in Jammu & Kashmir (majority), Lakshadweep (majority), West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Kerala, and Hyderabad.

Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists & Jains

Combined ~5.0%

Christians (2.3%): Nagaland, Mizoram, Meghalaya, Goa, Kerala. Sikhs (1.7%): Punjab (majority). Buddhists (0.7%): Maharashtra, Ladakh, Sikkim. Jains (0.4%): Rajasthan, Gujarat, Mumbai.

Religious Pluralism: High minority concentrations in border zones Jains: Urban trade

3. Rural-Urban Structure & Urbanization

68.8% Rural · 31.2% Urban

Rural Demographics (83.3 Crore)

68.8% of India

Inhabits 6,40,867 inhabited villages. Highest rural share in Himachal Pradesh (89.97%) and Bihar (88.71%). Heavy agrarian dependence.

Urban Demographics (37.7 Crore)

31.2% of India

Among major states, Goa (62.17%) has the highest urban share, followed by Mizoram (52.1%) and Tamil Nadu (48.4%). Lowest in Himachal Pradesh (10.03%).

Census Definition Criteria for "Urban Area"

Statutory & Census Towns

1. Municipality or Cantonment Board. 2. Minimum population of 5,000. 3. At least 75% male workers in non-agricultural pursuits. 4. Density ≥ 400 persons/km².

Mega-Cities: Greater Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru Driver: Rural-Urban migration

4. Occupational Matrix & Economic Status

Work Participation: 39.8%

Economic Work Status Classification

Census Criterion: 183 Days

Main Workers: Work for ≥ 183 days (6 months) in the year (77.8% of workers). Marginal Workers: Work for < 183 days (22.2%). Non-Workers: Dependent population (60.2% of India).

Four Standard Occupational Categories

Sectoral Shift

1. Cultivators: 24.6% (declining). 2. Agricultural Labourers: 30.0% (landless). 3. Household Industry: 3.8%. 4. Other Workers: 41.6% (manufacturing, trade, transport, services).

Gender & Spatial Variations in Participation

Male 53.3% · Female 25.5%

Higher female work participation in tribal, hill, and agrarian states (Himachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Chhattisgarh) compared to urban-industrial regions.

Structural Transition: Shift from Primary (54.6%) toward Tertiary services High dependency ratio
