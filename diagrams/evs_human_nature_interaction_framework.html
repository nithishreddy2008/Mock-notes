Evolutionary Paradigm of Human-Nature Interactions & Environmental Ethics

From Frontier Exploitation to Ecocentric Stewardship, Earth Democracy, and Rights of Nature

PARADIGM LIFECYCLE · FIG 2
Stage 01 · Frontier Paradigm
Anthropocentric Domination
• Nature viewed purely as an inexhaustible sink and resource quarry for human economic expansion.
• Mechanistic Cartesian-Baconian worldview: humans are masters and possessors of nature.
• Instrumental value only; zero intrinsic moral standing for non-human species.
Consequence: Rampant deforestation, industrial contamination, tragedy of open-access commons.
Stage 02 · Reform Paradigm
Resource Conservation & Stewardship
• Utilitarian conservation (Gifford Pinchot): "Greatest good for the greatest number for the longest time."
• Wendell Berry's agrarian stewardship: ethical obligation of humans as caretakers of living soil.
• Regulatory controls: Clean air and water mandates; sustained-yield forestry models.
Focus: Intergenerational equity, mitigation of wasteful resource depletion, scientific forestry.
Stage 03 · Ecocentric Paradigm
Biocentric Egalitarianism & Land Ethic
• Aldo Leopold's Land Ethic: expanding moral consideration to soils, waters, fauna, and flora.
• Arne Næss's Deep Ecology: all living beings possess inherent intrinsic worth independent of utility.
• Preservationist movement (John Muir): wilderness must be preserved untouched for spiritual value.
Principle: Ecosystem integrity, population reduction, structural downscaling of consumerism.
Stage 04 · Transformative Paradigm
Earth Democracy & Rights of Nature
• Vandana Shiva's Earth Democracy: living economies, cultural diversity, and reclaimed seed sovereignty.
• Legal personhood for rivers, glaciers, and ecosystems (e.g., Whanganui River, Te Urewera, Gangotri).
• Murray Bookchin's Social Ecology: dismantling social hierarchies as a prerequisite for ecological harmony.
Current Frontier: Constitutional rights of Mother Earth (Pachamama), biocultural conservation.
