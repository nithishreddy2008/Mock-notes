1. Patterns & Communication
10 MAY 1857
  • The Mutiny Pattern: Began at Meerut; signaled by evening gun/bugle; broke open jails; raided bell of arms (shastragaar); cut telegraphs.
  • Midnight March to Delhi: Reached Red Fort on 11 May during Ramadan; secured Bahadur Shah Zafar's symbolic leadership.
  • Circulation Networks: Distribution of chapatis from village to village and red lotuses as silent symbols of collective mobilization.
  • Regimental Panchayats: Sepoy representatives from different lines gathered in night councils to coordinate simultaneous uprisings.
★ Core Axis: Awadh in Total Revolt
CIVIL INSURRECTION
  • Dalhousie's Annexation (1856): Deposed Nawab Wajid Ali Shah on grounds of misgovernment; called Awadh "a cherry that will fall into our mouth".
  • Summary Settlement of 1856: Dispossessed traditional taluqdars of 50% to 67% of their villages, demolishing forts and disarming retainers.
  • "Nursery of the Bengal Army": Almost every peasant family in Awadh had a son in the sepoy ranks; sepoy grievances mirrored rural fury.
  • Total Mobilization: Begum Hazrat Mahal and young Birjis Qadr rallied court nobles, taluqdars, and peasants in an unyielding popular war.
2. Leaders & Rebellious Centers
COMMAND HIERARCHY
  • Delhi: Bahadur Shah Zafar (sovereign head) & General Bakht Khan (military commander from Bareilly).
  • Kanpur: Nana Sahib (Peshwa Baji Rao II's adopted son) and general Tatya Tope.
  • Jhansi: Rani Lakshmibai, dispossessed by Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse; fought valiantly in male attire.
  • Bihar & Local Heroes: Kunwar Singh (Arrah zamindar); Shah Mal (mobilized 84 Jat villages in Baghpat); Gonoo (Kol tribal leader in Singhbhum).
3. Rumours, Prophecies & Unity
POPULAR MENTALITY
  • The Enfield Cartridges: Rumour that bullet grease contained cow fat (sacred to Hindus) and pig fat (forbidden to Muslims).
  • Bazaar Flour Contamination: Widespread belief that British mixed bone dust of cows and pigs into flour (*atta*).
  • Centenary Prophecy: Prophesied that East India Company rule would end 100 years after the Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757).
  • The Azamgarh Proclamation (1857): Appealed to all castes and communities; enforced total ban on cow slaughter in rebel territories.
4. British Reconquest & Counter-Terror
1857–1858 CE
  • Recapture of Delhi: British forces under John Nicholson breached Kashmiri Gate (Sept 1857); Zafar arrested at Humayun's tomb.
  • Relief of Lucknow: Sir Colin Campbell, Outram, and Havelock broke through the Lucknow Residency siege in March 1858.
  • Savage Retribution: Martial law declared; indiscriminate burning of villages; rebels blown alive from the mouths of cannons.
  • Crown Rule (1858): Queen Victoria's Proclamation ended East India Company rule, transferring India directly to the British Crown.
5. Visual Arts & Nationalist Memory
REPRESENTATION
  • Thomas Jones Barker (1859): "Relief of Lucknow" celebrated British military commanders (Campbell, Havelock) as heroic imperial saviors.
  • Joseph Noel Paton (1859): "In Memoriam" depicted helpless British women and children awaiting death, justifying brutal retribution.
  • Satirical Cartoons: British magazine Punch published cartoons depicting imperial "Justice" slaying rebel "monsters".
  • Nationalist Transformation: Celebrated as the First War of Independence (V.D. Savarkar, 1909); Rani Lakshmibai romanticized in folklore.
