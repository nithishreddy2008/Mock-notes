FOUR CORE PILLARS OF GLOBAL ENVIRONMENTAL POLITICS

1. Evolution of Green Norms
Limits to Growth (1972) → Brundtland Report (1987) → Rio Earth Summit (1992) → Kyoto (1997) → Paris (2015).
2. Principle of CBDR
Principle 7 of Rio Declaration: Common But Differentiated Responsibilities; historic emissions equity between North & South.
3. Four Global Commons
Earth's Atmosphere, Antarctica (1959 Treaty), the Ocean Floor (UNCLOS 1982), and Outer Space (1967 Treaty).
4. Resource Geopolitics
Middle East oil concentration (64%); transboundary water wars (Euphrates-Tigris, Jordan, Nile, Indus rivers).

The Rio Earth Summit & Agenda 21 (1992)

Historic Conclave: UN Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED) met in Rio de Janeiro with 170 states.
Rio Declaration: Established 27 fundamental principles of sustainable development and international environmental law.
Agenda 21: Exhaustive 300-page action plan for sustainable economic growth; criticized as non-binding and underfunded.
Three Landmark Treaties: Opened for signature the UNFCCC (Climate Change), the CBD (Biodiversity), and Forest Principles.
Birth of CBDR: Codified the moral rule that developed nations must bear the financial and technological burden of cleanup.

India's Climate Strategy & National Action Plan

CBDR Champion: Demands carbon budget equity; points out India's per-capita emissions are less than half the global average.
Kyoto Ratification: Signed and ratified the Kyoto Protocol in August 2002; was exempt from binding emissions cuts as a non-Annex I nation.
NAPCC (2008): National Action Plan on Climate Change launched with 8 National Missions (National Solar Mission leading).
International Solar Alliance (2015): Jointly launched by India & France at COP21 Paris; global headquarters in Gurugram, India.
Panchamrit Targets: Pledged at COP26 Glasgow: 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030, and Net-Zero carbon emissions by 2070.

CHRONOLOGY: FROM STOCKHOLM TO PARIS & GLASGOW CLIMATE PACTS

1972

Limits to Growth: Club of Rome publishes warning; UN Stockholm conference leads to creation of UNEP in Nairobi.

1987

Brundtland & Montreal: 'Our Common Future' defines sustainable development; Montreal Protocol bans ozone-destroying CFCs.

June 1992

Rio Earth Summit: 170 nations adopt Rio Declaration, Agenda 21, UNFCCC, and the historic principle of CBDR.

1997

Kyoto Protocol: Legally binding greenhouse gas reduction targets set for Annex-I (industrialized) countries.

August 2002

India Ratifies Kyoto: India formally ratifies the Kyoto Protocol, maintaining the exemption for developing nations.

2015

Paris Agreement: COP21 adopts universal treaty targeting warming well below 2°C; India co-founds ISA.

PITFALL 1: Montreal vs Kyoto Target Gases

The Montreal Protocol (1987) bans Ozone Depleting Substances (CFCs). The Kyoto Protocol (1997) targets Greenhouse Gases (CO2, Methane, Nitrous Oxide, HFCs, PFCs, SF6).

PITFALL 2: Kyoto Binding Obligations

Under the Kyoto Protocol, developing countries including India and China were EXEMPT from binding greenhouse gas reduction quotas, because their historic per-capita emissions were negligible.

PITFALL 3: India's Stand on Indigenous Peoples

The Indian government maintains that the international concept of "indigenous peoples" applies to regions conquered by European settlers (Americas, Australia). In India, all Indians are indigenous; tribal groups are designated as Scheduled Tribes.
