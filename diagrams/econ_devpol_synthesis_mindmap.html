Indian Economic Development (1947–Present): Master Synthesis Mindmap

Colonial Legacies, 1950–1990 Five Year Plans, 1991 LPG Structural Transformation & Modern Economic Policy

COMPLETE SYNTHESIS · CLASS 12
1. Colonial Inheritances & Planning Core
1947 - 1950 Baseline
A. The Colonial Stagnation Matrix
Feudal Zamindari in Bengal; de-industrialisation of handicrafts; 1921 Year of Great Divide; drain of wealth via British Home Charges; < 0.5% per capita growth.
B. Planning Commission (1950) & Mahalanobis
Architect of Second Plan (1956-61) prioritised heavy capital goods. Succeeded by NITI Aayog on 1 Jan 2015.
C. The Four Common Goals of Planning
Growth (GDP capacity), Modernisation (technology & social outlook), Self-Reliance (avoiding import dependence), and Equity (social justice).
V.K.R.V. Rao's colonial national income estimates were statistically most rigorous.
2. Sectoral Trajectories (1950–1990)
State-Led Inward Era
A. Land Reforms & Green Revolution
Abolition of intermediaries brought 200 lakh tenants direct contact; Land Ceilings faced benami evasions; Green Revolution (HYV miracle seeds, fertilizers) generated national Marketed Surplus.
B. Industrial Policy Resolution (IPR 1956) & SSI
Schedule A (17 State-only), Schedule B (12 mixed), Schedule C (Private under strict licensing). Karve Committee (1955) protected labour-intensive Small Scale Industries (cap ₹5 lakh).
C. Inward-Looking Strategy: Import Substitution
Tariffs and Quotas protected infant domestic industries, creating domestic monopolies with low quality and zero global competitiveness.
License-Permit Raj stifled competition until 1991 BoP crisis forced structural changes.
3. The 1991 Reforms: The Three LPG Pillars
Structural Adjustment
A. Crisis & IMF-World Bank Bailout
Fiscal deficit 8.4% of GDP, double-digit inflation (>12%), forex < USD 1 billion (2 weeks imports), 47 tonnes gold pledged; USD 7 billion structural adjustment loan.
B. Liberalisation & Privatisation
Industrial licensing abolished (except 5 hazard sectors); RBI converted from regulator to facilitator; private banks opened; PSU disinvestment & Maharatna autonomy.
C. Globalisation & WTO Framework
Rupee devalued & 1993 LERMS float; WTO founding member (1 Jan 1995, successor to GATT 1948); India transformed into world IT/BPO outsourcing powerhouse.
CUET Rule: Stabilisation = Short-term liquidity; Structural = Long-term efficiency & competitiveness.
4. Appraisal Scorecard & Modern Era
1991 to Present Day
A. Successes vs Structural Deficits
Successes: GDP boom led by services, forex > USD 600 bn, consumer choice. Deficits: Neglect of agriculture (low public capital), "Jobless Growth" in formal factory jobs, disinvestment underpricing.
B. Demonetisation (8 November 2016)
Scrapped ₹500 & ₹1000 notes (86% of currency in circulation) to eliminate counterfeit currency, unearth black money, and accelerate formal digital payments.
C. Goods & Services Tax (GST - 1 July 2017)
101st Constitutional Amendment Act; replaced cascade of multiple indirect taxes with "One Nation, One Tax, One Market"; dual GST (CGST, SGST, IGST).
Post-1991 growth was overwhelmingly services-led rather than manufacturing-led!
Master Takeaway: From a vulnerable colonial agrarian colony to a state-controlled industrial planner, and finally to a globally integrated, digital service powerhouse.
