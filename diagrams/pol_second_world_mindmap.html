DISINTEGRATION OF THE SECOND WORLD
CONTEMPORARY WORLD POLITICS · CH. 1 (TOPIC 10)

1. The Soviet System & Its Flaws

1917 Bolshevik Revolution: Led by Vladimir Lenin; established socialist state opposed to capitalism.
State Supremacy: Planned economy, pin-to-car domestic manufacturing, guaranteed basic necessities (health, education).
Stagnation: Over-centralized bureaucracy, single-party CPSU monopoly, lack of free speech, arms race burden.
Afghan Quagmire (1979–89): Soviet invasion drained military resources, deeply discrediting the USSR.

2. Gorbachev's Dual Reforms & Fall

General Secretary (1985): Mikhail Gorbachev sought modernization to bridge technological lag with the West.
Perestroika & Glasnost: Economic restructuring + political openness unleashed uncontrollable nationalist aspirations.
Berlin Wall Falls (9 Nov 1989): Symbol of the Cold War dismantled by citizens; Germany reunified in Oct 1990.
August 1991 Coup: Hardline CPSU coup failed; Boris Yeltsin emerged as the popular hero of the Russian Federation.

3. The Final Collapse (Dec 1991)

Belavezha Accords: Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus under Yeltsin formally declared the USSR dissolved.
CIS Formed: Commonwealth of Independent States created; 15 sovereign republics emerged.
Russian Succession: Russia inherited the USSR's permanent UN Security Council seat and nuclear arsenal.
End of Cold War: Bipolar world order ended; Warsaw Pact abolished; era of US unipolar hegemony began.

4. Shock Therapy & Economic Ruin

IMF & World Bank Model: Sudden, abrupt transition from planned socialism to free-market capitalism.
Largest Garage Sale: 90% of state industries sold at throwaway prices; privatization vouchers bought up by mafias.
Financial Devastation: Russian Ruble collapsed; rampant inflation wiped out citizens' life savings; social safety net vanished.
Mafia & Oligarchs: Wealth concentrated in a tiny clique of oligarchs; mafia dominated commercial life.

5. Post-Communist Flashpoints & Wars

Chechnya & Dagestan: Brutal secessionist rebellions in Russia; Russian military bombarded Grozny.
Tajikistan Civil War: Sectarian/clan civil war raged for 10 years (1991–2001); Russian troops intervened.
Balkan Breakup (Yugoslavia): Violent ethnic cleansing in Bosnia, Croatia, and Kosovo; NATO bombing in 1999.
Velvet Divorce: Peaceful, democratic separation of Czechoslovakia into Czech Republic and Slovakia (1993).

6. India-Russia Enduring Partnership

Historical Grounding: 1971 Indo-Soviet Treaty; Soviet diplomatic vetoes on Kashmir; public sector industrial aid.
Strategic Partnership (2000): Annual bilateral summits instituted by Atal Bihari Vajpayee and Vladimir Putin.
Key Strategic Pillars: Joint defense production (BrahMos, Su-30MKI), Kudankulam nuclear reactors, space cooperation.
Multipolar World Order: Common foreign policy objective of a multipolar international system, co-existing with BRICS & SCO.
