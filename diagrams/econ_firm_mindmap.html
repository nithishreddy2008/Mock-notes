FIRM UNDER PERFECT COMPETITION
COURSE I MICRO · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Perfect Competition — Features

Many buyers and sellers — each too small to move the market price
Homogeneous product — identical units, so no branding, no premium
Free entry and exit — profits attract firms, losses send them out (long run)
Perfect knowledge + perfect mobility of factors; firm = price taker, industry = price maker

Profit Maximisation (3 conditions)

1. MC = MR — the last unit pays for itself exactly
2. MC RISING at that point — kills the false crossing on the falling arm
3. P ≥ AVC (short run) — below AVC's minimum, shut down, lose only TFC

Supply: Schedule, Curve, Market

Supply = willingness + ability to OFFER at each price, per period
Law of supply: own price ↑ → quantity supplied ↑ (direct relation, upward-sloping S)
Determinants: input prices, technology, taxes & subsidies, future price expectations, number of firms, natural shocks
Market supply = horizontal sum of firms' quantities at each price

Movement vs Shift (supply side)

Own price changes → movement ALONG the S curve (extension ↑P / contraction ↓P)
Non-price factors (costs, tech, taxes) → whole curve SHIFTS right (increase) or left (decrease)
Words carry marks: "change in quantity supplied" vs "change in supply"

Price Elasticity of Supply

PES = %ΔQ supplied ÷ %ΔP (absolute value; positive by nature)
Degrees: 0 (perfectly inelastic — land supply) < 1 > = 1 > 1 → ∞ (a single firm's demand-side view)
More elastic when: longer time, larger stocks, spare capacity, easy storage; less elastic for perishables and fixed land

Price Determination & Shift Effects

Equilibrium: D = S; above P* excess supply pushes price down; below P* excess demand pushes it up
D right → P↑ Q↑ · D left → P↓ Q↓
S right → P↓ Q↑ · S left → P↑ Q↓ (both supply cases flip the price)

Traps that catch most students

Own price never shifts the S curve — input costs, technology and taxes do; own price only moves you along it
Rising MC, not just MC = MR — the falling-arm crossing is a false equilibrium
Supply has a DIRECT price relation — upward-sloping S, opposite to demand's downward slope; MCQs love flipping this
PES uses absolute value too — and time is the biggest elasticity lever (momentary vs short vs long)
