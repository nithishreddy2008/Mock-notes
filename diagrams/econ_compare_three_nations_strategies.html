Comparative Development Strategies: India, China & Pakistan

Independence & Regimes, Command vs Mixed Models, Reform Chronology (1978 vs 1988 vs 1991) & Policy Trajectories

CLASS 12 · INDIAN ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
1. India (Democratic Mixed Model)
Reforms: 1991
Foundations & First Plan (1951)
• Independence in 1947. Mixed economy balancing public sector planning with private enterprise.
• First 5-Year Plan launched in 1951; Second Plan (Mahalanobis) prioritized heavy industry.
1950–1990 Inward Strategy
Import Substitution: High tariffs & quotas protecting infant industries.
License-Permit Raj: Severe bureaucratic stifling of private growth.
Green Revolution: Achieved food self-sufficiency in mid-1960s.
1991 Reforms (LPG Shift)
Deep BoP crisis forced historic transition to Liberalisation, Privatisation, and Globalisation. Service-sector powerhouse (IT/BPO).
Political Framework: Robust multi-party democracy with constitutional federalism and judicial oversight.
2. China (Command & Market Socialism)
Reforms: 1978
People's Republic & GLF (1958)
1949: PRC founded under Mao Zedong. 1st Plan in 1953.
Great Leap Forward (1958): Backyard steel furnaces; 26,000 rural Communes (collective farming). Famine followed.
Cultural Revolution (1966-76): Red Guards sent to countryside.
1978 Reforms (Deng Xiaoping)
Phase 1: Agriculture communes dismantled; household responsibility.
Phase 2: Dual Pricing system; Special Economic Zones (SEZs) attracting massive foreign FDI. "Factory of the World".
One-Child Policy (1979)
Arrested population growth drastically; created skewed sex ratios and an aging workforce (phased out 2016-2021).
Pre-1978 social foundation (universal literacy, health, land equity) fueled post-1978 export manufacturing boom.
3. Pakistan (Mixed & Aid-Reliant)
Reforms: 1988
Foundations & First Plan (1956)
• Independence in 1947. Mixed economy framework.
• First 5-Year Plan (Medium Term Plan) launched in 1956.
Policy Oscillations
1950s-60s: Import substitution tariffs & Green Revolution.
1970s: Nationalisation of capital goods under Bhutto.
Late 1970s-80s: De-nationalisation & private promotion under Zia.
1988 Reforms & Fragility
Adopted structural reforms in 1988. However, political instability, fiscal deficits, and excessive dependence on Gulf remittances and foreign aid hindered sustained industrial takeoff.
Reform Chronology Anchor: China initiated reforms FIRST (1978) → Pakistan followed (1988) → India launched NEP (1991).
