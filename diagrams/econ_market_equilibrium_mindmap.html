Interaction of market forces, self-correcting price adjustments, entry-exit industry equilibrium, and legal price controls.

1. Market Equilibrium & Self-Correction
D = S Clearance
  • Market Equilibrium: State of balance where planned market demand equals planned market supply: q_D(P*) = q_S(P*).
  • Equilibrium Price ($P^*$): The market clearing price where neither shortage nor surplus exists.
  • Excess Supply (Surplus): Occurs when $P > P^* \implies q_S > q_D$. Unsold stock forces competitive sellers to slash prices downward ($\downarrow$).
  • Excess Demand (Shortage): Occurs when $P < P^* \implies q_D > q_S$. Unmet consumer demand induces buyers to bid prices upward ($\uparrow$).
  • Adam Smith's "Invisible Hand": Price mechanism automatically restores equilibrium without centralized government planning.
CUET Rule: At equilibrium, quantity transacted in the market reaches its maximum sustainable volume with zero involuntary inventories!
2. Shift Dynamics & Elasticity Extremes
Shift Matrix
  • Both D & S Increase: Quantity definitely increases. Price rises if $\Delta D > \Delta S$; Price constant if $\Delta D = \Delta S$; Price falls if $\Delta S > \Delta D$.
  • Both D & S Decrease: Quantity definitely decreases. Price rises if $\Delta S > \Delta D$; Price constant if $\Delta S = \Delta D$; Price falls if $\Delta D > \Delta S$.
  • D Increases & S Decreases: Price definitely rises. Quantity change is indeterminate (depends on relative shift sizes).
  • D Decreases & S Increases: Price definitely falls. Quantity change is indeterminate.
  • Special Elasticity Cases:
    • Perfect Elastic Supply ($e_s = \infty$): Demand shift changes ONLY quantity; price stays unchanged!
    • Perfect Inelastic Supply ($e_s = 0$): Demand shift changes ONLY price; quantity stays unchanged!
Key Theorem: If both curves shift in the SAME direction, quantity is certain. If they shift in OPPOSITE directions, price is certain!
3. Equilibrium with Free Entry & Exit
P = min LAC
  • Free Entry/Exit Guarantee: Market price is anchored at the minimum point of Long-Run Average Cost: P* = min LAC.
  • Strict Zero Economic Profit: Every firm earns only normal profit ($TR = TC$). Supernormal profits attract entry; losses cause exit.
  • Equilibrium Output of Each Firm: $q^*$ is strictly fixed at the minimum point of $LAC$ ($LMC = \min LAC$).
  • Equilibrium Number of Firms ($n^*$): n* = Q* / q*. Total market quantity divided by each firm's optimal scale.
  • Demand Shift Impact: An increase in market demand *does not alter price* in the long run ($P^* = \min LAC$)! Instead, it expands market output by attracting new firms: $n^*$ increases!
CUET Trap: When free entry exists, a shift in market demand changes the number of firms, not the long-run market price!
4. Simple Applications: Ceilings & Floors
Government Controls
  • Price Ceiling ($P_c < P^*$): Legal maximum price for essential goods (wheat, sugar, medicines).
    • Result: Shortage ($q_D > q_S$).
    • Distortions: Rationing (ration cards), long queues, black marketing at $P_B > P^*$, quality degradation.
  • Price Floor / MSP ($P_f > P^*$): Legal minimum price for agricultural crops (MSP) or labour (Minimum Wage).
    • Result: Surplus ($q_S > q_D$).
    • Government Role: Must buy entire surplus ($q_S - q_D$) to store as buffer stock; in labour market, causes unemployment!
  • Non-Binding Interventions: A ceiling above $P^*$ or floor below $P^*$ has zero market effect! Market clears normally at $P^*$.
Golden Rule: A Ceiling is binding ONLY below $P^*$; a Floor is binding ONLY above $P^*$!
