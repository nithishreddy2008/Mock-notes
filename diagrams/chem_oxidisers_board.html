TWO WORKHORSE OXIDISERS — K₂Cr₂O₇ vs KMnO₄
The two transition-metal compounds NCERT singles out in Unit IV — side by side, reaction by reaction

POTASSIUM DICHROMATE — K₂Cr₂O₇

PreparationFrom chromite ore: roast with Na₂CO₃ → Na₂CrO₄; acidify → dichromate; exchange with KCl. Orange crystals.
StructureTwo CrO₄ tetrahedra sharing one corner — Cr is +6, tetrahedral, no unpaired electrons (diamagnetic).
pH switchChromate (yellow) and dichromate (orange) interconvert with pH:
2 CrO₄²⁻ + 2 H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O
Oxidising half-rxnIn acid, Cr(+6) → Cr(+3), green:
Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14 H⁺ + 6 e⁻ → 2 Cr³⁺ + 7 H₂O   (E° = +1.33 V)
Classic usesOxidises I⁻ → I₂, Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺, Sn²⁺ → Sn⁴⁺; used in volumetric titrations (primary standard — its solution keeps its strength).
ExtraAcidified dichromate + H₂O₂ in ether gives the deep-blue peroxo test for chromium.

POTASSIUM PERMANGANATE — KMnO₄

PreparationFrom MnO₂: fuse with KOH + oxidiser (KNO₃/air) → dark-green K₂MnO₄; then electrolytic oxidation (or Cl₂/ozonised air) → purple KMnO₄.
StructureMn(+7), tetrahedral MnO₄⁻ ion — d₀ configuration, diamagnetic, intense purple from charge transfer (not d–d).
pH switchThe medium decides both the product and the electron count:
Acid: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O (colourless)
Mild/faint alkaline: + 3e⁻ → MnO₂ (brown solid)
Strong alkaline: + 1e⁻ → MnO₄²⁻ (green manganate)
Classic usesOxidises I⁻, Fe²⁺, oxalate (hot, acid, autocatalytic with Mn²⁺), S²⁻, SO₃²⁻, NO₂⁻; bleaching, disinfectant, Baeyer's reagent (alkaline cold = alkene test).
ExtraNot a primary standard — it slowly decomposes in sunlight; solutions are re-standardised.
Highest oxidation state wins: both oxidisers work by falling from a top oxidation state (+6 for Cr, +7 for Mn) to a lower, more stable one.
Colour logic: orange/red-brown/purple all come from charge-transfer or d–d transitions of the d-block ions themselves — colour is a transition-metal signature.
Acid medium gives the biggest punch: 5 electrons per MnO₄⁻ in acid vs 3 or 1 in weaker media — the medium changes the stoichiometry, a favourite MCQ twist.
