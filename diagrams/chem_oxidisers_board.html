Workhorse Oxidisers: K₂Cr₂O₇ vs KMnO₄

Comparative structural synthesis, pH-dependent redox stoichiometry, and key analytical titrations

Potassium Dichromate Potassium Permanganate

Potassium Dichromate

K₂Cr₂O₇ · Cr(+6) · d⁰
1. Industrial Preparation (Chromite Ore)
Step 1: 4 FeCr₂O₄ + 8 Na₂CO₃ + 7 O₂ → 8 Na₂CrO₄ (yellow) + 2 Fe₂O₃ + 8 CO₂
Step 2: 2 Na₂CrO₄ + 2 H⁺ → Na₂Cr₂O₇ (orange) + 2 Na⁺ + H₂O
Step 3: Na₂Cr₂O₇ + 2 KCl → K₂Cr₂O₇ ↓ (less soluble orange crystals) + 2 NaCl
2. Geometry & Structure
Two tetrahedral CrO₄ units sharing one corner oxygen atom.
Cr–O–Cr bond angle = 126°. Bridging Cr–O length = 179 pm; six terminal Cr–O lengths = 163 pm (resonance equivalent). Diamagnetic (Cr(+6) has 3d⁰ configuration).
3. pH-Dependent Equilibrium (Dynamic Switch)
Chromate (yellow, tetrahedral) and dichromate (orange) interconvert reversibly:
2 CrO₄²⁻ (yellow) + 2 H⁺ ↔ Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange) + H₂O   [pH < 7: Orange; pH > 7: Yellow]
4. Oxidising Half-Reaction & Key Titrations
Acts as an oxidiser exclusively in acidic medium (H₂SO₄):
Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14 H⁺ + 6 e⁻ → 2 Cr³⁺ (green) + 7 H₂O   (E° = +1.33 V, n-factor = 6)
• I⁻ → I₂: 6 I⁻ + Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14 H⁺ → 3 I₂ + 2 Cr³⁺ + 7 H₂O
• Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺: 6 Fe²⁺ + Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14 H⁺ → 6 Fe³⁺ + 2 Cr³⁺ + 7 H₂O
• Primary Standard: Readily available in pure form, non-hygroscopic; standard solutions retain exact concentration.

Potassium Permanganate

KMnO₄ · Mn(+7) · d⁰
1. Industrial Preparation (Pyrolusite Ore)
Step 1: 2 MnO₂ + 4 KOH + O₂ → 2 K₂MnO₄ (dark green potassium manganate) + 2 H₂O
Step 2: Electrolytic oxidation in alkaline solution:
MnO₄²⁻ (green) → MnO₄⁻ (purple permanganate) + e⁻ (at anode)
Lab alternative: 3 MnO₄²⁻ + 4 H⁺ → 2 MnO₄⁻ + MnO₂ + 2 H₂O (disproportionation).
2. Geometry & Intense Purple Color Origin
Tetrahedral MnO₄⁻ ion with green MnO₄²⁻ manganate counterpart.
Paramagnetic MnO₄²⁻ (1 unpaired e⁻) vs Diamagnetic MnO₄⁻ (d⁰).
Intense purple colour is NOT due to d–d transition (d is empty!); it arises from Ligand-to-Metal Charge Transfer (LMCT): O(2p) → Mn(3d).
3. Medium-Dependent Redox Stoichiometry (Critical CUET Rule)
Medium Half-Reaction & Products ΔO.S. n-factor
Acidic (dil. H₂SO₄) MnO₄⁻ + 8 H⁺ + 5 e⁻ → Mn²⁺ (colourless) + 4 H₂O +7 → +2 5 (Eq = M/5)
Neutral / Faint Alkaline MnO₄⁻ + 2 H₂O + 3 e⁻ → MnO₂ (brown ppt) + 4 OH⁻ +7 → +4 3 (Eq = M/3)
Strongly Alkaline MnO₄⁻ + e⁻ → MnO₄²⁻ (green manganate) +7 → +6 1 (Eq = M/1)
4. Key Reactions & Practical Notes
• Acidic: C₂O₄²⁻ (oxalate) → CO₂ (hot ~60°C, autocatalysed by Mn²⁺); Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺; NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻.
• Faint Alkaline (Baeyer's Test): Oxidises I⁻ to IO₃⁻ (iodate!), thiosulfate S₂O₃²⁻ to SO₄²⁻.
• Self-Indicator: KMnO₄ serves as its own indicator (first permanent faint pink droplet). Not a primary standard (decomposes in light; standardized with oxalic acid).
HCl is Prohibited in Titrations

KMnO₄ oxidises Cl⁻ in HCl to toxic Cl₂ gas. HNO₃ cannot be used because it is an oxidising acid itself. Only dilute H₂SO₄ is suitable.

The Iodide Distinction Trap

In acidic medium, both oxidisers convert I⁻ → I₂. But in neutral/faint alkaline medium, KMnO₄ oxidises I⁻ → IO₃⁻ (iodate). Classic CUET trap!

Primary vs Secondary Standard

K₂Cr₂O₇ is a primary standard (stable, non-hygroscopic). KMnO₄ is a secondary standard because traces of MnO₂ catalyse its photolytic decomposition.
