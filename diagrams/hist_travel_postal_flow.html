The Delhi Sultanate Communication & Postal Grid: Process Architecture
Operational process flow comparing the Horse Post (Uluq) and Foot Post (Dawa) observed by Ibn Battuta in the Rihla
OurMocks Study Visuals CUET UG History · Postal Grid
System A · Horse Post (Uluq)
Mounted Relay · Every 4 Miles

Known as the Uluq, this mounted system operated between major administrative centers using royal horses stationed at regular intervals.

Node 1 · Stationing
Staging Posts (*Kurohs*)
Royal horses maintained at state expense stationed every 4 miles (one kuroh / kos) along trunk roads.
Node 2 · Dispatch
Mounted Couriers
Riders carried royal letters, decrees, and urgent fiscal warrants at high speed between regional capitals.
Node 3 · Handover
Relay Switch
Fresh rider and rested steed instantly took over packets at each post, eliminating fatigue delays.
System B · Foot Post (Dawa)
Acoustic Bell Relay · Faster than Horses

Known as the Dawa, this high-speed pedestrian relay was celebrated by Ibn Battuta as strictly faster and more reliable than horse couriers.

Node 1 · Density
Three Posts per Mile
Every mile divided into three equal stations (1/3 mile apart), each staffed with runners stationed in huts outside villages.
Node 2 · Acoustic Signal
Rod with Brass Bells
Runner held a 2-cubit rod topped with ringing brass bells; ringing alerted the next station well before arrival.
Node 3 · Sprint Handoff
Instant Relay Sprint
Next runner stood ready, snatched the packet, and sprinted at top speed to the subsequent station without pause.
1. Speed Multiplier: 10x Acceleration
While ordinary merchant caravans took 50 days to travel from the Sindh border to Delhi, intelligence reports via the dawa foot relay reached Sultan Muhammad bin Tughlaq in just 5 days.
2. Imperial Logistics & Fresh Produce
The network carried high-value perishables: fresh melons from Khurasan and holy Ganga water for the Sultan’s daily consumption were rapidly transported hundreds of miles without spoiling.
3. State Hegemony & Surveillance
The postal grid functioned as an all-seeing intelligence web: provincial rebellions, revenue defaults, and market price spikes were transmitted instantaneously to maintain centralized authoritarian control.
Key Distinction: Horse post = Uluq (every 4 miles) · Foot post = Dawa (3 stations per mile, bell acoustic warning, faster than horse relay).
