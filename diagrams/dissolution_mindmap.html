Dissolution vs Reconstitution

Firm dissolves: the business closes and the books are shut completely
Partnership dissolves: only the mix changes — the business carries on
Reconstitution (admission, retirement, death) = new ratio, same live books
Every firm-dissolution ends the partnership — the reverse is not true

Modes of Dissolution

By agreement: all partners consent (Sec 40) — the cleanest route
Compulsory: all (or all but one) insolvent, or the business turns unlawful (Sec 41)
Contingency: fixed term ends, venture completes, death or insolvency of a partner (Sec 42)
By notice (Sec 43) for a partnership at will; by court (Sec 44) for misconduct, losses, breach

Order of Settlement — Sec 48

Losses applied: from profits → from capitals → by partners in the old PSR
Assets applied: outside liabilities → partners’ loans → capitals → surplus in PSR
A partner’s loan ranks above capital but below outsiders’ claims
Final proof of correct work: the Cash/Bank A/c closes at nil

Realisation Account

Dr: all assets at book value; cash paid for liabilities, expenses
Cr: outside liabilities transferred; sale proceeds; unrecorded assets realised
Stays out: Cash/Bank balance itself — it is already money, nothing to “realise”
Result (profit or loss) → partners’ capitals in the OLD profit-sharing ratio

Partners’ Capital & Loan A/cs

Capitals absorb the realisation result in the old PSR, then get paid off
A partner’s loan is not capital — repaid in full, interest is a charge
Only a real loan gets a Loan A/c column — losses are never pushed into it
Closing line of every capital A/c: To Cash/Bank — final payment

Cash/Bank Account

Opens with the old cash balance; collects every rupee of realisation
Pays out in the Sec 48 order: outsiders → loans → capitals
The final proof — receipts and payments must match exactly
Mismatch means an entry is missing or on the wrong side — recheck

COMMON ERRORS & TRAPS

Realisation ≠ Revaluation. Realisation uses SALE values on shutdown; revaluation values a going business
Partner’s loan is not shared for losses — it is repaid in full before capitals
Accumulated reserves go to partners’ capitals (or Realisation, per method) — never forgotten
Expenses borne by a partner but paid by the firm — debit the partner’s capital, not Realisation
