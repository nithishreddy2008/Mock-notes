CARBONYL NUCLEOPHILIC ADDITIONS & HALLMARK NAME REACTIONS

Mechanisms, Geometry Inversion, Nitrogen Derivatives, Aldol/Cannizzaro Divergence, and Deoxygenation

1. Nucleophilic Addition Kinetics & Geometry
Addition Pathways
Geometry Inversion: sp² Planar → sp³ Tetrahedral
Nucleophile attacks electrophilic carbonyl carbon at ~107° (Bürgi-Dunitz angle). Planar carbonyl (120°) converts to tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate (109.5°), subsequently protonated.
Reactivity Order: Steric + Electronic Factors
HCHO > RCHO > R₂CO > ArCHO. Aldehydes are faster than ketones because alkyl groups: (1) donate electron density (+I) reducing Cδ+, and (2) crowd the transition state.
Key Reagents & Synthetic Applications
• HCN: Forms cyanohydrins (base catalyst OH⁻ generates strong Nu :CN⁻).
• NaHSO₃: Forms bisulphite crystalline adduct (used for purification/separation).
• ROH / dry HCl: Aldehyde + 1 ROH → Hemiacetal; + 2 ROH → Stable Acetal. Ketones yield cyclic ketals with ethylene glycol.
2. Ammonia Derivatives: C=O + H₂N-G → C=N-G
pH 3.5 - 4.5 Regulated
2,4-DNP (Brady's Reagent) — The Universal Test
Reacts with both aldehydes and ketones to form yellow/orange/red crystalline 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazones. Used to detect C=O group. >C=O + H₂N-NH-C₆H₃(NO₂)₂ → >C=N-NH-C₆H₃(NO₂)₂ + H₂O
Hydroxylamine & Hydrazine
• Hydroxylamine (NH₂OH): Forms crystalline Oximes (>C=N-OH).
• Hydrazine (NH₂NH₂): Yields Hydrazones (>C=N-NH₂); with excess yields azines.
Semicarbazide Monoreactivity Trap (CUET High-Yield)
Semicarbazide is H₂N-CO-NH-NH₂. Only the terminal -NH-NH₂ group acts as nucleophile! The amide NH₂ is delocalized into C=O by resonance and lacks nucleophilicity.
3. Aldol Condensation vs Cannizzaro Redox
α-H Regulated Fate
Aldol Condensation (Requires ≥ 1 α-Hydrogen)
Dilute NaOH abstracts acidic α-H to form resonance-stabilized enolate ion, which attacks another C=O molecule yielding β-hydroxyaldehyde (Aldol). Heating eliminates H₂O to give α,β-unsaturated carbonyl. 2 CH₃CHO + dil. NaOH → CH₃-CH(OH)-CH₂CHO → CH₃-CH=CH-CHO (Crotonaldehyde)
Cannizzaro Reaction (Absence of α-Hydrogen)
50% conc. NaOH triggers self-oxidation-reduction (disproportionation). Hydride ion (:H⁻) shift is the Rate-Determining Step. 2 HCHO + 50% NaOH → CH₃OH (Methanol) + HCOONa (Sodium Formate)
Cross-Cannizzaro Rule: HCHO + ArCHO
Formaldehyde is more reactive towards OH⁻ attack; therefore, HCHO is always oxidized to HCOO⁻ and ArCHO is reduced to ArCH₂OH!
4. Deoxygenation Reductions & HVZ Halogenation
C=O → CH₂ & α-Halogenation
Clemmensen Reduction (Acid-Stable Carbonyls)
Reagent: Amalgamated Zinc + concentrated HCl (Zn-Hg / conc. HCl). Reduces aldehydes/ketones to alkanes. Avoid if molecule contains acid-sensitive groups (-OH, -OR, C=C). CH₃-CO-CH₃ + 4[H] + Zn-Hg/HCl → CH₃-CH₂-CH₃ (Propane) + H₂O
Wolff-Kishner Reduction (Base-Stable Carbonyls)
Reagent: Hydrazine + KOH in ethylene glycol, heated (NH₂NH₂ / KOH, Δ). Reduces C=O to CH₂ with evolution of N₂ gas. Avoid if substrate has base-sensitive groups (esters, halides).
Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction (Carboxylic Acids)
Carboxylic acids having α-hydrogen react with Cl₂ or Br₂ in presence of red Phosphorus to yield α-halocarboxylic acids. Formic acid and benzoic acid FAIL (no α-H). R-CH₂-COOH + X₂ / red P → R-CH(X)-COOH (α-halocarboxylic acid)
CUET STRATEGY Aldol needs α-H; Cannizzaro forbids α-H. Clemmensen uses acidic Zn-Hg; Wolff-Kishner uses basic hydrazine. HVZ targets α-H of acids.
