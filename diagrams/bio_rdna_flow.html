How a Gene Is Cloned — the rDNA Journey
Seven stops from a cell full of DNA to a scaled-up product in a bioreactor
1

Isolate + cut

DNA is pulled out of the cell; a restriction enzyme cuts out the gene of interest.

2

Separate on a gel

Fragments run through agarose gel; the wanted piece is cut out and eluted (extracted).

3

Cut the vector

The plasmid vector is opened with the SAME enzyme — matching sticky ends result.

4

Ligate

DNA ligase seals the gene into the vector — recombinant DNA (rDNA) is born.

5

Transfer into host

rDNA enters a competent host cell — CaCl₂ treated, or shot in by a gene gun.

6

Select the transformed

Antibiotic plates or insertional inactivation pick out only cells carrying rDNA.

7

Scale up + purify

Cells multiply in a bioreactor; downstream processing purifies the product.

Side note — PCR

PCR multiplies the gene in the tube — no cell needed. Three temperature steps double the copy number every cycle, so a few molecules become billions.
