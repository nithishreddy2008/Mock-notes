FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT
THREE DECISIONS · EVERY ONE AFFECTS WEALTH

1. Investment Decision

WHERE TO PUT THE MONEY
Long-term (capital budgeting): which machines, plants and projects to buy — money locked in for years
Short-term: how much to keep in current assets — cash, inventory, receivables
Factor — cash flows: the project must generate usable inflows, not just paper profits
Factor — rate of return: compare the expected return with the cost of funds
Factor — risk: higher return usually hides higher risk — weigh both together
The rule: invest where return beats cost at acceptable risk

2. Financing Decision

WHERE THE MONEY COMES FROM
The mix: how much from shareholders' equity and how much from borrowed debt
Factor — cost: the cheapest source wins — debt usually costs less than equity
Factor — risk: debt brings fixed interest and repayment duty — more risk
Factor — control: more equity = more owners sharing control; debt dilutes nothing
Factor — floatation cost: issuing shares or debentures costs money — count it
Factor — state of capital markets: a bullish market welcomes equity issues

3. Dividend Decision

DISTRIBUTE OR RETAIN
The choice: pay profit to shareholders as dividends — or retain it inside the firm for growth
Factor — earnings: stable, healthy earnings support steady dividends
Factor — growth prospects: firms with big expansion plans prefer to retain
Factor — cash flow position: dividends need cash, not just profits on paper
Factor — shareholder preference: income-seekers want dividends; growth-seekers accept retention
Factor — taxation: tax rates on dividends vs retention tilt the choice
Exam anchor: every question hands you a situation — "a firm with stable earnings and no expansion plans declares a high dividend" — and asks which decision and which factor it shows. Memorise the three headings and their factor lists as one package.
