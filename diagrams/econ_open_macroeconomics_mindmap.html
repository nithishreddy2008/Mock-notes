Open Economy Macroeconomics: Master Synthesis Mindmap

Macroeconomic Linkages, BoP Identity, Exchange Rate Economics & Open Economy Multiplier

COMPLETE SYNTHESIS · CLASS 12
1. The Three Open-Economy Linkages
Channels of Exposure
A. Output Market Linkage (Goods & Services)
Consumers and firms choose between domestic and foreign goods via Exports ($X$) and Imports ($M$). Introduces trade balance to aggregate demand.
B. Financial Market Linkage (Assets & Capital)
Investors choose between domestic and foreign financial assets (shares, bonds) through capital mobility, creating capital inflows and outflows.
C. Factor Market Linkage (Labour & Capital Services)
Cross-border migration of labour and multinational corporate facilities, leading to wage transfers and factor income.
Linkages expand choice set: Openness ratio = (Exports + Imports) / GDP.
2. Balance of Payments Identity & Deficit
Accounting vs Economic
A. Current Account + Capital Account + E&O = 0
Double-entry accounting guarantees that total credits equal total debits across the entire balance sheet.
B. Autonomous vs Accommodating Transactions
Autonomous = independent commercial transactions (above the line). Accommodating = central bank official reserve transactions (below the line) to finance the gap.
C. BoP Deficit / Surplus Formula
BoP Deficit = Autonomous Payments > Autonomous Receipts = Official Reserve Decline (Credit in ORT).
CUET Rule: BoT = Merchandise X - M | CAB = BoT + Net Invisibles | Overall BoP = Autonomous Balance.
3. Real Exchange Rate (RER) & Parities
Relative Competitiveness
A. Nominal Exchange Rate ($e$)
Price of foreign currency in units of domestic currency (e.g. ₹80 per 1 USD). Quotation in bilateral units.
B. Real Exchange Rate ($RER$) Formula
Ratio of foreign prices to domestic prices: $RER = (e \cdot P_f) / P$. If $RER > 1$, foreign goods are more expensive, boosting domestic export competitiveness.
C. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) Theory
In the long run, exchange rates adjust to equalize purchasing power across countries: $e = P / P_f$, so that $RER = 1$ (Law of One Price).
Real Depreciation: When RER increases (higher e or higher Pf relative to P) → Domestic goods cheaper.
4. Open Economy Multiplier & Net Exports
Income Determination
A. Aggregate Demand in Open Economy
$AD = C + I + G + (X - M) = \bar{A} + c(1-t)Y - m Y$, where $m$ is the Marginal Propensity to Import ($MPI$).
B. Open Economy Multiplier Formula
$$k_{\text{open}} = \frac{1}{1 - c + m} = \frac{1}{s + m}$$ Since $m > 0$, the denominator is larger, so $k_{\text{open}} < k_{\text{closed}}$.
C. Import Leakage Effect
Part of any additional income leaks abroad to purchase foreign goods, diminishing domestic rounds of re-spending.
Key Deduction: The Open Economy Multiplier is ALWAYS smaller than the Closed Economy Multiplier!
Master Takeaway: Open economies gain trade gains and investment capital, but face foreign demand shocks, currency fluctuations, and smaller domestic multipliers due to import leakages.
