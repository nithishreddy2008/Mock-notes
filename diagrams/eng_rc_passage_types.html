THE THREE PASSAGE TYPES — AND HOW TO READ EACH
CUET UG · ENGLISH · READING COMPREHENSION · PASSAGES UP TO 300 WORDS
TYPE 1

Factual

SOUNDS LIKE
A news report or textbook page: facts, numbers, dates, findings — science, history, economy, environment.
QUESTIONS LEAN ON
Locate-and-match: dates, causes, figures, "which of the following is true".
HOW TO READ
Read once for the main point of each paragraph; underline numbers as you pass them.
WATCH OUT
Options love swapped numbers and dates — always return to the exact line.
TYPE 2

Narrative

SOUNDS LIKE
A story in order: someone does something, something happens, it ends a certain way.
QUESTIONS LEAN ON
Sequence and motive: what happened first, why did the character act, what changed.
HOW TO READ
Track who, then what, then why — a tiny timeline in your head as you read.
WATCH OUT
Options often reorder events — check the order the passage actually gives.
TYPE 3

Literary

SOUNDS LIKE
A poem extract or descriptive prose: feelings, images, mood — the words suggest more than they say.
QUESTIONS LEAN ON
Tone, mood and meaning: how the writer feels, what an image suggests, the best title.
HOW TO READ
Ask after each line: "Is the writer happy, sad, curious, critical?" Feelings are clues.
WATCH OUT
Extreme answers ("always", "never") are almost never right — pick the moderate one.

The 4-step method that works on all three types

1
Read the passage once
Normal speed. Get the main idea of every paragraph — do not memorise.
2
Read the questions
Note what each question wants: a fact, a reason, a feeling or a word's meaning.
3
Return and locate
Find the exact lines that answer each question. The passage is your only evidence.
4
Eliminate, then pick
Strike out options the passage never says. Choose from what is left.

Traps that catch most readers

Answering from memory — your general knowledge is not evidence; the passage is
Half-true options — three true words, one wrong word makes the option wrong
Out-of-scope options — true in real life, but never said in the passage
