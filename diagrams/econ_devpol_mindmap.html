Eve of Independence

Agriculture stagnant: zamindari siphoned rent; no irrigation push; productivity among the world's lowest
Industry de-industrialised: British machine goods killed handicrafts; only cotton/jute mills — jute region lost to Pakistan
People: 16% literacy, life expectancy ~32, high birth and death rates
Railways: the one modern gift — built to serve British trade, not India

Planning — Goals & Plans

System: mixed economy — public sector commands the heights, private sector coexists
Four goals: growth, modernisation, self-reliance, equity
First Plan 1951-56: agriculture, Harrod-Domar model — it worked
Second Plan 1956-61: Mahalanobis — heavy industry first; the industrial decade begins

Agriculture Policy

Institutional reforms: zamindari abolition, land ceilings, tenancy reform — promises only partly kept
Green Revolution (1966): HYV seeds + irrigation + fertilisers + pesticides — wheat and rice double
Limits: Punjab-Haryana-west UP first; big farmers gained first
Subsidy debate: adoption vs misuse — both sides for the answer

Industry Policy

IPR 1956: the "Economic Constitution" — Schedule A/B/C, state at the command post, expansion of public sector
SSI role: employment, decentralisation, equality — products reserved for small units
SSI problem: staying small means losing scale economies
Licence raj: every expansion needed a licence — delays, corruption, no competition, inefficiency

Foreign Trade — Inward Looking

Strategy: import substitution — make at home what we used to buy
Tools: tariffs (tax on imports) + quotas (quantity limits)
Logic: protect infant industry, save foreign exchange, self-reliance
Cost: no competition → inefficiency, poor quality, exports stagnate, reserves tighten

1991 Crisis → LPG Reforms

Triggers: Gulf War oil spike, remittances fall, reserves ≈ two weeks of imports, inflation high
Immediate fixes: gold airlifted as collateral, IMF loan, rupee devalued
NEP: stabilisation + structural adjustment = Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation
Appraisal: growth & reserves up — but jobless growth, agriculture neglected, inequality up

Traps that catch most students

Green Revolution is 1966 — not the Second Plan; the Second Plan is the industry turn
IPR 1956 ≠ 1951 — the First Plan had no industrial policy resolution; 1956 belongs to the Second Plan era
Import substitution ≠ export promotion — pre-1991 India protected the home market; exports were the afterthought
Privatisation ≠ disinvestment only — disinvestment is the biggest TOOL; privatisation is the wider strategy
