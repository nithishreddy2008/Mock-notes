The Mughal Empire and Agrarian Society (c. 16th–17th Century)

1. Sources & Abu'l-Fazl's Ain
1598 CE
  • Akbarnama Third Book: Written by Abu'l-Fazl; documented Akbar's imperial administration and agrarian statistical accounts.
  • Five Books (Daftars): Manzil-abadi (royal household), Sipah-abadi (military/mansabdars), and Mulk-abadi (fiscal/revenue tables).
  • Non-Mughal Sources: Revenue records of Gujarat, Maharashtra (*watan* documents), and Rajasthan (17th–18th century).
  • Historiographical Bias: Viewed society strictly through elite court eyes, assuming peasant submissiveness.
★ Core Axis: Land Revenue & Zabti
TODAR MAL'S BANDOBAST
  • Zabti System: Assessment of measured land at fixed cash rates (dastur) per bigha.
  • Dahsala System (1580): 10-year rolling average of crop production and state demand (one-third).
  • Four Land Types: Polaj (annual), Parauti (fallow 1–2 yrs), Chachar (fallow 3–4 yrs), and Banjar (fallow 5+ yrs).
  • Jama vs Hasil: Jama = theoretical assessed tax; Hasil = actual realized revenue collected.
2. Peasants & Agricultural Crops
AGRARIAN BASE
  • Two Categories: Khud-kasht (resident landholders) and Pahi-kasht (non-resident contractual migrant cultivators).
  • Jins-i-Kamil: "Perfect crops" (cotton, sugarcane, oilseeds) actively promoted by state for higher cash revenue.
  • New World Influx: Introduction of American crops via global trade — tobacco (banned by Jahangir in 1617), maize, tomatoes, potatoes, chillies, papaya.
  • Irrigation Aids: Persian wheel (rahat), seasonal monsoons, and state-subsidized canal maintenance.
3. The Village Community & Gender
COMMUNITY DYNAMICS
  • Village Headman: Muqaddam or Mandal, chosen by village elders and ratified by the zamindar; handled village revenue accounts.
  • Patwari & Panchayat: Caste elder assembly governed social norms; financed by a common village fund (khisam).
  • Artisan Reciprocity: Hereditary services compensated via harvest shares or watan/miras land rights in Maharashtra.
  • Gender Division: Men plowed and tilled; women sowed, weeded, threshed, winnowed, and spun cotton thread.
4. Zamindars & Feudal Retainers
LANDED GENTRY
  • Landed Aristocracy: Held hereditary proprietary lands called milkiyat worked by hired agricultural laborers.
  • State Intermediaries: Collected land revenue for the Mughal state in exchange for a fixed fiscal commission.
  • Military Might: Maintained fortified castles (qilachas), cavalry squadrons, artillery, and infantry retinues.
  • Commercialization: Zamindari rights (*taaluqdari*) could be bought, mortgaged, and sold through registered commercial title deeds.
5. Forests, Tribes & Silver Currency
MONEY & ECOLOGY
  • Forest Dwellers (Jangal): Tribes (Bhils, Gonds, Santhals) practiced shifting cultivation (jhum), hunting, and gathering.
  • State Penetration: Mughals demanded forest products (lac, honey, wax) and elephants as state tribute (peshkash).
  • Flow of Silver: India's favorable foreign export trade brought massive influx of American silver via European merchants.
  • Silver Rupee (Rupiya): Standardized silver coinage facilitated nationwide tax monetization and vibrant rural markets.
