1. Poisson Distribution — Rare Events & Count Processes
Poisson Probability Mass Function & Equality Identity
P(X = r) = (e⁻ᵝ · λʳ) / r! quad (r = 0, 1, 2, ...)
Mean = E(X) = λ, quad Variance = Var(X) = λ ⟹ Mean = Variance
Derived as the limiting form of Binomial distribution when n → ∞ and p → 0 with constant product λ = np.
Property Mathematical Expression Practical Utility in CUET
Recurrence Relation P(r + 1) = [ λ / (r + 1) ] · P(r) Enables fast computation without evaluating factorials
Zero Count Probability P(X = 0) = e⁻ᵝ Given P(0), λ = -ln(P(0)); instantaneous parameter discovery
Additive Property X + Y ~ Poisson(λ₁ + λ₂) Sum of independent Poisson variables is always Poisson
Mode of Distribution If λ is integer: λ - 1 and λ; if fraction: ⌊λ⌋ Identifies highest probability outcome
The Defining Poisson Signature: Whenever a problem states "the variance of a discrete distribution equals its mean", it is unambiguously a Poisson distribution. For Binomial, Mean > Variance ($np > npq$).
CUET Trap Alert (At Least One Event): "Probability of at least one occurrence" is calculated as $P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - e^{-\lambda}$. Never compute $\sum_{r=1}^\infty P(r)$ manually; use the complement rule!
2. The Gaussian Normal Distribution & Standard Normal Z-Score
Normal Probability Density Function & Z-Score
f(x) = (1 / (σ√(2π))) · e^[ - (x - μ)² / (2σ²) ]
Standard Normal: Z = (X - μ) / σ ~ N(0, 1)
Symmetrical bell-shaped density curve centered at mean μ with scale determined by standard deviation σ. Total area under curve equals 1.
Symmetry / Empirical Band Range on Z-Scale Enclosed Area / Probability
Central 1-Sigma Band -1.0 ≤ Z ≤ +1.0 (μ ± σ) 68.27% of total distribution area
Central 2-Sigma Band -2.0 ≤ Z ≤ +2.0 (μ ± 2σ) 95.45% of total distribution area
Central 3-Sigma Band -3.0 ≤ Z ≤ +3.0 (μ ± 3σ) 99.73% (virtually entire population)
Symmetry Half-Plane Z ≥ 0 or Z ≤ 0 50.00% (P(Z > 0) = P(Z < 0) = 0.50)
Points of Inflection & Quartiles: Inflection points (where curvature changes from concave to convex) occur at $x = \mu \pm \sigma$. First quartile $Q_1 = \mu - 0.6745\sigma$, third quartile $Q_3 = \mu + 0.6745\sigma$. Quartile Deviation $QD \approx \frac{2}{3}\sigma$.
CUET Trap Alert (Continuous Exact Point Probability): For any continuous random variable governed by a normal distribution, the probability of taking an exact individual point value is ALWAYS zero: $P(X = c) = 0$. Probabilities exist strictly over intervals $[a, b]$ as areas under the curve.
