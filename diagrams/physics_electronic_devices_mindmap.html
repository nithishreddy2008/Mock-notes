Unit 9 Mindmap
1. Energy Bands & Classification
Band Theory
E_g > 3 eV (Insulator)  |  0 < E_g < 3 eV (Semiconductor)  |  E_g = 0 (Metal)
  • Band Formation: Interatomic Coulomb interactions split isolated atomic levels into continuous energy bands (Valence Band & Conduction Band).
  • Forbidden Gap: No allowed electron states in $E_g$. For Diamond $E_g \approx 6\text{ eV}$; Silicon $E_g = 1.1\text{ eV}$; Germanium $E_g = 0.7\text{ eV}$.
  • Temperature Effect: At $T = 0\text{ K}$, semiconductor acts as a perfect insulator (CB empty). As $T$ rises, electrons jump to CB, giving negative temp coefficient ($\alpha < 0$).
2. Intrinsic & Extrinsic Doping
Carrier Density
n_e · n_h = n_i² (Mass Action)  |  σ = e(n_e μ_e + n_h μ_h)
  • Intrinsic ($n_e = n_h = n_i$): Thermal generation creates equal free electrons and valence holes. Conduction is poor at room temperature.
  • n-Type (Pentavalent Doping: P, As, Sb): $N_D \approx n_e \gg n_h$. Donor level lies $\sim 0.05\text{ eV}$ just below CB. Electrons are majority carriers.
  • p-Type (Trivalent Doping: B, Al, In): $N_A \approx n_h \gg n_e$. Acceptor level lies $\sim 0.05\text{ eV}$ just above VB. Holes are majority carriers. Both remain electrically neutral!
3. p-n Junction & I-V Bias Dynamics
Diode Barrier
V_barrier: 0.7 V (Si) | 0.3 V (Ge)  |  Dynamic R_d = ΔV / ΔI
  • Junction Formation: Diffusion of majority carriers exposes immobile uncompensated ions ($N_D^+$ on n-side, $N_A^-$ on p-side), building internal field $E_i$ and depletion width $W$.
  • Forward Bias: Positive to p, negative to n $\implies$ opposing external field lowers barrier to $(V_0 - V)$, narrows $W$; exponential mA current beyond knee voltage.
  • Reverse Bias: Aiding external field widens barrier to $(V_0 + V)$, expands $W$; only minute minority drift saturation current $I_s \sim \mu\text{A}$ (Si: nA) flows.

4. Diode Rectifiers (AC to DC)
Power Diode
f_out(HW) = f_in, η = 40.6%  |  f_out(FW) = 2f_in, η = 81.2%
  • Half-Wave Rectifier: 1 diode conducts on positive AC half-cycles only; ripple factor $r = 1.21$; output frequency equals AC line frequency $f$.
  • Full-Wave Rectifier: Center-tapped (2 diodes) or Bridge (4 diodes); conducts alternately on both half-cycles; ripple factor $r = 0.48$; output frequency is $2f$.
  • Capacitor Filter: Shunt capacitor $C$ charges to peak voltage $V_m$ and discharges slowly through load $R_L$ ($R_L C \gg T$), drastically flattening ripple.
5. Zener Diode Voltage Regulator
Breakdown Diode
V_L = V_Z = const  |  I_S = I_Z + I_L  |  R_S = (V_in - V_Z) / I_S
  • Zener Breakdown: Extremely heavy doping creates ultra-thin depletion region ($< 1\,\mu\text{m}$); strong electric field ($> 10^6\text{ V/m}$) ruptures covalent bonds.
  • Constant Voltage Shunt: Operates strictly in reverse breakdown region. Voltage remains constant ($V_Z$) across wide swings in reverse current ($I_Z$).
  • Series Resistor $R_S$: Drops fluctuating input voltage $\Delta V_{\text{in}}$ as $\Delta I_Z R_S$, safeguarding the diode from exceeding maximum power rating $P_{Z,\max}$.
6. Optoelectronic Junction Devices
Photonic Diodes
LED: hν ≤ E_g (FB)  |  Photodiode: hν ≥ E_g (RB)  |  Solar Cell (0 Bias)
  • Photodiode: Operated in reverse bias; incident photons ($h\nu > E_g$) generate minority carrier pairs in depletion layer; fractional current change $\Delta I / I$ is high.
  • LED: Operated in forward bias; spontaneous electron-hole recombination releases energy as photons ($GaAs$, $GaAsP$); requires bandgap in optical spectrum ($E_g > 1.8\text{ eV}$).
  • Solar Cell: Operates without external bias; photovoltage generated across open terminals ($V_{oc}$) and short-circuit current ($I_{sc}$) operates in the 4th quadrant of $I-V$.
