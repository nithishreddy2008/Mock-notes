1. Galvanic & Daniell Cells Redox Spontaneity

  • Fundamental Principle: Converts chemical free energy of spontaneous redox reaction directly into electrical current (ΔG < 0).
  • Daniell Cell: Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(1 M) || Cu²⁺(1 M) | Cu(s); generates standard potential E°cell = +1.10 V.
  • Anode (LOAN): Left, Oxidation, Anode, Negative (−). Zn dissolves into Zn²⁺.
  • Cathode (RED CAT): Right, Reduction, Cathode, Positive (+). Cu²⁺ deposits as Cu.
  • Salt Bridge: Inverted U-tube with inert KCl in agar-agar. Maintains electrical neutrality and eliminates liquid-junction potential.

2. Electrode Potential & EMF Standard Scale

  • Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE): Zero reference electrode: Pt | H₂(1 bar) | H⁺(1 M) with E° = 0.00 V.
  • EMF Subtraction Rule: E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode (always subtract reduction potentials; never add).
  • Electrochemical Series: Arranged in increasing order of standard reduction potentials: Li (−3.05 V) < Zn (−0.76 V) < H₂ (0.00 V) < Cu (+0.34 V) < F₂ (+2.87 V).
  • Displacement Power: Lower E° metal displaces higher E° metal from aqueous solution (Zn displaces Cu²⁺).

3. Nernst Equation & Energetics Non-Standard Cell

  • Nernst Equation at 298 K: Ecell = E°cell − (0.0591 / n) log Q, where Q is reaction quotient.
  • For Daniell Cell: Ecell = 1.10 − (0.0591 / 2) log ([Zn²⁺] / [Cu²⁺]).
  • Equilibrium State: When cell is fully discharged, Ecell = 0, Q = Kc: log Kc = (n · E°cell) / 0.0591.
  • Gibbs Free Energy: ΔrG° = −n · F · E°cell | Electrical work Wmax = −ΔrG. Spontaneous when E°cell > 0 and ΔG° < 0.

4. Electrolytic Conductance Ion Transport

  • Conductivity (κ) & Cell Constant: κ = (1 / R) · (l / A). κ decreases on dilution because ion count per cm³ falls.
  • Molar Conductivity (Λm): Λm = (1000 · κ) / C. Λm increases on dilution due to volume expansion.
  • Debye-Hückel-Onsager (Strong): Λm = Λ°m − A√C (extrapolates linearly to intercept Λ°m).
  • Kohlrausch's Law: Λ°m = ν₊ λ°₊ + ν₋ λ°₋. Allows exact determination of weak electrolytes: Λ°m(CH₃COOH) = Λ°m(CH₃COONa) + Λ°m(HCl) − Λ°m(NaCl).
  • Degree of Dissociation: α = Λm / Λ°m | Ka = (C · α²) / (1 − α).

5. Electrolysis & Faraday's Laws Non-Spontaneous

  • Electrolytic Cell: External EMF drives non-spontaneous reaction. Anode is Positive (+); Cathode is Negative (−).
  • Faraday's 1st Law: w = Z · I · t = (M · I · t) / (n · F) (where 1 F = 96,500 C/mol e⁻).
  • Faraday's 2nd Law: For same charge Q: w₁ / w₂ = E₁ / E₂.
  • Discharge Competition: • Aqueous NaCl: Cathode yields H₂(g) (not Na); Anode yields Cl₂(g) due to O₂ overpotential.
    • Molten NaCl: Cathode yields Na(s); Anode yields Cl₂(g).

6. Batteries, Fuel Cells & Corrosion Applied Power

  • Primary Cells (Non-Rechargeable): Dry cell (1.5 V, Zn/MnO₂); Mercury cell (1.35 V, constant voltage as no net ions).
  • Secondary Cells (Rechargeable): • Lead-Acid Battery: Discharging: Pb + PbO₂ + 2H₂SO₄ → 2PbSO₄ + 2H₂O (acid consumed). Charging reverses this.
    • Nickel-Cadmium (Ni-Cd): Cd anode + NiO(OH) cathode. Long life but higher cost.
  • H₂-O₂ Fuel Cell: Combines H₂ and O₂ with ~70% thermodynamic efficiency. Pollution-free (H₂O byproduct).
  • Corrosion (Rusting): Electrochemical process (Fe₂O₃ · xH₂O). Prevented by galvanisation (sacrificial Zn protection).
