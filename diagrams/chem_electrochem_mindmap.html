Galvanic (Voltaic) Cells

Idea: a spontaneous redox reaction converted into electricity — Daniell cell: Zn + Cu2+ → Zn2+ + Cu
Oxidation at anode (Zn, negative); reduction at cathode (Cu, positive)
Salt bridge: keeps solutions neutral, completes the circuit
Cell notation: anode | anode solution || cathode solution | cathode

Electrode Potential & EMF

SHE: the zero reference — Pt | H₂ (1 bar) | H⁺ (1 M), E° = 0.00 V
E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode (reduction potentials, subtract, never add)
Positive E° = spontaneous; E° = 0 at equilibrium
Series trick: the metal lower in the series pushes out the ion of a metal higher up

Nernst Equation & Gibbs Energy

E = E° − (0.059/n) log Q at 298 K — Q = reaction quotient
At equilibrium: E = 0 and Q = K ⇒ log K = nE°/0.059
ΔG° = −nFE°cell — links cell voltage to spontaneity
More negative ΔG° = bigger K = a battery worth building

Conductance & Kohlrausch

κ (conductivity): conductance of 1 cm³ of solution — falls on dilution (fewer ions per cm³)
Λm = κ × 1000 / c (molar conductivity) — rises on dilution (more freedom per mole)
Strong electrolytes: Λm = Λ°m − A√c (small change); weak ones: sharp rise
Kohlrausch's law: Λ°m = Λ°m of cation + Λ°m of anion — independent migration at infinite dilution

Electrolysis & Faraday

Electrolytic cell: electricity forces a non-spontaneous reaction — anode is (+) here!
Faraday's 1st law: w = ZIt, Z = M/(nF); 2nd law: same charge, mass ∝ equivalent weight
1 F = 96,500 C per mole of electrons
Products: molten NaCl → Na + Cl₂; aqueous NaCl → H₂ + Cl₂ + NaOH (water discharges first at cathode)

Batteries, Fuel Cells, Corrosion

Primary: dry cell, mercury cell — not rechargeable; mercury cell keeps a steady voltage (ions not involved in net reaction)
Secondary: lead-acid car battery, Ni-Cd — rechargeable
Fuel cell: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O — ~70% efficient, runs forever on fuel supply
Corrosion: rusting = electrochemistry — Fe → Fe2+ at anodic spots; prevent by coating, galvanising (sacrificial Zn), or cathodic protection

WATCH-OUTS THAT SET MCQ TRAPS

Anode sign flips: galvanic anode is (−); electrolytic anode is (+) — oxidation happens at BOTH anodes
Subtract potentials: E°cell = E°cat − E°an — adding electrode potentials is always wrong
κ falls, Λm rises on dilution — same fact, opposite direction, two units
Recharge = reverse: a lead-acid battery acts as a galvanic cell discharging, an electrolytic cell charging
