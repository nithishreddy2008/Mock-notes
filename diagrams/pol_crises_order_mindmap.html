CRISES OF THE CONSTITUTIONAL ORDER
POLITICS IN INDIA SINCE INDEPENDENCE · CH. 5 (TOPIC 6)

1. Prelude & Economic Strain (1973–74)

Post-war burden: 1971 Bangladesh war, 10 million refugees, US aid stopped, international oil crisis 1973.
Rampant inflation: Prices rose 23% in 1973 and 30% in 1974; widespread rural distress and industrial recession.
Railway Strike (May 1974): Led by George Fernandes; 20-day nationwide paralysis; crushed sternly.
Committed Judiciary push: Supersession of three senior judges (1973) ⇒ A.N. Ray appointed CJI.

2. Student Movements: Gujarat & Bihar

Navnirman in Gujarat (Jan 1974): Student revolt against food prices and corruption; Morarji Desai's fast.
Gujarat outcome: Assembly dissolved; fresh elections (June 1975) resulted in Congress defeat.
Bihar Movement (March 1974): Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) took leadership on condition of non-violence.
"Total Revolution": JP demanded Sampoorna Kranti across moral, political, social, and economic spheres.

3. The Catalyst & Emergency Declared

Allahabad HC ruling (12 June 1975): Justice Jagmohan Lal Sinha unseated Indira Gandhi on Raj Narain's plea.
SC conditional stay (24 June): Justice Krishna Iyer allowed PM post, but barred voting in Lok Sabha.
Ramlila Maidan Rally (25 June): JP declared civil disobedience; called police/military to disobey unlawful orders.
Article 352 invocation: Night of 25 June 1975; declared under "internal disturbance" without prior Cabinet meet.

4. The Emergency Excesses (1975–77)

Preventive detention: MISA used to jail ~111,000 opposition leaders; Fundamental Rights suspended.
Press censorship: News electricity cut; Indian Express and Statesman left blank editorial columns.
Extra-constitutional power: Sanjay Gandhi led coercive sterilisation drives and Turkman Gate demolitions.
42nd Amendment (1976): Extended Lok Sabha to 6 years; added "Secular, Socialist, Integrity" to Preamble.

5. The 1977 Elections & Janata Victory

Elections announced (Jan 1977): Detainees freed; Opposition formed Janata Party under JP's mentorship.
Democratic referendum: Voters treated the election as a direct verdict: Democracy vs Authoritarianism.
Congress wiped out in North: Zero seats in UP, Bihar, Punjab, Haryana; Indira and Sanjay lost their own seats.
First non-Congress PM: Morarji Desai took oath as Prime Minister in March 1977.

6. Legacy & Democratic Safeguards

Shah Commission: Headed by Justice J.C. Shah to investigate Emergency abuses and illegalities.
44th Amendment (1978): Replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion"; Article 20 & 21 non-suspendable.
Written Cabinet advice: Made mandatory before the President can proclaim any National Emergency.
Civil liberties boom: Birth of vibrant rights bodies: PUCL and PUDR; Indian democracy proved irrepressible.

KEY EXAM TRAPS & FREQUENT PITFALLS

Emergency Grounds: Declared in 1975 under "internal disturbance" (not armed rebellion; that was introduced by 44th Amendment).
The Judges: Justice J.L. Sinha struck down Indira's election; Justice A.N. Ray was the superseded CJI appointee.
42nd vs 44th Amendment: 42nd (1976) expanded executive power; 44th (1978) restored constitutional checks.
