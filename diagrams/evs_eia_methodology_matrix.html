FIGURE 1: EIA METHODOLOGY MATRIX
Methodology Operational Architecture Primary Strengths Core Limitations Type / Origin
Leopold Matrix 100 project actions × 88 environmental factors (8,800 cells). Each scored for Magnitude (1–10) and Importance (1–10) separated by a slash (M/I). Comprehensive two-dimensional identification; visual impact distribution; identifies acute hotspots. Subjective scoring; does not capture secondary/indirect impacts or temporal dynamics. Matrix / USGS
Checklists Systematic inventory of parameters. Four types: Simple (presence/absence), Descriptive, Scaling (graded), and Weighting-Scaling (parameter weights). Structured baseline; prevents overlooking sensitive factors; rapid initial screening and scoping. Cannot establish cause-effect interrelationships; static without feedback loops. Inventory
Sorensen Network Tree and network diagrams linking initial project actions to primary, secondary, and tertiary ecological chain reactions. Explicitly models indirect effects and cumulative ecological cascades; causal path traceability. Becomes extremely complex and cluttered for large multi-sector mega projects. Network / Causal
McHarg Overlay / GIS Thematic map layering (topography, hydrology, vegetation, land use) to synthesize composite environmental sensitivity surfaces. Superior spatial visualization; ideal for corridor selection (roads, pipelines, transmission lines). Does not quantify non-spatial or transient impacts (odors, socioeconomic shifts). Spatial / GIS
Battelle BEES 78 environmental parameters grouped into 4 categories: Ecology, Environmental Pollution, Esthetics, Human Interest. Uses Environmental Quality (EQ 0–1) × Weight (PIU). Rigorous quantitative index; enables mathematical comparison of alternative project designs. High data requirement; parameter weights are pre-fixed and reflect developed-nation priorities. Index / Quantitative
