Environment & Sustainable Development Framework

Functions of the Environment, Ecological Limits, Indian Environmental Pressures & Sustainable Strategies

CLASS 12 · INDIAN ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT
1. The Four Functions of the Environment
Ecosystem Roles
A. Supplies Resources (Renewable & Non-Renewable)
Provides inputs for production: Renewable (water, forests, fish) and Non-Renewable (coal, petroleum, mineral ores).
B. Assimilates Waste
Absorbs the by-products and effluents generated by human production and consumption activities.
C. Sustains Life & Genetic Biodiversity
Maintains sunlight, air, water, and biological diversity essential for planetary survival.
D. Provides Aesthetic Services
Scenery, mountains, oceans, and natural wilderness enhancing human quality of life.
CUET Rule: Environment performs all four functions simultaneously until thresholds are breached.
2. Ecological Limits: Carrying Capacity
Threshold Mechanics
A. Resource Extraction vs Regeneration Rate
Extraction of resources must NOT exceed the natural rate of resource regeneration. Over-extraction causes resource depletion.
B. Waste Generation vs Absorptive Capacity
Generation of waste must remain strictly within the Absorptive Capacity of the environment. Breach causes environmental crisis.
C. Global Environmental Crises
Breaching thresholds creates Global Warming (greenhouse gas accumulation) and Ozone Depletion (CFC emissions). Montreal Protocol (1987) banned ODS.
Environmental Crisis occurs when Carrying Capacity or Absorptive Capacity is breached!
3. India's Environmental Pressure Points
Resource Strain
A. Land Degradation & Soil Erosion
Deforestation, overgrazing, waterlogging, excessive chemical salinity, and shifting cultivation threaten agricultural topsoil fertility.
B. Air & Water Pollution
Severe urban smog (vehicular exhaust, thermal power plants, crop stubble burning); industrial effluent discharge polluting Ganga and Yamuna.
C. Regulatory Watchdog: CPCB (1974)
Central Pollution Control Board established under the Water Act 1974; enforces emission standards and monitors air/water quality.
Dichotomy of environmental degradation in India: Poverty-induced vs Affluence-induced!
4. Sustainable Development Strategies
Brundtland 1987
A. Brundtland Definition (1987 - "Our Common Future")
"Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."
B. Clean Energy Shift
Solar energy (International Solar Alliance), wind power, mini-hydel plants; rural LPG (Ujjwala) and Gobar gas replacing firewood.
C. Urban & Agricultural Greening
CNG in public transport; traditional knowledge (Ayurveda); organic farming with neem-based biopesticides and biological predators.
Sustainable Development bridges intergenerational equity with environmental preservation.
Master Takeaway: True development balances present economic welfare with natural carrying capacity, preserving life-support systems for future generations.
