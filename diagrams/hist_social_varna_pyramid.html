The Normative 4-Tier Varna Hierarchy
Purusha Sukta Model
1. Brahmanas (The Priestly Apex) Mythic: Mouth of Purusha
Prescribed Duties: Study and teach the Vedas, perform sacrifices for themselves and others, receive and give gifts (*dana*). Claimed highest divine status by birth.
2. Kshatriyas (The Rulers & Warriors) Mythic: Arms of Purusha
Prescribed Duties: Engage in warfare, protect subjects, administer royal justice, study the Vedas, get sacrifices performed, and donate wealth. Only Kshatriyas were theoretically entitled to rule.
3. Vaishyas (The Economic Producers) Mythic: Thighs of Purusha
Prescribed Duties: Agriculture, pastoral cattle-rearing, and commerce/trade. Entitled to Vedic initiation and sacrificial sponsorship alongside upper varnas.
4. Shudras (The Service Tier) Mythic: Feet of Purusha
Prescribed Duties: Assigned only one primary duty: serving the three higher varnas. Strictly barred from Vedic initiation (*upanayana*) and sacrificial performance.
Brahmanical Enforcement Strategies: 1. Claimed divine origin via Rigveda; 2. Advised monarchs to enforce varna order; 3. Instilled belief that status was determined strictly by birth.
Historical Realities: Non-Kshatriya Kings, Jatis & Outcastes
Epigraphic & Textual Reality
Non-Kshatriya Sovereigns Political Subversion
Mauryas were described by Brahmanical sources as of "low origin"; the Shungas and Kanvas who succeeded them were Brahmanas; the Shakas from Central Asia were considered Mlechchhas (barbarians); the Satavahana ruler Gautamiputra Satakarni claimed to be the "unique Brahmana" (*Eka Bamhana*) and destroyed Kshatriya pride.
Jatis & Occupational Guilds (Mandasor Inscription) Fluid Jati System
Unlike the 4 fixed varnas, Jatis had no fixed limit. Groups with shared professions (e.g. Suvarnakara / goldsmiths) were grouped as jatis. The 5th c. CE Mandasor inscription records a guild of silk weavers (*reshama-vanik*) migrating from Lata (Gujarat) to Dashapura (MP), adopting varied roles like soldiers, astronomers, and temple patrons.
Forest Dwellers: Nishadas & The Ekalavya Episode Beyond the Pale
Populations who lived in forests and spoke non-Sanskrit dialects were branded Nishadas. The Mahabharata records how Dronacharya demanded the right thumb of the skilled forest archer Ekalavya to protect Arjuna's royal Kshatriya supremacy.
The Untouchables: Chandalas & Foreign Records Ritual Pollution
Brahmanas classified corpse handlers and executioners as Chandalas, whose very sight was polluting. Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Faxian (5th c. CE) noted they had to strike clappers when entering streets so people could avoid them; Xuanzang (7th c. CE) observed executioners and scavengers forced to live strictly outside city walls.
Historical Synthesis: Shastric Varna was an idealized normative framework, repeatedly challenged and subverted by political power, economic wealth, and regional migrations.
