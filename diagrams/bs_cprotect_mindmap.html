Meaning & Importance

Consumer protection = protecting consumers from unfair, exploitative and deceptive trade practices
Why needed: consumers are unorganised, often ignorant of rights, and exploitation is widespread — adulteration, spurious goods, hoarding, black marketing, false ads
Importance for business too: long-term interest, ethical duty, government pressure, social responsibility — satisfied consumers are repeat consumers

Six Consumer Rights

1 Right to Safety — protection from hazardous goods
2 Right to be Informed — quality, quantity, purity, price, standards
3 Right to Choose — access to a variety at fair prices
4 Right to be Heard — grievances heard at proper forums
5 Right to Seek Redressal — relief against unfair practices
6 Right to Consumer Education — learn rights and remedies

Consumer Responsibilities

Check quality marks — ISI, Agmark, Hallmark
Ask for a cash memo / bill — always
Read labels honestly; buy only what is needed
Be ecologically responsible; recycle where possible
Form consumer societies; complain genuinely, not frivolously

Consumer Protection Act, 2019

Replaced the 1986 Act — covers goods and services, e-commerce included
Three-tier machinery: District (≤ ₹50 lakh) $arrow$ State (₹50 lakh–₹2 crore) $arrow$ National (> ₹2 crore) — limits revised in 2021
Appeals: District $arrow$ State $arrow$ National $arrow$ Supreme Court
Complaint within 2 years of the cause of action

Ways of Protection + Role of NGOs

Ways: self-regulation by business · business associations' codes · consumer awareness · consumer organisations · government / legal redressal (CPA 2019)
Consumer organisations & NGOs: educate consumers · publish journals · test product samples · file complaints and PILs · give legal aid · run awareness campaigns

Traps that catch most students

Rights vs responsibilities — asking for a bill is a responsibility; being heard is a right
The two money walls: ₹50 lakh and ₹2 crore — District, State, National in order
CPA 2019 replaced 1986 — and added e-commerce coverage
Complaint time limit = 2 years from the cause of action — not 1, not 3
