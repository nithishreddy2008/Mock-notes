Rate of a Reaction

Average rate: change over a chosen interval — Δ[R]/Δt
Instantaneous rate: rate at one moment — slope of the tangent, −d[R]/dt
Stoichiometry matters: divide by the coefficient — rate = −½d[N₂]/dt = +½d[NH₃]/dt
Units: mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹ (always the same, whatever the order)

Rate Law, Order & Molecularity

Rate law: Rate = k[A]x[B]y — exponents come from EXPERIMENT only
Order: x + y — can be 0, fractional, even negative; found from data
k's units shift with order: zero: mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹; first: s⁻¹; second: L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
Molecularity: molecules colliding in one elementary step — whole number 1-3, never zero
Order = experimental; molecularity = theoretical — they need not match

Integrated Laws & Half-Life

Zero order: [A] = [A]₀ − kt; t½ = [A]₀/2k — falls as reaction proceeds
First order: k = (2.303/t) log([A]₀/[A]); t½ = 0.693/k — constant, concentration-independent
First-order plots: log[A] vs t is straight; [A] vs t curves down
75% done = two half-lives — add t½'s, don't double the rate

Factors That Change the Rate

Concentration: more crowding, more collisions, faster (until order says otherwise)
Temperature: rough rule — every 10°C rise nearly doubles the rate (k climbs via Arrhenius)
Catalyst: offers a lower-energy path; speeds up forward AND backward equally; never consumed
Surface area & pressure: powdered solids and compressed gases react faster

Pseudo First Order

Trick: a second-order reaction where one reactant (usually water) is in such huge excess that its concentration barely moves
Ester hydrolysis: CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O → acid + alcohol — water stays ~55.5 M, so rate depends only on [ester]
Sugar inversion: sucrose + water, acid-catalysed — again water's excess hides it
Test for it: kinetics that behave first-order despite a two-molecule equation

Arrhenius & Collision Theory

Arrhenius: k = A e−Ea/RT; log form: log k = log A − Ea/2.303RT
Two-temperature shortcut: log(k₂/k₁) = Ea(T₂−T₁)/2.303RT₁T₂
Slope of log k vs 1/T = −Ea/2.303R — read Ea straight off the graph
Collision theory: rate = fraction of collisions with energy ≥ threshold AND correct orientation; weak at explaining complex molecules (treats them as hard spheres)

WATCH-OUTS THAT SET MCQ TRAPS

Order is experimental: never read exponents off the balanced equation
k's units are a fingerprint: they reveal the order when the question hides it
First-order t½ is constant: independent of starting concentration (zero-order t½ is not)
Catalyst changes neither ΔH nor K — it lowers Ea for both directions equally
