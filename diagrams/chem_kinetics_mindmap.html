1. Rate of Reaction Average vs Instant

  • Average Rate (rav): rav = −Δ[R]/Δt = +Δ[P]/Δt over macroscopic time interval.
  • Instantaneous Rate (rinst): rinst = −d[R]/dt = +d[P]/dt (tangent slope at time t).
  • Stoichiometric Equivalence: For a A + b B → c C + d D: Rate = −(1/a)d[A]/dt = −(1/b)d[B]/dt = +(1/c)d[C]/dt = +(1/d)d[D]/dt.
  • Rate Units: Always mol · L⁻¹ · s⁻¹ (or atm/s for gaseous reactions).

2. Rate Law, Order & Molecularity Experimental Law

  • Rate Law: Rate = k [A]ˣ [B]ʸ (powers x, y found strictly from experiment).
  • Overall Order: n = x + y (can be zero, integer, fraction, or negative).
  • Units of Rate Constant (k): (mol L⁻¹)¹⁻ⁿ · s⁻¹. • Zero: mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹ | First: s⁻¹ | Second: L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.
  • Molecularity: Number of species colliding in an elementary step (1, 2, or 3 only; never zero or fractional).

3. Integrated Laws & Half-Life Mathematical Dynamics

  • Zero Order: [R] = [R]₀ − kt | t₁/₂ = [R]₀ / (2k) (proportional to [R]₀). Goes to 100% completion in t₁₀₀% = 2 t₁/₂.
  • First Order: k = (2.303/t) log([R]₀/[R]) | t₁/₂ = 0.693 / k (independent of [R]₀). Never completes (t₁₀₀% = ∞).
  • Progressions: t₇₅% = 2 × t₁/₂ | t₈₇.₅% = 3 × t₁/₂ | t₉₉.₉% ≈ 10 × t₁/₂.
  • Radioactive Decay: All natural/artificial nuclear decays follow 1st order.

4. Pseudo-First-Order Reactions Solvent Excess

  • Concept: Higher-order reaction behaving as first order because one reactant is present in huge stoichiometric excess ([B] ≈ const).
  • Effective Rate Constant: Rate = k' [A] [B] = k [A], where k = k' [B]ₑₓ𝚌ₑₛₛ.
  • Ester Hydrolysis: CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O(excess) ⟶ CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH.
  • Cane Sugar Inversion: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O(excess) ⟶ Glucose + Fructose. Dextrorotatory sugar hydrolyses into a laevorotatory mixture.

5. Arrhenius Theory & Ea Temperature Impact

  • Exponential Formulation: k = A · e^(−Ea / RT) (Boltzmann fraction: e^(−Ea/RT)).
  • Logarithmic Form: log k = log A − (Ea / 2.303 R T) (slope of log k vs 1/T is −Ea / 2.303 R).
  • Two-Temperature Equation: log(k₂ / k₁) = (Ea / 2.303 R) · [(T₂ − T₁) / (T₁ T₂)].
  • Temperature Coefficient: A 10°C rise doubles or triples k because the fraction of molecules with E ≥ Ea doubles.

6. Collision Theory & Catalysis Reaction Mechanics

  • Rate Equation: Rate = P · Z_AB · e^(−Ea / RT) (P = steric / orientation factor; Z_AB = collision frequency).
  • Requirements for Effective Collision: (1) Energy Barrier (E ≥ Threshold Energy ET), (2) Proper steric orientation.
  • Role of Catalyst: Provides an alternate mechanism with a lower activation energy (Ea,cat < Ea). Speeds up forward and reverse rates equally.
  • Thermodynamic Invariance: A catalyst does not alter ΔH, ΔG, or equilibrium constant Kc!
⚠ EXAM TRAP CLINIC:
Order vs Coefficients: Exponents in rate law are strictly experimental; never equate them to balanced coefficients! 1st Order t₁/₂: Constant and independent of [R]₀. Doubling [R]₀ has zero effect on t₁/₂. Catalyst Impact: Alters rate and Ea, but leaves ΔH and Kc completely unchanged!
