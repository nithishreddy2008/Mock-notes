1. Drift Velocity & Mobility

Microscopic Transport
Thermal Motion vs Drift Zero Net Flux

Thermal Speed: Random Maxwellian motion (~10⁵ m/s at 300 K); average thermal velocity ⟨v_th⟩ = 0.

Relaxation Time (τ): Mean time between two successive electron-ion lattice collisions (~10⁻¹⁴ s).

Drift Velocity Formulation v_d = e E τ / m
v_d = (e E τ) / m_e = (e V τ) / (m_e L)

Magnitude: Typically ~10⁻⁴ m/s (fraction of mm/s). Directed strictly opposite to electric field vector E.

Signal Speed: Electrical impulse propagates at speed of light in dielectric (~10⁸ m/s), despite microscopic sluggish drift.
Electron Mobility (μ) μ = v_d / E
μ = v_d / E = (e τ) / m_e

Definition: Drift velocity acquired per unit electric field [m²·V⁻¹·s⁻¹]. Independent of applied field; depends solely on material and temperature.

2. Current & Ohm's Law

Constitutive Relations
Current & Drift Relation I = n e A v_d
I = n e A v_d = n e A (e E τ / m)

Current Density (J): Current per unit cross-sectional area: J = I / A = n e v_d [A/m²]. J is a true vector quantity.

Microscopic Ohm's Law J = σ E
J = σ E = E / ρ

Conductivity (σ): σ = (n e² τ) / m [Ω⁻¹·m⁻¹ or S/m].

Resistivity (ρ): ρ = 1/σ = m / (n e² τ) [Ω·m]. Independent of dimensions; depends on material properties (n, τ).

Macroscopic Ohm's Law V = I R
R = ρ L / A = (m L) / (n e² τ A)

Resistance (R): Ratio of potential difference to current at constant temperature. Slope of V-I characteristic gives resistance.

3. Resistivity & Temperature

Thermal Coefficients
Temperature Dependence ρ(T) = ρ₀(1 + α ΔT)

Temperature Coefficient (α): Fractional change in resistivity per degree rise: α = (1/ρ₀)(dρ/dT) [K⁻¹ or °C⁻¹].

Key Mechanism: In metals, electron density n is constant; temperature rise increases lattice vibrations, reducing relaxation time τ (dρ/dT > 0).
Classification of Materials Conductors / Alloys / Semi

Metals (Cu, Al, Ag): α > 0 (positive). Resistivity increases linearly with temperature near room temp.

Standard Alloys (Manganin, Constantan, Nichrome): Extremely high ρ and almost negligible α (~10⁻⁵ K⁻¹); used for standard resistor coils.

Semiconductors & Electrolytes (Si, Ge, C): α < 0 (negative). Thermal energy ionizes covalent bonds, exponentially increasing carrier density n.

Non-Ohmic Devices Non-Linear V-I

Deviations from Ohm's Law: p-n junction diodes (asymmetric V-I), thyristors, GaAs (negative resistance region), electrolytes, discharge tubes.

4. Electrical Energy & Power

Joule Heating
Joule's Law of Heating H = I² R t
P = V I = I² R = V² / R

Energy: W = P · t = V I t [Joules]. Commercial billing unit: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J (1 B.O.T. Unit).

Bulbs in Series & Parallel Rated vs Actual

Rated Resistance: R = V_rated² / P_rated (constant property).

Series (Equal Current): P_actual = I² R ⟹ Lower wattage bulb has higher resistance and glows brighter!

Parallel (Equal Voltage): P_actual = V² / R ⟹ Higher wattage bulb draws more current and glows brighter!

Maximum Power Transfer Theorem Matching Load
P_max = E² / (4 r)   (when R_L = r)

Maximum power is delivered from a real source of EMF E and internal resistance r to a load resistor R_L when R_L = r (Efficiency η = 50%).
