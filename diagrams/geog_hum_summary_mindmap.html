Human Geography: Nature & Scope

FUNDAMENTALS · CHAPTER 1

1. Definitions & Conceptual Roots

Theory
  • Ratzel: Synthetic study of relationship between human societies and earth's surface.
  • Ellen Semple: Study of changing relationship between unresting man and unstable earth.
  • Vidal de la Blache: Synthetic knowledge of physical laws governing earth and living beings.
  • Core Focus: Dynamic interrelationship between physical nature and human culture.

2. Dualism & Organic Metaphors

Methodology
  • Methodological Dualism: Nomothetic (law-seeking) vs. Idiographic (descriptive); Systematic vs. Regional.
  • Anatomical Metaphors: "Face of the earth", "eye of the storm", "mouth of the river", "snout of the glacier".
  • Circulatory Arteries: Networks of roads, railways, and waterways described as veins and arteries.
  • Living Organism: Earth conceptualized as a living entity integrated with human society.

3. Paradigms of Interaction

Core Models
  • Environmental Determinism: Primitive humans enslaved to nature; "State of necessity" (Benda in Abujh Maad).
  • Possibilism: Technology unlocks nature's opportunities; "State of freedom" (Kari in Trondheim).
  • Neo-Determinism: Griffith Taylor's "Stop and Go Determinism"; middle path (*Madhyam Marg*).
  • Traffic Analogy: Red (stop), Amber (prepare), Green (proceed within ecological carrying capacity).

4. Corridors of Time Evolution

Evolution
  • Early Colonial: Exploration and description driven by imperial discovery and trade.
  • Later Colonial: Regional analysis studying parts to understand the global whole.
  • 1930s Inter-War: Areal differentiation uncovering spatial uniqueness of specific regions.
  • 1950s–1960s: Spatial organisation and Quantitative Revolution using statistical laws.

5. 1970s Radical Schools of Thought

Schools
  • Welfare / Humanistic School: Emphasized social well-being, housing, health, and education (David Smith).
  • Radical School: Employed Marxian theory to critique poverty, deprivation, and capitalist class structures.
  • Behavioural School: Investigated lived experience, cognitive space, and perceptions based on social identity.
  • 1990s Post-Modernism: Questioned universal grand theories; focused on localized historical contexts.

6. Fields, Sub-Fields & Linkages

Taxonomy
  • Social Geography: Behavioural, social well-being, leisure, gender, historical, medical.
  • Political Geography: Electoral geography (psephology) and military geography.
  • Economic Geography: Resources, agriculture, industries, marketing, tourism, international trade.
  • Population & Settlement: Demography and rural/urban settlement planning.
