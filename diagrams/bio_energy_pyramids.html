Ecological pyramids — three lenses on the same ecosystem

COUNT THE ORGANISMS · WEIGH THE BIOMASS · TRACK THE ENERGY

Pyramid of number

What is counted: how many organisms sit at each trophic level.
T4 · top carnivores
T3 · carnivores
T2 · herbivores
T1 · producers (grass plants)
▲ WIDEST LAYER = MOST ORGANISMS (GRASSLAND)
Upright in a grassland. INVERTED in a tree ecosystem — one single tree (T1) supports hundreds of fruit-eating insects and birds (T2), so the top layer outnumbers the base.

Pyramid of biomass

What is weighed: the dry living mass present at each level at a given time.
T4 · top carnivores
T3 · carnivores
T2 · herbivores
T1 · producers
▲ WIDEST LAYER = MOST LIVING MASS (ON LAND)
Upright on land. INVERTED in the ocean — the standing crop of phytoplankton is small at any instant, yet it keeps feeding a larger zooplankton mass because phytoplankton multiply fast.

Pyramid of energy

What is tracked: energy stored in new living matter at each level (kJ/m²/yr).
T4 · 10 kJ
T3 · 100 kJ
T2 · 1,000 kJ
T1 · 10,000 kJ
▲ EACH LAYER = 10% OF THE ONE BELOW
ALWAYS upright — never inverted. Why: at every transfer about 90% of the energy is lost as heat (second law of thermodynamics), so no level above can ever hold more energy than the level below.
Whichever way the upper layers bend, the producers always form the base — and energy always narrows on the way up.
