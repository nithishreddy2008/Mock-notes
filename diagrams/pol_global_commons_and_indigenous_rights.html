The Four Global Commons
Res Communis Beyond National Jurisdiction
Defining Global Commons Jurisdiction
  • Resources not owned by any single sovereign state, requiring common global governance.
  • Four Recognized Commons: The Earth's Atmosphere, Antarctica, the Ocean Floor (High Seas), and Outer Space.
  • Governed under the international legal principle of res communis (common heritage of mankind).
Antarctica & Polar Treaties Treaty Regimes
  • Antarctic Treaty (1959): Demilitarized Antarctica; froze territorial claims; dedicated continent to peaceful scientific research.
  • Madrid Protocol (1991): Protocol on Environmental Protection to the Antarctic Treaty; banned commercial mining exploration indefinitely.
Ozone Layer & Atmosphere Montreal 1987
  • Montreal Protocol (1987): Legally binding global treaty mandating the phase-out of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and ozone-depleting substances.
  • Considered the most successful international environmental agreement in history.

North-South Divide & CBDR
Historical Responsibility vs Right to Development
Foundational Reports Intellectual Roots
  • Club of Rome (1972): Published Limits to Growth, warning of finite resource exhaustion.
  • Brundtland Report (1987): Published Our Common Future, formalizing the concept of Sustainable Development.
Rio Earth Summit (June 1992) UNCED
  • Convened in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil; produced Agenda 21 (action blueprint).
  • Birthed the UNFCCC (Climate Change) and the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD).
  • Enshrined CBDR (Principle 7): Common But Differentiated Responsibilities.
Kyoto Protocol vs Paris Agreement Emission Regimes
  • Kyoto Protocol (1997): Set legally binding cuts (5% below 1990 levels) exclusively for developed Annex-I countries. India ratified in August 2002.
  • Paris Agreement (2015): Shifted to bottom-up Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) targeting well below 2°C / 1.5°C limits.

Resource Geopolitics & Indigenous Rights
Petroleum, Water Wars, Adivasis & India's Leadership
Geopolitics of Oil & Water Strategic Resources
  • Oil Hegemony: The Persian Gulf houses ~64% of global crude oil reserves. Major driver of Gulf War (1990–91) and Iraq invasion (2003).
  • Water Wars: Upstream damming vs downstream flow conflicts across the Jordan river, Euphrates-Tigris basin, and the Nile river basin.
Indigenous Peoples' Rights WCIP 1975
  • UN Definition: Descendants of original inhabitants prior to conquest who maintain distinct socio-cultural institutions.
  • WCIP (1975): World Council of Indigenous Peoples formed to lobby the UN.
  • Indian Context: Refers to Scheduled Tribes (Adivasis), who constitute ~8% of the population, rather than European settler-colonial categories.
India's Environmental Leadership National Strategy
  • NAPCC (2008): National Action Plan on Climate Change with 8 National Missions (Solar, Water, Green India, etc.).
  • International Solar Alliance (ISA, 2015): Co-founded by India & France at COP21; headquartered in Gurugram, India.
