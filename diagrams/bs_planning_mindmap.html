PLANNING
UNIT IV · COMPLETE CHAPTER MAP

Meaning & Features

Planning = deciding in advance what to do, how to do it, when to do it and who is to do it
Features: focuses on objectives · primary function (comes first) · pervasive · continuous · futuristic · involves choice (decision-making) · mental exercise
Bridges the gap between where we are and where we want to reach

Importance (6)

Provides direction — tells everyone where to act
Reduces risk of uncertainty — anticipates change
Reduces overlapping & wasteful activities — coordination without chaos
Promotes innovation · facilitates decision-making · establishes standards for controlling

Limitations (6)

Leads to rigidity — managers stick to the plan even when better options appear
May not work in a dynamic environment — the future resists prediction
Reduces creativity — middle levels just follow, not think
Involves huge cost · time-consuming · does not guarantee success — plans fail without good execution

Planning Process (7 Steps)

1 Setting objectives — the destination
2 Developing premises — agreed assumptions about the future
3 Identifying alternatives — list every sensible route
4 Evaluating alternatives — cost, risk, fit
5 Selecting the best — the decision moment
6 Implementing the plan — action
7 Follow-up — compare, correct, feed the next plan

Standing Plans (reused)

Objectives — the end results to achieve (sales target ₹arrow 10,000 units)
Policy — general guidelines for thinking (no discounts without approval)
Procedure — exact sequence of steps for a task (how to admit a student)
Method — one prescribed way to do one step (lecture vs activity)
Rule — a specific statement, no discretion (wear identity cards)

Single-Use Plans (once)

Strategy — comprehensive plan for competition + environment (entering rural markets with low-cost packs)
Programme — combination of objectives + policies + procedures for a project (launching a new product)
Budget — plan stated in numbers (₹2 lakh advertising budget for a quarter)
They are developed for one situation and end when the goal is achieved

Traps that catch most students

Rule vs policy: rule = no discretion; policy = guides thinking, some judgment allowed
Procedure vs method: procedure = sequence of steps; method = one way to do one step
Single-use = programme, budget (+ strategy); standing = objectives, policy, procedure, method, rule
Planning is primary — it precedes organising, staffing, directing, controlling; and it never guarantees success
