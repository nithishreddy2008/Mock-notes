ALTERNATIVE CENTERS OF POWER
CONTEMPORARY WORLD POLITICS · CH. 2 (TOPIC 11)

1. The European Union (EU)

Treaty of Rome (1957): Established European Economic Community (EEC); evolved from 1948 Marshall Plan.
Maastricht Treaty (1992): Formally established the European Union; created common citizenship and single market.
Euro Currency (2002): Adopted by Eurozone states; EU flag with 12 gold stars (symbolizing perfection and unity).
Global Clout: World's second largest economy; France holds a permanent veto seat on UN Security Council.

2. ASEAN: Regional Economic Dynamo

Bangkok Declaration (1967): Founded by 5 nations: Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand.
The 'ASEAN Way': Unique diplomatic culture of informal, non-confrontational, and consensus-based cooperation.
Three Pillars (2003): ASEAN Security Community, ASEAN Economic Community, and ASEAN Socio-Cultural Community.
ARF (1994): ASEAN Regional Forum established to coordinate security and defense dialogue in the Asia-Pacific.

3. The Rise of China as Superpower

Four Modernisations (1973): Zhou Enlai proposed agriculture, industry, defense, and science & technology.
Open Door Policy (1978): Deng Xiaoping ended Maoist isolationism; phased privatization (agriculture 1982, industry 1998).
Special Economic Zones (SEZs): Attracted massive foreign direct investment; joined the WTO in 2001.
Economic Dominance: World's factory; projected to surpass the US as the largest economy by 2040.

4. India-China Relations: Trade vs Rivalry

Panchsheel (1954) to 1962 War: Friendship collapsed over Tibet asylum to Dalai Lama (1959) and 1962 border war.
Rajiv Gandhi Visit (1988): Breakthrough visit restored diplomatic normalisation while delinking border dispute.
Economic Integration: Bilateral trade crossed $100 billion; India remains dependent on Chinese manufacturing imports.
Strategic Friction: Doklam standoff (2017), Galwan clash (2020), CPEC passing through PoK, and border standoffs on LAC.

5. India's Ties with Japan & South Korea

Japan (Special Strategic Partner): Co-financing Mumbai-Ahmedabad High Speed Rail (Shinkansen); Quad member.
Tokyo Declaration (2014): Elevated ties to Special Strategic and Global Partnership; joint Malabar naval drills.
South Korea (CEPA 2010): Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement; major investments by Samsung, Hyundai, LG.
Act East Synergy: Integration of Indian economic growth with East Asian manufacturing supply chains.

6. BRICS & Alternative Financial Bodies

BRIC to BRICS: Brazil, Russia, India, China formed bloc in 2006; South Africa joined in 2010.
New Development Bank (NDB): Established in 2014 at Fortaleza Summit (headquartered in Shanghai) as alternative to IMF/WB.
South-South Cooperation: Promotes local currency settlement, infrastructure lending, and reform of Bretton Woods institutions.
Expansion (2024): Inducted new members (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE), accelerating global multipolar transition.
