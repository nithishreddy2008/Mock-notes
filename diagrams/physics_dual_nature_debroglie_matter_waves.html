de Broglie Relations & Particle Matrix
Fundamental de Broglie Relation
λ = h / p = h / (m v) = h / √(2 m E_k)
For a charged particle accelerated from rest through potential difference V:
λ = h / √(2 m q V)
Accelerating Potential Formulas (V in Volts):
Particle Mass, Charge Wavelength λ (Å)
Electron (e⁻) m_e, e λ = 12.27 / √V Å = 1.227 / √V nm
Proton (p) m_p, e λ = 0.286 / √V Å
Deuteron (d) 2 m_p, e λ = 0.202 / √V Å
Alpha (α) 4 m_p, 2e λ = 0.101 / √V Å
Thermal Neutrons in Equilibrium at T (Kelvin)
E_k = (3/2) k_B T ⟹ λ = h / √(3 m_n k_B T)
Numerical shorthand: λ_neutron ≈ 25.2 / √T Å (at 300 K, λ ≈ 1.45 Å).
  • Micro vs Macro: For baseball (0.15 kg, 30 m/s), λ ~ 10⁻³⁴ m (unobservable). For electron (100 eV), λ ~ 1.2 Å (comparable to atomic lattice spacing).
Davisson-Germer Experiment (Wave Proof)
Core Experimental Discovery (1927): A prominent peak in electron scattering intensity was observed at:
V = 54 Volts  and  Scattering Angle ϕ = 50°
1. Bragg's Law (Wave)
θ = (180° - 50°)/2 = 65°
d_Ni = 0.91 Å (from X-ray)
λ = 2d sin θ
λ = 2(0.91) sin 65° = 1.65 Å
2. de Broglie (Theory)
λ = 12.27 / √V Å
V = 54 Volts
λ = 12.27 / √54
λ = 12.27 / 7.348 = 1.67 Å
✨ 1.65 Å ≈ 1.67 Å: Conclusively proved electron behaves as a wave!
Bohr Orbit Waves & CUET Traps
Bohr's Postulate via de Broglie Standing Waves
2 π r_n = n λ = n (h / m v)
⟹ m v r_n = n h / (2 π)
An electron orbit is stable only when its circumference accommodates an integral number of de Broglie wavelengths!
High-Yield CUET Ratio Quick-Solvers:
  • Same Potential (V):
    λ ∝ 1 / √(m q) ⟹ λ_p : λ_d : λ_α = 1 : 1/√2 : 1/(2√2) ≈ 2√2 : 2 : 1
  • Same Kinetic Energy (E_k):
    λ ∝ 1 / √m ⟹ Independent of charge! Ratio depends strictly on mass.
  • Same Velocity (v):
    λ ∝ 1 / m ⟹ Lighter particle has longer de Broglie wavelength.
  • Same Momentum (p):
    λ = h / p ⟹ Exactly identical wavelength for all particles!
Electron Microscope Advantage: Since de Broglie wavelength of 50–100 keV electrons is ~0.04 Å (100,000× smaller than visible light λ ~ 5000 Å), electron microscopes provide resolving power orders of magnitude superior to optical microscopes.
