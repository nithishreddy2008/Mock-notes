CHALLENGE TO & RESTORATION OF CONGRESS SYSTEM
POLITICS IN INDIA SINCE INDEPENDENCE · CH. 4

1. The Succession Trials (1964–66)

Nehru dies (May 1964): Peaceful transition proved Indian democracy's resilience to foreign sceptics.
Lal Bahadur Shastri: Chosen unanimously by Syndicate; led 1965 war; coined "Jai Jawan Jai Kisan".
Tashkent Agreement (Jan 1966): Signed with Ayub Khan (USSR mediation); Shastri died suddenly hours later.
Indira Gandhi succeeds (1966): Defeated Morarji Desai in secret ballot; Syndicate backed her expecting compliance.

2. 1967 "Political Earthquake"

Economic backdrop: Severe drought, food shortage, price inflation, devaluation of rupee from ₹5 to ₹7/$.
Non-Congressism: Rammanohar Lohia urged opposition unity to prevent anti-Congress vote splitting.
1967 General Election: Congress scraped Lok Sabha (283 seats); lost assembly power in 8 states.
Non-Congress belt: Stretched unbroken from Punjab to West Bengal; half of Indira's cabinet lost seats.

3. Coalitions & Defections

SVD Governments: Samyukta Vidhayak Dal coalitions united ideological opposites (Jan Sangh + Socialists + CPI).
Extreme instability: Inherent contradictions led to rapid friction, collapse and frequent President's Rule.
"Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram": Coined after Haryana MLA Gaya Lal switched parties thrice in a fortnight.
Impact of defections: Toggled balance of power in Haryana, MP and UP; prompted anti-defection law debates.

4. The Split of 1969: Cong (O) vs Cong (R)

Syndicate vs Indira: Senior leaders (Kamaraj, Nijalingappa, Patil) clashed over PM's growing autonomy.
Socialist turn: 10-Point Programme (May 1967); nationalised 14 banks; abolished Privy Purses.
1969 Presidential poll: Syndicate backed Sanjeeva Reddy; Indira backed V.V. Giri with "conscience vote".
Formal split (Nov 1969): Indira expelled ⇒ Congress (O) (Organisation) vs Congress (R) (Requisitionists).

5. 1971 Election & Garibi Hatao

Grand Alliance: Anti-Congress united front (Swatantra, Jan Sangh, SSP, PSP, Cong-O) with slogan "Indira Hatao".
Indira's masterstroke: Positive programmatic slogan "Garibi Hatao"; targeted poor, Dalits, youth, minorities.
Landslide victory: Cong (R) + CPI won 375 seats (Cong-R alone 352 seats); Grand Alliance decimated to <40.
Hegemony sealed: December 1971 Bangladesh Liberation War victory gave Indira unmatched national authority.

6. Nature of the Restored Congress

Reinvention, not restoration: The old umbrella accommodating autonomous internal factions was gone.
High centralisation: Party turned into a command structure resting solely on the supreme charismatic leader.
Weakened organization: Internal elections lapsed; state leadership depended completely on High Command favour.
Fragility: Won mass elections easily, but lost grassroots institutional capacity to absorb popular agitations.
