INFECTIOUS DISEASES & PARASITOLOGY BLUEPRINT
Part 1: Diagnostic Matrix of Common Infectious Diseases
Etiology & Diagnosis
Typhoid Fever Bacterial
• Pathogen: Salmonella typhi.
• Spread: Contaminated food & drinking water.
• Site: Small intestine; can cause intestinal perforation & death.
• Diagnostic Test: Confirmed by Widal test. Sustained high fever (39°–40°C), stomach pain, constipation.
Pneumonia Bacterial
• Pathogens: Streptococcus pneumoniae & Haemophilus influenzae.
• Spread: Inhaling droplets/aerosols; sharing utensils.
• Site: Alveoli fill with fluid → severe respiratory distress.
• Severe Case: Lips and fingernails turn grey to bluish colour.
Common Cold Viral
• Pathogen: Rhino viruses (one of most infectious groups).
• Spread: Inhaled droplets; contaminated objects (pens, cups).
• Site: Infects nose and respiratory passage, but NOT lungs.
• Duration: Nasal congestion, discharge, sore throat, cough (3–7 days).
Amoebiasis / Dysentery Protozoan
• Pathogen: Entamoeba histolytica (large intestine).
• Carrier Vector: Houseflies serve as mechanical carriers.
• Transmission: Fecal contamination of food and drinking water.
• Symptoms: Stools with excess mucous and blood clots, abdominal cramps.
Ascariasis Helminthic
• Pathogen: Ascaris lumbricoides (common roundworm).
• Site: Intestinal parasite.
• Transmission: Eggs excreted in stool contaminate soil, water, plants.
• Symptoms: Internal bleeding, muscular pain, blockage of intestinal passage.
Filariasis (Elephantiasis) Helminthic
• Pathogens: Wuchereria bancrofti & Wuchereria malayi.
• Vector: Transmitted by female Culex mosquito bite.
• Site: Chronic inflammation of lymphatic vessels of lower limbs and genital organs leading to gross deformities.
Ringworms Fungal
• Pathogens: Genera Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton.
• Transmission: Acquired from soil or using towels/clothes of infected persons.
• Symptoms: Dry, scaly circular lesions on skin, nails, scalp with intense itching; thrive in heat & moisture.
Malaria (Malignant Form) Protozoan
• Species: P. vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum.
• Malignant Malaria: Caused by Plasmodium falciparum (most fatal).
• Chills & High Fever: Triggered by release of toxic Haemozoin upon RBC lysis every 3–4 days.
CUET High-Yield Diagnostic Traps:
• Pneumonia vs. Common Cold: Pneumonia infects alveoli (lungs fill with fluid); Common Cold infects upper respiratory passage, NEVER the lungs!
• Typhoid Mary (Mary Mallon): Classic historical asymptomatic carrier who spread typhoid for years through cooked meals.
Part 2: Plasmodium Life Cycle Across Two Hosts
Digenetic Life Cycle
Phase A: Human Host (Asexual Schizogony Cycle) Intermediate Host
1
Infection: Female Anopheles mosquito bites human, injecting infectious sporozoites with saliva into bloodstream.
2
Liver Schizogony: Sporozoites reach liver parenchymal cells within 30 minutes, reproducing asexually to release thousands of merozoites.
3
Erythrocytic Cycle & Haemozoin: Parasites invade RBCs, reproduce asexually, and burst RBCs. Lysis releases toxic haemozoin granule pigment, inducing recurring chills and high fever every 3–4 days.
4
Gametocyte Formation: Some merozoites differentiate in RBCs into male (micro) and female (macro) gametocytes.
Phase B: Mosquito Host (Sexual Sporogony Cycle) Definitive Host
5
Ingestion: Female Anopheles feeds on infected human blood, ingesting mature gametocytes into its digestive tract.
6
Fertilization: Fertilization and zygote formation occur in the mosquito's stomach / gut. Zygote becomes motile ookinete and forms oocyst on gut wall.
7
Sporogony: Oocyst undergoes sporogony, releasing thousands of sporozoites which migrate and lodge in the mosquito's salivary glands, ready to infect a new human host.
CUET Exam Parasitology Checklist:
• Infectious Stage to Humans: Sporozoite (injected with mosquito saliva).
• Infectious Stage to Mosquito: Gametocytes (ingested with blood meal).
• Site of Fertilization: Mosquito gut (stomach), NOT human body!
