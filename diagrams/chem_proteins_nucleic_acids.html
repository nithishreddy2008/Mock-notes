1. Amino Acids & Zwitterion
Monomer Foundations
General Formula: H₂N-CH(R)-COOH ($\alpha$-amino acids). All naturally occurring proteinogenic amino acids have L-configuration.
Chirality Rule: All $\alpha$-amino acids are optically active EXCEPT Glycine (R = H, achiral).
Zwitterion & Isoelectric Point (pI): In aqueous solution, internal proton transfer creates a dipolar ion: ⁺H₃N-CH(R)-COO⁻. At pI, net charge = 0 $\implies$ no migration in an electric field!
Essential vs Non-Essential: 10 Essential amino acids must come from diet: Val, Leu, Ile, Lys, Met, Phe, Thr, Trp, His, Arg.
2. Four Protein Structural Floors
Folding Hierarchy
1° Structure (Primary): Linear covalent sequence of amino acids joined by rigid, planar trans peptide bonds (-CO-NH-).
2° Structure (Secondary): Regular localized folding governed by intramolecular H-bonds ($\alpha$-helix: between C=O of residue $n$ and N-H of $n+4$) or intermolecular H-bonds ($\beta$-pleated sheets).
3° Structure (Tertiary): Overall 3D spatial folding into fibrous or globular shape. Stabilized by: Disulfide bridges (-S-S-), H-bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic forces.
4° Structure (Quaternary): Spatial assembly of two or more distinct polypeptide subunits (e.g. Haemoglobin: $2\alpha + 2\beta$ tetramer).
Denaturation Rule: Heat or extreme pH disrupts 2°, 3°, and 4° structures, causing protein uncoiling and biological inactivation. The primary (1°) covalent structure remains COMPLETELY INTACT!
3. Nucleic Acid Chemistry
Genetic Code
Nucleoside vs Nucleotide:
  • Nucleoside: Nitrogenous Base + Pentose Sugar (attached at C-1').
  • Nucleotide: Nucleoside + Phosphate group (esterified at C-5').
Backbone & Linkage: Nucleotides are linked sequentially via 3', 5'-phosphodiester linkages to form a directional polynucleotide strand ($5' \to 3'$).

Chargaff's Equivalence Rule (DNA): In double-stranded DNA, the molar ratio of Adenine to Thymine and Guanine to Cytosine is strictly 1:1 $\implies A = T$, $G = C$, and $\text{Purines} (A+G) = \text{Pyrimidines} (T+C)$.
4. Enzymes & Hormones
Biochemical Regulators
Enzymes (Biocatalysts): Globular proteins that accelerate metabolic reactions under physiological temperature ($310\text{ K}$) and pH ($7.4$) with extreme substrate specificity via lock-and-key active sites.
Hormone Classification:
  • Steroids: Estrogen, Progesterone, Testosterone, Cortisone.
  • Peptides / Proteins: Insulin (51 residues), Glucagon, Oxytocin.
  • Amine Derivatives: Adrenaline (Epinephrine), Thyroxine (iodinated).
